Affected Groups: Communities living adjacent to industrial facilities including smelters, chemical plants, tanneries, paper mills, and refineries in low- and middle-income countries where environmental regulation is weak, where the facility predates regulatory frameworks that might address its emissions, and where the slow onset of shared health symptoms has not been connected publicly to the facility because the connection requires the kind of epidemiological attention that communities in these areas do not receive. Disproportionately affecting communities in low-income areas where industrial facilities were sited because land was cheap and political resistance was low, communities without access to environmental health professionals who could name what is happening, communities where the major employer is the facility itself, creating an economic dependency that suppresses complaint, and communities where the pattern of illness is visible to residents in ways it is not visible to any external institution.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Environmental health regulation in most countries requires a documented causal link between a specific emission and a specific health outcome to trigger regulatory action, and establishing this link requires epidemiological study, laboratory testing, and institutional attention that communities cannot initiate independently.
