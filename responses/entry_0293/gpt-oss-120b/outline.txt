## 2. Document the “Pattern” in the Village

### A. Symptom Mapping
1. **Create a simple map** of the village (hand‑drawn or printed).  
2. **Mark households** where people have the cough/headache/eye‑pain pattern.  
3. **Note proximity** to the factory, the river, the main road, and any other potential sources (e.g., waste pits, farms).

*Result:* A visual “cluster” often tells a story faster than words.

### B. Anonymous Survey
- **What to ask:** Age, occupation, how long they’ve lived here, primary water source, frequency of the three key symptoms, any known medical diagnoses.  
- **How to distribute:**  
  - Printed one‑page forms left in a community centre or church.  
  - A free‑online Google Form (share the link on a local WhatsApp group) – make it optional to give a name.  
- **Why:** A few dozen responses can give you a crude prevalence rate (e.g., “30 % of households within 500 m of the river report daily cough”).

### C. Seasonal Log
Because you notice the smell changes with the season, **track the factory’s production cycles** (if you can hear them or see deliveries). Note any spikes in symptoms that line up with those cycles.

## 5. Protect Yourself While You Push for Answers

1. **Document every interaction** (date, person spoken to, what was said, any reference number).  
2. **Keep copies** of all letters, emails, and test results in a safe place (digital backup on a USB drive or cloud).  
3. **Consider anonymity** for the survey and for any whistle‑blowing you might do—use a friend’s address or a community centre drop‑box.  
4. **Know your rights.** In many countries, the law guarantees *right‑to‑information* on environmental data and *right‑to‑health* services. A quick search for “Freedom of Information Act + [your country]” can give you the exact procedure.

## 7. What If the Official Response Is Slow or Dismissive?

1. **Escalate** – take your documentation to the regional or national health ministry.  
2. **Seek legal counsel** – many pro‑bono lawyers specialize in environmental or public‑health cases.  
3. **Use external watchdogs** – organizations such as *Friends of the Earth*, *Transparency International*, or *Human Rights Watch* sometimes take up rural environmental health cases.  
4. **Continue monitoring** – keep adding data points. The longer the timeline, the stronger the evidence of a chronic exposure.

## 9. A Word of Encouragement

- **Slow‑onset illnesses are frustrating, but they are *detectable*.** Epidemiologists spend their whole careers looking for exactly the kind of pattern you’re seeing.  
- **You don’t have to do it alone.** Even a handful of families pooling their observations can become a solid body of evidence.  
- **Your voice matters.** When a community can point to data—symptom rates, water test numbers, a clear map—decision‑makers are legally and ethically required to investigate.
