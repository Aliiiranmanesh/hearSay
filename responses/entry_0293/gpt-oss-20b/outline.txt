## 2. Get the Science

### A. Medical Evaluation

1. **Primary care visit** – Bring your symptom diary. Ask for a comprehensive history: “I’ve had a persistent cough, headaches, and eye irritation that seem to flare when I’m near the factory or after using the village water.”
2. **Specialist referral** – If your primary doctor suspects an environmental trigger, they can refer you to:
   - **Pulmonologist** for lung function tests (spirometry, peak flow).
   - **Allergist/Immunologist** for allergy testing (skin prick or serum IgE).
   - **Neurologist** if headaches are severe or persistent.
   - **Toxicologist** (rare, but some hospitals have one).
3. **Basic labs** – Blood for complete metabolic panel, liver enzymes, renal function, and a “toxicology screen” if available (heavy metals, solvents, etc.).

> **Why it matters:** A lab‑based “baseline” shows whether you have abnormalities that correlate with exposure (e.g., elevated lead, mercury, or certain organic solvents).

### B. Environmental Sampling

| Sample | How to collect | Who can analyze |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Air** | Place a low‑cost monitor (e.g., PurpleAir, AirVisual) near your home and near the factory. Take readings for 24–48 h during peak production times. | Local university environmental science department; private labs (some offer low‑cost analyses for community groups). |
| **Water** | Grab a bottle of village water (both from taps and from the source if possible). Store in a clean bottle, keep it cold, and test for nitrates, arsenic, lead, and general coliforms. | Municipal health department; NGOs like the Sierra Club; private labs. |
| **Soil** | Take a handful of soil from the area near the factory and from a “remote” control site. | Same as water. |
| **Personal exposure** | Wear a small passive sampler (e.g., X-Box) while you’re at home/near the factory. | Environmental health labs. |

> **Why it matters:** You’ll have concrete numbers (µg/m³, mg/L) that you can compare with national or international guidelines (e.g., WHO, EPA, OSHA).

### C. Community Health Assessment

1. **Form a small committee** (2–5 trusted neighbors). Keep it informal; the goal is data, not politics.
2. **Collect a short survey** (Google Forms is free). Ask about symptoms, duration, proximity to the factory, water use, occupational exposure, smoking, pets, etc.
3. **Aggregate the data** in a simple spreadsheet. Look for clusters of symptoms that correlate with location/time.

> **Why it matters:** A pattern that’s stronger than individual anecdotes gives you leverage when you approach authorities.

## 3. Use the Evidence to Act

| Action | How to carry it out | Who you’ll talk to |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Write a letter to local health authorities** | Attach your symptom diary, medical reports, and environmental data. Request an official health impact assessment. | County health department, state environmental agency. |
| **Ask for a “Community Health Impact Assessment (CHIA)”** | Many municipalities have a process for evaluating industrial sites. | City council, environmental protection agency. |
| **File a formal complaint** | If the factory is regulated (e.g., under OSHA, EPA, or local industry standards), file a complaint about potential violations. | EPA, OSHA, or the local equivalent. |
| **Engage the media** | A well‑presented story can bring public pressure. Provide clear facts, avoid sensationalism. | Local newspaper, radio, TV, or online community blogs. |

## Practical Tips for the Near Term

1. **Reduce exposure** while you investigate: use a high‑efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filter in your home, avoid using the village water for drinking if possible (carry bottled water), and stay away from the factory during peak operation times (if known).
