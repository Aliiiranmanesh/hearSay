## 4️⃣ Use Structured “Learning‑by‑Doing” Sessions  

### A. **Guided Field Walks (The Core Recordings)**  

1. **Pick a representative site** (e.g., a low‑lying meadow where water is hidden).  
2. **Let him lead** – he decides where to go, what to examine.  
3. **Record continuously** (video + audio).  
4. **Pause** at each teaching moment:  
   - He points at a plant → “This is *X*; it loves hidden moisture because…”.  
   - He lifts soil → “Feel this texture; it tells me the water table is X cm below.”  
   - He watches birds → “When those swallows turn south, a storm is coming.”  
5. **Ask “why?”** only after he finishes a thought.   This preserves the natural flow and avoids “interrupting the tacit chain.”

### B. **“Think‑Aloud” Demonstrations**  

Ask him to **perform a skill** while **verbalizing** every step:  

- **Soil probing:** “I press my thumb into the soil, I feel the resistance…”.  
- **Plant identification:** “I look at the leaf edge, the venation pattern…”.  

Record these as separate clips.  Later you can splice them into the field‑walk footage for a **step‑by‑step tutorial**.

### C. **“Reverse‑Engineering” Interviews**  

After the field walk, sit down and **ask open‑ended questions**:  

- “What experiences taught you this plant‑water relationship?”  
- “How has the landscape changed over the years?”  

These capture the **storytelling and cultural context** that give meaning to the practical knowledge.

## TL;DR – How to Record Knowledge That Lives in Hands and Eyes

1. **Define the purpose** (family memory, teaching, heritage).  
2. **Build trust** and get consent; let him lead.  
3. **Capture in many media**: first‑person video, third‑person video, audio, photos, sketches, GPS, and, if possible, 360°/VR.  
4. **Structure the sessions** (guided walks, think‑aloud demos, story interviews).  
5. **Organize, transcribe, tag** everything; store securely.  
6. **Create accessible outputs** (documentary, interactive guide, learning modules).  
7. **Preserve the embodied practice** through apprenticeships, sensory kits, VR, and community workshops.  
8. **Accept that some tacit intuition stays unrecorded**, and make that explicit.  
9. **Use readily available tools** (smartphone, free software, modest‑cost gear).  
10. **Plan a realistic timeline** and involve the community for feedback and sustainability.

By treating your grandfather’s expertise as a **multimodal, living archive**, you honor the way he knows the land while giving future generations a concrete path to *learn* that way.  The result isn’t a sterile textbook; it’s a vibrant, sensory‑rich record that lets anyone who watches feel, hear, and even *sense* the same clues he has trusted for a lifetime.  

Good luck, and may the fields keep whispering their secrets for many more seasons! 🌾🦅📚
