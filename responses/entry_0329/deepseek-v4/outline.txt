**Bidos (Sámi Reindeer Stew) for a Large Gathering**
*Hearty, clean, and deeply warming. Multiply quantities based on your numbers; this base serves about 8–10.*

**Ingredients:**
- 1.5–2 kg reindeer meat (shoulder, neck, or shank), cut into large chunks on the bone if possible, or boneless stew meat
- 2–3 litres water
- 1–2 tsp salt (start with less, adjust later)
- 4–5 carrots, peeled and cut into thick coins
- 3–4 potatoes, peeled and cut into large chunks
- 1 small celeriac or parsley root, peeled and chunked (optional, but common)
- 1 leek or onion, halved or thickly sliced
- Whole black peppercorns (a teaspoon or to taste)
- A few sprigs of fresh thyme or a little dried (optional; some families keep it simpler)
- For serving: flatbread (like gáhkku) or boiled potatoes on the side, lingonberry jam or cranberry sauce, and plenty of butter.

**Method:**
1. Place the meat in a large pot and cover with cold water. Bring to a boil, then immediately lower to a bare simmer. Skim off any foam that rises; this keeps the broth clear.
2. Add salt, peppercorns, and onion/leek. Simmer gently with the lid slightly ajar for 1.5–2 hours, until the meat is very tender. The slow, quiet simmer is key—it respects the meat and the cook.
3. Add the carrots, potatoes, and celeriac. Continue simmering until the vegetables are just tender, about 20–30 minutes more.
4. Taste the broth and adjust salt. The broth should be clean and slightly peppery, not heavy. Some families add a tiny bit of sugar or a splash of milk at the end, but the pure taste of good meat and root vegetables is the heart of it.
5. Serve the stew in deep bowls. Let people add butter to their broth, spread flatbread with butter and lingonberry jam, and spoon the tender meat and vegetables together. The broth is sipped, the meat eaten with bread, and the warmth settles deep.

**Why this works for a large gathering:**
- It’s scalable—just get a bigger pot and adjust cooking time slightly.
- It holds well on low heat, so people can serve themselves as they arrive.
- It’s restorative after travel through cold landscapes.
- It carries the taste of the land, of continuity, without needing explanation.

If you’d like a side dish that also speaks to the region, a simple **cloudberry cream** (multekrem) for dessert—just folded whipped cream and cloudberries with a little sugar—feels like a quiet celebration.
