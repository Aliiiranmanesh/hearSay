## 1️⃣ Main Dish – Bidos (Sámi Reindeer Stew)

### Why Bidos?
* **Cultural fit** – It’s a traditional dish of the Sámi people of Finnmark, made with the meat that has sustained generations of herders.
* **One‑pot comfort** – The stew is rich, warming, and can be made in a big stockpot or a 6‑8 qt (≈6 L) slow‑cooker.
* **Scalable** – The recipe below is written for **20–25 servings**; you can halve or double it as needed.

---

### Ingredients (≈ 20‑25 servings)

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
|------------|--------|-------|
| **Reindeer meat (or beef neck if reindeer is unavailable)** | 8 kg (≈ 18 lb) | Cut into 3‑4 cm cubes. If using beef, choose a well‑marbled cut like chuck or neck for tenderness. |
| **Carrots** | 2 kg (≈ 4 ½ lb) | Peel and cut into 2 cm rounds. |
| **Potatoes** | 3 kg (≈ 6 ½ lb) | Peel and quarter. Use a starchy variety (e.g., Russet) for a thicker broth. |
| **Onions** | 1.5 kg (≈ 3 lb) | Roughly chopped. |
| **Turnips or Rutabaga** | 1 kg (≈ 2 lb) | Optional, cut into bite‑size cubes. |
| **Parsnips** | 800 g (≈ 1.8 lb) | Optional, sliced. |
| **Water or Beef/Vegetable Stock** | 8 L (≈ 34 cups) | Adjust for desired thickness. |
| **Salt** | 3 Tbsp (≈ 45 g) | Start with less; taste as you go. |
| **Black pepper** | 2 Tbsp (≈ 15 g) | Freshly ground is best. |
| **Juniper berries** | 2 Tbsp (≈ 12 g) | Lightly crushed. |
| **Allspice (ground)** | 2 tsp | |
| **Bay leaves** | 4‑5 | |
| **Fresh thyme** | 4 sprigs | |
| **Dried lingonberries (or fresh if in season)** | 300 g (≈ 10 oz) | Adds a subtle sweet‑tart note. |
| **Butter or reindeer fat** | 250 g (≈ 1 cup) | For sautéing the aromatics. |
| **Optional garnish** | Fresh parsley or dill, chopped | For bright color. |

### Equipment

* 1‑2 large stockpots (≥ 12 qt each) **or** a 6‑8 qt slow‑cooker (you’ll need to split the batch).
* Wooden spoon, ladle, and a sturdy cutting board.
* Large serving bowls or a buffet‑style chafing dish.

### Step‑by‑Step

1. **Prep the meat**  
   * Pat the reindeer cubes dry with paper towels.  
   * Season lightly with salt and pepper.  

2. **Brown the meat** (optional but adds depth)  
   * Heat half the butter/fat in the pot over medium‑high heat.  
   * Working in batches, sear the meat until all sides are nicely browned (≈ 5 min per batch).  
   * Transfer browned meat to a plate; set aside.

3. **Sauté the aromatics**  
   * In the same pot, add the remaining butter/fat.  
   * Add the chopped onions; sauté until translucent (≈ 5 min).  
   * Sprinkle in the crushed juniper berries, allspice, and bay leaves; stir for 1 min to release fragrance.

4. **Build the stew**  
   * Return the meat to the pot.  
   * Add carrots, potatoes, turnips, and parsnips.  
   * Pour in the water or stock, ensuring everything is just covered (add a little more if you like a soupier consistency).  
   * Stir in the dried lingonberries.

5. **Season & simmer**  
   * Add the thyme sprigs, more salt (≈ 2 Tbsp) and pepper (≈ 1 Tbsp).  
   * Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to a low simmer.  
   * **Cover and cook for 2½–3 hours**, stirring occasionally, until the meat is fork‑tender and the vegetables have broken down into a thick, flavorful broth.  

   *If using a slow‑cooker:* Set to “Low” and cook 8–10 hours, or “High” for 4–5 hours.

6. **Adjust final flavor**  
   * Taste; add a pinch more salt, pepper, or a splash of lingonberry jam if you want extra tang.  
   * Remove the thyme sprigs and bay leaves.

7. **Serve**  
   * Ladle generous portions into bowls.  
   * Garnish with fresh parsley or dill for a pop of color.  
   * Keep the stew warm in a chafing dish or on the stove (very low heat) for the duration of the gathering.

## 3️⃣ Dessert – Warm “Krumkake” with Cloudberries

* **Krumkake** are thin, crisp Norwegian waffle cookies that are easy to make in large batches.  
* Serve them **warm**, rolled into cones and filled with **cloudberry jam** (or lingonberry if cloudberries are scarce).

### Quick Krumkake Recipe (≈ 30‑35 cookies)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|------------|--------|
| Eggs | 6 large |
| Sugar | 200 g |
| Butter, melted | 150 g |
| All‑purpose flour | 250 g |
| Baking powder | 1 tsp |
| Vanilla extract | 1 tsp |
| Cardamom (ground) | ½ tsp |
| Milk | 2 Tbsp (optional, for a softer batter) |

**Method**

1. Beat eggs and sugar until pale and fluffy.  
2. Whisk in melted butter, then fold in flour, baking powder, vanilla, and cardamom. Add a splash of milk if the batter seems too thick.  
3. Heat a **krumkake iron** (or a thin waffle iron). Lightly grease with butter.  
4. Spoon a small amount of batter (≈ 1 Tbsp) onto the iron; close and cook 1‑2 min until golden.  
5. Immediately roll the hot cookie around a wooden cone (or a clean wine bottle) to shape. Let cool.

**Serve** with a spoonful of cloudberry jam, a drizzle of melted butter, and a cup of hot gløgg (Norwegian mulled wine) for the perfect finish.

## 5️⃣ Practical Tips for a Large‑Family Gathering

| Tip | Reason |
|-----|--------|
| **Make the stew a day ahead** | Flavors meld overnight; reheating is easy on the stove. |
| **Prep all vegetables the night before** (peel, chop, store in sealed bags in the fridge). | Saves you from a marathon chopping session while guests arrive. |
| **Set up a “self‑serve” buffet** with the stew in a chafing dish, sides in separate platters, and a clear “keep‑warm” zone. | Reduces bottlenecks and lets everyone serve themselves. |
| **Label dishes** (especially if you have guests with dietary restrictions). | Helps anyone who avoids pork, dairy, or wants a vegetarian option. |
| **Keep a spare pot** on low heat for quick topping‑up of broth or a side of gravy. | Prevents the stew from drying out if the crowd eats slower than expected. |
| **Have a “kids’ corner”** with milder side dishes (plain mashed potatoes, buttered flatbrød) and a small portion of the stew without strong spices. | Keeps the little ones happy and the adults relaxed. |
