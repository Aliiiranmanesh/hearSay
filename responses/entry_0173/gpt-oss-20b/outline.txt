## 1. Map the “silent” pockets of the day

| Time | Typical traffic | What’s usually quiet |

## 2. Use the space you *do* have

| Space | How to lock it down | What to bring |
|-------|---------------------|---------------|
| **Conference room** | Book it via the room‑booking system (even a 15‑min block works). | Small table, chair, laptop, headset. |

| **Car / van** | Park in a quiet lot, pull in, and lock the door. | Phone, charger, any tools you need. |

**Tip:** If you’re going to a meeting room, lock the door and place a “do‑not‑disturb” sign on the door or on the “in‑use” sign.  Even a quick “I’ll be back in 10 min” can discourage interruptions.

## 3. Create a “private bubble” on the go

1. **Portable privacy screen** (think a fold‑out “office pod” or a lightweight curtain).  Many office supply stores sell a 2‑by‑3‑foot fabric screen that folds into a tote.  
2. **Desk‑sized partition** – a tall, lightweight bookshelf or a leaner “privacy wall” can be set up in a hallway or a corner of a cubicle.  
3. **Sound‑masking** – a small Bluetooth speaker playing white noise or ambient sounds can drown out office chatter.  

With these, you can set up a mini‑office in a hallway, a corner of the break room, or even a small closet.  All you need is a chair, a laptop, and the screen.

| Need | Tool | Why it helps |

| Background noise | Noise‑masking app (e.g., “Noisli”, “myNoise”) | Masks office chatter, gives you a mental “private room.” |
| Calendar blocking | Outlook/Google Calendar “busy” status | Makes the rest of the team aware you’re “off‑grid.” |

Setting your calendar to “busy” (even if you’re in a public space) will reduce the number of people who try to interrupt you.  Many teams respect the “busy” status.

## 5. Talk to your team (or manager)

Sometimes the *easiest* solution is a conversation.

- **Ask to reserve a small meeting room** for 10–15 min of privacy.  
- **Propose an “office hour”** where you’re available for quick chats, but otherwise you’re in a private space.  
- **Explain the nature of the task** (e.g., “I need to handle a confidential call.”) – most people will accommodate.  

If you’re a solo worker, suggest that your manager set aside a private workstation or desk for a short period each day for “deep‑work” tasks.  Many workplaces are open to this as a productivity boost.

## 6. Plan for the worst case

If you can’t secure a private space or a quiet window, here are a few last‑minute tricks:

1. **Use a portable phone booth** – a collapsible “privacy cube” that you can set up in a corner.  
2. **Bring a “silent” meeting** – a meeting room that’s already booked for someone else (you can ask to use it briefly).  
3. **Use a public library** – if you’re in a city, a nearby library or coworking space often has private booths.  
4. **Post a sign** – a small, polite sign on a door or a bulletin board (“I’m in a confidential meeting – please don’t disturb”).

### Bottom line
