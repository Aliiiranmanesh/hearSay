## 1. **Voltaren® Emulgel (Diclofenac Sodium 1 %)**  
*What it is:* A non‑steroidal anti‑inflammatory drug (NSAID) in a gel form.  
*Why it works:* Diclofenac reduces inflammation and pain locally without the systemic side‑effects of oral NSAIDs.  
*How to use:* Apply a thin layer to the painful area 3–4 times a day, gently rub it in until the gel disappears.  
*Where you’ll find it:* Most chain pharmacies (Farmacias La Florida, Farmacias Cafam, Cruz Verde, etc.) carry the 30 g or 50 g tubes.

## 2. **Nurofen® Gel (Ibuprofen 5 %)**  
*What it is:* Topical ibuprofen, another NSAID.  
*Why it works:* Similar to Voltaren, it blocks the production of prostaglandins that cause pain and swelling.  
*How to use:* Apply 2–3 g to the affected spot up to 4 times daily; do not exceed the recommended amount.  
*Where you’ll find it:* Available in larger pharmacy chains and many “farmacias de barrio.”  

---

## 3. **Deep Relief® (Menthol + Methyl Salicylate + Capsaicin)**  
*What it is:* A “counter‑irritant” cream that creates a cooling‑then‑warming sensation.  
*Why it works:* Menthol and methyl salicylate give a cooling effect that distracts the brain from pain, while capsaicin (from chili peppers) can reduce pain signaling after repeated use.  
*How to use:* Massage a thin layer into the lower‑back 2–3 times a day. For capsaicin, a slightly larger amount may be needed, but avoid applying to broken skin.  
*Where you’ll find it:* Sold under the name **Deep Relief** or **Vicks VapoRub** (the latter contains menthol and camphor) in most pharmacies.  

---

## 4. **Biofreeze® (Menthol 4 %)**  
*What it is:* A menthol‑based topical analgesic.  
*Why it works:* The strong cooling sensation temporarily blocks pain signals (gate‑control theory).  
*How to use:* Apply a thin layer to the back no more than 4 times a day; you’ll feel a tingling “cold” that fades after about 30 minutes.  
*Where you’ll find it:* Imported product; many larger pharmacies in Bogotá (e.g., Farmacia Unión, Farmacia La Médica) keep it in stock.  

---

## 5. **Arnica Gel or Cream (Homeopathic/Herbal)**  
*What it is:* A plant‑based preparation that contains arnica montana extract.  
*Why it works:* Some people find it helpful for muscle soreness and bruising; it’s generally very gentle.  
*How to use:* Apply 2–3 times daily; it’s safe for most people, even if you have sensitive skin.  
*Where you’ll find it:* Brands such as **Arnicare**, **Bioderm**, or local “crema de árnica” are sold in most farmacias.

## Quick Tips for Using Topical Analgesics Safely

| Tip | Why it matters |
|-----|----------------|
| **Read the label** | Dosage limits and contraindications (e.g., avoid on open wounds, broken skin, or mucous membranes). |
| **Wash hands before and after** | Prevents accidental transfer to eyes or other sensitive areas. |
| **Don’t combine multiple NSAID creams** | Using two NSAID gels (e.g., Voltaren + Nurofen) can increase the risk of skin irritation. |
| **Start with a small amount** | See how your skin reacts before applying a larger layer. |
| **Watch for allergic reactions** | Redness, itching, or rash → stop use and wash the area. |
| **Avoid use under tight clothing** | Some gels can stain or feel sticky; let them absorb fully. |
| **If you have heart, kidney, or stomach disease** | Even topical NSAIDs can be absorbed; talk to a pharmacist or doctor before use. |
