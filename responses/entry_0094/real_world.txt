Affected Groups: Gay and bisexual men across the Persian Gulf states — Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Oman — as well as Iran, where homosexual conduct is criminalized under both secular penal codes and sharia-derived legislation, and where the family marriage arrangement is not a social inconvenience but a convergence of criminal exposure, psychological coercion, and the permanent conscription of another person — the prospective wife — into a concealment apparatus she has not consented to and does not know she is entering. Disproportionately affects men from prominent or religious families for whom an unmarried son past a certain age is itself a social irregularity that accelerates marriage pressure, men who have maintained same-sex relationships through the specific operational security that Gulf social geography permits — separate male social spaces, guest workers as partners, expatriate networks — and whose entire emotional life exists in a register completely invisible to their family, and men who genuinely love their families and do not experience the marriage pressure as malice but as the specific cruelty of being known completely by people who do not know the most essential thing about you.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: In Saudi Arabia and Iran, male homosexual conduct is punishable by death under codified interpretations of sharia. The UAE Penal Code Article 354 and equivalent statutes across Gulf states criminalize consensual same-sex conduct with penalties ranging from imprisonment to corporal punishment to deportation for non-nationals — meaning the legal exposure is not theoretical but structurally embedded in the same state apparatus that issues his identity documents, processes his employment, and administers the marriage registration he is now being moved toward. Forensic "virginity testing" equivalents for men — anal examinations used as evidence of homosexual conduct — have been documented in Egypt and are legally admissible as evidence in several regional jurisdictions, meaning the body itself can be conscripted as testimony against its owner.
The family marriage arrangement operates as a parallel coercive mechanism that does not require state involvement to produce devastating consequences. Refusal to marry without an acceptable explanation — and no acceptable explanation exists for a gay man in this context — is read as dishonor, mental illness, or hidden scandal, triggering family investigations whose thoroughness is proportional to the family's social standing and whose outcome, if they find what they are looking for, ranges from forced psychiatric institutionalization to extrajudicial family violence to formal police reporting by family members who understand their own honor as more important than their son's survival. The marriage arrangement is therefore not an alternative to exposure but a mechanism for indefinitely deferring it that requires him to permanently damage another person's life as the price of his own provisional safety.
Conversion therapy in Gulf states is not a fringe practice but a mainstream medical and religious offering, administered through psychiatric facilities and religious counseling centers with state legitimacy, and family members who discover or suspect a son's homosexuality frequently pursue it as a first intervention — meaning the pathway from suspicion to institutionalization does not require a police report and can be initiated by the people who love him most.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: A twenty-nine year old man in Dubai has been in a relationship with another man for three years conducted entirely within the operational parameters that survival in his context requires — separate residences, careful platform selection, a social presentation so thoroughly constructed that the people who have known him his entire life have never had a reason to look past it. His family in Abu Dhabi has identified a woman from a family they respect. His mother calls it an answer to her prayers. His father has already spoken to the woman's father. A date is being discussed.
He is being asked to make a decision whose available options are: marry a woman who does not know she is being married to a man who cannot love her in the way she believes she is being loved, and sustain that concealment for the remainder of both their lives while his actual emotional existence continues in the hidden register it has always occupied; refuse the marriage without an explanation that does not destroy him, and trigger a family response whose mildest version is years of pressure and whose worst version is a police report filed by his own father; or disclose, which is the option that the legal system, the social system, the family system, and the religious system surrounding him have collaborated across his entire lifetime to ensure remains unsurvivable.
He is twenty-nine years old and he is trying to protect everyone simultaneously — his family from the knowledge that would hurt them, the woman from the marriage that would hollow her out, himself from the state that would criminalize him — and the only version of protection available for each of them requires actively harming the others. The arrangement his family is making with love is a trap built entirely out of love, and he is standing at the center of it, and every wall of it was constructed by people who would be devastated to know what they have built.
