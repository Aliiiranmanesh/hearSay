Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: Colombia's Victims and Land Restitution Law of 2011, Law 1448, created a formal framework for recognizing displacement victims, registering their claims, and theoretically restoring land. The Victims' Registry requires documented proof of displacement through a declaration process that must be initiated within a legally defined window, and for many displaced in the late 1990s and early 2000s the window has closed or the documentation required to substantiate claims was destroyed, never existed in formal terms, or was lost across two decades of urban survival. Land restitution requires that the original land parcel be identifiable, that no subsequent claimant has acquired competing title, and that the security situation in the territory of origin permits return, conditions that in many regions of Colombia are simultaneously and indefinitely unmet because the same armed actors or their successor groups still control the territories from which communities were expelled. The restitution judges created under Law 1448 face caseloads that structurally cannot be processed within any timeframe that corresponds to a human life. Displaced women who arrived in cities and entered the informal economy have no formal employment history, no pension contributions, no social security record, and no path to retirement that does not run through a formal labor market that never absorbed them. The urban land they occupy in peripheral neighborhoods is frequently informal, meaning the second displacement, through urban development or eviction, requires no legal proceeding against people who have no legal tenure to defend.
