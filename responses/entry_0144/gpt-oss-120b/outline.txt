## 5. Engage With the Community – You Don’t Have to Do It All Alone

1. **Local disability‑rights groups** – Many cities have a *Blind/Visually Impaired Association* that offers orientation‑and‑mobility (O&M) training. A short O&M class (often 4–6 sessions) can dramatically boost confidence.  
2. **Ask shopkeepers for a “consistent spot”** – Even if the layout changes weekly, most owners will be happy to **reserve a corner** for you (e.g., “the basket with oranges is always on the right of the entrance”). A simple sticky note on the door can remind them.  
3. **Request audible traffic signals** – Write a polite letter (or email) to the municipal traffic department. Many cities have a budget for installing **“audible crossing beeps”** and may prioritize areas with higher need.  
4. **Neighbourhood “check‑in” apps** – Apps like *Nextdoor* or local Facebook groups let you post “I’ll be at the market at 10 am, could anyone give me a quick wave if you see me?” It’s a low‑effort safety net.

## 7. Putting It All Together – A Sample First‑Trip Plan

1. **Night before** – Write a short route script:  
   *“Leave house, turn right at the blue mailbox, walk 20 m, listen for the bakery’s bell, turn left at the bakery, cross the street (wait for the beep), walk 15 m, market entrance on the right.”*  

2. **Morning** – Charge phone, enable Google Maps voice navigation, and open the script on a sticky note taped to your cane.  

3. **At the curb** – Feel for the tactile paving with your cane tip (the “click” tells you you’re on the curb).  

4. **Crossing** – Listen for the audible signal (or feel the vibration if your city has it).  

5. **Mid‑route** – When the bakery bell rings, pause, count the steps you’ve taken, and confirm you’re at the expected distance (use the phone’s “distance traveled” read‑out if you like).  

6. **Arrival** – Market entrance should be on the right; if you’re unsure, ask a staff member for help (a quick “Can you point me to the entrance?” works).  

7. **Return** – Use the same voice navigation in reverse; the app will automatically give you “turn left/right” cues for the way back.  

8. **After** – Write down any differences you noticed (e.g., a new construction detour). Update your script for next time.
