Twenty-three years on one floor isn’t just a job. It’s a rhythm, a place in the world, a thing your hands knew how to do without thinking. When that disappears, especially when the building itself gets erased, it’s normal to feel unmoored. You don’t need a grand reinvention. Here are four realistic, hands-on paths for a man in his fifties that don’t require starting over from scratch, don’t demand heavy physical strain, and pay modest but reliable wages:

**1. Facilities / Maintenance Technician**  
*What:* Schools, hospitals, municipal buildings, small commercial properties, or senior living facilities need people who can change filters, patch drywall, fix leaky faucets, maintain HVAC basics, and keep things running.  
*Why it fits:* These places value reliability, safety awareness, and calm problem-solving over speed or youth. Training is usually on-the-job. Hours are structured, often with benefits.  
*How to start:* Check your local school district, county, or hospital job boards. Look for “Maintenance Worker I,” “Facilities Aide,” or “Building Technician.” Many hire without certifications.  
*Pay:* $18–$28/hr depending on location and employer. Often includes health benefits and pension eligibility.

**2. Handyman / Property Maintenance (Self-Directed)**  
*What:* Small repairs for homeowners or landlords: shelf mounting, gutter cleaning, door adjustments, minor plumbing, deck staining, appliance installation, etc.  
*Why it fits:* You set the pace, work with your hands, see immediate results. Starts small and scales only if you want it to. No boss, no course, just tools and reputation.  
*How to start:* Buy a solid basic toolset. Post on Nextdoor, local Facebook groups, or leave cards at hardware stores. Partner with one local property manager for steady small jobs. In most areas, you don’t need a license for jobs under $500–$1,000.  
*Pay:* $25–$40/hr once you have a few repeat clients. Can start part-time while you figure out the rhythm.

**3. Municipal Parks & Grounds / Public Works Crew**  
*What:* Maintaining parks, trails, public buildings, or street infrastructure. Tasks include equipment operation, landscaping, minor repairs, snow/ice control, and facility upkeep.  
*Why it fits:* Structured hours, outdoor but paced, community-facing, and often unionized. Municipal hiring heavily favors consistency and work ethic over age.  
*How to start:* City/county job portals. Search “Groundskeeper,” “Maintenance Worker,” or “Public Works Laborer.” Many offer seasonal-to-permanent tracks.  
*Pay:* $17–$25/hr, usually with solid benefits, overtime, and long-term stability.

**4. Appliance or Small Engine Repair (Entry-Level Tech)**  
*What:* Diagnosing and fixing washers, dryers, lawn mowers, generators, or pressure washers. Growing demand as people repair instead of replace.  
*Why it fits:* Tactile, methodical, and deeply satisfying. You work at a bench or in driveways. No corporate ladder, just problems and solutions.  
*How to start:* Look for a 6–12 week certificate at a community college, or walk into a local repair shop and ask to shadow or start as a helper. YouTube + practice on broken units builds real skill fast.  
*Pay:* $20–$30/hr. Can go independent later with minimal overhead.
