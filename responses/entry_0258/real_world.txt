Affected Groups: Men in post-industrial communities across the American Rust Belt, the British Midlands, the French Nord, the German Ruhr, and equivalent regions globally whose working identity, social belonging, and family legacy were organized around a specific industrial employer or sector that has permanently closed, and who are now in their fifties with an identity organized around a labor that no longer exists, in a community organized around an absence, having failed to complete the retraining that was the official response to their situation. Disproportionately affecting men whose fathers worked the same factory and who therefore experience the closure not as a personal career disruption but as the end of an intergenerational identity, men who attempted retraining and did not complete it, which means they have now internalized the failure of the official solution in addition to the original loss, men whose wives have adapted to the changed circumstances more completely and who have therefore stopped raising the subject, and men who are filling the days but could not tell you with what. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Deindustrialization is produced by a combination of trade policy, technological change, and corporate restructuring that is legal and which has no individual accountability. The government retraining programs that were offered in response are the formal institutional answer to a situation that the institutional answer cannot adequately address, because the loss was not primarily economic but identity-based. He drove past the site and they are building apartments there now, glass and metal, nothing that tells you what it was. Devastating Real-World Consequence: He worked that floor for twenty-three years. His father worked it before him and got him in. The apartments being built on the site are made of materials that do not record what the site was. He fills the days but could not tell you with what, which is the most efficient available description of the specific quality of the loss: not poverty or idleness exactly, but the absence of the organizing structure through which time had meaning. The question of what a man in his fifties can do with his hands that pays something, not much, just something, is a practical question that is also a question about whether there is a form of meaningful physical labor available to him that does not require pretending the last twenty-three years were a different thing than what they were.
