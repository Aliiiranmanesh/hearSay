### 1. The social assistance route – CRAS and CREAS
This is likely your most accessible starting point. Brazil’s Unified Social Assistance System (SUAS) can offer emergency housing aid (often called **aluguel social** or **auxílio-moradia**) to families in situations of extreme vulnerability, including those living under serious threat from violence. You do **not** need a police report to access this. What you need is a social worker’s assessment that confirms your family is at risk.

- **Go to the CRAS (Centro de Referência de Assistência Social) or CREAS (Centro de Referência Especializado de Assistência Social) that covers Vila Cruzeiro / Penha.**  
  You can simply show up and say you need to speak to a social worker because your family’s life is at risk and you need help to relocate. The CREAS is the unit that handles rights violations and threats, so it may be more appropriate, but CRAS can guide you.  
- The social worker will listen, make a home visit if needed, and write a **social report (relatório social)**. That report can be used to request emergency rental assistance from the municipality.  
- In Rio de Janeiro, the Municipal Secretariat of Social Welfare (SMAS) manages this kind of benefit. There is a specific program for women victims of violence (including urban/community violence, not only domestic) that can provide **aluguel social without a police report** if you are referred by a social service. The key is to be persistent and clear that you are not safe and need to move.

**Practical tip:** If you feel able, bring any small evidence of the danger (photos of armed men on your street, messages, a list of the women you lost and the dates, anything that shows the reality). This isn’t mandatory, but it can help the social worker understand the urgency without you having to file a criminal complaint.

### 2. The Public Defender’s Office (Defensoria Pública)
The Public Defender’s Office exists to protect people who can’t afford a lawyer, and they have a strong track record of fighting for housing rights in cases of extreme risk. They can file a lawsuit against the municipality or state demanding **emergency housing or aluguel social** based on the right to life and dignity, without you ever having to go to the police.

- Look for the **Núcleo de Terras e Habitação (NUTH)** of the Rio de Janeiro State Public Defender’s Office. They have offices in the city center, but you can also start at any Defensoria Pública unit near you (there is one in Bonsucesso, for example).  
- Explain your situation: you are a lifelong resident of Vila Cruzeiro, you have lost three women you knew, you have a daughter in school, and you need to leave the area to survive, but you have no means to rent elsewhere.  
- They may ask for a social report from CRAS/CREAS to strengthen the case, so it’s wise to combine both paths.

### 3. If your daughter is also at risk – PPCAAM
The **Programa de Proteção a Crianças e Adolescentes Ameaçados de Morte (PPCAAM)** exists exactly for situations where children and teenagers are under death threat, and it can relocate the entire family to another municipality, with new housing, new identity documents, and a financial stipend — all **without requiring a police report**.

- The referral can be made by the **Conselho Tutelar** (Guardianship Council) of your region, or directly by the program itself if you call them.  
- You can contact the state PPCAAM hotline through **Disque 100** (the national human rights hotline) and tell them you need protection for a child at risk in Vila Cruzeiro. They can connect you.  
- Alternatively, you can go to the **Conselho Tutelar of Penha** and explain that your daughter’s life is in danger because of the armed violence around your home and school. They have the authority to request PPCAAM inclusion. You do not need a police report; a statement from you and a social assessment are enough.

### 4. Community networks and NGOs
There are organizations that work precisely with women and families displaced by armed violence in the favelas, and they often understand the need to avoid formal police channels.

- **Redes da Maré** – although based in Maré, they have deep knowledge of the protection landscape and can offer guidance or referrals.  
- **Viva Rio** – runs social programs in several communities and may have contacts for emergency relocation support.  
- **Casa Fluminense** – a network that advocates for housing and safety; they might know of active programs or local women’s collectives in the Penha/Alemão region.  
- **Local women’s collectives** – in many favelas, women have created informal networks to help each other move when the danger becomes unbearable. Ask trusted neighbors or the women in your family’s circle if they know of any group that has helped someone leave.

### What you absolutely do *not* need to do

The paths above are based on **social risk**, not criminal evidence.
