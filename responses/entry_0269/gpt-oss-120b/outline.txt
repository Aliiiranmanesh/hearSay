## 2. A simple “odor‑out” routine (5 minutes total)

1. **Change out of work clothes** *before* you get into the car.  
   - If you can’t change at work, keep a **plastic “change‑room” bag** (the kind used for travel toiletries) in your locker. Slip your shirt/pants into it, zip it, and toss it into your work bag. The sealed bag prevents the smell from escaping.

2. **Shake and air‑out the bag**  
   - Open the main bag (or the inner garment bag) in a well‑ventilated area (e.g., a hallway, a restroom with a fan). Give it a quick shake to dislodge any loose particles. This also helps the charcoal and baking soda start working.

3. **Spritz the interior** (if you’re using a fabric refresher)  
   - One quick spray, let it dry. The spray does not replace washing; it just “freezes” odor molecules so they don’t migrate.

4. **Seal the bag** with the liner/inner bag in place.  
   - The liner catches any stray particles that might otherwise cling to the bag’s fabric.

5. **Store the bag in the car** *away from the passenger seat* (e.g., the trunk or the floor behind the front seats).  
   - Even with the best odor‑absorbers, the bag will still have a faint “background” scent. Keeping it out of the cabin air circulation zone prevents that scent from spreading.

| **Using regular plastic zip‑top bags only** | Plastic is non‑breathable, so moisture and odor are trapped inside and can become *worse* over time. |
