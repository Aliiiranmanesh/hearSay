That’s a really honest question, and I can tell you’ve thought a lot about how to manage something that follows you home in more ways than one. It makes sense that you’d want to keep the smell from settling into your car—once it’s in the upholstery, it’s hard to get out.

The most effective thing you can put *in* your work bag to stop the smell from escaping is a combination of **containment** and **absorption**. Here’s what works for people who deal with strong industrial, biological, or smoke odors:

**1. An odor-proof, sealed inner bag**  
This is the real key. You don’t just want a regular duffel—you want a barrier that traps volatile compounds. Look for:
- **Heavy-duty dry bags** (like those used for kayaking). Roll the top down 3-4 times and clip it shut; they’re airtight and waterproof, which also means odor-proof.
- **Loksak OPSAK or similar odor-proof bags** – originally made for backpacking/hunting to keep food smells away from bears, they’re completely scent-tight. You can put your work clothes or soiled gear inside one of these, seal it, and then put that inside your regular work bag. They come in multiple sizes.
- A simple **heavy-duty unscented trash compactor bag** twisted shut and secured with a tight rubber band can work surprisingly well in a pinch, but it’s not as reliable as a true seal.

**2. Activated charcoal or zeolite pouches inside the bag**  
Even with a sealed inner bag, the outside of your work bag can pick up odor. Place a few **activated charcoal deodorizer bags** (the kind you can “recharge” in the sun) inside your main work bag. They absorb airborne smells continuously without adding any fragrance. For something stronger, you can find granular activated charcoal in small fabric pouches at hunting supply stores—they’re designed to eliminate human scent.

**3. A dedicated, non-porous work bag**  
If your current bag is fabric, it’s likely holding onto smells and transferring them. Switching to a bag made of **TPU (thermoplastic polyurethane)** or heavy vinyl with welded seams means you can wipe down the entire interior and exterior. Some mechanics and food processing workers use a simple **5-gallon bucket with a Gamma Seal lid** as a “work bag”—it’s completely airtight, easy to clean, and doubles as a seat if you need to change shoes.

**A few extra things that might help the car situation:**  
- **Change before you get in the car.** If possible, strip off the outermost layer (coveralls, boots, hair covering) and bag them immediately before sitting down. Many people keep a clean set of sweatpants and a T-shirt in the trunk for the drive home.
- **Keep a dedicated “car seat cover”** —a thick cotton towel or a washable pet seat cover—that you remove and wash regularly.
- **Shoe covers or a separate shoe bin.** Shoes often carry the strongest smell. Slip-on silicone shoe covers (or even plastic grocery bags) over work boots before entering the car, then remove them once you’re seated with your feet on a rubber mat.
