Affected Groups: Workers in slaughterhouses, meat processing facilities, and other industrial animal processing settings in high-income countries, who perform work that is economically essential, legally permitted, and socially necessary for the food system that surrounds them, and who cannot discuss their work in ordinary social contexts because the gap between what they do and what the people eating the food they process are willing to know about produces a social dynamic that requires them to manage their occupational identity through strategic vagueness. Disproportionately affecting workers in communities where the slaughterhouse is a major employer and whose social environment therefore includes many people doing the same work and few people to whom it can be discussed across the occupational divide, workers whose partners have developed a form of protective non-inquiry as the mechanism for managing the knowledge gap within the household, and workers who are managing the sensory carryover of the work, particularly smell, as a daily decontamination ritual.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The mechanism is the social contract around meat consumption that requires the work to be done and the work to be invisible, and that produces a social environment in which the person doing the work must manage their occupational identity as a liability rather than a straightforward answer to what do you do. She asked what kind and he said large scale and she nodded and moved on, which is the social dynamic the language is designed to produce.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He showers twice before he feels right again and sometimes three times. His wife has never asked about a shift, which he understands as kindness, which it is, and which is also the household version of the same protective non-inquiry that the social dinner required him to produce in the other direction. He knows which words make people move on. The question about what to put in a work bag so the smell does not follow him into the car is a practical occupational hygiene question that is also the form his agency takes in managing the sensory evidence of work that the social world around him has decided it does not want to know about.
