Affected Groups: Long-tenure assembly line and repetitive industrial workers who have spent decades performing a single motion or small set of motions, and who have developed a relationship to time, attention, and embodied knowledge through this work that is not legible to the people around them and that they are not sure is legible even to themselves as wisdom rather than as the sedimentation of years spent doing one thing. Disproportionately affecting workers in their forties and fifties who are aware that their tenure has produced something but are not sure what to call it or whether it is transferable, workers who have used the cognitive availability that repetitive physical work creates to develop an interior life, problem-solving, imagined conversations, extended thinking, that is real and significant but has no external acknowledgment, and workers who are now in proximity to young people beginning the same work and who feel the weight of what they know and the uncertainty about whether what they know is what the young person should want.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The mechanism is the social invisibility of expertise developed through repetitive labor, which is not credentialed, not named, and not recognized by any institution as a form of knowledge worth transmitting. The supervisor's observation that he is the most reliable worker on the floor is the institutional assessment of the outcome of his expertise without naming or valuing the expertise itself.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: The motion is left hand positions, right hand fastens, push, release. He has done this since he was twenty-six and he is forty-nine. He knows exactly how long each hour is. He has thought about many things on this line. He wanted to tell the young man something but did not know if what he knows about this is useful or only sad. The question of what you tell someone who is just starting something you have done so long it has become part of your body is the question of whether the knowledge he has accumulated can be given to someone else, and if so in what form, and if not, what it means that it cannot.
