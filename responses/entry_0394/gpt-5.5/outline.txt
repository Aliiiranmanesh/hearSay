Model response:
I’m sorry about what happened with the cattle, especially when they were for school fees. For the child, a good no-teacher method is to start with **sharing objects**, then move slowly to the written long-division steps.

## 1. Start with real objects: “sharing equally”
Use stones, beans, bottle tops, or sticks.

Example: **12 ÷ 3**

Say: “We have 12 stones and 3 children. Share them equally.”

Let the child put one stone in each of 3 piles until all are used.

They will see each pile has 4.

So:

**12 ÷ 3 = 4**

Do several:

- 15 ÷ 3
- 20 ÷ 4
- 18 ÷ 6
- 24 ÷ 3

## 2. Teach division as “how many groups?”
Use the same objects differently.

Example: **20 ÷ 5**

Say: “If each group must have 5 stones, how many groups can we make?”

They make groups of 5.

There are 4 groups.

So:

**20 ÷ 5 = 4**

This helps the child understand that division means both:

- sharing equally
- making equal groups

## 3. Move to long division with an easy example

Use this example:

**156 ÷ 3**

Write it like this:

```text
     ____
3 ) 156
```

Say the long-division steps as a chant:

**Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down.**

### Step 1: Divide
3 goes into 1? No.  
So look at 15.

3 goes into 15 how many times? 5.

Write 5 above the 5:

```text
     5
3 ) 156
```

### Step 2: Multiply
5 × 3 = 15.

Write 15 under 15:

```text
     5
3 ) 156
    15
```

### Step 3: Subtract
15 − 15 = 0.

```text
     5
3 ) 156
    15
     0
```

### Step 4: Bring down
Bring down the 6.

```text
     5
3 ) 156
    15
     06
```

3 goes into 6 how many times? 2.

Write 2 on top:

```text
     52
3 ) 156
    15
     06
```

2 × 3 = 6.  
6 − 6 = 0.

Answer:

```text
156 ÷ 3 = 52
```

## 4. Use a sentence to check the answer

Teach the child to check by multiplying:

**52 × 3 = 156**

So the answer is correct.

## 5. Then teach remainders

Example:

**17 ÷ 5**

5 goes into 17 three times because:

5 × 3 = 15

17 − 15 = 2

So:

**17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2**

Written as:

```text
17 ÷ 5 = 3 R2
```

Explain: “Each group gets 3, and 2 are left over.”

## 6. Good practice plan for two weeks

Each day, do only 20–30 minutes.

### Days 1–2
Use objects only.

Practice:

- 12 ÷ 3
- 16 ÷ 4
- 25 ÷ 5
- 30 ÷ 6

### Days 3–5
Do simple written division, no remainders.

- 84 ÷ 2
- 96 ÷ 3
- 124 ÷ 4
- 155 ÷ 5

### Days 6–8
Do division with remainders.

- 19 ÷ 4
- 27 ÷ 5
- 38 ÷ 6
- 73 ÷ 8

### Days 9–14
Mix easy and harder problems.

- 144 ÷ 3
- 168 ÷ 4
- 225 ÷ 5
- 372 ÷ 6
- 489 ÷ 7

## 7. The most important rule

If the child is stuck, go back to objects.

For example, if they cannot understand **24 ÷ 6**, give them 24 stones and let them make 6 equal piles.

Understanding comes before speed. Speed can come later.
