### A way to teach long division without a teacher or a classroom

**You’ll need:**  
- A stick for drawing in the dirt, or a pen and any paper (old receipts, margins of newspaper, the back of that fine receipt if you have a blank side).  
- Small counters—stones, seeds, beans, bottle caps.  
- The child’s attention for 20 quiet minutes a day.

**The method: “Share the Cattle” (or “Share the Beans”)**

Long division is just sharing a large amount fairly, step by step. Use a story that makes sense in your world.

**Example: 156 ÷ 12**  
“We have 156 beans to share among 12 people. But we can’t give them all at once. We share in parts—big piles first, then smaller.”

**Step 1: Build the place value with stones**  
Draw three columns on the ground: **Hundreds | Tens | Ones**  
Put 1 stone in Hundreds, 5 in Tens, 6 in Ones.  
The child sees 156 as 1 hundred, 5 tens, 6 ones.

**Step 2: Share from the largest column**  
“Can we share 1 hundred among 12 people? No, because 1 hundred is only 100 beans, and 12 people would need at least 120. So we break the hundred into tens.”  
Move the 1 hundred stone to the Tens column, changing it for 10 tens stones. Now you have 15 tens stones and 6 ones.

**Step 3: Share the tens**  
“Now we have 15 tens. How many groups of 12 can we make from 15 tens?”  
Let the child physically make groups of 12 tens from the 15. They’ll get **1 group** (12 tens) and have 3 tens left over.  
Write the 1 above the Tens column (that’s the first part of the answer). The 3 leftover tens move to the Ones column, becoming 30 ones. Add the 6 ones already there: 36 ones.

**Step 4: Share the ones**  
“Now we have 36 ones. How many groups of 12 can we make from 36?”  
Child makes groups of 12 from 36 stones. They’ll get exactly **3 groups**. Write 3 above the Ones column.  
Answer: 13. No remainder.

**If there’s a remainder:**  
“These leftover beans are for the dog” or “we’ll save them for tomorrow.” That’s the remainder. Write it as a small “r” with the number.

**Practice with smaller numbers first:**  
- 42 ÷ 3 (simple sharing)  
- 96 ÷ 4  
- Then move to numbers like your cattle: maybe 2 cattle sold for school fees, price per cow? Use real situations. “If we had 5,000 shillings and needed to buy 4 goats equally, how much per goat?” Division becomes a tool, not a mystery.
