## 2️⃣ Break Down the Process – The “4‑Step” Method  

Long division can be remembered as **D‑M‑S‑B** (Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring‑down). Write these letters in big letters on the anchor sheet.

1. **Divide** – How many times does the divisor go into the current part of the dividend?  
2. **Multiply** – Multiply that quotient digit by the divisor.  
3. **Subtract** – Subtract the product from the current part of the dividend.  
4. **Bring‑down** – Drop the next digit of the dividend down and repeat.

> **Tip:** Say the steps out loud together each time you do a problem:  
> “*Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring‑down.*”

---

## 3️⃣ Use Concrete Manipulatives First  

### A. “Bean‑Catcher” Method (Great for ages 8‑10)

1. **Write the problem** on a sheet (e.g., **576 ÷ 12**).  
2. **Lay out beans or small stones** in rows of the divisor (12 beans per row).  
3. **Count how many full rows** fit into the first part of the dividend (57).  
   - If 4 rows (4 × 12 = 48) fit, write **4** above the division bar.  
4. **Remove those beans**, leave the remainder (57‑48 = 9).  
5. **Bring down the next digit** (6) → you now have 96 beans.  
6. **Repeat**: 96 ÷ 12 = 8 rows → write **8** next to the 4.  

Result: **48** (the quotient).  

> **Why it works:** The child *sees* the subtraction and the “bring‑down” physically, turning an abstract algorithm into a tactile puzzle.

### B. “Paper‑Fold” Strips

- Cut a strip of paper 12 cm long.  
- Write the dividend across the strip, spacing each digit a little apart.  
- Fold the strip at each “bring‑down” point to keep the next digit visible.  
- The child can slide a small sticky note with the current quotient digit onto the strip.

## 4️⃣ Move to Pencil‑Paper – Guided Practice  

### Step‑by‑Step Example (576 ÷ 12)

| Step | What the child writes | What you say/ask |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|
| 1. Divide | **_5_** (first digit) → “How many 12’s fit into 5?” → **0** (write 0 above the 5, but we usually skip leading zeros). | “We can’t fit a 12 into 5, so we look at the first two digits together.” |
| 2. Bring‑down | Write **57** under the bar. | “Now we have 57. How many 12’s fit into 57?” |
| 3. Divide | **4** (because 4 × 12 = 48). Write 4 on top. | “4 times 12 is 48. That’s the biggest multiple that’s still ≤57.” |
| 4. Multiply & Subtract | Write **48** under 57, subtract → **9**. | “What’s left after we take away 48?” |
| 5. Bring‑down | Drop the next digit **6** → **96**. | “Now we have 96. How many 12’s fit into 96?” |
| 6. Divide | **8** (because 8 × 12 = 96). Write 8 on top. | “8 times 12 is exactly 96—no remainder!” |
| 7. Finish | No digits left → **Quotient = 48**. | “So 576 ÷ 12 = 48.” |

Repeat with **different numbers** but keep the same scaffold.

## 6️⃣ Check Understanding – Quick “Exit Tickets”  

At the end of each session, ask one of these:

1. **Explain the 4‑step process** in your own words.  
2. **Solve a new problem** on a blank sheet (no notes allowed).  
3. **Identify the remainder** in a division problem (e.g., 43 ÷ 5).  

If the child can do at least two of the three, you’re ready to move on; otherwise, revisit the step that caused trouble.
