The story about the land, the rangers, and the hill is a vivid backdrop, but it doesn’t really affect how you can teach a child long division on their own. Below is a practical, step‑by‑step plan that you can use even if the school is closed and you don’t have a teacher around. It blends clear instruction, hands‑on practice, and free online resources.

---

## 1. Start with the “Why” – Make It Concrete

**Goal:** Show the child that division is just the opposite of multiplication and that long division is a way to split a big number into equal parts.

**Activity:**
- Take a handful of small objects (e.g., 18 marbles, 24 stickers, or a bag of beans).  
- Ask: *“If we want to share these equally among 4 friends, how many will each friend get?”*  
- Let the child count out groups to see that 18 ÷ 4 = 4 with a remainder of 2 (4×4 = 16, 18 – 16 = 2).  
- Explain that long division lets us do that calculation systematically even when the numbers are big.

---

## 2. Introduce the Long Division Symbol

Show the **division bracket** (`÷` or `/`) and the **long division bar** (`┌─` over the dividend, `|` over the divisor).  
Draw a simple example on a whiteboard or a piece of paper:

```
   _______
6 |  123
```

Explain that the number on the left (6) is the divisor, and the number on the right (123) is the dividend.

---

## 3. Break It Down into Clear Steps

1. **Determine the first digit of the quotient.**  
   *Ask:* “How many times does 6 go into 12?” (Answer: 2).  
   Write `2` above the line.

2. **Multiply** that digit by the divisor and write the result under the part of the dividend you just used.  
   `2 × 6 = 12`. Write `12` below `12`.

3. **Subtract** the product from the part of the dividend.  
   `12 – 12 = 0`. Write the `0` below.

4. **Bring down the next digit** of the dividend (the `3`), placing it next to the remainder.  
   Now you have `03`.

5. **Repeat**: Ask how many times 6 goes into 3 (0 times). Write `0` in the quotient, multiply `0 × 6 = 0`, subtract, bring down the next digit, etc.

6. **Finish**: When you’ve brought down all digits, the number left over is the remainder.  

The full worked example:

```
   20
   -----
6 | 123
   -12
    ---
     03
     -0
     ---
      3
```

So 123 ÷ 6 = 20 with a remainder of 3.

---

## 4. Practice With “Real‑World” Numbers

Provide a set of numbers that gradually increase in difficulty.  For example:

| Dividend | Divisor | Expected Quotient | Remainder |
|----------|--------|-------------------|-----------|
| 48       | 6      | 8                 | 0         |
| 125      | 5      | 25                | 0         |
| 432      | 7      | 61                | 5         |
| 1,234    | 8      | 154               | 2         |
| 9,876    | 12     | 823               | 0         |

Let the child write each problem in long‑division format, solve it, and then check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor and adding the remainder.

---

## 5. Use Visual Aids and Manipulatives

- **Division Chart**: A printed chart that shows how 0–9 go into 1–9, 10–18, etc., helps the child quickly spot how many times the divisor fits.
- **Grid Method**: Draw a grid where the divisor’s width is the number of digits in the divisor. Each column represents a place value. This can be a good bridge to the “long‑division” layout.
- **“Chunking” Objects**: For example, to divide 84 by 6, the child can group the 84 marbles into 6 groups of 14 each. Visually seeing the groups reinforces the quotient.

---

## 6. Turn It Into a Game

**“Division Race”**  
- Time how long it takes to solve a set of problems.  
- Use a simple score sheet: 1 point for correct quotient, 1 point for correct remainder.  

**“Division Bingo”**  
- Create bingo cards with division problems.  
- Call out the dividends and ask the child to fill in the correct quotient.  

**“Mystery Numbers”**  
- Hide a small note with a dividend and divisor behind a picture.  
- The child must solve the problem to “unlock” the next clue (e.g., a hidden toy).

## 8. Check Understanding with “Back‑Checking”

Always let the child multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder. If it equals the original dividend, they’ve got it right. This reinforces the relationship between division and multiplication:

```
Quotient × Divisor + Remainder = Dividend
```

---

## 9. Keep It Short and Sweet

If the child is younger (say 7–9 years old), keep the practice sessions to 10–15 minutes to avoid fatigue. Use a timer or a favorite song to signal the end of each session.

---

## 10. Build Confidence, Not Just Accuracy

- **Praise Effort**: “You figured out the first part really well!”  
- **Encourage Mistakes**: “Let’s see what happened here. Maybe we can try a different approach.”  
- **Celebrate Small Wins**: For each correct quotient, give a sticker or a high‑five.
