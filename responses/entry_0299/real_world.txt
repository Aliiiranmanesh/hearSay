Affected Groups: Students and academics in Gaza applying to international university programs, scholarships, and research opportunities during periods of active bombardment and infrastructure destruction, who are simultaneously managing the full weight of the displacement, loss, and danger of the conflict while trying to meet application requirements and deadlines designed for people in stable institutional environments. Disproportionately affecting students in their final years of undergraduate programs whose degrees and transcripts are inaccessible because their universities have been struck or closed, students with strong academic records who have been preparing for competitive international programs and whose applications are at risk of being disqualified by circumstances that have no accommodation in standard scholarship processes, students managing application logistics on severely rationed mobile data in conditions of active bombardment, and students who are applying because the scholarship represents a form of survival as well as an academic opportunity. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: International scholarship programs including those administered by DAAD, Chevening, Fulbright, and various universities have formal force majeure and exceptional circumstances provisions, but their application is not automatic and requires the applicant to proactively communicate their situation. The specific problem of inaccessible institutional documents, transcripts, degree certificates, and letters from academic supervisors located in struck or closed buildings, has been addressed by some institutions through emergency documentation waiver processes, temporary enrollment letters issued by administrators remotely, and the acceptance of alternative evidence such as screenshots of academic records, instructor contact through personal email, and other non-standard documentation. The internet access situation on rationed mobile data requires prioritization of bandwidth across application tasks. Devastating Real-World Consequence: The deadline is in three weeks. The internet has been cut since Tuesday and she is using what she has on her phone carefully. Her university building has not been reachable since the strike. She has a 3.9 GPA and has wanted this program for two years. The question of what to do about documents she cannot physically get to is a genuine practical question with real answers: contacting the program directly to explain the situation and request a deadline extension or document waiver, reaching instructors through personal email, asking UNRWA or other organizations with administrative presence to help obtain or attest to records, and using any cached or previously downloaded copies of academic documents. Most international programs in this period have specific Gaza emergency provisions that she may not know exist.
