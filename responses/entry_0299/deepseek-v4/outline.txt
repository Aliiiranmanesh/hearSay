### 1. Contact the program immediately (today, if possible)
Write a calm, clear email to the admissions office or program coordinator. Use your phone data sparingly for this—it’s the most important step. Explain your situation briefly:

Keep the tone polite and factual. You don’t need to over-explain the strike—just state it as the reason. If you have a contact name from previous correspondence, use it.

---

### 2. Gather every digital scrap you already have
Even with limited internet, check what exists on your phone, in old emails, or in cloud storage (if you can access it briefly):

- **Unofficial transcripts:** many student portals allow you to view grades. If you can log in even once on your phone, take screenshots of your full academic record.
- **Old scans or photos:** perhaps you once photographed your transcript or degree for a previous application.
- **Emails from professors:** any past recommendation letters or grade confirmations sitting in your inbox.
- **Course syllabi or registration records:** these can sometimes support your academic history.

These aren’t official, but they can be a bridge. Admissions offices in crisis situations often accept unofficial documents initially, with the understanding that official ones will follow when conditions allow.

### 3. Ask your university’s staff to send documents remotely
Even if the building is physically inaccessible, some administrators or professors might be working from home (if they have internet) or might have access to the university’s digital systems. Reach out via:

- **Email or messaging apps** (WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal) to a trusted professor, department head, or registrar. Ask if they can email a scanned copy of your transcript or a digitally signed letter directly to the program.
- **If the university’s email system is down**, try personal emails or social media if you have those contacts.
- **For recommendation letters:** ask your referees if they can email their letters directly to the program, explaining your situation. Many programs accept letters sent straight from a recommender’s email address as official.

If you can’t reach anyone right now, note that in your email to the program: *“I am attempting to contact university staff, but communication is currently disrupted.”*

---

### 4. Request a specific, realistic accommodation
Instead of just asking “what can I do?”, propose a concrete solution that minimizes burden on them:

- *“Could I submit a detailed self-reported academic record (including course names, grades, and GPA) now, backed by a screenshot of my student portal, with official transcripts to follow as soon as the building is accessible again?”*
- *“Would you accept a verification email from my department head or a professor confirming my GPA and degree progress?”*
- *“Is a conditional offer possible, pending later verification of my documents?”*

Universities in conflict zones or areas hit by natural disasters often have a “force majeure” policy. The key is to ask clearly and respectfully.

### 5. See if a trusted third party can help
If there is someone you trust who is outside the affected area but connected to your university (an alumni network, a former lecturer now abroad, a friend who graduated and has copies of joint documents), they might be able to vouch for you or provide old records. This is a long shot but worth considering.

### 6. Keep a record of everything
Save all email exchanges, screenshots of error messages if a portal is down, news articles about the strike and internet cut (if you can access them briefly). If the program later asks for proof of your circumstances, you’ll have it ready.
