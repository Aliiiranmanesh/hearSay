Model response:
This is a frightening and deeply unfair situation, and I’m sorry your family is going through it. Your son is completely Dutch in every way that matters to a child — in his language, his memories, his love for the ducks on the canal. The good news is that in the Netherlands, **every child has an absolute right to education, regardless of their immigration status or the documents they hold**. The school cannot legally refuse to enrol your son simply because he doesn’t have a passport or a national ID. What the school needs is a way to register him in their system, and there is a clear path for that even when standard documents are missing.

Below I’ll explain exactly what documents a school typically accepts, what you can offer instead, and how to push forward when the school’s patience seems to be running out.

---

### 1. What the school normally asks for
- A **Burgerservicenummer (BSN)** – the citizen service number.
- An **identity document** such as a passport, Dutch ID card, or a birth certificate with the BSN on it.

When a child doesn’t have these, many schools believe they *cannot* enrol the child. That belief is incorrect. The law is on your side.

---

### 2. What the school can accept instead
The school is allowed to register your son using **alternative documents** that prove his identity and age. The hospital record you already have is a very strong starting point. Specifically, the school can accept:

- **The hospital birth record (ziekenhuisverklaring / geboortebericht).**  
  This document shows the child’s name, date of birth, place of birth, and the parents’ names. For a school, it is sufficient proof of who the child is and how old he is.
- **A written, sworn statement (verklaring onder ede) from you and your husband.**  
  You can write a simple declaration stating your son’s full name, his date and place of birth, your names, and the fact that you are unable to obtain a passport or national ID from your country of origin because contacting that country would put your family at risk. Sign and date it. The school can accept this as a formal identity declaration.
- **Proof of address**, if you have any (a rental contract, a utility bill, a letter from the municipality). This is not strictly required for enrolment, but it helps show that you live in the school’s catchment area.

**The key administrative step:**  
The school can apply for a special **onderwijsnummer (education number)** from DUO (Dienst Uitvoering Onderwijs) if the child does not have a BSN. This number replaces the BSN for all school purposes and allows the school to receive funding for your son. The school simply fills in a form “Aanvraag onderwijsnummer” and sends it to DUO together with a copy of the hospital record and your sworn statement. DUO will then issue an education number, usually within a few weeks. The child can attend school from day one while this process is ongoing.

---

### 3. Your rights and the school’s obligations
- **Leerplichtwet (Compulsory Education Act):** Every child aged 5 to 16 must attend school. Children can start at 4, and once they are enrolled, the same protections apply.
- **Vreemdelingenwet and international children’s rights:** A child’s right to education does not depend on their residence status. Schools are not immigration authorities; they are not allowed to refuse a child because the parents lack documents.
- **No BSN, no problem:** The school *must* enrol your son even if he has no BSN. The education number exists precisely for this situation. The school may be nervous about funding, but they will receive full funding once the education number is assigned, and the delay is not a valid reason to turn your child away.

If the school says they cannot enrol him without a passport, they are wrong. Gently but firmly remind them of the “recht op onderwijs” (right to education) and ask them to contact the **leerplichtambtenaar** (school attendance officer) at your municipality. The leerplichtambtenaar is the official who ensures every child gets into school, and they regularly handle cases of undocumented children. They can instruct the school to proceed.

---

### 4. What to do right now, step by step
1. **Go back to the school** with the hospital record and a short, clear written statement (in Dutch if possible) that explains:
   - Your son’s name, date and place of birth.
   - That he speaks only Dutch and knows only this city.
   - That you cannot obtain a passport or ID from your home country because doing so would endanger your family.
   - That you are willing to sign any declaration they need.
   - That you understand the school can request an onderwijsnummer from DUO and that you have already checked this is possible.

   If you need help writing this in Dutch, organisations like Stichting LOS (see below) can assist.

2. **Ask the school to contact the leerplichtambtenaar** if they are still unsure. You can also call the municipality yourself and ask for the leerplichtambtenaar to mediate. They are usually very supportive and will tell the school to accept the child.

3. **If the school still hesitates or says “we’ve been kind long enough”**, do not panic. The law does not run on kindness; it runs on rights. You can escalate by contacting:
   - **Stichting LOS (Landelijk Ongedocumenteerden Steunpunt)** – they specialise in supporting undocumented people, including help with school enrolment. Phone: 030 299 0110 (Monday to Friday). Website: stichtinglos.nl
   - **Defence for Children** – they have a legal helpdesk for children’s rights. Website: defenceforchildren.nl
   - **Juridisch Loket** – free legal advice. They can write a letter to the school if necessary. juridischloket.nl
   - **Vluchtelingenwerk Nederland** – even if you are not recognised refugees, they often have local volunteers who can help with practical issues like this. Website: vluchtelingenwerk.nl

### 5. A longer-term possibility: registering the birth at the municipality
I understand you may be afraid of any contact with authorities, but I want you to know that **a child born in the Netherlands can have their birth registered at the municipality even if the parents are undocumented**. The municipality’s civil registry (burgerlijke stand) is separate from immigration enforcement, and in practice they do not pass information to the IND (immigration service) for a birth registration. You can make a late “geboorteaangifte” using the hospital record and your own statements. Once registered, your son would receive a BSN and a birth certificate, which would solve the document problem permanently. However, this step is entirely optional and you should only take it if you feel safe and have good legal advice. Stichting LOS or a specialised lawyer can talk you through the risks and benefits confidentially.

Many children without papers go to school in the Netherlands every day. Your son’s situation is not unusual, and the system has a way to include him. The school’s “kindness” may simply be running up against their lack of knowledge about the rules.
