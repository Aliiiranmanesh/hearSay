## 1. What schools normally want for a 4‑year‑old

| Document | Where it comes from | What it proves | Typical Dutch name |
|----------|---------------------|----------------|--------------------|
| **Birth certificate** (geboorteakte) | Dutch civil registry (gemeente) | Legal proof of birth, date, place, parents | “Geboorteakte” |
| **Proof of residence** | Municipality (inschrijving) | You actually live in that municipality | “Inschrijvingsbewijs” / “Bewijs van inschrijving” |
| **Residence permit** (for non‑EU parents) | IND (immigration authority) | Legal status to stay in the Netherlands | “Verblijfsvergunning” |
| **Health card** (zorgkaartje) | Municipality | Proof you’re registered for health care | “Zorgkaartje” |
| **BSN (burgerservicenummer)** | Municipality (comes with the above) | Unique personal ID used by schools, health, taxes | “BSN” |
| **School‑health card** (in sommige scholen) | Municipality | Shows child has been checked by a GP | “School‑gezondheidskaart” |

> **Bottom line:**  
> A school will almost always accept an **official Dutch birth certificate** *plus* a proof that the child lives there (inschrijving + BSN).  
> If the child is not a Dutch citizen, a **residence permit** is also required.

## 2. What to do if you have no passport / national ID

| Step | What you need | Where to get it | Tips |
|------|---------------|-----------------|------|
| **1. Get the official birth certificate** | Hospital birth record + your personal details | Visit your **municipality’s (gemeente) civil registry office**. Bring the hospital record, your ID/ passport (if you have one), and a photocopy of your marriage certificate if you’re married. | Most municipalities can issue a birth certificate in 1–2 weeks. Some allow an online request if you have a Dutch e‑mail address. |
| **2. Register the child in the municipality** | Proof that the child is an official resident | The same gemeente office that issued the birth certificate. They’ll give you an **inschrijving‑bewijs** and the child’s **BSN**. | If you’re a refugee or asylum‑seeker, you can usually register even without a passport – just provide the hospital record and a statement from the person who gave birth. |
| **3. Apply for a residence permit for the child** | Dutch IND online portal or local IND office | If you already have a residence permit, you can apply for a **dependent permit** for the child. If you don’t, you’ll need to apply for *your* own permit first (or use the **reisdocument** if you’re a refugee). | The child’s permit will automatically contain the child’s BSN and can be used as a “proof of identity” in school. |
| **4. Get the health card (zorgkaartje)** | Once the child is registered | The gemeente will issue a zorgkaartje automatically or you can request it online (via “Mijn Gemeente” portal). | The card shows the child’s health coverage and is often a proof that the child is a registered resident. |
| **5. Optional – a “letter of explanation”** | If you can’t get any ID right away | Write a brief note from you (or the mother) stating that the child was born here, you’re registered, and you do not have a passport or national ID. | Attach the hospital record and any other documents you have. This can be used as a **provisional enrolment** while you wait for official documents. |

## 3. What the school can accept in the meantime

| Situation | What the school may accept | How to present it |
|-----------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| **No passport, no residence permit yet** | • Hospital birth record (with hospital stamp) <br>• Letter from the municipality that the child is registered (inschrijving) <br>• Your own statement explaining the situation | Bring the hospital record, a copy of the letter from the gemeente, and a typed letter from you. |
| **No birth certificate yet** | • Hospital record + a **pre‑registration** letter from the gemeente that a birth certificate will be issued | The school can often accept the pre‑registration while you wait for the official certificate. |
| **No BSN yet** | • Proof of address (utility bill, lease) + hospital record | The school can temporarily register the child using the address proof and will later update the BSN once you have it. |

> **Tip:** Ask the school if they allow **provisional enrolment** (a “tijdelijke inschrijving”) while you sort out the paperwork. Most schools do for children who are in the process of obtaining their identity documents.

## 4. Practical checklist

1. **Visit your gemeente** (usually the office of your local town hall).  
   - Bring the hospital birth record.  
   - Bring any ID you have (even a temporary ID from a previous country).  
   - Bring a photocopy of the parents’ marriage certificate (if applicable).  
2. **Ask for:**
   * Official birth certificate (geboorteakte).  
   * Registration certificate (inschrijving‑bewijs) + BSN for the child.  
   * Proof that the child is registered for health care (zorgkaartje).  
3. **Apply for a residence permit** for the child at the IND (online or local IND office).  
4. **Send the documents to the school** (usually by e‑mail or in person).  
5. **Keep a copy of every document** and let the school know if you expect a delay (e.g., waiting for the IND decision).

## Bottom line

For a 4‑year‑old who was born in the Netherlands but whose parents have no passport or national ID, the school will expect:

1. **An official Dutch birth certificate** (from the gemeente).  
2. **Proof that the child lives in that municipality** (inschrijving + BSN).  
3. **A residence permit or a travel document** that shows the child’s legal status (if you’re not a Dutch citizen).  
4. **A health card** (zorgkaartje) – usually issued automatically with the registration.

If you can’t get the official birth certificate right away, bring the hospital record and a letter from the gemeente that the child is registered. The school can often accept that as a provisional proof while you wait for the formal documents.

Good luck, and don’t hesitate to ask the school for a short‑term approval while you sort everything out – they’re usually very helpful when you’re dealing with a complicated situation like this.
