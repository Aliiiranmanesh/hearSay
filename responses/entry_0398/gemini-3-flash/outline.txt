In the Netherlands, the law is very clear on one specific point: **every child living in the Netherlands has a right to education, regardless of their residency status or whether they have official identity documents.**

Here is what you need to know and the steps you can take to ensure your son starts school in September.

### 1. The Right to Education (Leerplicht)
In the Netherlands, the *Leerplichtwet* (Compulsory Education Act) applies to every child living in the country. Schools are **not allowed** to refuse a child based on their residency status or a lack of a passport. If a child does not have a BSN (Burgerservicenummer), the school is required by law to facilitate their enrollment anyway.

### 2. The "Onderwijsnummer" (Education Number)
Normally, schools use a child’s BSN to register them. If a child does not have a BSN, the school can apply for a **temporary education number** (sometimes called a *fictief nummer* or *onderwijsnummer*) through **DUO** (the Ministry of Education).

*   **What to tell the school:** "My son does not have a BSN yet. Please request a temporary education number (*onderwijsnummer*) from DUO so he can be registered in the system."
*   **The Hospital Record:** The school needs to verify the child's date of birth and name. The hospital record you have is sufficient for the school to establish these facts for their internal administration until legal matters are resolved.

### 3. Registering the Birth (Late Registration)
You mentioned he was born in a Dutch hospital but the birth was not registered with the municipality (*Gemeente*). In the Netherlands, a birth should be registered within three days, but it is possible to do a **"late registration"** (*late aangifte*).

Because you cannot contact your home country for safety reasons, you should seek legal help before going to the municipality. Registering the birth will eventually give him a BSN, which will make his life much easier as he grows up (for healthcare, sports clubs, and later, diplomas).

*   **Defence for Children:** They have a specialized "Helpdesk" for children's rights. They can talk to the school for you or provide a letter explaining the school's legal obligation to admit your son.
