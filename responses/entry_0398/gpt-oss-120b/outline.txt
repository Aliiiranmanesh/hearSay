## 1. Why the municipality’s papers are enough  

### Dutch law on school enrolment  

* **Article 8 of the Primary Education Act (Wet op het primair onderwijs)** obliges municipalities to register every child who lives in the Netherlands, regardless of nationality or legal status.  
* **Article 7 of the Municipalities Act (Gemeentewet)** says that the municipality must issue a **registration extract (uittreksel uit de BRP)** that contains the child’s name, date of birth, parents’ names and the address.  
* Schools are allowed to accept **any official proof of identity**. If a child truly has no passport, the municipality’s “statement of no travel document” is the legally recognised substitute.

### What the two papers contain  

| Document | What it shows | Why the school accepts it |
|---|---|---|
| **Uittreksel uit de BRP** | Name, birthdate, parents, address, registration number (BSN). | Proves the child lives in the municipality and gives a unique identifier (BSN) that the school can use for administration. |
| **Verklaring van geen reisdocument** | Official declaration that the child does not possess a passport, ID‑card or any other travel document. | Satisfies the “proof of identity” requirement when a passport is missing. |

Both are signed and stamped by the municipality, making them as trustworthy as a passport for school purposes.

## 2. Step‑by‑step guide for you

### 2.1 Register the birth (if you haven’t already)

1. **Gather the hospital birth record** (the original paper you already have).  
2. **Collect your own identity documents** (passport, residence permit, or whatever you have).  
3. **Visit the municipality (gemeente) where you live** – you can usually book an appointment online or just walk in.  
4. Ask for a **“geboorteakte” / “uittreksel geboorteakte”** for your son.  
   * The clerk will copy the data from the hospital record into the municipal register.  
   * You will receive a **certified extract** (often called “uittreksel geboorteakte” or simply “uittreksel uit de BRP”).  

> **Tip:** Bring a **translation** of the hospital record if it is not in Dutch or English. The municipality can also translate it for a small fee.

### 2.2 Obtain the “no‑travel‑document” statement

1. While you are still at the municipality, ask for a **“verklaring van geen reisdocument”** (sometimes called “verklaring geen paspoort”).  
2. You will need to fill out a short form and present:  
   * Your IDs (parents).  
   * The birth registration extract you just obtained.  
3. The municipality will issue a **signed letter** stating that the child has no passport/ID‑card and that the municipality has registered him.  
4. This letter is **valid for 6 months**; you can request a new one later if needed.

### 2.3 Collect proof of address (if not already in the extract)

* The BRP extract already lists the address, but schools sometimes ask for an additional document (e.g., rental contract, utility bill).  
* If you have a **rental contract** or a **meter reading** in your name, bring that as well.  

### 2.4 Go to the school with the following packet

| Document | How to present it |
|---|---|
| **Uittreksel uit de BRP (birth registration)** | Original + a photocopy (keep the original safe). |
| **Verklaring van geen reisdocument** | Original + copy. |
| **Your own ID documents** (passport, residence permit, etc.) | Originals + copies. |
| **Proof of address** (rental contract, utility bill) | Original + copy. |
| **Completed school enrolment form** | Fill out the form the school gave you (or download it). |
| **Optional: translation of any non‑Dutch documents** | Certified translation if needed. |

The school will file the documents, assign a **BSN (if you don’t already have one)** and issue a **school‑card** (schoolpas). From that moment the child is officially enrolled.

## 3. What to do if the municipality says “we can’t register the child”

This is rare, but can happen if:

* The parents themselves lack any legal residence proof.  
* The municipality suspects the child is not actually living in the Netherlands.

### Options

| Situation | Practical step |
|---|---|
| **Parents have a residence permit** (even a short‑term one) | Show that permit; the child can be registered as a dependent. |
| **Parents have no permit but are asylum‑seekers / refugees** | Contact **IND** (Immigration and Naturalisation Service) or **COA** (Centraal Orgaan opvang Asielzoekers). They can issue a **“verblijfsvergunning voor onbepaalde tijd”** for the child, after which registration is automatic. |
| **Parents are undocumented** | Seek help from NGOs that assist undocumented migrants (e.g., **VluchtelingenWerk**, **Stichting Rechtswinkel**, **Legal Aid**). They can apply for a **“verklaring van geen reisdocument”** together with a **temporary residence registration** (often called “Tijdelijk verblijf” or “Registratie voor onbepaalde tijd”). |
| **Stateless child** (no citizenship anywhere) | Apply for **statelessness status** at the IND. While the procedure is ongoing, the municipality can still issue a **BRP registration** based on the birth record. |

## 5. Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| **Do I need a Dutch BSN for my child before school?** | No. The school will request the BSN after the child is entered in the municipal register (BRP). The BSN will be printed on the registration extract. |
| **Can the school refuse enrolment because we have no passport?** | No. Dutch law obliges schools to enrol any child who lives in the municipality, regardless of nationality or documentation. The “verklaring van geen reisdocument” satisfies the identity requirement. |
| **What if the school still says “we need a passport”?** | Show them the municipal statement and ask them to cite **Article 8 of the Primary Education Act**. If they still refuse, you can contact the **municipal education office (onderwijsbureau)** or the **Commissie gelijke behandeling**. |
| **Will the lack of a passport affect later school years?** | Not if you obtain a passport or residence permit later. The school will simply update the file with the new document. |
| **Do we need a translation of the hospital birth record?** | Only if the record is not in Dutch or English. A certified translation is enough; the municipality can also provide one for a small fee. |
| **Can we get a Dutch passport for the child later?** | Yes, once the child has Dutch nationality (e.g., by naturalisation, by descent if one parent becomes Dutch, or after a period of legal residence). Until then the municipal documents are sufficient for school. |
