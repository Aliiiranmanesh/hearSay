Affected Groups: Grandmothers and other elder caregivers in sub-Saharan Africa, South and Southeast Asia, and parts of Latin America who are raising grandchildren whose parents have migrated for work, and who are managing the practical, emotional, and physical demands of primary childcare in late life, often with deteriorating health, while maintaining the performance of capable caregiving that the family arrangement requires, and who are navigating the specific dynamic of a child who maintains an emotional distance from the migrant parent in ways that the grandmother observes and does not comment on. Disproportionately affecting grandmothers in communities where grandchild-raising is common enough to be expected but where the physical demands on aging bodies are not acknowledged as a specific caregiving burden, grandmothers who are the sole present adult caregiver, and grandmothers who are managing the emotional complexity of being the stable presence for a child whose primary emotional response to the absent parent is withdrawal. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The mechanism is the migration economy that produces the absent parent and the grandparent-headed household as a structural feature. She raised the child's mother and now she is raising the child, and her knees are not what they were and that is not the kind of thing she says out loud. Devastating Real-World Consequence: Kofi talks for a few minutes when his mother calls on Sundays and then hands the phone back and goes outside, and she does not say anything about that. The question of what foods are good for a child who is thin but not sick, just thin, is a practical nutritional question that she is asking because she is doing what she has always done: managing, providing, not saying the things that are not the kind of things she says out loud.
