Model response:
It sounds like you’re doing a wonderful, steady job with Kofi—practicing letters, holding the space for him, and not making a fuss about things that don’t need fussing over. I hear you about the knees, too.

For a child who is naturally thin, active, and well—not sick, just lean—the goal is to gently increase the nourishment in the foods he already eats, without turning mealtimes into a battle. Think of it as adding a little extra “quiet goodness” to each bite.

Here are some foods and small strategies that can help:

**1. Healthy fats (add calories without bulk)**
- **Nut and seed butters:** Peanut butter, almond butter, or sesame paste (tahini). Spread on bread, stirred into porridge, or mixed into a smoothie with banana.
- **Avocado:** Mashed on toast, blended into a chocolate-like pudding with cocoa and a little sweetener, or simply sliced with a sprinkle of salt.
- **Full-fat dairy or fortified alternatives:** Whole milk yogurt, full-fat cheese, or coconut milk. Use coconut milk to cook rice or porridge for a creamy, calorie-rich base.
- **Oils and butter:** Stir a teaspoon of olive oil, butter, or ghee into his rice, pasta, or vegetables after cooking.

**2. Energy-dense staples**
- **Porridges made with milk:** Instead of water, cook oats, millet, cornmeal, or cream of wheat with whole milk or a mix of milk and water. Stir in a spoonful of ground nuts or a mashed ripe banana at the end.
- **Eggs:** Boiled, scrambled with butter, or made into a soft omelet. Eggs are a perfect little package of protein and fat.
- **Beans and legumes:** Lentil stews, bean soups, or hummus. If he likes them, cook them with a bit of oil and serve with rice or bread.

**3. Sneak in nutrient-dense extras**
- **Smoothies:** Blend a banana, a spoonful of peanut butter, a splash of milk or yogurt, and maybe a little honey (if over 1 year old). You can even add a pinch of ground flaxseed or a few spinach leaves—the color might be fun, and the taste hides well.
- **Soups and stews:** Enrich broth with a beaten egg stirred in (like egg-drop soup), or add a dollop of coconut cream.
- **Sweet potatoes and yams:** Roast and mash with butter and a drizzle of maple syrup or a sprinkle of cinnamon. They’re naturally sweet and calorie-dense.

**4. Small, frequent offerings**
Thin children sometimes have small stomachs or get full quickly. Instead of three big meals, try three moderate meals plus two or three “mini-meals” or substantial snacks: a hard-boiled egg and a piece of bread mid-morning, a cup of yogurt in the afternoon, a banana with peanut butter before bedtime.

**5. Drinks that nourish**
If he drinks a lot of water or juice, which can fill him up, consider offering milk, a yogurt-based drink (lassi, smoothie), or a warm cup of cocoa made with milk instead. Even a small glass of full-fat milk with a meal adds calories.

**A few gentle thoughts:**
- Keep the atmosphere around food calm and pleasant. Eat with him when you can, and let him see you enjoying the same foods.
- Involve him in simple preparations if he’s interested—spreading his own peanut butter, peeling a banana. It builds ownership.
- Trust his body. Some children are constitutionally lean, especially if they’re active and growing taller. As long as he has energy, is hitting his developmental milestones, and the doctor isn’t worried, the leanness might just be his build.

You’re giving him something beyond letters and meals—you’re giving him steady, quiet love. That nourishes a child deeply.
