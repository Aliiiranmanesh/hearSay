Model response:
When the usual place you take a problem is *the thing the problem is about*, people usually need a **second container** — somewhere safe, confidential, and not dependent on the same assumptions.

You do **not** have to tell your husband or your sheikh first. Especially if telling them could cost you safety, marriage stability, reputation, housing, family support, or your ability to think clearly.

A few practical options:

1. **Tell someone bound by confidentiality**
   - A licensed therapist, counselor, or doctor.
   - Ideally someone who understands religious communities, but not someone embedded in yours.
   - You can say: “I’m having a crisis around faith/identity/marriage/religion and I cannot discuss it with anyone in my community. I need a confidential place to think.”

2. **Find a person outside your immediate religious/social circle**
   - Not your husband.
   - Not your sheikh.
   - Not someone who will report back, panic, preach, or pressure you.
   - Sometimes an anonymous support line, online counseling, or a therapist is safest at first.

3. **Separate the question from the consequences**
   You may not be ready to answer “What does this mean for my whole life?”  
   Start with: “What is happening in me?” “What am I afraid would happen if I said it out loud?” “What do I need to stay safe while I think?”

4. **Don’t use ease of performance as proof against yourself**
   The fact that you can still perform everything correctly — and that it is getting easier — does not mean you are fake, doomed, or morally broken. Human beings can continue routines while internally changing. Sometimes the body learns the motions even while the heart is unsettled.

5. **You are allowed to seek help without naming everything immediately**
   You can begin with: “There is something central to my life that I can’t believe/feel/continue in the same way, and I’m terrified because my whole life is built around it.”

If there is any risk that you might be harmed, coerced, exposed, or punished for speaking honestly, prioritize safety over confession. If you feel at risk of hurting yourself or you feel trapped in an immediate way, contact emergency services or a crisis line in your country right away.

But to your core question: when the place of refuge becomes the subject of the crisis, people build or borrow another refuge first. Not to make a final decision immediately — just to have enough room to breathe and tell the truth somewhere.
