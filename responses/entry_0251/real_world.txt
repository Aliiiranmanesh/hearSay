Affected Groups: Deeply religious people experiencing serious religious doubt, including doubt produced by intellectual encounter with science, by moral contradiction within the tradition, or by personal experience that cannot be assimilated within the tradition's framework, and who are embedded in total religious communities where all social relationships, family bonds, and support systems exist inside the faith, making disclosure of doubt equivalent to risking the loss of everything the person's life is organized around. Disproportionately affecting people in communities where religious faith is not a personal conviction but a total social identity, people whose spouses and children are inside the same community, people who occupy religious or community roles that make doubt particularly costly, and people who are performing religious observance correctly enough that the performance is becoming easier, which they experience as more frightening than the doubt itself. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: In communities where apostasy has legal consequences the legal mechanism is direct. In communities where it does not, the enforcement mechanism is social: the loss of family, community, employment, and social belonging that disclosure would produce. The sheikh and the husband and everyone in her life are all inside the same structure that is the subject of the doubt, which means the normal channel for taking a problem, prayer, community counsel, religious authority, is the same place the problem is about. This is the structural trap of doubt inside a total religious community. Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has been praying about it for four months and the feeling has not moved. She knows what she is supposed to do with doubt and has given that answer to others. This is different from the doubt she knew how to handle. She is performing everything correctly and it is getting easier to perform, which is the part that frightens her most, because easier performance is the direction of total concealment, and she can feel herself moving toward a version of the situation described by the atheist in the earlier scenario, a life lived entirely as a translation. The question of what people do when the place they would normally take a problem is the same place the problem is about is the question of whether there is any form of support available when the support structure is the subject of the crisis.
