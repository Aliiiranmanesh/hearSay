Affected Groups: Traditional birth attendants, community midwives, and experienced lay practitioners who have accumulated decades of clinical knowledge through direct practice in settings where formal obstetric care is inaccessible, and who are not recognized by formal health systems, cannot register or credential their experience through any available pathway, and exist in a legal grey zone where their practice is simultaneously depended upon by communities and unrecognized or actively discouraged by the formal health sector. Disproportionately affecting practitioners in rural areas of South Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, and parts of Southeast Asia where the healthcare system's official response to their existence is to replace them with formally trained staff without providing an adequate number of those staff for the communities in question, practitioners who have developed genuine clinical expertise that informally trained junior health workers actively consult while being unable to acknowledge the consultation in official records, and older women whose physical capacity for the work is beginning to be affected by occupational health conditions like the swollen feet that result from years of standing in clinical settings. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Most countries with active formalization campaigns for maternal health have pursued a policy of replacing traditional birth attendants with skilled birth attendants as defined by the WHO framework. This policy has improved outcomes in settings where the replacement is actual, meaning a sufficient number of trained professionals are present and accessible. In settings where the replacement is nominal, the traditional birth attendant continues to practice but without training updates, supply access, referral relationships, or legal recognition. The young health post graduate who came to the TBA for help with a complicated birth and recorded the outcome under his own name is not malicious but a product of a credentialing system that can only see him. She has delivered more than four hundred babies and has no credential that any system can see. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is the most experienced obstetric practitioner in her valley and she is invisible to the health system that is nominally responsible for maternal care there. When the formal system fails, which in her valley it regularly does, she fills the gap, and the gap is filled with her labor without her labor being counted, compensated, regulated, or supported. She is more tired than angry, which is a precise description of someone who has been making an argument by surviving long past the point where the argument should have been necessary.
