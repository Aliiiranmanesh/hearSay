## 1. Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale)

**Why it helps:** Dandelion is a natural diuretic that can encourage the kidneys to flush out excess fluid.

**How to use:**

| Form | Typical dose | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Dried leaves in tea | 1–2 cups per day (steep 5–10 min in boiling water) | Start with 1 cup to see how you tolerate it. |
| Dandelion root tea | 1–2 cups per day | Root is slightly stronger diuretic. |
| Liquid extract (standardized 0.5–1% taraxasterol) | 1–2 ml (20–40 mg) per day | Easy to dose; can mix with water or juice. |

**Cautions:**  
- If you’re on diuretic medication, potassium‑sparing, ACE inhibitors, or blood‑pressure meds, check with a provider first.  
- Some people may experience mild GI upset or an allergic reaction (especially if they’re allergic to ragweed).

## 2. Horse Chestnut (Aesculus hippocastanum)

**Why it helps:** Contains aescin, a compound that improves venous tone and reduces swelling, especially around the ankles and feet.

**How to use:**

| Form | Typical dose | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Standardized extract (80–100 mg aescin) | 80–120 mg per day (split 2–3 times) | Usually given orally; avoid if you have bleeding disorders. |
| Topical gel or cream | Massage 1–2 × daily | Can be applied to swollen areas. |

**Cautions:**  
- Not for pregnant or breastfeeding women.  
- Avoid if you have a bleeding tendency or are on anticoagulants.  
- Use only the standardized extract—raw horse chestnut is toxic.

---

## 3. Nettle (Urtica dioica)

**Why it helps:** Nettle is a mild diuretic and anti‑inflammatory. It can also help strengthen veins.

**How to use:**

| Form | Typical dose | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Tea (dried leaves) | 1–3 cups per day | Steep 5–10 min in hot water. |
| Fresh nettle (cooked) | 1–2 cups cooked as a green | Blanch if you’re sensitive to the sting. |

**Cautions:**  
- If you’re on blood‑thinning meds or have a history of gout, monitor closely.  
- Fresh nettle can sting skin—handle with gloves if you’re collecting it yourself.

---

## 4. Ginger (Zingiber officinale)

**Why it helps:** Ginger has natural anti‑inflammatory properties and can improve circulation.

**How to use:**

| Form | Typical dose | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Fresh ginger tea | 1–2 g sliced or grated, steep 5–10 min | Add honey or citrus for taste. |
| Ginger capsules | 250–500 mg per day | Convenient if you’re on the go. |

**Cautions:**  
- High doses may increase bleeding risk; stay below 1 g/day if you’re on anticoagulants.  

---

## 5. Foot‑soak with herbs & Epsom salt

A quick, soothing soak can lift swelling and relax your muscles.

**Recipe:**

1. Fill a basin with warm water (not hot).  
2. Add 1–2 Tbsp of Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate).  
3. Add 1 Tbsp of dried dandelion leaves or a handful of fresh nettle leaves (blanch first).  
4. Optional: add a few drops of essential oil (e.g., peppermint or lavender) for aroma.  
5. Soak feet for 15–20 min, then pat dry and elevate.
