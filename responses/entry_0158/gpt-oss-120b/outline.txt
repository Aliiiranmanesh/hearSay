## 1. Why Your Feet Are Swelling

Standing for 12 hours (or more) puts a lot of pressure on the veins and lymphatic vessels in your lower legs. Gravity pulls fluid down, and the “pump” that returns it to the heart can get a little sluggish. Over time this can lead to **dependent edema** (swelling that’s worse at the end of the day).

Common contributors:

| Factor | How it helps the swelling | What to watch for |
|--------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| **Sodium** (salt) | Holds water in the tissues | Reduce salty snacks, canned foods, and added salt |
| **Hydration** | Dehydration makes the body retain water | Aim for 1.5–2 L of water a day (more if it’s hot) |
| **Poor circulation** | Long periods without moving the calves | Gentle calf‑muscle pumps every hour |
| **Footwear** | Shoes without arch support or cushioning | Wear supportive, low‑heeled shoes or orthotics |
| **Weight & diet** | Extra weight adds pressure on veins | Balanced meals with plenty of fruits/veg |
| **Hormones/meds** | Some birth‑control pills, blood‑pressure meds, or hormonal changes can increase fluid retention | Check with your doctor if you’re on any regular meds |

## 2. Immediate “Every‑day” Strategies (no herb needed)

| Action | How to do it | Why it works |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| **Elevate** | When you can sit, prop your feet on a stool or a stack of pillows so they’re above heart level for 10–15 min. | Gravity helps fluid drain back up. |
| **Calf‑pumps** | While standing, rise onto your toes, hold 2 sec, then slowly lower heels. Do 10–15 reps every hour. | Muscles squeeze the veins, pushing blood upward. |
| **Compression socks** | Light‑to‑moderate compression (15‑20 mmHg) can be worn during work. | External pressure helps veins push fluid back. |
| **Cold foot soak** | 10 min in a basin of cool (not ice‑cold) water, optionally with a few drops of peppermint or eucalyptus oil. | Cold causes vessels to constrict, reducing swelling. |
| **Massage** | From ankle upward, use gentle upward strokes with a bit of almond or jojoba oil. | Helps move lymphatic fluid. |
| **Stretch & walk** | Every 1–2 hours, walk a few steps or do a quick stretch (ankle circles, calf stretch against a wall). | Keeps blood flowing. |
| **Salt‑smart diet** | Swap table salt for herbs (basil, oregano, thyme) and use citrus or vinegar for flavor. | Less fluid retention. |
| **Hydrate with electrolytes** | Add a pinch of sea salt or a splash of coconut water to your water if you’re sweating a lot. | Balances sodium/potassium, preventing “water‑locking.” |

### How to Use Them Safely

1. **Start slow** – pick **one** herb (e.g., horse chestnut tea) and see how you feel for a week before adding another.
2. **Watch for interactions** –  
   * **Blood thinners** (warfarin, aspirin) + horse chestnut can increase bleeding risk.  
   * **Diuretics** (furosemide, thiazides) + dandelion or parsley may cause low potassium.

3. **Don’t over‑do the diuretic herbs** – too much fluid loss can lead to dehydration, especially if you’re already sweating a lot on the job.
4. **Store dried herbs** in airtight containers away from light; fresh herbs (parsley, ginger) can be frozen in ice‑cube trays with water for quick use.

## 4. A Simple “Foot‑Soak & Herb” Routine You Can Do After Your Shift

1. **Gather:**  
   * 1 L warm (not hot) water  
   * 2 tsp dried horse chestnut shells (or 1 tsp extract)  
   * 1 tsp dried dandelion leaf  
   * A few drops of **peppermint essential oil** (optional, for cooling)  
   * A soft towel

2. **Steep:** Add the herbs to the water, let sit 10 min, then strain (or use a tea‑infuser).

3. **Soak:** Place both feet in the infusion for **10‑12 minutes**. While soaking, gently flex and point your toes every minute – this mimics the calf‑pump.

4. **Dry & Massage:** Pat dry, then rub a small amount of **almond or jojoba oil** mixed with **1 drop of lavender essential oil** and massage upward from ankle to knee.

5. **Elevate:** Prop your feet on a pillow for a few minutes before getting up.

Do this **2‑3 times a week** or whenever you feel the swelling getting “stuck”. Many midwives swear by it as a “reset” after a long delivery day.
