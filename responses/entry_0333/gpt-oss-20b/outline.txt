## 1. Stop and Count

**Why?** Knowing how many stitches you *should* have at the point of the drop is the quickest way to locate the missing loop.

1. **Look at the row where you first noticed the problem.**  
   - If it’s a *garter* row, every stitch should be a knit.  
   - If it’s a *stockinette* or *rib* row, you’ll have a mix of knit and purl stitches.

2. **Count the stitches on the needle.**  
   - If you’re supposed to have, say, 30 stitches and only see 29, you know a stitch is missing.

3. **Check the row before** (the one you just finished).  
   - If that row has 30 stitches, the drop most likely happened *after* you cast on or *while* working that row.

---

## 2. Trace the Drop

**Why?** The dropped stitch is usually somewhere on the row *below* the one you’re looking at, because the loop goes under the needle and disappears.

1. **Look at the row beneath the one with the missing stitch.**  
   - That row will have one stitch *fewer* than it should.  

2. **Feel for a “gap”** where a loop should be.  
   - In a knit row, a dropped stitch appears as a *short* loop that’s not there.  
   - In a purl row, it appears as a *missing* loop that’s not caught.

3. **Use a small needle or a crochet hook** (any thin hook will do) to gently lift the yarn in that spot.  
   - If you feel a loop that’s not there, you’ve found the drop.

## 3. Fix It

### If the drop is just one stitch back
1. **Insert your knitting needle into the spot where the loop should be.**  
2. **Pull the yarn from the ball and let it fall into the missing loop.**  
3. **Knit the stitch as usual.**  
4. **If the yarn is a bit loose, you can gently tug to tighten it.**

### If the drop is several rows back
1. **Rework from that spot.**  
   - **Option A:** Drop the needle back to the row where you think the stitch fell.  
     - Knit the missing stitch, then continue forward.  
   - **Option B:** If you’re in a pattern and the dropped stitch is disrupting the design, it may be safer to start over from the last completed pattern repeat.  
     - Yes, it feels like you’re “losing” progress, but patterns are designed to be repeatable.

2. **Mark the spot for future reference.**  
   - Use a stitch marker, a small piece of tape, or a colored thread loop on that stitch.  
   - Next time you work that area, you’ll know where you’re supposed to be.
