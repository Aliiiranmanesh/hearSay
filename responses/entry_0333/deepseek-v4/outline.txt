**In knitting, when you drop a stitch and don’t notice until several rows later:**

You have two main choices, and both are valid.

1. **Pick up the dropped stitch and ladder it back up.**  
   You take a crochet hook (or the tip of a knitting needle) and carefully hook the dropped stitch, then draw it through each horizontal strand of yarn, one row at a time, all the way to the current row. This works beautifully if the stitch dropped cleanly and you can see the ladder of threads. It takes patience, good light, and a steady hand. The fabric will be a little tighter in that column for a while, but blocking and wear will ease it. No one will ever know unless they look very, very closely.

2. **Unravel (rip back) to the row where the stitch dropped, then re-knit.**  
   This is harder to face, but sometimes it’s the right choice—especially if the dropped stitch has run, twisted, or pulled too much. You take a deep breath, slide the needles out, and gently pull the yarn until you’re back before the mistake. Then you pick up the stitches and start again from that point. It feels like losing progress, but the fabric will be whole and correct, and the memory of the flaw won’t keep catching your eye.

A third, quieter option: if the stitch is secure and not unraveling further, some knitters deliberately sew it in place with a bit of yarn and call it a design feature. That works for a single dropped stitch in a non-structural spot. It’s not “fixing” it; it’s accepting it and making sure it doesn’t get worse.
