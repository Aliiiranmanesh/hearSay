Affected Groups: Small and micro-business owners, independent traders, freelancers, and online sellers in countries with high rates of politically motivated or security-related internet shutdowns, including Ethiopia, Iran, Myanmar, Sudan, India's conflict-affected states, and numerous others, whose business operations depend on continuous internet connectivity for order processing, customer communication, payment receipt, and supplier relationships, and who experience each shutdown as an uncontrollable and unpredictable business interruption that has no equivalent in the experience of their suppliers and business partners in connected markets. Disproportionately affecting sellers in regions where shutdowns are most frequent and longest, businesses that are structurally dependent on real-time communication rather than on inventory or physical service delivery, sellers whose supplier relationships are with urban partners who have more stable connectivity and for whom the seller's intermittent availability reads as unreliability rather than as external constraint, and young entrepreneurs in conflict-adjacent areas whose business models are calibrated to the online market as the primary accessible market because physical market access is itself constrained. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Internet shutdowns are ordered by governments under national security, public order, or emergency provisions that have no appeal mechanism for affected businesses, no compensation obligation, and no advance notice requirement. The legal frameworks in most affected countries do not recognize internet access as a right that creates government liability for its disruption. The seller who experiences a shutdown has no recourse, no predictability, and no legal standing to claim business losses from the shutdown authority. The supplier who dropped him for unreliability made a rational business decision based on observed behavior in a context where the cause of that behavior was invisible to the supplier. The business partner who does not understand why he sometimes disappears is operating in a connected context where intermittent availability is most legibly explained as disinterest or disorganization rather than as infrastructure failure. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He worked from 2am to 7am on the work that had accumulated while the connection was gone, processing fourteen orders, eleven customer messages, pending payments, and new stock uploads, and then it went again. Last month it was eleven days. He does not know how long this time. The question of how you explain to a business partner that your internet is a weather event is the question of how you make legible a structural condition that has no equivalent in the partner's experience, and the answer is that you often cannot, because the partner's framework for interpreting unreliability is calibrated to a connectivity environment where unreliability is a choice.
