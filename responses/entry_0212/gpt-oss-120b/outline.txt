### 3. Why “weather” is a Legitimate Business‑Continuity Issue  

| Factor | How it translates to a real‑world impact |
|--------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Physical damage to fiber/copper** | A downed pole or a flooded conduit can sever the line for **hours to days**. |
| **Satellite rain‑fade** (if you rely on satellite) | Heavy rain or snow can reduce signal strength, causing **intermittent drops**. |
| **Power outages at the ISP’s node** | Even if you have on‑site UPS, the ISP’s equipment may lose power, taking the whole service offline. |
| **Storm‑related ISP overload** | Many customers trying to reconnect simultaneously can **slow or stall** the network. |
| **Regulatory/municipal repairs** | After a storm, city crews may need **weeks** to replace buried cables. |

These are **industry‑standard risk factors** that most telecom providers list in their Service Level Agreements (SLAs). Citing them shows you understand the technical reality, not just making an excuse.

### 5. How to Re‑build Trust with a Supplier Who “Dropped You”  

1. **Personal outreach** – Call them directly, acknowledge the past issue, and share the new mitigation plan.  
2. **Show evidence** – Offer a short demo of the backup connection or a screenshot of the outage‑alert system.  
3. **Offer a pilot** – “Let’s run a two‑week trial where I’ll keep you posted on every network event. If you’re satisfied, we can reopen the partnership.”  
4. **Provide a contingency clause** – In any new contract, include a **“Force‑Majeure – Internet Outage”** clause that defines how orders will be handled (e.g., you’ll confirm via phone).  
5. **Give them a “first‑look” advantage** – Early access to new stock or a small discount as a goodwill gesture for their patience.
