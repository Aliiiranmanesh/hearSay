## 2️⃣ The “Low Ponytail + Front Braid” – Step‑by‑Step

> **Time needed:** 5‑7 minutes (once you’ve practiced)  
> **Tools you’ll need:** wide‑tooth comb (or detangling brush), light leave‑in conditioner or smoothing serum, soft hair ties (no metal), a small elastic hair band for the braid, optional “no‑dent” hair clip or a tiny hairpin for the braid’s end.

### 2.1 The Night‑Before (or at least a few hours before)

1. **Wash & condition** – Use her regular shampoo & conditioner. Thick hair often feels softer when it’s well‑conditioned.  
2. **Apply a tiny bit of leave‑in** – About a pea‑size amount of a light serum or detangling spray. Rub between your palms, then run it through the hair from mid‑length to ends. This reduces fly‑aways.  
3. **Brush while damp** – Use a wide‑tooth comb to gently work out knots. Start at the ends and work upward; never pull harshly.  
4. **Let it air‑dry or use a low‑heat dryer** – If you use a dryer, keep it on a cool/low setting and point the airflow down the hair shaft (helps the hair lie flat).  

> **Tip:** If you can, let her hair dry naturally overnight. Dry hair is easier to style in the morning because it’s less slippery.

### 2.2 The Morning of the Photo

1. **Gather a quick “touch‑up” brush** (a small paddle brush works well). Run it through the hair once more to smooth any new tangles.  
2. **Part the hair** – Choose the side she likes best (most kids naturally favor one side). Use the comb to make a clean, straight part from the forehead to the crown.  
3. **Create the front braid (≈2‑3 inches long)**  

   a. Take a small piece of hair from just above the part, near the hairline.  
   b. Split it into three equal strands.  
   c. Braid a simple three‑strand braid (tight enough to stay, but not so tight it hurts).  
   d. Secure the braid’s tip with a tiny elastic band (the kind used for “ponytail‑tails”).  

4. **Gather the rest of the hair** – Using both hands, pull the remaining hair (including the braid) back toward the nape of the neck.  

   *If the hair is *very* thick, you might need a second person to hold the braid while you pull the rest together, but most of the time one hand can do it.*  

5. **Secure the ponytail** – Use a soft, stretchy hair tie (fabric‑covered or “no‑dent”). Wrap it once, then gently tug the tie to make it snug but not cutting into the scalp.  

   *If the ponytail feels too bulky, you can pull a tiny section of hair out of the tie (a “fluff”) to give it a softer silhouette.*  

6. **Optional finishing touches**  

   - **Smooth the front**: With a tiny dab of the leave‑in (or a light hairspray for kids), smooth any stray wisps around the face.  
   - **Add a tiny clip** (optional) to hide the braid’s elastic if you want a cleaner look.  
   - **Check the back in a mirror** – The ponytail should sit low, just above the nape, and the braid should be visible but not dominating.  

7. **Final “photo‑check”** – Have her sit down, tilt her head slightly forward (as most photographers ask), and look at her reflection together. If any stray hairs peek out, smooth them with the brush.

## 6️⃣ Common Mistakes & How to Fix Them (quick cheat‑sheet)

| Mistake | Quick Fix |
|--------|-----------|
