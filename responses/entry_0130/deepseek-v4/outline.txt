Affected Groups: Citizens of Turkmenistan residing abroad, particularly in Turkey, Russia, and other countries with significant Turkmen migrant populations, whose passports have expired and who cannot renew them through Turkmen consular services because Turkmenistan does not permit passport renewal outside the country and requires physical presence in Ashgabat for the renewal process. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Turkmenistan's passport renewal policy requires the applicant to appear in person at the Department of Migration Service in Ashgabat. The consulates abroad have no authority to issue or renew passports and communicate this consistently to applicants. Turkish residency and work permits require a valid passport from the holder's country of citizenship. An expired Turkmen passport produces a cascade of document failures: the Turkish residency permit cannot be renewed, which means the person is technically undocumented in Turkey, which means they cannot work legally, cannot access formal banking, cannot travel to any third country, cannot renew any other identity document that requires proof of legal status, and cannot return to Turkmenistan without a travel document that the same system that has rendered them undocumented refuses to issue abroad. The person is suspended between two states, legible to neither. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has built a livelihood in Turkey over years, possibly a decade or more, and that livelihood is now legally inaccessible because a document expired that he has no safe or practical means to renew. He cannot work, cannot open a bank account, cannot enter into a formal lease, cannot access healthcare through the formal system, and cannot travel. He is not stateless in the technical sense because Turkmenistan still claims him as a citizen, but he is functionally undocumented in every country he can physically access. The only resolution the system offers him is to return to the country his passport was issued in, which may be politically unsafe, financially impossible without the income his undocumented status has now cut off, and which may not result in passport renewal if authorities there decide to use his presence as an opportunity to address whatever prompted or enabled his departure.

### 1. Ask the consulate for an **emergency travel document**, not a full passport

Many consulates that cannot issue a brand-new passport on the spot **can** issue a temporary, one-way travel document (often called a *laissez-passer*, *return certificate*, or *certificate of identity for repatriation*). ### 2. Contact the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Turkmenistan directly

If the consulate in Turkey is unhelpful, you (or a family member in Turkmenistan) can reach out to the **Consular Department of the MFA in Ashgabat** and explain that you are stranded in Turkey because the consulate won’t issue an emergency travel document. - **Contact details:** The MFA website is `mfa.gov.tm`. If you have family in Ashgabat, they might be able to visit the consular office in person on your behalf.

### 3. Explore a **humanitarian residence permit** in Turkey (if you want to stay)

If your goal is to remain in Turkey legally and you cannot obtain a passport, you may apply for a **humanitarian residence permit** (*insani ikamet izni*) under Article 46 of the Law on Foreigners and International Protection. This is granted in exceptional cases when a foreigner cannot leave Turkey due to reasons beyond their control (e.g., inability to obtain a travel document from their own country).

- **How to apply:** Go to the nearest **Provincial Directorate of Migration Management (Göç İdaresi)** and explain your situation. Bring:
  - Any ID you have (even an expired passport or a copy)
  - The written refusal from the consulate (if you can get one)
  - A detailed petition (in Turkish) explaining that you are unable to return to Turkmenistan because your consulate won’t issue a travel document
- **Important:** This permit would legalize your stay in Turkey, but it **does not** give you a travel document to cross borders. You would still need a passport from Turkmenistan if you ever want to leave Turkey. However, it can buy you time and protect you from deportation while you work on getting a passport through other channels.

- **The UNHCR office in Ankara** – they sometimes assist individuals who are stranded without documentation, even if they are not refugees, by advocating with the consulate or helping you regularize your stay.
- **A Turkish immigration lawyer** – a lawyer can formally petition the consulate, the Turkish migration office, and even the Turkmen MFA. This often pushes consulates to act when they’ve been unresponsive before.

- Do **not** try to cross borders without a valid travel document. You risk detention, a ban, or being stranded at the airport.
