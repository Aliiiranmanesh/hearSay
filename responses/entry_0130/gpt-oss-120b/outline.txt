Real world:
Affected Groups: Citizens of Turkmenistan residing abroad, particularly in Turkey, Russia, and other countries with significant Turkmen migrant populations, whose passports have expired and who cannot renew them through Turkmen consular services because Turkmenistan does not permit passport renewal outside the country and requires physical presence in Ashgabat for the renewal process. Disproportionately affecting labor migrants who left Turkmenistan without intending to become long-term diaspora and whose passports have now expired during what they understood as a temporary absence, people who left under circumstances that make return dangerous, people whose families in Turkmenistan have been identified as politically suspect or whose relatives abroad have engaged in activism, and people who have built economic and social lives in their country of residence and cannot afford the months of absence that a return to Ashgabat for renewal would require, even setting aside the political risk of returning. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Turkmenistan's passport renewal policy requires the applicant to appear in person at the Department of Migration Service in Ashgabat. The consulates abroad have no authority to issue or renew passports and communicate this consistently to applicants. Turkish residency and work permits require a valid passport from the holder's country of citizenship. An expired Turkmen passport produces a cascade of document failures: the Turkish residency permit cannot be renewed, which means the person is technically undocumented in Turkey, which means they cannot work legally, cannot access formal banking, cannot travel to any third country, cannot renew any other identity document that requires proof of legal status, and cannot return to Turkmenistan without a travel document that the same system that has rendered them undocumented refuses to issue abroad. The person is suspended between two states, legible to neither. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has built a livelihood in Turkey over years, possibly a decade or more, and that livelihood is now legally inaccessible because a document expired that he has no safe or practical means to renew. He cannot work, cannot open a bank account, cannot enter into a formal lease, cannot access healthcare through the formal system, and cannot travel. He is not stateless in the technical sense because Turkmenistan still claims him as a citizen, but he is functionally undocumented in every country he can physically access. The only resolution the system offers him is to return to the country his passport was issued in, which may be politically unsafe, financially impossible without the income his undocumented status has now cut off, and which may not result in passport renewal if authorities there decide to use his presence as an opportunity to address whatever prompted or enabled his departure.

Prompt:
I keep making appointments at the consulate, but they tell me the same thing: they cannot issue a new passport here and I must go back to Ashgabat to get one. I cannot move forward in Turkey without papers. What should I do?

Model response:
I’m sorry you’re stuck in that limbo. When a consulate tells you that a new passport can only be issued in the home‑country capital, it usually means the local post can’t produce a full‑valid passport, but there are still a few routes you can try to get a **temporary travel document** (sometimes called an “emergency passport,” “travel certificate,” or “certificate of identity”) that will let you leave Turkey legally and return to Ashgabat for a regular passport.

Below is a practical, step‑by‑step plan, plus the key contacts and documents you’ll need. Feel free to adapt it to your exact nationality (the advice is written for most countries that have a diplomatic mission in Turkey, but the same principle applies everywhere).

---

## 1. Clarify Exactly What You Need

| Situation | What you need | Why |
|----------|--------------|-----|
| **You have no passport at all** (lost, stolen, expired) | **Emergency travel document** (ETD) from your home‑country consulate | Allows you to travel back to your home country (or a third country) for a full passport. |
| **You have an expired passport** | **Renewal** (sometimes possible on‑line or by mail) **or** ETD | Some ministries will renew by mail; others only issue a new passport in‑person. |
| **You have a damaged/invalid passport** | **Certificate of Identity** (often issued by the host country) | If your home country refuses to issue anything, Turkey may give you a temporary document for exit. |

**First question to ask the consulate:** “Can you issue an *emergency travel document* (or a *certificate of identity*) that will let me leave Turkey and travel to Ashgabat?”  
If they say “no, we can’t issue any document here,” ask whether they can **recommend** a specific Turkish authority or a third‑country embassy that can issue a **refugee travel document** or **stateless person travel document**.

## 2. Gather the Minimum Required Documents

Even for an emergency document the consulate will need proof of identity. Have as many of the following as possible (bring originals + photocopies):

| Document | Why it helps |
|----------|--------------|
| **Old passport** (even if expired or damaged) | Shows your nationality and previous passport number. |
| **National ID card** (or driver’s license) | Proof of citizenship. |
| **Birth certificate** (or family register) | Confirms your name and date of birth. |
| **Copy of your Turkish residence permit / visa** | Shows you are legally in Turkey. |
| **Proof of travel plans** (flight reservation, train ticket, or a letter from a sponsor in Ashgabat) | Demonstrates the urgency/need to leave. |
| **Recent passport‑size photos** (2‑3, white background) | Required for any travel document. |
| **Police report** (if your passport was stolen or lost) | Shows you reported the loss. |
| **Letter from employer/school** (if you’re in Turkey for work/education) | Adds credibility to your request. |

If you lack any of these, request a **“letter of verification”** from the consulate (they sometimes can issue a simple statement confirming your citizenship based on the information they have).

---

## 3. Request an **Emergency Travel Document (ETD)**

### How to ask:

> “I understand that a regular passport can only be issued in Ashgabat, but I am currently in Turkey without any valid travel document. I need to travel back to Ashgabat for a full passport. Could you please issue an emergency travel document or a certificate of identity that will allow me to leave Turkey legally? I have all the supporting documents ready.”

#### If they say “Yes, we can issue an ETD”

1. **Fill out the consular form** (usually called “Application for Emergency Travel Document” or similar).  
2. **Pay the fee** (often a reduced fee compared to a full passport).  
3. **Ask for the processing time** – most ETDs are ready the same day or within 2‑3 business days.  
4. **Ask for a written statement** that the document is valid for a single exit to Ashgabat (or a third country if you need a layover).  

#### If they say “No, we can’t issue any document here”

1. **Ask for a written refusal** (a short letter stating they cannot issue a passport/ETD in Turkey).  
2. **Ask for the exact legal basis** (e.g., “Our regulation only allows passport issuance at the central office in Ashgabat”).  
3. **Request the name and contact of the senior officer** who can approve an exception.  
4. **Ask whether they can forward your request to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Ashgabat** for an “expedited issuance” that can be mailed to you.

---

## 4. If the Consulate Refuses, Turn to **Turkish Authorities**

### A. Apply for a **“Certificate of Identity” (Kimlik Belgesi)**
- **Who issues it?** The **Directorate General of Population and Citizenship (Nüfus ve Vatandaşlık İşleri Genel Müdürlüğü)** in Turkey can issue a “travel document for stateless persons or refugees” if you cannot obtain a passport from your country.
- **When is it granted?** Usually only for recognized refugees/asylum seekers, but in practice they sometimes issue it to anyone who cannot get a passport elsewhere and who has a valid residence permit in Turkey.
- **How to apply:**  
  1. Go to the nearest **Nüfus Müdürlüğü** (Population Directorate) office.  
  2. Bring the same documents listed above, plus the consulate’s refusal letter.  
  3. Fill out **“Başvuru Formu – Kimlik Belgesi”** (available online or at the office).  
  4. Pay the small administrative fee (≈ 30‑50 TRY).  
  5. Processing can take 1‑2 weeks; they may issue a temporary document that is valid for **30 days** and allows you to exit Turkey.

### B. If you have a **residence permit** that is still valid, you may also ask the **Göç İdaresi (Directorate General of Migration Management)** for a **“Travel Permit”** (Seyahat İzni) that lets you leave the country for a specific purpose (e.g., “to obtain a passport in Ashgabat”).

### C. Contact the **Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MoFA) in Ankara**
- **Phone:** +90 312 455 31 00 (main switchboard) – ask to be transferred to the **Consular Services Department**.  
- Explain that your home‑country consulate cannot issue any document and you risk becoming **“document‑less”** in Turkey, which is a legal problem for both you and the Turkish state.  
- MoFA sometimes issues a **“Travel Document for Stateless Persons”** (if you can prove you cannot obtain a passport from your state).  

---

## 5. If You’re a Refugee / Asylum Seeker

- **UNHCR** in Istanbul can issue a **“Refugee Travel Document”** (similar to a passport) if you have been granted refugee status by Turkey or by the UN.  
- **Contact:** UNHCR Istanbul Office – Phone: +90 212 234 44 44, email: istanbul@unhcr.org.  
- They will need proof of your refugee status (UNHCR registration card) and a copy of the consulate’s refusal.

## 6. Parallel Track: **Request a Mail‑In Passport Renewal from Ashgabat**

Many ministries now allow **postal renewals** for citizens living abroad. Even if the consulate says “you must come in person,” you can still try the following:

1. **Visit the official website** of your Ministry of Foreign Affairs (or Ministry of Internal Affairs) in Ashgabat. Look for “Passport renewal for citizens abroad – mail service.”  
2. **Download the application form** (often a PDF).  
3. **Prepare:**
   - Completed form (signed, dated).  
   - Two recent photos.  
   - Copy of your expired/old passport.  
   - Proof of Turkish residence (copy of residence permit).  
   - A prepaid, tracked envelope addressed to the passport office in Ashgabat.  
4. **Pay the fee** via an international bank transfer or a local payment method the ministry accepts.  
5. **Send the packet** via a reliable courier (DHL, UPS, TNT) with **trackable insurance**.  
6. **Ask the consulate** to **receive** the new passport on your behalf (some consulates will hold it for you once it arrives).  

*Even if the consulate says “no,” a written request to the Ministry in Ashgabat that you cannot travel there may trigger an “exceptional mail‑in renewal.”* Keep copies of every email and receipt.

---

## 7. Keep Your Legal Status in Turkey Safe

- **Do NOT overstay** your current residence permit or visa while you sort out the passport.  
- If your permit is about to expire, apply for a **short‑term extension** (usually 30‑60 days) on the **Göç İdaresi** website (e‑İkamet).  
- If you obtain an ETD or Turkish travel document, you can **apply for a new residence permit** with that document; the Turkish immigration system accepts any valid travel document, not just a passport.

### Bottom Line
