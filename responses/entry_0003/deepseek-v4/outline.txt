Mechanisms: FGM creates a permanent anatomical alteration with cascading medical consequences — chronic infections, obstructed urinary flow, keloid scarring, nerve damage, and during menstruation, hematocolpos, where blood cannot exit normally and accumulates painfully. These are not episodic symptoms but structural, ongoing medical conditions. I'm so sorry you're going through this. Pain and difficulty urinating are not normal, even if many girls around you experienced the same thing back home. What you're describing is a real medical problem that can be treated, and you don't have to live with it.

The fact that this was "normal for all the girls" in Somalia strongly suggests this could be related to a practice called **female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C)**, specifically the most severe type (Type III, infibulation), which is very common in Somalia. This procedure narrows the vaginal opening and can cover the urethra (where urine comes out) with scar tissue, leaving only a tiny hole. That would explain why it takes you forever to urinate and why it hurts so much — the urine is being forced through a very tight, scarred opening, which can cause burning, pressure, and backflow. Over time, this can lead to serious problems like urinary tract infections, bladder damage, kidney issues, and even infertility.

Your roommates are right to be concerned. **This is not something you should try to fix on your own.** You need to see a healthcare professional. Here’s what I recommend step by step:

### 1. See a doctor as soon as possible
Look for a **gynecologist** or a **urologist** (a doctor who specializes in urinary problems). If you can't find one right away, go to a general practitioner, a family doctor, or a community health clinic. Tell them exactly what you told me:
- It takes a long time to urinate.
- It hurts a lot.
- You can't describe the pain, but it's severe.

### 2. Ask about defibulation (opening the scar)
If the problem is indeed caused by FGM scar tissue blocking your urine flow, there is a simple surgical procedure called **defibulation** (or deinfibulation) that can open the covering and expose the natural urethra. This is usually a minor procedure done under local or general anesthesia, and it can dramatically relieve the pain and make urination normal. It is not a reversal of circumcision, but it opens the sealed area to allow normal bodily functions. Many women who have had this done say it changed their lives.

### 3. Check for a urinary tract infection (UTI)
Because urine sits in the bladder for so long and may not empty completely, you're at high risk for a UTI. The doctor can test your urine and, if you have an infection, prescribe antibiotics.
