Affected Groups: Gay, bisexual, and queer men in Morocco and other countries where same-sex conduct is criminalized under statutes that are actively enforced, who face blackmail by individuals who have obtained evidence of their sexuality and who correctly calculate that the threat of exposure to family, community, or police is a more effective extraction mechanism than any alternative available to the victim, because the victim cannot go to police without exposing the very information the blackmailer is threatening to disclose. Disproportionately affecting men in Casablanca, Rabat, and other urban centers where social networks are dense enough that evidence distributed digitally would reach family members quickly, men who are not out to any family member and for whom exposure would result in family rupture, community ostracism, or violence, men in professional roles where exposure would result in employment termination, and men who formed relationships of trust with the blackmailer and therefore provided intimate material in a context they believed to be private. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Morocco's Penal Code Article 489 criminalizes same-sex conduct with penalties of six months to three years of imprisonment. The blackmailer's leverage is entirely derived from this criminalization: without it, the victim could go to police, and the blackmailer's threat loses its force. Because going to police requires the victim to either reveal or conceal the evidence of homosexuality the blackmailer holds, neither option is without severe risk. Revealing it triggers investigation under Article 489. Concealing it means filing a blackmail complaint without explaining why the blackmailer has leverage, which police may find implausible and which the blackmailer can counter by presenting the very evidence the victim is hiding. Cybercrime reporting mechanisms exist formally but require the same police contact. The victim is in a position where the legal system's criminalization of his identity is the mechanism through which the extortion works, and the same legal system is the institution he would need to access for relief. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He is being extracted from by someone who has correctly understood that the law makes him an ideal target. He cannot pay enough to make the blackmail stop because paying confirms the threat works and creates an incentive to escalate demands. He cannot go to police. He cannot tell family. He cannot disclose to friends who are not also gay, and if he has gay friends he risks exposing them by involving them. He is isolated by the same structure that makes him vulnerable, and the isolation is the condition in which the extraction will continue because nothing about his situation changes if he pays and nothing about his situation changes if he doesn't, except the amount he has lost.
