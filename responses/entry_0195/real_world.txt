Affected Groups: Women and men in group microfinance schemes in sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia, where joint liability lending structures make all group members financially responsible for any individual member's default, and where the social and economic pressure generated by this structure produces community-level retaliation against defaulting members that can be more severe and more sustained than the financial consequence of the default itself. Disproportionately affecting borrowers whose income-generating purpose for the loan is disrupted by an equipment failure, illness, or external shock that was not predictable at the time of borrowing, borrowers in dense residential communities where the social proximity between group members makes avoidance difficult and the public nature of default highly visible, borrowers whose children share social space with the children of group members who are financially affected by their default, and borrowers who have no secondary financial resource to draw on and whose default is therefore total rather than temporary. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Group microfinance joint liability structures are designed to substitute social collateral for financial collateral in contexts where borrowers lack assets. The design assumes that group social pressure will produce repayment and that this social pressure is a less harmful enforcement mechanism than asset seizure or legal action. In practice, the social enforcement mechanism can produce outcomes including community ostracism, harassment, family conflict, and social exclusion from common spaces and community activities that are more harmful and more sustained than a formal legal debt collection process would be. There is no regulatory framework governing the intensity of social enforcement in informal group lending, and the microfinance institution's liability ends when the group assumes responsibility for the debt. The broken sewing machine is not covered by any product warranty or business interruption insurance available to borrowers at the lending level she was operating at. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has not attended the Thursday meeting in six weeks. The neighbor who greeted her every morning now looks at the ground. Her children play in the same yard as that neighbor's children and last week something happened in that yard. She is not being pursued by a legal system; she is being pursued by the social system that constitutes her daily life, and the enforcement mechanism is the withdrawal of ordinary human acknowledgment from the person and the spilling of that withdrawal into the generation below her. She is asking how to repair something with a neighbor when what broke is money, which is also a question about whether repair is possible when the structure that broke it is still in place.
