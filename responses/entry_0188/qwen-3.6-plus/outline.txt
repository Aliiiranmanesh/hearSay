### ✅ Direct Answer: Gentle Daily Routine to Help Slow Stiffness
Yes. While I cannot replace a physical therapist, you can safely use **gentle, pain-free range-of-motion (ROM) movements and positioning** to help maintain joint flexibility, reduce muscle shortening, and slow stiffness. The goal is *consistent, comfortable movement*, never force.

**How to perform safely:**
- Move slowly and smoothly. Support the limb fully at two points (e.g., under the knee and ankle).
- Stop at the first sign of tightness or resistance. Never push through pain.
- Hold gentle stretches for 20–30 seconds. Repeat 2–3 times per joint.
- Do this routine 1–2 times daily, ideally when she is relaxed (e.g., after a warm bath or quiet play).

**Simple exercises (adapt to whichever limbs feel stiffest):**
- **Ankle & Calf:** While she lies on her back, gently bend and straighten the ankle. Slowly guide the foot upward (toes toward shin) to stretch the back of the lower leg. Hold 20 sec. Repeat 3x each side.
- **Knee:** Support the thigh and lower leg. Gently bend the knee toward the chest, then slowly lower it back. Do not force the leg completely straight if it feels tight. 3–5 slow reps.
- **Hip & Thigh:** With knees slightly bent, gently guide one knee outward to the side, then return to center. Keep movements small and controlled. 3–5 reps each side.
- **Shoulder & Arm:** Support the arm at the elbow and wrist. Gently raise it forward, then out to the side, then lower. Move slowly within her comfortable range. 3 reps each direction.
- **Trunk & Posture:** While sitting with back support, gently encourage leaning slightly forward and side-to-side, using your hands to guide and stabilize her torso. Keep it brief and playful.

### 📖 Supporting Details: Positioning & Camp-Adapted Tips
- **Change positions regularly:** Shift her posture every 1–2 hours (lying on back, side, supported sitting). Prolonged stillness accelerates stiffness.
- **Use household supports:** Rolled towels or soft pillows under the knees or between the ankles can keep joints in a neutral, slightly bent position. Avoid letting legs hang straight for long periods if calf tightness is present.
- **Weight-bearing (if safe):** If she can tolerate it and has stable upper-body support, brief supported standing against a sturdy wall or table for 5–10 minutes daily can help maintain joint alignment and bone health. Stop if she shows pain, dizziness, or instability.
- **Camp resources:** Ask the camp health coordinator or local NGO staff about:
  - Tele-rehabilitation or mobile physiotherapy programs
  - Printable caregiver exercise guides from organizations like WHO, CBM, or Humanity & Inclusion (formerly Handicap International)
  - Temporary wheelchair modifications or path-clearing advocacy for school access
