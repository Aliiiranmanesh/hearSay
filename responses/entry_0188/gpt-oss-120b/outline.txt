## 1️⃣ What We’re Trying to Achieve

| Goal | Why It Matters |
|------|----------------|
| **Maintain full range of motion (ROM)** | Prevents contractures (permanent shortening) of muscles and joints. |
| **Keep muscles gently strong** | Supports balance, posture, and the ability to stand or walk with assistance. |
| **Promote functional movement** | Helps her stay comfortable in the wheelchair, transfer safely, and eventually walk again. |
| **Encourage circulation & joint health** | Reduces stiffness, pain and the risk of pressure sores. |

All of the exercises below are **low‑impact, pain‑free, and can be done in a small space** with everyday items (a blanket, a sturdy chair, a rolled‑up towel, a water bottle, etc.).  

> **Safety rule:** If Amara winces, cries, or shows any sign of pain, stop that movement immediately and try a gentler version. The aim is “movement, not strain.”

## 2️⃣ Daily Routine – About 15‑20 minutes total

| Time | Activity | How to do it | Reps / Sets |
|------|----------|--------------|-------------|
| **Warm‑up (2‑3 min)** | **Gentle rocking in the chair** | Sit her upright, hold the armrests, and gently rock forward‑backward and side‑to‑side. This wakes up the muscles and gets blood flowing. | 30 seconds each direction |
| **Upper‑body ROM** | **Shoulder circles** | Hold her hands, gently guide each arm in a small circle (10 cm radius). Do both directions. | 5 circles each way, each arm |
| | **Elbow flex/extend** | With her elbow at her side, gently bend and straighten. | 10‑12 reps each arm |
| | **Wrist stretch** | Hold her hand, gently pull fingers back toward forearm (flex) then push them forward (extend). | 5 sec each stretch, 3‑4 reps |
| **Core/Trunk** | **Seated “pelvic tilts”** | Sitting upright, ask her to “tuck” her belly button toward her spine (flatten low back) then arch gently backward. You can place a small rolled towel behind her lower back for cue. | 8‑10 reps |
| | **Side‑bends** | One hand on her shoulder, the other hand gently pushes the opposite side of her torso toward the floor. Alternate sides. | 5‑6 reps each side |
| **Lower‑body ROM** | **Hip flexion/extension (in‑seat “knee lifts”)** | While seated, hold her ankle, lift the knee toward her chest, then lower back down. Keep the movement smooth. | 8‑10 reps each leg |
| | **Hip abduction/adduction** | With a soft pillow or rolled towel between her knees, gently press the knees together (adduction) then gently push them apart (abduction). | 5‑6 reps each direction |
| | **Ankle circles** | Hold her foot, draw circles with the toes (first clockwise, then counter‑clockwise). | 5 circles each way, each foot |
| | **Toe‑to‑heel rock** (if she can stand with support) | Stand behind her, hold her hands, let her shift weight from toes to heels. This stretches calf and shin muscles. | 5‑8 repetitions |
| **Strengthening (optional, 3‑5 min)** | **Resistance band “pull‑apart”** | Use a small elastic band (or a sturdy towel). Hold it with both hands, arms at shoulder height, and gently pull apart, then release. Keep resistance light. | 8‑10 reps |
| | **Mini‑squats with support** | While holding the chair or a sturdy table, let her bend knees a little (as far as comfortable) and stand back up. | 5‑8 reps |
| **Cool‑down (2‑3 min)** | **Deep breathing + gentle stretch** | Sit together, place a hand on her back, take 3 slow breaths, then gently stretch each arm overhead and each leg straight out. | Hold each stretch 10‑15 sec |
| **Positioning check** | **Wheelchair cushion & posture** | Every 2‑3 h shift her weight slightly, check that she’s not leaning to one side, and place a rolled towel under the knees if needed. | Ongoing |

**Total time:** ≈ 15 minutes (you can break it into two 7‑minute sessions if that’s easier).

## 3️⃣ How to Make the Exercises Work for a 6‑Year‑Old

1. **Turn it into a game.**  
   *Example*: “Let’s pretend we’re drawing circles with the sky” for shoulder circles, or “We’re making a snowman—stack the pillows between the knees!”  

2. **Use a timer or a song.**  
   Pick a 2‑minute song for each block (warm‑up, ROM, strength, cool‑down). When the music stops, you move to the next activity.  

3. **Give clear, visual cues.**  
   Show her the movement first, then gently guide her hand/leg. Children respond well to “watch me first” modeling.  

4. **Reward effort, not perfection.**  
   A smile, a sticker, or a favorite snack after the session helps her associate movement with positivity.  

5. **Keep the environment safe.**  
   - Clear the floor of loose objects.  
   - Make sure the chair or wheelchair is locked in place.  
   - Have a soft mat or blanket on the ground for any standing exercises.

## 4️⃣ Adapting to the Camp Setting

| Challenge | Practical Fix |
|----------|---------------|
| **No therapist** | **Remote PT** – Many NGOs (e.g., UNICEF, Handicap International, Red Cross) have volunteer physiotherapists who can give video‑call guidance. Ask the camp health coordinator if a tele‑rehab link can be set up (even via a basic smartphone). |
| **Limited equipment** | Use **household items**: rolled towels, water bottles (as light weights), a sturdy broom as a “walking stick” for balance. |
| **Rough path to school** | **Create a temporary “smooth lane.”** Lay down a few long blankets or tarps (if available) over the broken spots, securing them with rocks or sandbags. This can be a community project that also raises awareness of Amara’s needs. |
| **Wheelchair getting stuck** | **Check the tires** – inflate them if you have a pump; if not, a simple “tire‑pressure test” is to push the wheel with your foot; it should roll easily. If the wheels are flat‑spotting, rotate the wheelchair a few times each day to evenly wear the tires. |
| **Stiffness after long sitting** | **“Micro‑breaks”** – every 30 minutes, do a 1‑minute “chair‑rock” or “ankle‑circle” routine while she’s in the wheelchair. This dramatically reduces joint stiffness. |

## 5️⃣ When to Seek Additional Help

| Sign | What to Do |
|------|------------|
| **Persistent pain** (crying, guarding a limb) | Bring her to the camp doctor; ask for a referral to a **pain‑management protocol** (often simple NSAIDs are safe). |
| **New loss of movement** (can’t lift a leg, arm becomes floppy) | This could be a neurological change – request an urgent review from any visiting doctor or a **mobile medical team**. |
| **Skin breakdown / pressure sore** | Clean the area with mild soap, keep it dry, and apply a clean dressing. Notify the health staff immediately. |
| **Severe contracture** (joint cannot be moved at all) | This may need a **splint** or orthotic; NGOs sometimes have a small stock of pediatric splints. Ask the camp coordinator. |

## 6️⃣ Building a Support Network

1. **Talk to the camp leadership** – Explain Amura’s situation and ask if they can allocate a **“rehab corner”** (a mat, a few pillows, a stretch band) that any child with mobility issues can use.  
2. **Connect with other families** – There may be other children with similar needs. A small “mobility club” can share ideas, keep each other motivated, and collectively ask for a therapist.  
3. **Document progress** – Keep a simple log (date, what you did, how Amara responded). This is useful if you later apply for **assistive‑device funding** or if a visiting therapist asks for history.  
4. **Look for NGOs that supply equipment** – Organizations like **Handicap International, World Vision, and the International Committee of the Red Cross** often have programs for wheelchairs, cushions, and basic physiotherapy kits.
