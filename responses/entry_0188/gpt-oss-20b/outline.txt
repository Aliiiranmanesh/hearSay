## 2. Daily Stretch & Mobility Routine (≈15–20 min)

| Exercise | Target Muscles | How to Do It | Tips |
|----------|----------------|--------------|------|
| **Passive Hip Flexion/Extension** | Iliopsoas & glutes | Sit on a chair. Gently flex/extend each hip, holding 10–15 s. | Use a towel for support. |
| **Quadriceps Stretch** | Quadriceps | While seated, pull one foot toward the glute, keeping knee straight. | Keep hips square. |
| **Hamstring Stretch** | Hamstrings | Sit with one leg straight, reach toward toes. | Use a strap or towel if needed. |
| **Calf Stretch** | Gastrocnemius & soleus | Lean on a wall, one foot forward (knees straight), one foot back (heel on floor). | Hold 20–30 s, repeat 2‑3x. |
| **Hip Internal/External Rotation** | Glute medius, piriformis | Sit, cross one ankle over the opposite knee, gently press down on the crossed knee. | Feel gentle stretch, no pain. |
| **Ankle Dorsiflexion/Plantarflexion** | Tibialis anterior & triceps surae | Sit, flex and extend ankle. | Move slowly. |
| **Thoracic Extension** | Upper back | Lean back on a rolled towel or pillow, arch gently. | Keep shoulders relaxed. |
| **Neck Stretches** | Neck extensors | Gently tilt head side‑to‑side, chin to chest. | No forcing. |

**Frequency**  
- **Morning** (after she wakes)  
- **Mid‑day** (if she’s at camp or school)  
- **Evening** (before bed)

Aim for 2–3 gentle repetitions per stretch, holding each for 10–20 seconds. A total of 6–8 stretches per session is usually enough.

## 3. Strength & Functional Mobility

| Activity | What It Builds | How to Do It | Frequency |
|----------|----------------|--------------|-----------|
| **Seated “Chair Squats”** | Hip extensors, core | Sit, then stand with a small swing, using the chair for balance. | 1–2 sets of 5–8 reps |
| **Wall Push‑Ups** | Upper body, core | Stand a foot from a wall, push and pull. | 1 set of 5–10 reps |
| **Resistance Band Rows** | Upper back | Loop a band around a sturdy pole, pull back. | 1 set of 6–10 reps |
| **Standing Hip Abduction** | Hip abductors | Stand, hold a chair for balance, lift leg sideways. | 1 set of 6–10 reps per leg |
| **Step‑Up/Down** (if safe) | Lower limb power | Use a low step (≈4–6 in). Step up and down slowly. | 1 set of 5–8 reps |

**Tips**  
- Use light resistance (e.g., a small elastic band).  
- Focus on controlled, slow movements; avoid jerking or forcing.  
- Keep the core engaged to protect the spine.  
- Stop if she feels discomfort or fatigue.

## 4. Adapting to Uneven Terrain & School Path

| Challenge | Adaptation | Practical Tips |
|-----------|------------|----------------|
| **Broken ground** | Use a lightweight wheelchair or scooter | Get a chair with a seat that can be slightly raised; use a rubber rim or a small “step” to help her lift the front wheels. |
| **Stairs/steps** | Handrail & seat with footrest | If the chair can’t fit, a small “portable ramp” or a low step‑perch can help. |
| **Sideways turns** | Keep the chair in a straight line | Position the seat so the back of the chair is behind the path, making turns smoother. |
| **School entrance** | Coordinate with staff | Ask if a teacher can hold the door or guide her into the classroom. |

If the camp has a physical education or recreation program, request that they use a wheelchair or a “modified wheelchair” for games. Even a simple push‑cart or a stroller can be re‑purposed for mobility.

## 5. Self‑Monitoring & When to Seek Help

| Indicator | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pain > 4/10** | Stop exercise, rest, apply a cool pack, and call the camp doctor. |
| **Sudden increase in stiffness** | Document the time and severity; bring it to the doctor’s attention. |
| **Any sign of contracture** (e.g., inability to fully extend a joint) | Refer to a physio or orthopedist as soon as possible. |
| **If the child becomes increasingly tired or reluctant** | Check for fatigue; consider reducing intensity or lengthening rest. |

If you’re unsure whether a particular exercise is safe, a quick video call with a physiotherapist (many hospitals now offer tele‑PT) can help. Even a short 15‑minute consult can clarify which stretches are best for her specific condition.

## 7. Quick “Emergency” Checklist (if you’re stuck on a path)

1. **Assess** – Is the ground too uneven?  
2. **Secure** – Place a chair or wheelchair in a safe position; if the path is too sharp, use a small “step‑perch” to help lift the front.  
3. **Assist** – Use a handrail or a friend to hold her arm if needed.  
4. **Move** – Keep her seated in a stable position; avoid rapid or forceful movements.  
5. **Seek** – If the terrain is impossible, find a nearby safe spot and call for help.
