## Safety rules first
- Do movements **slowly and gently**. Stop if she cries from pain, her breathing changes, or the limb “fights back” strongly.
- A stretch should feel like a gentle pull, not pain.
- Hold stretches **20–30 seconds**, repeat **2–3 times**.
- Try to do a little **every day**, even 15–20 minutes helps.
- Warm muscles stretch better: after washing/bathing, or after a few minutes of play/movement.

## Daily routine for stiffness

### 1) Ankles/calves — to help feet stay flatter
Amara lies on her back or sits with her knee straight.

- Hold her heel with one hand.
- With the other hand, gently bring the foot upward toward her shin.
- Keep the foot straight, not turned in.
- Do with the **knee straight**, then again with the **knee bent**.

Hold 20–30 seconds, 2–3 times each foot.

### 2) Knees/hamstrings — back of the thigh
Amara lies on her back.

- Keep one leg flat on the ground.
- Lift the other leg upward with the knee as straight as she can comfortably manage.
- You should feel the stretch behind the thigh.

Hold 20–30 seconds, 2–3 times each leg.

### 3) Hip opening — if her legs cross or get tight together
Amara lies on her back with knees bent and feet on the floor/bed.

- Let her knees slowly move apart like “butterfly wings.”
- You can support her knees gently.
- Do not push hard.

Hold 20–30 seconds, repeat 2–3 times.

Another option: with legs straight, gently move one leg out to the side and back in, keeping the kneecap facing up.

### 4) Hip stretch — lying on tummy
If she can tolerate it, have her lie on her stomach for **5–10 minutes**, once or twice daily.

This helps stretch the front of the hips, which often gets tight when a child sits a lot.

Make it play: reading, singing, toys in front of her.  
If she cannot tolerate tummy lying, try side-lying and gently bring the top thigh slightly backward while keeping her body straight.

### 5) Knee straightening position
If her knees are getting bent and hard to straighten:

- Have her lie on her back.
- Put a small rolled cloth under her ankle, not under the knee.
- Let the knee relax toward straight.

Do this for a few minutes if comfortable. Do **not** press hard on the knee.

## Active exercises — very important
Stretching helps, but **using her muscles** helps even more.

### Sit-to-stand practice
From a chair/bed where her feet touch the floor:

- Help her lean forward.
- Encourage her to push through her feet and stand.
- Help only as much as needed.

Do 5–10 times, once or twice daily.

### Supported standing
If safe, let her stand holding a table, bed frame, wall, or your hands.

Aim for a little each day — even **5 minutes at a time**.  
Feet should be as flat as possible. Knees and hips should be as straight as comfortable.

### Supported walking/stepping
If she used to walk with support, keep practicing short distances:

- Hold her at the trunk/hips rather than pulling arms upward.
- Go slowly.
- Encourage feet flat and steps forward.

Even a few minutes daily can help maintain ability.

### Play movements
Use games:
- Reaching for objects to left/right.
- Rolling on the mat/blanket.
- Kicking a soft ball.
- Crawling or moving on hands/knees if she can.
- Tall kneeling while holding a support.

## Positioning during the day
Because she is in the chair more now, stiffness can worsen.

Try to change position often:
- Sitting
- Side-lying
- Tummy lying
- Supported standing
- Lying with legs straight

In the chair:
- Feet supported, not dangling.
- Hips and knees around 90 degrees if possible.
- Try to keep knees from crossing — a rolled towel between knees can help.
- Avoid her sitting in a “W” shape on the ground, if she does that.
