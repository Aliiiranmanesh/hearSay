### 📋 Direct answers to your questions

**1. Finding care with minimal paperwork**  
Legitimate medical care inherently involves some clinical documentation for safety, medication tracking, and follow-up. However, you can significantly reduce administrative or external reporting by:
- Seeking **self-pay/cash-pay urgent care clinics**, **direct primary care (DPC) practices**, or **sports medicine/orthopedic clinics** that offer transparent out-of-pocket visits.
- Calling ahead and asking: *“Do you offer self-pay visits? What clinical documentation is created, and is it shared externally unless I request it?”*
- Note: If imaging (X-ray or ultrasound) is needed, the facility will still generate a standard medical record for radiology safety and quality control. You can typically request that records remain within the treating clinic unless legally required to be shared.

**2. Safe at-home checks to gauge seriousness**  
Remote assessment cannot replace an exam or imaging, but you can safely monitor:
- **Pain location:** Sharp pain right at the top of the shoulder (near the collarbone) often aligns with an AC joint issue. Pain with overhead reaching or at night may suggest rotator cuff or bursitis.
- **Visible changes:** Compare both shoulders in a mirror. Look for asymmetry, swelling, or a step-off/bump.
- **Gentle range of motion:** Slowly raise your arm forward and to the side. Note exactly where it stops and whether it’s blocked by pain, stiffness, or a mechanical catch. **Do not force it past the stopping point.**
- **Neurovascular check:** Ensure normal sensation in all fingers and the ability to grip/move them without weakness.
- **Stop immediately** if pain spikes, you feel instability, or symptoms worsen.

### 🔍 Supporting details & safe home monitoring
- **What it might be:** A “shoulder separation” typically refers to an acromioclavicular (AC) joint sprain or tear, often from a fall or direct impact. Other common causes of limited lifting include rotator cuff strains, subacromial bursitis, or impingement. These are distinguished by specific physical exam maneuvers and usually an X-ray.
- **Home care while you decide:** 
  - Rest from overhead lifting, pushing, or pulling.
  - Apply ice wrapped in a cloth for 15–20 minutes, 3–4 times daily for the first 48–72 hours.
  - Keep the arm supported in a comfortable position (e.g., resting on a pillow when seated).
  - Avoid stretching, massage directly over the painful area, or “testing” the joint.
  - Over-the-counter pain relief may be considered only if you normally tolerate it and have no kidney, stomach, bleeding, or medication-interaction concerns.
- **Monitoring window:** Track pain, swelling, and range of motion daily. If symptoms plateau or worsen, or if lifting remains blocked, clinical evaluation is recommended.
