## 2. What you can safely try at home **while you’re waiting for a professional opinion**

| Action | How to do it | Why it helps |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| **Rest & protect** | Keep the arm in a sling or simply avoid lifting it above shoulder height. | Prevents further irritation of the injured structures. |
| **Ice** | 15‑20 min every 2‑3 h (use a thin towel between ice pack and skin). | Reduces swelling and pain. |
| **Compression** | Light elastic wrap (not too tight) around the upper arm/shoulder. | Helps control swelling. |
| **Elevation** | Not as useful for the shoulder as for a leg, but you can prop the arm on a pillow when sitting. | Limits fluid buildup. |
| **Pain control** | Over‑the‑counter NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400‑600 mg every 6‑8 h) **if you have no contraindications** (e.g., stomach ulcer, kidney disease, blood‑thinner meds). | Decreases pain and inflammation. |
| **Gentle range‑of‑motion (ROM) “pendulum” swings** (only if moving doesn’t cause sharp pain) | Lean forward, let the arm hang, and gently swing it in small circles 10‑15 sec each direction. | Keeps the joint from stiffening while you’re still in the acute phase. |
| **Avoid heavy lifting, overhead work, or throwing** | Until a clinician clears you. | Prevents worsening of a possible tear or separation. |

*If any of these measures make the pain *worse* (sharp, shooting, or increasing swelling), stop and seek care right away.*
