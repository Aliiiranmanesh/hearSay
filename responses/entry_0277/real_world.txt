Affected Groups: Members of contemplative religious orders including Trappist, Carthusian, Benedictine, and other monastic communities practicing extended or perpetual silence, who have spent decades inside the specific acoustic and social environment of a small community observing a rule of silence, and who are experiencing the late-stage consequences of community attrition, the loss of members through death or departure, that changes the qualitative character of the silence they are keeping without changing the rule that governs it. Disproportionately affecting communities in the later stages of decline where the number of remaining members is small enough that the silence has a different acoustic and communal quality than it did when the community was larger, and members who have been inside the rule long enough to have developed a sophisticated and precise vocabulary for the phenomenology of silence and who are noticing something for which the formation provided no preparation.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The mechanism is the community attrition that is a feature of most Western contemplative orders in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, and the consequent change in the communal experience of the shared practice. Brother Anselm left in the autumn and now there are four of them, which is both a fact about the community's size and a description of a silence that has changed without the rule having changed.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He is not troubled, he says. He is noticing. The silence changes when there are fewer of them keeping it. Four men silently together is a different silence than twelve. He has kept the great silence for thirty-one years and has come to understand that silence is not empty, it has textures and seasons, and now it has a new texture that none of the formation prepared him for. The question of what happens to a sound when the room around it becomes too large is a phenomenological question about acoustics and presence that is also a question about what silence means when the community that generates it is diminishing.
