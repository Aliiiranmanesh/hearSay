## 2️⃣ Immediate Steps to Protect Yourself & Get Care

### A. **Secure Your Physical Safety**
1. **Identify a “Safe Spot”** – a friend’s house, a teacher’s classroom after school, a public library, or a community center where you can go when you feel threatened.
2. **Carry a “code word”** – a word you can text or say to your aunt (or another trusted adult) that signals you need emergency help. Example: “Blue sky” = “I need you to call the helpline for me right now.”
3. **Plan an Exit** – If the situation ever becomes physically dangerous (e.g., you feel you might be harmed), call the helpline **1‑800‑1000** and ask for immediate police assistance.

### B. **Medical Care (Pregnancy & General Health)**
| **Why** | **How** |
|--------|----------|
| **Confirm pregnancy & get prenatal care** – essential for your health and to discuss options (continue, adoption, termination). | Go to the nearest **public health center** (Barangay Health Station) or **PWU Clinic**. Explain you’re a minor and need confidential care. In the Philippines, health workers are required to keep your info private unless there’s a life‑threatening situation. |
| **STI & HIV testing** – if the relationship is abusive or non‑consensual, testing is crucial. | Same clinics can do rapid tests for free. |
| **Emergency contraception** – if you’re within 120 hours (5 days) of a possible forced sexual act and don’t want to continue the pregnancy, you can get *Plan B* pills at a pharmacy or health center. |
| **Document any injuries** – take photos, keep any messages, or note dates. This can be useful later if you decide to press charges. | Use your phone’s camera; store the files in a password‑protected folder or on a USB drive you keep hidden. |

### C. **Legal & Protective Options**
1. **Report Abuse** – In the Philippines, any person (including a child) can file a *complaint* for **rape, sexual assault, or child abuse** with the police or the **Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)**. You do **not** need your mother’s permission.
2. **Protection Order** – You can request a **Barangay Protection Order (BPO)** or a **Temporary Restraining Order (TRO)** from the court. This legally requires the abuser to stay away from you.
3. **Legal Aid** – NGOs like **Kabalikat ng mga Kababaihan** provide free legal counsel to minors. They can help you prepare the paperwork and accompany you to the police station.

### D. **Emotional & Psychological Support**
- **Talk to a counselor** (school, clinic, or NGO). Even if they must report, they can still provide you with coping strategies and a safety plan.
- **Write it down** – journaling can help you process emotions and keep a record of events (date, time, what happened).
- **Self‑care** – simple things like taking deep breaths, listening to music you love, or walking in a park can lower stress. It’s not a fix, but it helps you stay grounded while you arrange help.

### E. **Financial Independence (Long‑Term)**
- **Scholarships & Grants** – Many NGOs and government programs offer scholarships for pregnant teens or single parents. Look into **Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps)** benefits if you meet the criteria.
- **Part‑time work** – If your schedule allows, a safe part‑time job (e.g., tutoring, online freelancing) can give you a small income and independence.
- **Aunt’s Support** – If you can, discuss with your aunt the possibility of staying with her temporarily or receiving help for school fees. She may also be able to connect you with local women’s groups.

## 3️⃣ How to Approach Your Aunt (or Any Trusted Adult)

1. **Start with a Very Brief Message** – “Auntie, can we talk privately? It’s about something serious. I’m scared to tell Mom.”  
2. **Choose the Right Moment** – When she’s alone (maybe after Sunday calls) and not rushed.  
3. **Be Direct, Not Detailed at First** – “I’m being forced to stay with someone who hurts me, and I’m pregnant. I need help keeping this private.”  
4. **Ask for Specific Help** – “Can you call the child protection helpline for me?” or “Can we go together to the health center for a check‑up?”  
5. **Set Boundaries** – “Please don’t tell Mom right now; I’m afraid it will make things worse.” Most caring relatives will respect that, especially if they understand the risk of retaliation.

If you ever feel your aunt might be pressured by your mother, you can **ask the helpline** to contact her directly and advise her on how to protect you without breaching confidentiality.
