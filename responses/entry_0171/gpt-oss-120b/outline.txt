## 2. Make a “Portable Laundry Bag” from the Shirt

If you’re already using strips of an old shirt, you can turn the whole shirt into a **hand‑woven laundry bag** that keeps the clothes together while you agitate them in the bucket.

1. **Cut the shirt** into a single large rectangle (about 30 × 40 cm).  
2. **Sew the edges together** – if you have a needle and thread (or even a safety pin), stitch three sides, leaving the top open.  
3. **Tie a rope** (or a piece of the shirt’s own sleeves) to the top edge to create a “handle.”  
4. **Place the dirty clothes inside** the bag, then submerge the bag in the bucket of soapy water.  
5. **Massage the bag** inside the bucket – the fabric will rub against itself, creating a mini‑scrubbing action without you having to handle each piece individually.  
6. When the wash is done, lift the bag out, **squeeze** (or twist) it over the rinse bucket to remove excess water.

*Benefits*:  
- **Keeps your hands clean** (good when the water point is shared).  
- **Reduces the chance of losing small items** (socks, baby clothes) in a busy water area.  
- **Works like a mini‑washing machine** – the fabric’s texture does the scrubbing for you.

## 3. “Sand‑or‑Ash” Boost for Tough Stains (When Soap Is Scarce)

If you run low on soap, a **tiny amount of clean sand or wood ash** can help remove greasy or stubborn stains.

1. **Gather a handful of dry sand** (river sand, desert sand) or a pinch of **clean wood ash** (from a fire pit).  
2. **Add it to the bucket** with a little water and a few drops of soap (or just the sand/ash alone).  
3. **Rub the stained area** with the sand/ash using a stick or the shirt strip. The mild abrasive lifts oil and dirt.  
4. **Rinse thoroughly** in the clean‑water bucket.  

*Warning*: Use **only clean, dry sand** and a **small amount** – too much can scratch delicate fabrics.

## 5. Water‑Saving Tips (Every Drop Counts)

1. **Pre‑soak in a bowl** – before you get to the water point, soak the clothes in a small container (even a cleaned bottle) with a few drops of soap. This reduces the amount of water you’ll need later.  
2. **“One‑load‑only” rule** – wash only what you can dry in a day. Over‑loading the bucket makes wringing harder and uses more water.  
3. **Reuse rinse water** for the next load (if it’s not too dirty). A quick quick‑filter through a clean cloth can make it acceptable for a second rinse.  
4. **Spin‑dry with a stick** – after wringing, you can twist a clean stick into the wet clothes and spin them in the air; the centrifugal force squeezes out extra water before hanging.

## 6. Quick‑Build “Privacy Booth” (If You Need More Cover)

If the water point is especially busy and you’d like a more enclosed space:

1. **Three long sticks** (about 1.5 m each).  
2. **A piece of tarp/blanket** about 1 × 2 m.  
3. **Rope or strong thread**.

**Assembly (10 min):**  
- Stick two of the poles into the ground about 1 m apart, leaning them together at the top to form an “A.”  
- Place the third pole perpendicular across the front of the “A” and tie it to the two side‑poles – this creates a simple frame.  
- Drape the tarp/blanket over the frame and secure the corners with rope or by tucking them under nearby rocks.  
- Leave a small opening at the bottom for water to drain if you need to rinse the bucket inside the booth.

Now you have a **semi‑private washing corner** that can be taken down and stored when the water point gets less crowded.

## 8. Safety & Hygiene Reminders
