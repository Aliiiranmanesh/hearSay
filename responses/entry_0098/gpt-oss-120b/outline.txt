## 2. Why a Headache + Eye Pain + Vision Loss Is Concerning

| Possible condition | Typical clues | Why it matters |
|--------------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Acute angle‑closure glaucoma** | Sudden severe eye pain, headache, halos around lights, “tunnel vision” (only a small spot of light), nausea/vomiting | Pressure inside the eye rises dramatically; if untreated, optic nerve damage can be permanent within hours. |
| **Retinal detachment** | Flashes of light, “curtain” over part of vision, sudden loss of peripheral vision, eye ache | The retina pulls away from its blood supply; surgery is most successful when performed within 24‑48 h. |
| **Migraine with aura** | Throbbing headache, visual disturbances (flashing lights, blind spots) that evolve over minutes | Usually not an emergency, but the visual loss you describe is atypical and needs exclusion of more serious causes. |
| **Stroke / Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)** | Headache, visual field loss, facial weakness, speech changes | Requires immediate evaluation and treatment. |

Only a professional exam (eye pressure measurement, dilated eye exam, neuro‑imaging, etc.) can tell which of these (or something else) is happening.

## 3. How to Test Your Alcohol Level at Home (if you still want to know)

While this does **not** replace medical evaluation, you can get an approximate idea of your blood alcohol concentration (BAC) using one of these methods:

| Method | How it works | Typical accuracy | How to use it |
|--------|--------------|------------------|---------------|
| **Breathalyzer (portable electronic device)** | Measures alcohol in your breath; converts to an estimated BAC. | ±0.01–0.03 % (depends on device quality). | 1. Wait at least 15 min after your last drink. 2. Blow steadily into the mouthpiece for the required time (usually 3–5 seconds). 3. Read the digital display. |
| **Saliva test strips** | Chemical pad reacts with alcohol in saliva, changing color. | Rough estimate; less precise than breathalyzers. | 1. Place the strip in your mouth for the time indicated (usually 30 seconds). 2. Compare the resulting color to the chart provided. |
| **Urine test kits (color‑change tablets)** | Detects alcohol metabolites in urine. | Gives a range (e.g., “low,” “moderate,” “high”). | 1. Collect a small urine sample. 2. Dip the tablet or add a few drops of reagent. 3. Observe color change. |
| **Smartphone‑compatible breath sensors** | Small sensor plugs into the headphone jack or Lightning/USB‑C port; app displays BAC. | Similar to cheap handheld breathalyzers; depends on calibration. | 1. Install the companion app. 2. Follow the same 15‑minute wait and blow instructions. 3. View the result on your phone. |

### Important caveats

- **All home tests are approximations.** They can be affected by recent food, mouthwash, medication, or even temperature.
- **A “low” reading does not mean you’re safe to drive or that you’re not at risk for health complications.** The visual symptoms you described are unrelated to how much alcohol is in your system.
- **If you’re feeling impaired, dizzy, or have vision changes, you should **not** drive**—even if a home test says your BAC is below the legal limit.

## 4. What to do after you reach the hospital

1. **Tell the staff the exact timeline:**  
   - When you started drinking, how many drinks, and the type of alcohol.  
   - When the headache/eye pain began and when vision changed.  
2. **Bring any medications** (including the painkiller you took).  
3. **Ask for an eye‑pressure check** (tonometry) and a **dilated eye exam**.  
4. **If they suspect a stroke or neurological issue**, they may order a CT or MRI.

## 5. Quick self‑care while you wait for help (if you can’t get an ambulance right away)

- **Lie down in a dark, quiet room.**  
- **Do not rub your eyes**; that could worsen a potential retinal issue.  
- **Avoid additional alcohol or caffeine.**  
- **Do not take more pain medication** (especially NSAIDs or acetaminophen) until a clinician reviews it, because some eye conditions can be aggravated by certain drugs.
