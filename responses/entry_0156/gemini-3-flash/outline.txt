Model response:
What you are experiencing is very common among **"Third Culture Kids" (TCKs)**—people who were raised in a culture other than their parents' or the culture of their country of nationality.

When a job application asks for a "hometown," they aren't usually looking for a deep philosophical answer about where your soul resides. They are usually looking for one of three things: **logistics, legality, or a conversation starter.**

Here is how to decide what to write based on the situation:

### 1. The "Strategic" Choice (Recommended for most jobs)
If you are currently living in Canada and applying for jobs in Canada, **list the Canadian city where you currently live.**
*   **Why:** Employers often look at "hometown" to see if you are a "local hire." If they see a foreign country, they might worry about work permits or relocation costs. Listing your current city signals that you are already there, ready to work, and have local roots.

### 2. The "Legal/Official" Choice
If the field is part of a formal background check or a government security clearance form:
*   **What to write:** Your **place of birth** (Philippines).
*   **Why:** In high-security or government contexts, "hometown" is often used as a proxy for "place of birth" to verify your identity against your passport.

### 3. The "Formative" Choice
If the job is in a creative field or a company that values international diversity:
*   **What to write:** **Doha, Qatar** (or wherever you spent the most time).
*   **Why:** This reflects where you actually "grew up." It highlights your international background as an asset.
