## 1. Give the worry a “home” – Contain It, Don’t Chase It  

| **Step** | **What to do** | **Why it helps** |
|----------|----------------|------------------|
| **Name the worry** | Write a single sentence: “I’m worried my mother didn’t send her signal this month.” | Labeling turns vague anxiety into a concrete thought you can see. |

| **Schedule a “next‑step”** | Even if you can’t call, decide on a tiny action you *can* take (e.g., send a quick text, write a postcard, or check a community board). | Turns a feeling of helplessness into a concrete, doable step. |

**Try it now:** Grab a piece of paper or a notes app. Write:

> “I’m worried my mother didn’t send her signal this month. I will spend 15 minutes thinking about it at 7 p.m., then I’ll write a short message to a friend asking if they have any news from the area.”

When the timer goes off, close the page, take a few deep breaths, and move on to another activity.

## 2. Ground Yourself in the Present – Physical & Sensory Anchors  

When worry feels like a cloud that never lifts, grounding brings you back to what you can *actually* sense right now.

| **Technique** | **How to do it** | **When it works best** |
|---------------|------------------|------------------------|
| **5‑4‑3‑2‑1** | Identify 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. | When thoughts race. |
| **Box breathing** | Inhale 4 sec, hold 4 sec, exhale 4 sec, hold 4 sec. Repeat 4–6 cycles. | Before bedtime or during a sudden spike of anxiety. |
| **Movement reset** | Stand, stretch, then do 10 quick jumping‑jacks or walk briskly for 2 min. | When you feel “stuck” in mental loops. |

Pick one that feels easiest and keep it in your mental toolbox. You can practice it anytime the worry pops up.

## 4. Reframe the Uncertainty with Compassion

1. **Acknowledge the limits** – “I don’t have any new information, and that’s uncomfortable, but it’s not my fault.”
