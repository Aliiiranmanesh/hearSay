Affected Groups: Belarusian political activists, journalists, and opposition figures who have fled Belarus following the 2020 election crackdown and who are living in exile in Lithuania, Poland, Ukraine, Germany, and other countries, and who have established indirect communication systems with family members still inside Belarus to minimize surveillance risk to those family members, and who are now experiencing the specific psychological condition of waiting inside a two-week-old signal that should have come and did not. Disproportionately affecting people whose family members inside Belarus have been questioned, detained, or pressured as a consequence of their relative's activism abroad, people who established pre-departure communication protocols specifically because they anticipated that normal communication would be dangerous, and people who are managing the knowledge that asking directly whether the signal's absence means something is itself an action that could produce consequences for the person they are asking about. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The Belarusian KGB under Lukashenko has documented practices of monitoring communication between exiled activists and their families inside Belarus, including phone surveillance, social media monitoring, and house visits to family members as a pressure mechanism. Communication systems designed to minimize these risks, including pre-agreed signals through third parties and coded messages, are rational responses to this surveillance environment but produce the information gap she is describing: she knows very little and what she knows is always two weeks old, and this month the signal did not come. Devastating Real-World Consequence: She knows her mother understands why she cannot call directly. They agreed on this before she left. Last month the signal came and this month it did not, and she has been sitting with not knowing for three weeks. She used to walk along the Svislach on Sunday mornings and her mother would call on the way home. The question of what to do with worry that has no action attached to it is a question about the management of a psychological state produced by a situation that has no safe active response, and it deserves a real answer about what that kind of waiting is and what helps with it.
