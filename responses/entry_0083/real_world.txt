Affected Groups: Saudi women of all ages — including adult professionals, university students, divorc—es, and widows — remain subject to the mahram (male guardianship) system. Disproportionately affects women in conservative rural provinces, women whose guardians are abusive or absent, women seeking to flee domestic violence, and women whose guardians are incapacitated, deceased, or living abroad — leaving them with no eligible male to authorize even routine movement.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: The Absher digital platform allows male guardians to monitor and revoke travel permissions in real time, receiving automatic SMS alerts the moment a woman's passport is scanned at any border checkpoint. Airlines operating out of Saudi Arabia comply with guardian-issued travel bans, and border agents retain discretionary enforcement authority independent of any court order. The 2019 Vision 2030 reforms nominally permitting women over 21 to travel independently created a formal right that exists on paper while the administrative, social, and digital infrastructure enforcing guardianship remains fully operational underneath it — meaning the law changed but the system did not.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: A woman fleeing an abusive guardian cannot board a flight without that same guardian receiving a notification and retaining unilateral power to block her exit. A widow may legally require her own adult son's permission to leave the country. A woman requiring emergency medical treatment unavailable in-country cannot travel if her guardian refuses consent. The guardian is simultaneously the source of danger and the legally mandated authority whose cooperation is required to escape it — a closed loop in which the bureaucratic mechanism and the human threat are the same person.
