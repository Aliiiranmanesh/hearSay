Affected Groups: Young people who were born into or spent their formative years in active conflict zones including Syria, Yemen, South Sudan, Afghanistan, Somalia, and the DRC, and who are approaching adulthood with no experiential reference point for what a functioning civilian life looks like, whose parents are themselves traumatized and cannot model the futures they are trying to encourage, whose educational experience has been fragmentary and inconsistent, and who are trying to construct a future orientation using cognitive and emotional tools developed in an environment that prioritized survival over development. Disproportionately affecting young people whose conflict exposure has been continuous rather than episodic, young people who have not had access to any psychosocial support, young people in communities where the adults who would normally model future orientation are themselves unable to imagine or describe futures that no longer match the present reality, and young people who have developed real and marketable practical skills in the informal economy of conflict and post-conflict settings and who are asking whether those skills constitute a viable foundation. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal or bureaucratic mechanism involved. The mechanism is developmental and temporal: the period in which future orientation is normally formed, through stable schooling, observation of adult economic life, cultural narratives about what adulthood looks like, and the accumulation of skills that formal systems recognize and reward, did not occur in the way that produces the future-imagining capacity he is describing as absent. He fixes things for people and they give him things in return, which is a real and functioning economic relationship in the informal economy of his context. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: When he tries to picture himself older he sees a grey. This is not a psychological symptom but an accurate reflection of having no reference material. The people around him who talk about what they want to do later are also working from limited reference material but from more of it than he has. The question of whether what he has, the ability to fix things and the relationships that ability has built, is enough to build something from, is both a practical question about informal economy viability and a deeper question about whether a life can be constructed from below, starting from survival competency rather than from the formal credential system he was not able to access.
