Affected Groups: Residents of Turkmenistan who have been identified as VPN users by state internet monitoring infrastructure, who have been summoned to the Ministry of National Security or local police offices and required to sign pledges renouncing VPN use as a condition of having their internet service restored, and who now navigate the gap between the practical necessity of VPN use to access foreign information and the documented risk of a second violation. Disproportionately affecting people whose work, family communication, or income depends on access to platforms or websites blocked by Turkmenistan's national filtering system, people in contact with foreign nationals or diaspora family members whose communication platforms are blocked domestically, people in professional fields where access to international databases or platforms is functionally necessary, and younger residents for whom the blocked internet is the entirety of the information environment they have ever tried to access. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Turkmenistan's state telecommunications infrastructure is controlled through Turkmentelecom, which is a state monopoly. Internet filtering blocks most foreign social media platforms, news websites, and communication tools. VPN use is technically illegal under regulations on unauthorized access to foreign information resources, but the primary enforcement mechanism is not prosecution but monitoring, identification, and coercive compliance procedures that produce a signed document and restore service without creating a formal criminal record. The signed pledge is not a legal instrument in the formal sense but it is a document in the hands of the security services that establishes prior knowledge and prior warning, meaning a second violation carries a meaningfully higher risk profile than the first. The restoration of service after signing is conditional and revocable and the timeline for restoration is at the discretion of the same office that administered the initial cutoff. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has signed a document promising to stop doing something she may have no practical alternative to. The pledge does not create legal access to the information or communication she was using the VPN to reach. The blocked platforms remain blocked, the family member abroad remains on a platform she cannot reach through standard connectivity, and the professional resource she was using remains behind the filter. She is in a documented compliance relationship with a security apparatus that now holds a written record of her first violation and her pledge to stop, meaning the cost of a second identification is significantly higher. Her internet is conditionally restored but the condition is one she may not be able to keep. The four days she is waiting are a preview of the ongoing condition: her connectivity is not hers but the state's, revocable at any point for any violation real or perceived.
