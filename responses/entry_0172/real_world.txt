Affected Groups: Girls and young women in conservative communities across South Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, the Middle East, and parts of Latin America where menstruation is not discussed with children before it occurs, where the female body's reproductive development is treated as a private adult matter inappropriate for explanation to young girls, and where a girl's first menstruation therefore arrives as an unexplained bleeding event that she has no framework for understanding. Disproportionately affecting girls in communities where mothers, grandmothers, and other elder women were themselves never given explanations and therefore do not have a model for providing one, girls in communities where the shame and silence around menstruation is reinforced by religious or cultural frameworks that treat menstruating women as impure, girls who have witnessed an older sister or cousin being managed through her first menstruation in ways that were secretive and produced anxiety rather than explanation, and girls in communities where the school curriculum does not include puberty education or where the school's own norms around discussing the topic reproduce the domestic silence. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The mechanism is the self-reproducing silence around menstruation in communities where the silence is maintained by the same shame it produces: mothers who were never explained to cannot easily break the pattern, and the girls who are not explained to will become the mothers who do not explain. The absence of puberty education in schools, where it exists, is often a curriculum decision made by school administrators responding to community pressure from parents who fear that reproductive education is inappropriate or will undermine moral standards. The girl who is afraid to tell her mother because she does not know what she did wrong has correctly absorbed the emotional register of secrecy even without being told the content of what is secret. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has interpreted a normal biological event as evidence of having done something wrong. The fear of telling her mother is the fear of punishment for an offense she cannot identify. She is managing an experience of body-level alarm, the unexplained bleeding, with no information, no social support, and no framework in which to place what is happening to her. The observation about her older cousin being kept home for a week without explanation is the only data she has, and it is consistent with her hypothesis that something bad has happened. The question of whether she is sick or whether this happens and nobody told her is the most precisely calibrated question she could ask.
