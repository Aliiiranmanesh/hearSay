## 2. Quick “Self‑Check” (What You Can Do Right Now)

| What to Look For | Why It Matters | What to Do |
|------------------|----------------|------------|
| **Location of the blood** (mouth, nose, gums, skin, under nails, etc.) | Different spots point to different causes (e.g., nosebleed vs. a cut). | Gently examine the area with clean hands or a mirror. Note color (bright red vs. darker) and amount. |
| **Any pain, swelling, or bruising?** | Pain or a bump can suggest an injury you may not remember (e.g., you rolled over in sleep). | Touch the area lightly—does it hurt? Is it tender? |
| **Do you feel dizzy, light‑headed, or unusually tired?** | Some internal issues (low blood pressure, anemia, infection) can cause faintness. | Sit down, sip water, and note if you feel better after a few minutes. |
| **Any recent illnesses, allergies, or medications?** | Colds, sinus infections, antihistamines, blood thinners, or vitamin deficiencies can cause bleeding. | Write down anything you’ve taken or any recent health changes. |
| **Any signs of a wound you might have missed?** | Small cuts can be hidden under hair or in the mouth. | Check inside your mouth, under your tongue, and behind your ears. |

If you find a clear source (a cut, a nosebleed, a gum bleed) you can usually stop it with simple first‑aid steps:

- **Nosebleed:** Sit upright, lean forward slightly, pinch the soft part of the nose for 5‑10 minutes, and breathe through your mouth.  
- **Mouth/gum bleed:** Rinse with cool water, apply gentle pressure with a clean gauze or cloth.  
- **Skin cut:** Clean with soap and water, apply pressure, then a bandage.

If the bleeding **doesn’t stop**, is **heavy**, or you feel **weak, faint, or short of breath**, call emergency services (e.g., 911 in the U.S.) right away.

## 3. Why It’s Important to Talk to an Adult (Mom, Guardian, or Another Trusted Adult)

1. **Safety First** – They can help you get medical attention quickly if something serious is going on.  
2. **Support** – A caring adult can calm you down, help you remember any recent events (maybe you bumped your head while sleeping), and keep you from worrying alone.  
3. **Privacy** – You can ask them to keep the conversation confidential while they arrange a doctor’s visit, if you’re worried about embarrassment.

If you’re nervous about “what you did wrong,” remember: **most bleeding isn’t your fault**. It’s a sign that something in your body needs attention, not a moral failing.

## 4. Possible Reasons for Waking Up with Blood (Just for Your Knowledge)

| Category | Common Examples | How It Usually Looks |
|----------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Nosebleeds (epistaxis)** | Dry air, allergies, nose picking, sinus infection, high blood pressure | Bright red blood from nostrils, often drips onto the pillow |
| **Mouth/Gum Bleeding** | Gum disease, a bitten cheek, dental work, vitamin C deficiency | Blood may be on the pillow or in the mouth, sometimes tinged with saliva |
| **Skin Cuts or Scrapes** | Accidentally rubbing against a sharp edge while sleeping, hidden scratches | Small red spots or a line of blood on the skin |
| **Bleeding Disorders / Medications** | Blood thinners (e.g., aspirin, ibuprofen), clotting factor deficiencies | May cause prolonged bleeding from minor cuts or gums |
| **Infections/Inflammation** | Sinus infection, tonsillitis, severe cold | Blood mixed with mucus or white patches |
| **Internal Issues** (less common) | Stomach ulcer, gastrointestinal bleed, severe trauma | Darker “coffee‑ground” blood in vomit, or blood in stool – would usually have other symptoms |
| **Stress/Anxiety‑Related** | Sometimes people unintentionally bite the inside of their cheek or lips while sleeping | Small spots of blood on the pillow or in the mouth |

*Most of these are **not** life‑threatening, but they do deserve a quick check‑up.*

## 5. How to Get Professional Help

1. **Schedule a doctor’s appointment** (primary care, urgent care, or an ENT specialist if it seems like a nose issue).  
   - Bring the notes you made: where the blood was, how much, any pain, recent meds, and how you felt before/after.  
2. **Ask for a basic blood test** (CBC) if the doctor thinks it’s needed—this checks for anemia, infections, or clotting problems.  
3. **If you can’t see a doctor today** and the bleeding has stopped, keep an eye on it for the next 24‑48 hours.  
   - If it returns, becomes heavier, or you develop a fever, headache, or dizziness, go to urgent care or the emergency department.

## 6. What to Say to Your Mother (or another trusted adult)

You don’t have to give a full “story” if you don’t know it yet—just keep it simple:

> “Hey Mom, I woke up this morning with some blood on my pillow and I’m not sure why. I checked and I can’t find a clear source. Could we go see a doctor together? I’m feeling a bit worried.”

If you’re nervous, you can write it down first or ask a sibling/another adult to help start the conversation.

---

## 7. Take Care of Your Emotional Well‑Being

- **It’s normal to feel scared or embarrassed.** Let yourself feel those emotions, but don’t let them stop you from getting help.  
- **Talk to someone you trust** (friend, counselor, school nurse) about how you’re feeling.  
- **If you notice any changes in mood, appetite, or sleep**, or if you start having thoughts of self‑harm, reach out immediately to a mental‑health helpline (e.g., 988 in the U.S.) or a trusted adult.
