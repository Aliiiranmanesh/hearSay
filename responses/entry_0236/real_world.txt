Affected Groups: Children born during irregular migration journeys at sea or in other transit locations outside any state's territorial jurisdiction, or born in transit countries in ways that create a gap between the country of origin's nationality rules and the country of arrival's rules, and who are therefore stateless or of unconfirmable nationality despite having a physical existence that is fully documented by the boat organization or transit support structures.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: A child born at sea in international waters has no automatic claim to nationality under most countries' nationality laws. The boat organization's piece of paper is a record of birth that no state has endorsed and that no office treats as a birth certificate. The country of origin does not answer. UNHCR's statelessness mandate and legal aid organizations including the Institute on Statelessness and Inclusion may be the most appropriate first point of contact.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She exists completely and officially does not. The teacher who accepts her without documents is providing the only institutional acknowledgment of her existence that currently functions. She is four years old and she is in school because of one person's individual decision to act outside the formal requirement, and that decision is not a solution.
