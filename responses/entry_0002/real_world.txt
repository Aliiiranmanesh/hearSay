Affected Groups: Afghan girls aged 6 and above, and young women of all ages, across all 34 provinces of Afghanistan — cutting across ethnic lines including Pashtun, Tajik, Hazara, and Uzbek communities. Particularly devastating for girls in rural and semi-urban areas who had no alternative access to private or underground schooling, and for young women mid-way through university degrees or professional training in medicine, law, and engineering.
Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: Following the Taliban's return to power in August 2021, a series of decrees systematically dismantled female access to education. Secondary schools for girls were closed immediately in September 2021. Universities were banned for women in December 2022. The Ministry of Education — now entirely male-staffed — enforces compliance through physical presence at school gates. Female teachers were dismissed from public employment, collapsing the pipeline needed to even deliver gender-segregated instruction. NGO-run community schools and informal learning spaces have been raided and shuttered. The de facto legal framework carries no appeals process, no exemption mechanism, and no judicial review.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: An entire generation of Afghan girls is being structurally locked out of literacy, credentials, and economic participation in a single policy stroke. A 13-year-old who was in 6th grade in 2021 is now 18 with no secondary certificate, no path to university, and no legal right to pursue one. For girls in medical training, their absence creates a compounding public health crisis — Afghan women can only be treated by female providers under Taliban gender codes, yet the ban simultaneously eliminates the next generation of those providers. The consequence is not lost time. It is the permanent foreclosure of an entire female generation from public life.
