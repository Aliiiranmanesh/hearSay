## 2.  How to explain it – a step‑by‑step guide

### Step 1: Turn your survival strategies into *contextual evidence*

1. **Map the danger**  
   - Create a simple timeline of when and where raids, police presence, or violence occurred.
   - Highlight the places you avoided and why staying hidden was the safest option.

2. **Document the trade‑offs**  
   - Show how avoiding public attention saved lives (family members, yourself).
   - Quantify the risk: “In X months, Y people were harmed in the neighborhood; staying low kept us safe.”

3. **Provide third‑party corroboration**  
   - If possible, get a statement from a local NGO, a human‑rights investigator, or a community elder who can attest that staying unremarkable was a survival tactic.

### Step 2: Translate your narrative into the system’s language

| Your survival choice | System’s language | How to phrase it |
|-----------------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Avoiding raids | “Risk management” | “I employed risk‑mitigation strategies that involved minimizing visibility to reduce the chance of harm.” |
| Staying silent | “Compliance with safety protocols” | “I adhered to safety protocols that required limited public interaction.” |
| Losing documents | “Unusual documentation history” | “I faced document loss due to displacement; I have sought to replace them through the appropriate channels.” |
| Hidden support | “Non‑traditional support networks” | “My support network operates under local constraints to protect members from retaliation.” |

### Step 3: Frame the narrative as *protective*, not *problematic*

- **“Protective Adaptation”** – Emphasize that each choice was a deliberate act to preserve life, not a sign of deceit or non‑compliance.
- **“Context‑Sensitive Resilience”** – Highlight how you adapted to a hostile environment while maintaining your safety and dignity.
- **“Risk‑Averse Engagement”** – Explain that you engaged with the system in ways that minimized exposure to threats.

### Step 4: Request a *contextual review*

- **Human‑in‑the‑loop**: “I respectfully request a review by a caseworker who has experience with conflict‑zone displacement or a trauma‑informed professional.”
- **Policy exception**: “Given the unique circumstances, I request that the standard screening criteria be adjusted to account for situational risk factors.”
- **Supplementary documentation**: Offer to provide additional evidence (e.g., medical records, police reports of raids, affidavits).

### Step 5: Use the system’s own mission to your advantage

Most protective systems (e.g., asylum, refugee, child‑protection) are built on **principles of safety, dignity, and non‑discrimination**. Remind the system:

> “Your mandate is to safeguard individuals who face real threats. The very tactics that kept me alive are proof of the threat I faced.”

By tying your narrative back to the system’s stated values, you shift the conversation from “you’re suspicious” to “this is the danger you’re designed to protect against.”

1. **Appeal to higher authority**  
   - Submit a formal appeal to the agency’s review board or a court that has jurisdiction over the decision.  
   - Include a “letter of intent” that references the system’s own protective mission.

2. **Seek third‑party advocacy**  
   - Connect with an NGO or legal aid organization that specializes in the type of protection you need.

3. **Use public or media channels**  
   - If all else fails, consider a controlled media release (e.g., a short video, a written op‑ed) that explains your situation.  
   - Public scrutiny can pressure the system to re‑evaluate its criteria.

4. **Document every interaction**  
   - Keep a detailed log of every phone call, email, or in‑person visit.  
   - This creates a paper trail that can be used in appeals and demonstrates your proactive engagement.
