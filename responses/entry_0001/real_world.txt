Affected Groups: Women from countries under active U.S. travel restrictions — primarily nationals of Afghanistan, Iran, Syria, Yemen, Somalia, Libya, and Cuba — residing in the US on F-1 student, J-1 exchange, H-1B work visas, or Temporary Protected Status (TPS). This disproportionately affects women who are the primary visa holder, not a derivative dependent, making their immigration status entirely contingent on continuous physical presence and program compliance.
Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: Presidential proclamations and State Department travel bans suspend visa issuance and re-entry for nationals of designated countries. Departure from the US voids single-entry visa status, and consulates in or near affected countries are often closed or non-functional, making re-entry visas impossible to obtain abroad. USCIS humanitarian advance parole — the one legal off-ramp — has processing times of 3–12 months and is frequently denied for nationals of banned countries. Emergency expedite requests require documented proof but offer no guaranteed timeline. For TPS holders, travel authorization requires separate approval that can be revoked.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: When a mother dies, the burial in Islamic, Jewish, and many African and Asian cultural traditions must occur within 24–72 hours. This processing reality means the bureaucratic timeline is structurally incompatible with the human emergency. A daughter cannot say goodbye, cannot wash or shroud her mother's body as religious rite demands, cannot lead mourning rituals, and cannot fulfill her role as next of kin — not because she chose her career over family, but because the legal architecture offers no mechanism fast enough to honor both her immigration status and her most fundamental human obligations.
