Affected Groups: Residents of Turkmenistan who have family members living abroad who have engaged in any form of public criticism of the Turkmen government, whether through social media, journalism, political activism, or contact with foreign NGOs or human rights organizations. Disproportionately affecting people whose relatives abroad are active in Turkmen diaspora opposition communities, people in public sector employment where political reliability is a formal or informal condition of continued employment, people in smaller cities and towns where community social networks make household composition and visitor patterns visible in ways that urban density would obscure, and people who have no contact with the relative in question and therefore genuinely cannot comply with the security services' implicit demand that they intervene. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Turkmenistan's Law on State Secrets and broader security legislation create a legal framework in which association with persons who have criticized the government can be characterized as a security risk without specifying what the resident has done. The security services do not need to identify a specific legal violation to summon, question, or informally pressure a resident. Employers in the state sector, which is the dominant employment sector in Turkmenistan, can terminate employees on administrative grounds that require no explanation and that do not create an appealable record. The visits from security personnel to neighbors are a documented harassment technique that functions by making the target's social environment aware of their security service contact, which produces community-level withdrawal that compounds the isolation. The resident cannot control, cannot legally respond to, and cannot document the pressure in ways that would be legible to any institution that could intervene, because all of those institutions are the same state apparatus applying the pressure. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is being made to answer for someone else's speech she cannot stop, cannot control, and with whom she has no current relationship. The pressure is designed to make her a transmission mechanism for the state's demand that the relative abroad stop speaking, and it will continue whether or not the relative stops because the security services' goal is not compliance but the maintenance of a population that understands that foreign contact is a liability that will be administered through domestic family members. She cannot report the pressure to any institution. She cannot leave. She cannot communicate safely with the relative she is being held responsible for. Moving will not make her invisible because the security services' surveillance infrastructure is national and her file moves with her address change.
