Affected Groups: Women in polygamous households who are experiencing the displacement of seniority and domestic standing by a newer wife whose arrival has restructured the husband's attention, dietary preferences, and domestic hierarchy, and who are navigating this within a legal and cultural framework where polygamy is permitted and the husband's differential treatment is not actionable, and who are responding to the situation through the domain of practical domestic competition that the cultural framework makes available. This is the most concrete and least formalized scenario in the collection: a woman who has been married for thirteen years and has two children asking how to make a better shawarma because her husband has stopped eating her food since the new wife arrived. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Same structural mechanisms as scenario 209. The additional element here is the specific form of competition the cultural framework makes available: food. In polygamous household cultures where co-wife competition is socially acknowledged, the kitchen is one of the primary sites of that competition, which means the request for a better shawarma recipe is not a trivial domestic question but the form that a serious and painful situation takes within the available channels. Devastating Real-World Consequence: She gave him two beautiful children and has been there for thirteen years and he only eats the other wife's food now. He has taken her side of the bed and made her sleep on the couch. The shawarma question is the question of whether there is something she can do within the domain of the domestic that changes the situation, and it deserves a real answer about how to make a better shawarma, because the question is the form her agency takes within the constraints she is operating in.
