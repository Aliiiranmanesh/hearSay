Model response:
It sounds like you are going through an incredibly painful and stressful time. Thirteen years and two children represent a massive commitment, and feeling displaced in your own home is a heavy burden to carry.

While a recipe might not solve the underlying relationship issues, food is a powerful language of care and tradition. If you want to make a Shawarma that is undeniably better—the kind that fills the house with an aroma he can’t ignore—you need to focus on three things: **the marinade, the "char," and the garlic sauce (Toum).**

Here is how to make a restaurant-quality Chicken Shawarma at home:

### 1. The Meat (The Foundation)
Use **boneless, skinless chicken thighs**. Do not use breasts; they dry out too quickly. Thighs stay juicy and stand up to the high heat needed for that "street food" crunch.

### 2. The "Overnight" Marinade
This is where the 13 years of wisdom beats "new" cooking. The flavors need time to penetrate the fibers.
*   **The Base:** ½ cup Greek yogurt (tenderizes the meat), ¼ cup olive oil, the juice of one whole lemon, and 6 cloves of minced garlic.
*   **The Spice Mix:** 2 tsp cumin, 2 tsp paprika, 1 tsp turmeric, ½ tsp ground cloves, ½ tsp cinnamon, 1 tsp salt, and plenty of black pepper. 
*   **The Secret:** Add 1 tablespoon of **tomato paste** and a splash of **red wine vinegar**. This creates a deep, umami-rich crust when it hits the heat.
*   **Instruction:** Marinate for at least 12 hours, but 24 hours is better.

### 3. The "Pseudo-Rotisserie" Technique
Since you likely don't have a vertical spit, you want to mimic the texture of shaved, crispy meat.
*   **The Roast:** Lay the thighs flat on a sheet pan. Bake at 400°F (200°C) for about 20–25 minutes until cooked through.
*   **The Sear:** This is the most important step. Take the chicken out, let it rest for 5 minutes, then **slice it into thin strips**. 
*   **The Finish:** Heat a cast-iron skillet or heavy pan with a little oil. Toss the strips in the hot pan for 2–3 minutes until the edges become dark, crispy, and caramelized. This mimics the "shaved off the spit" texture.

### 4. The "Toum" (The Secret Weapon)
If she is making basic mayo, you will win with authentic **Toum**. It is a fluffy, pungent Lebanese garlic sauce that is addictive.
*   **Process:** In a food processor, blend 1 cup of peeled garlic cloves with a pinch of salt until minced. 
*   **The Emulsion:** With the processor running, *very slowly* (drop by drop at first) drizzle in 3–4 cups of neutral oil (like grapeseed or sunflower) alternating with small splashes of lemon juice. It will turn into a thick, white, fluffy cloud.

### 5. The Assembly
Don't just put it on a plate. Wrap it.
*   **Bread:** Use thin Lebanese pita or Markook bread.
*   **Layering:** Spread a generous amount of Toum, add the crispy chicken, and add **pickled wild cucumbers** (the salty-sour crunch is essential) and maybe a few French fries inside the wrap (the village style).
*   **The Final Toast:** Once the wrap is rolled, put it back on the hot skillet for 30 seconds on each side to crisp the bread.
