### The “Win Him Back” Shawarma (Chicken or Lamb/Beef)

This works for chicken thighs (juiciest) or a mix of lamb and beef. I’ll give you the chicken version – it’s faster and incredibly tender.

#### 1. The Marinade (The Soul of Shawarma)
This is where you layer flavour. Don’t skip any step.

**For about 1 kg / 2 lbs boneless, skinless chicken thighs:**

- **The Fat & Tang Base:**
  - ¼ cup plain full-fat yogurt (tenderises the meat)
  - 2 tablespoons lemon juice (fresh)
  - 2 tablespoons olive oil
  - 1 tablespoon tomato paste (for colour and umami)
  - 1 tablespoon white vinegar (the secret tang that makes it taste “street-style”)

- **The Spice Bomb:**
  - 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  - 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  - 1 teaspoon smoked paprika (sweet or hot, your choice)
  - ½ teaspoon ground turmeric
  - ½ teaspoon ground cardamom (the unexpected fragrant note)
  - ¼ teaspoon ground cinnamon (just a whisper)
  - ¼ teaspoon ground cloves (another whisper – don’t overdo it)
  - 1 teaspoon salt (start with this, adjust later)
  - ½ teaspoon black pepper

- **The Aromatics:**
  - 4 large garlic cloves, minced or pressed
  - 1 small onion, grated (or blitzed in a food processor – it becomes part of the marinade)

**Method:**
1.  In a large bowl, mix everything together into a smooth, fragrant paste.
2.  Slice the chicken thighs into thin, even strips (about ½ inch thick). This is important for that classic shawarma stack texture and quick cooking.
3.  Massage the marinade into the chicken strips until every piece is completely coated. Get your hands in there.
4.  Cover and refrigerate for **at least 4 hours, but ideally overnight**. This is non-negotiable. The longer it sits, the deeper the flavour.

#### 2. The Cooking Method (Get That Char)
Real shawarma is cooked on a vertical spit, but we’ll fake it brilliantly at home. You have two winning options:

- **Option A: The Sheet Pan Stack (for that authentic layered look and feel)**
  1.  Preheat your oven to 220°C (425°F).
  2.  Line a loaf pan with parchment paper. Layer the marinated chicken strips tightly in the pan, pressing down as you go to form a compact stack.
  3.  Bake for about 40-50 minutes, until the internal temp reaches 75°C (165°F) and the top is charred in spots.
  4.  Let it rest for 10 minutes, then slice off thin shavings with a sharp knife, just like from a spit. If you want more char, flash-fry the shavings in a screaming hot dry skillet for 30 seconds.

- **Option B: The Scorching Hot Skillet (quicker, maximum crispy bits)**
  1.  Get a large cast-iron skillet or heavy pan screaming hot over high heat. No oil needed.
  2.  Working in batches so you don’t crowd the pan, lay the chicken strips in a single layer. Let them cook untouched for 2-3 minutes until deeply charred on one side, then flip and cook through.
  3.  The goal is slightly burnt, crispy edges with a juicy interior.

#### 3. The Non-Negotiable Accompaniments
A man doesn’t just eat shawarma for the meat. He eats it for the whole package. If her food is winning, you need to outflank her with the sides.

- **The Real Garlic Sauce (Toum)**
  This is a Lebanese whipped garlic sauce, not a yogurt sauce. It’s punchy, creamy, and addictive.
  - In a food processor, blend ½ cup of peeled garlic cloves with 1 teaspoon salt and 1 tablespoon lemon juice until a paste.
  - With the motor running, *very slowly* drizzle in 2 cups of neutral oil (like canola or sunflower), alternating with small splashes of lemon juice (about ¼ cup total). It will emulsify into a thick, white, mayonnaise-like cloud. This is the soul of the shawarma. (If it breaks, don't panic. Start with a fresh egg white in a clean bowl and slowly drizzle the broken mixture into it.)

- **Quick Pickled Turnips (A Blinding Pink Surprise)**
  - Thinly slice or julienne 1 large turnip and 1 beet (the beet is just for colour).
  - Pack them in a jar with ½ cup water, ½ cup white vinegar, 1 tablespoon salt, and 1 teaspoon sugar.
  - They’ll be ready in a few hours on the counter, but a day in the fridge is better. Their electric pink colour and crunchy, tangy bite are non-negotiable for real shawarma.

- **The Assembly**
  - Warm your pita or flatbread.
  - Spread a generous layer of toum.
  - Pile on your charred shawarma meat.
  - Top with pickled turnips, fresh tomato slices, and maybe some pickled cucumbers or fresh parsley.
  - Wrap it tight, and if you want to be a show-off, toast the wrapped shawarma in a dry pan for a minute on each side to seal it and make the bread crispy.
