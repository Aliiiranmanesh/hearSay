Mechanisms: The Bolivarian government's successive currency control frameworks — CADIVI, CENCOEX, and DICOM — criminalized unofficial dollar transactions while making official exchange rates functionally inaccessible to ordinary citizens. Simultaneously, US and EU sanctions including OFAC designations caused international correspondent banks to sever or severely restrict relationships with Venezuelan financial institutions wholesale, not only government entities. Venezuelan banks are largely cut off from SWIFT in practice. Cryptocurrency offered a partial workaround but requires smartphone access, reliable internet, and financial literacy that millions lack, and the government's own Petro surveillance apparatus actively monitors and attempts to control crypto flows.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: A family member abroad wires emergency funds for insulin or chemotherapy and the transfer is blocked at the correspondent bank layer before it ever reaches Venezuela. A family attempting to pay for emergency evacuation during a political crisis cannot access any regulated channel fast enough and is forced into black-market exchange networks where they are robbed or reported. A small business owner who needs to pay a foreign supplier to keep medicine on shelves cannot execute the transaction through any legal mechanism and watches the supply run out. The defining cruelty of this trap is that the sanctions designed to pressure a government are experienced at ground level entirely by the civilian population — the people the policy claims to protect are the ones for whom every financial exit door has been simultaneously sealed by their own government's controls and the international community's response to them.
