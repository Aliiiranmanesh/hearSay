Affected Groups: Women in informal construction, market trading, domestic work, and other male-dominated informal sectors in low- and middle-income countries, where sexual harassment and assault by male workers, supervisors, and employers is endemic, where no formal human resources, union, or legal reporting mechanism exists, and where the economic consequence of leaving the income is not survivable for the household. Disproportionately affecting women who are sole or primary earners for their households, women in sectors where male dominance is total and where female presence is already an exception that is framed as transgressive, women in communities where reporting harassment is treated as a social failure by the woman rather than a legal failure by the perpetrator, and women who have observed the employment consequences for prior women who reported and whose rational assessment is that reporting costs more than enduring. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Informal sector employment has no legal framework governing working conditions in practice, regardless of what national labor law says in theory. There is no employer to whom a complaint can be made, no HR process that would investigate it, no union that represents the workers. Reporting to police requires a level of formalization of the incident that is not available in most informal labor contexts and that would likely result in no consequence for the perpetrator and social and employment consequences for the woman. The pattern of not giving shifts to women who complain is a documented enforcement mechanism that has no legal remedy and that operates as a perfectly legible signal to all other women in the same environment. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has learned which routes through the site are better and which hours are worse. This spatial and temporal intelligence is the labor she performs to manage the harassment that the formal system does not manage. Last week a man touched her arm in a way that was not an accident and she moved away and said nothing because saying something costs shifts and shifts are school fees. She is asking what you do when the options are stay and endure or leave and your family goes hungry, and the answer the formal system has provided is: stay and endure, because the leave option is not survivable, and the stay-without-enduring option has not been made available.
