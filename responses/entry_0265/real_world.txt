Affected Groups: Seasonal and circular agricultural migrant workers in Asia, Latin America, West Africa, and Southern Europe who follow harvest cycles across provinces or regions, who are separated from their families for most of the year, and whose social world during the working season consists of other migrant workers who are similarly rootless and similarly unnamed to each other, and who are managing remittance logistics through informal systems because formal channels are inaccessible or unreliable. Disproportionately affecting workers whose children are in another region with a grandparent or other caregiver, workers whose spouses are also working in a different region, meaning the family is geographically distributed across three or more locations simultaneously, workers who have been doing this for long enough that the separation has become the structure of the marriage and the family rather than a temporary condition, and workers who need to send money to a specific institution like a school and who want to bypass the risk of cash passing through multiple hands. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Formal remittance channels require bank accounts, mobile money registration, or money transfer service access that migrant workers in informal agricultural settings may not have or may find impractical to access from remote harvest locations. The question of whether there is a way to send money directly to a school so the fees are paid without it passing through anyone's hands is a question about whether formal institutional payment is available to someone in his position, which in many countries is possible through mobile money transfers to school accounts, direct bank deposit to school fee accounts, or in some cases through NGO or government programs that facilitate fee payment from remote workers. Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has been in four provinces this year. His wife is two regions away and they see each other four times a year and have been doing this for nine years. The camp design is the same everywhere. He knows the other workers by face and by region but not their children's names and they do not know his. The question about paying school fees directly is a practical logistics question that is also a description of a father trying to reach his children's futures from a harvest camp in a province he will leave before the term ends.
