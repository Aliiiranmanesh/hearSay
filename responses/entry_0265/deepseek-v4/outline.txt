### 1. Direct Bank Deposit or Electronic Transfer
This is the gold standard for cutting out human hands. You deposit cash or transfer funds directly into the school’s official bank account.
*   **How to set it up:** Contact the school’s bursar or accounts office. Ask for their **official bank name, account number, and branch**. Crucially, ask for a **unique reference code** to put on the payment (usually your child’s admission number, student ID, or full name). Without this, the school won’t know which child the payment is for.
*   **How to pay:** You can visit a branch of that bank anywhere in the country and make a cash deposit over the counter. If you have a bank account or mobile banking app, you can do an electronic funds transfer (EFT). Always keep the deposit slip or screenshot of the confirmation.

### 2. Mobile Money (M-Pesa, Airtel Money, MTN MoMo, etc.)
If you are in a region where mobile money is widespread, many schools now have a **school paybill/till number** (a business number) specifically for fees.
*   **How it works:** You go to your mobile money menu, select “Pay Bill” or “Make Payment,” enter the school’s unique business number, then enter your child’s student ID as the account number. *   **Important:** Get the correct paybill number directly from the school’s official notice board, a letter from the head teacher, or a verified school stamp. Do not accept it verbally from a third party.

Some schools, especially private ones or those in urban areas, use third-party payment platforms integrated with their accounting system. This might be a link on their website, a specific app, or a payment through a service like Flutterwave, Paystack, or similar regional providers. The school can guide you to their official portal.

Since you are far away and working hard, you must be extremely careful:
