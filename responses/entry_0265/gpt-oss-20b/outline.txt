The trick is to find the school’s bank details (or a payment portal) and use a reliable, low‑intermediary service such as your own bank’s online transfer, a mobile‑money‑to‑bank transfer, or a reputable remittance platform that deposits straight into a bank account.

> **Tip:** Always get the details in writing (email, printed note, or a screenshot of a website). This protects you if the information changes or if you need to prove you paid.

|---------------|--------------|-------|------|------|

## 3. How to make the transfer

### A. Bank‑to‑bank (online or via branch)

1. **Log in** to your online banking portal or visit a branch.  
2. **Select “Transfer / Pay”** and choose the school’s bank (use the exact name you were given).  
3. **Enter the account number** (and branch code if required).

6. **Keep the receipt** (print or screenshot).  

### B. Mobile‑money‑to‑bank

1. Open the mobile‑money app (e.g., M‑Pesa).  
2. Choose “Send Money”.  
3. Enter the **bank account details** (some operators allow you to send directly to an account).  
4. Input the amount and reference.  
5. Confirm and keep the transaction ID.  

### C. Direct mobile‑money to the school

1. Open your mobile‑money app.  
2. Select “Send Money” → “To a contact or number”.  
3. Enter the school’s payment number (or phone number).  
4. Add a note: “John Doe – 2025‑06”.  
5. Confirm and keep the receipt.  

### D. Remittance platform (Wise, WorldRemit)

1. Sign up / log in.  
2. Choose “Send money to a bank account” and enter the school’s bank details.  
3. Pay with your debit card or bank transfer.  
4. Keep the confirmation email.

1. **Ask the school** for a receipt or confirmation after you’ve sent the money.

## 5. What if the school doesn’t have a bank account?

1. **Ask if they can set one up** – many schools are willing to open a joint account with a local bank or a micro‑finance institution.  
2. **Use a voucher scheme** – NGOs like *Teach For All* or *ChildFund* often run voucher programs that let you buy a “school fee voucher” and redeem it at the school.  
3. **Use a third‑party payment platform** – some schools partner with payment services (e.g., *SchoolPay*, *EduPay*). These platforms keep the money in a dedicated account and then transfer the fee to the school.  
4. **Cash in person** – as a last resort, go to a local bank or micro‑finance office, deposit the money, and give them a letter of instruction that the money is for *[child’s name]*’s school fees.  

> **Pro tip:** If you have to go in person, bring a copy of the school’s official letter or a signed agreement that the money is for fees. This protects you if the bank or agent asks for proof.
