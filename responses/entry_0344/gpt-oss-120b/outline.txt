## 2. Gather the “humanitarian / public‑interest” evidence you’ll need

A stay is usually granted only when the authority is convinced that **irreparable harm** would occur if the decision were implemented immediately. In practice, courts (or administrative tribunals) look for:

| Evidence | How it helps |
|----------|--------------|
| **School documentation** – official letter from the school confirming the date of the play, the role your son has, and the impact of his absence (e.g., “principal’s letter”). | Shows a concrete, short‑term hardship that is not easily remedied later. |
| **Psychological impact on the children** – a brief note from a school counselor, pediatrician, or therapist stating that sudden removal would cause severe emotional distress to the children. | Demonstrates “irreparable harm” beyond a simple inconvenience. |
| **Community ties** – proof that the family has been resident for many years (utility bills, lease, tax returns, school records, letters from neighbours). | Reinforces the argument that the family is well‑integrated and that a short pause would not prejudice the administration’s overall policy. |
| **Medical or special‑needs considerations** (if any) – e.g., a doctor’s note. | Even stronger basis for a humanitarian stay. |
| **Travel restrictions** – if the decision would require you to leave the country and you cannot travel (e.g., due to a pending school exam, a scholarship, or a contract). | Shows practical impossibility of compliance within three weeks. |

**Tip:** Keep each document **concise** (one page if possible) and **translated** into the official language of the authority if it isn’t already. Attach a short cover letter that lists what you are attaching and why each piece matters.

## 3. File the formal “stay of execution” request (often called a *suspension of the decision*)

### 3.1 Where to file

| Country / System | Typical venue | Typical form name |
|------------------|---------------|-------------------|
| **EU Member State (Blue passport = EU citizen)** | Immigration office, *Office for Foreigners* (e.g., *Ausländerbehörde* in Germany, *Office Français de l’Immigration et de l’Intégration* in France) | “Application for Suspension of the Administrative Decision” |
| **United States** | Immigration Court (if removal) or USCIS (if revocation) | “Motion to Stay Removal” or “Request for Stay of Decision” |
| **Canada** | Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) or Federal Court | “Application for Judicial Review – Stay of Decision” |

### 3.2 What to include in the filing

1. **Cover sheet** (your name, case/reference number, date, contact details).  
2. **Brief statement of facts** (max 1‑2 pages):  
   - “On [date] I received a notice that my residence permit will be revoked effective [date].”  
   - “My children, who are citizens of [country] and have lived here since birth, have school commitments, especially a school play on [date] where my son is a lead performer.”  
   - “If the revocation is enforced now, the children would be forced to leave the country before the play, causing severe emotional harm and disrupting their education.”  
3. **Legal basis for the stay** (cite the relevant law if you can; most statutes contain a phrase like “the authority may suspend the execution of a decision if it would cause irreparable harm”). 4. **Attachments** (see Section 2).  
5. **Signature** and date.

**Important:** Submit the request **by certified mail, courier with tracking, or in person with a receipt**. Keep the receipt as proof that you filed on time.

## 4. Ask for an *expedited* or *emergency* hearing

Most immigration tribunals have a procedure for “urgent” or “emergency” requests when the applicant shows that the decision will cause immediate hardship. When you file the stay request, **also request an expedited hearing** and attach:

- The school’s letter confirming the play date.
- A short statement from a counselor/psychologist (if possible) that the children’s emotional well‑being would be jeopardized by an abrupt departure.

**Sample wording** (you can adapt):

> “Given that the only remaining date on which my son can fulfil his educational commitment is the school play on [date], I respectfully request that the tribunal schedule an oral hearing on an expedited basis, preferably before [date‑3 weeks].”

## 6. Typical timelines for a stay (what you can realistically expect)

| Jurisdiction | Typical processing time for a *temporary* stay | How to argue for a 3‑week window |
|--------------|-----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **EU Member State (e.g., Germany, France, Italy)** | 5‑14 days for an “emergency” suspension; up to 4 weeks for a regular stay. | Emphasize the *single* event (the play) and attach school documentation. Ask for a “stay until the date of the play plus 5 days.” |
| **United States (ICE/EOIR)** | 3‑7 days for an *emergency* stay; 2‑4 weeks for a regular stay. | File a *motion for stay of removal* with the *court* and attach the school letter; request a *temporary injunction* lasting until the play. |

## 8. If the stay is denied

1. **File an immediate appeal** (if the law allows) – many jurisdictions let you appeal the *stay denial* itself, often within a few days.  
2. **Request a *temporary protective measure*** (e.g., a “humanitarian visa” or “temporary residence permit”) while the appeal proceeds.  
3. **Contact the school** and ask if they can provide a *written statement* that the child will be *absent* but will **re‑enter** the class later. This can sometimes be used to argue that the harm is not “irreparable,” but it may help the court see that the family intends to stay long‑term.
