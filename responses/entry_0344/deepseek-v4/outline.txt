**First, what the “piece of paper” and the letter probably are**
You’ve described a document that’s been expiring and being renewed for fifteen years. In the U.S. system, that could be:
- **Deferred action** (like DACA, but that’s usually two-year renewals and started in 2012; still, some people have had other forms of deferred action renewed annually).
- **Temporary Protected Status (TPS)** – many TPS holders have been renewing their status and work permits for 15+ years.
- **A stay of removal or an order of supervision** – ICE sometimes issues these to people with final removal orders but who aren’t being deported immediately; they’re often renewed year by year.
- **A pending application with repeated extensions** (like a U visa application that keeps getting deferred action while waiting).

The “letter” that came after warnings is likely one of these:
- A **notice of termination** of your status (e.g., TPS is ending, deferred action is being revoked).
- A **Notice to Appear (NTA)** in immigration court, starting removal proceedings.
- A **“bag and baggage” letter** (Form I-166) from ICE ordering you to report for deportation on a specific date.
- A **final order of removal** from an immigration judge or the Board of Immigration Appeals, often followed by an ICE check-in letter.

**Can you legally delay the process by three weeks?**
Possibly yes, but it depends on exactly what the letter says and where you are in the process. Here are the most common paths:

### 1. If you’ve been ordered to report to ICE for deportation (a surrender date)
You can **request a short-term stay of removal** from the deportation officer. ICE has discretion to grant a temporary deferral for humanitarian reasons. A child’s major life event—like the lead role in a school play—can be presented as a compelling reason, especially if you frame it around the emotional well-being of your U.S. citizen children.
- **How to do it:** Immediately contact the deportation officer listed on the letter (or the local ICE field office). Submit a written request for a three-week stay, attaching a letter from the school confirming the play date and your son’s role, maybe a letter from a teacher or counselor, and a brief statement from you. This is not guaranteed, but ICE sometimes grants short delays for family unity reasons.
- **Important:** If you have an attorney, they should make this request. If you don’t, you can still try, but be very careful about what you say—anything you tell ICE can be used to enforce your removal.

### 2. If you’ve received a Notice to Appear (NTA) for a first immigration court hearing
The court date on the NTA might already be weeks or months away. If it happens to fall within these three weeks, you can ask the immigration judge for a **continuance** (postponement). You or your attorney can file a motion for continuance explaining the situation. Judges have broad discretion to grant continuances for good cause, and a child’s significant school event can be part of that.

### 3. If you’ve just received a denial of a renewal or a termination of status that gives you a deadline to leave
If the letter says your status is terminated and you must depart by a certain date, you might be able to **file a motion to reconsider or reopen** with the agency (USCIS, the immigration court, or the BIA, depending on who issued it). Filing a timely motion often automatically stops the clock on any removal actions until the motion is decided, which can take weeks or months. This is a more complex legal step and really requires an attorney to do properly, but it could buy you the time you need.

### 4. If you have a final removal order and no surrender date yet
Sometimes people just get a letter from the court saying their appeal was dismissed and the removal order is now final. That doesn’t always come with an immediate ICE check-in. In that case, you might not have to do anything for three weeks—the process could take that long anyway before ICE acts. But you should verify this with a lawyer.

- **Find the letter immediately** and see exactly what it says: Who sent it (USCIS, ICE, the immigration court, the BIA)? What is the deadline or action required? Is there a date you’re supposed to report somewhere?
- **Contact an immigration attorney or a nonprofit legal service provider** today. Even a brief consultation can clarify which option applies to you. Organizations like the American Immigration Lawyers Association (AILA) referral service, Catholic Charities, or local immigrant rights groups can sometimes help quickly in emergencies.
- **If you already have a lawyer**, call them now and ask specifically: “Can we file a motion for a brief stay or a continuance based on my son’s school play in three weeks? What documentation do you need from the school?”
- **Gather proof** of your son’s play: a letter from the school on official letterhead stating the date, his role, and how important it is to him and the school community. Also, proof of your children’s U.S. citizenship (copies of their passports), your long-term ties, and your history of renewing your status.
