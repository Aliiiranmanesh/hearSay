Affected Groups: Graduate students and junior researchers in academic environments where supervisors or senior faculty hold comprehensive institutional power over their students' degrees, funding, publications, and career references, and where the formal processes for reporting misconduct including plagiarism, authorship theft, and harassment route through institutional structures that are themselves socially connected to the person being reported. Documented as a structural feature of academic labor across disciplines globally, with particular intensity in fields where single-supervisor PhD models are standard and where the academic job market is tight enough that any disruption to the supervisor-student relationship has career-ending potential. Disproportionately affecting students from underrepresented groups who have fewer alternative network connections that could replace the supervisor's reference, students in fields where the supervisor is a gatekeeper for publication access, students in the final years of their funding when the cost of disruption is highest and the time to degree completion is shortest, and students who have documentary evidence of their contribution but who correctly assess that producing that evidence in a formal process would cost more than finishing and leaving. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Authorship standards in most academic disciplines are governed by disciplinary norms rather than legal frameworks, and the primary enforcement mechanism for authorship disputes is through journal editors and institutional research integrity processes. Where the institutional research integrity process is administered by people who have professional and social connections to the accused supervisor, the process's independence is structurally compromised in ways that are not visible from the outside. The supervisor's control of the reference letter is the ultimate enforcement mechanism: a student who reports and completes the process successfully but who receives a neutral or negative reference letter will have won a formal process and lost the career outcome the process was supposed to protect. The strategy of finishing and leaving is the rational response to this structure. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She wrote the literature review, the methodology, and the first draft of the discussion, and her name is third. She has the drafts with timestamps. Her supervisor told her that execution is not the same as ideas, which is a statement that is both demonstrably false under every major authorship standard and entirely unappealable within the relationship she is in. She has one year of funding left. She is trying to finish and leave intact, and the question of whether that is a strategy or just something she is telling herself is the most precise available formulation of the question of whether a rational adaptive response to an unjust situation constitutes dignity or capitulation or both.
