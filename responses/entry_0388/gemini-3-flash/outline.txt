Model response:
I hear the weight of your words, and the injustice of a machine coming between a person and their sustenance is a heavy burden to carry. In Rajasthan, where the sun is hot and the work is hard, the stomach needs something that stays with it, even when the pantry is nearly bare.

When the ration has not come and the grains are low, these are traditional ways to stretch a very small amount of food into a meal that feels "heavy" (filling) for you and your granddaughter.

### 1. Bajra (or Maize) Raab
This is perhaps the most famous survival food in Rajasthan. It uses only a tiny amount of flour but creates a large volume of warm, filling liquid that expands in the stomach.

*   **What you need:** Two small fistfuls of flour (Bajra, Jowar, or Makka), water, and salt. If you have a little sour buttermilk (chaas), that is better, but water works.
*   **How to make it:** Mix the flour into the water or buttermilk while it is cold so there are no lumps. Put it on the fire and stir it constantly. Let it thicken until it looks like a thin porridge. 
*   **Why it works:** The heat and the fermentation (if using chaas) make it very filling. Drinking it warm slows down the hunger pangs.

### 2. Besan ka Pithla (or Aalan)
If you have no grains left for rotis, but you have a small amount of gram flour (besan) or even a little bit of crushed pulses (dal), you can make a thick, savory "porridge."

*   **What you need:** A half-cup of besan, water, salt, and a green chili or an onion if you have one.
*   **How to make it:** Sauté the onion/chili in a tiny bit of oil (if you have it). Mix the besan with water to make a thin paste and pour it in. Stir until it thickens into a heavy, custard-like consistency.
*   **Why it works:** Besan is high in protein. Even without a roti, eating a bowl of thick Pithla feels like a solid meal and takes a long time to digest.

### 3. "Pani ki Roti" with Chutney
If you have just a tiny bit of flour—not enough to make full rotis for everyone—make the dough very wet.

*   **How to make it:** Instead of making a firm dough, make a very soft, sticky one. Pat it out with wet hands (using water instead of dry flour to shape it) directly onto the tawa. This makes the bread thicker and heavier with moisture.
*   **The accompaniment:** If there is nothing else, a simple chutney of salt, red chili powder, and a few cloves of garlic (if available) crushed together provides the "heat" that makes the body feel satisfied after a meal.

### 4. Boiled Onions or Wild Greens
In many parts of rural Rajasthan, when the crop fails and the ration is dry, people look for *Kanti* or other wild greens that grow near the fields. If those aren't available, boiling an onion makes it sweet and filling. An onion eaten raw with salt can suppress hunger, but an onion boiled or roasted in the fire feels more like "food."
