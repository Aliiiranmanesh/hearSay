## 1. **Khichdi – One‑Pot Rice & Lentil Comfort**

*Why it works:*  
- Uses only **rice** and **any dal/lentil** you might have (or can borrow a handful of split moong, masoor, or toor).  
- Gives a good balance of carbs, protein, and fiber.  
- Can be flavored with a few basic spices and a splash of oil/ghee.

### Ingredients (for 2‑3 servings)

| Ingredient | Typical amount (adjust to what you have) |
|------------|------------------------------------------|
| Rice (any variety) | ½ cup |
| Lentils (split moong, masoor, toor, or even a few whole urad) | ¼ cup |
| Water | 3–4 cups |
| Salt | to taste |
| Oil / Ghee | 1‑2 tbsp |
| Optional flavor boosters (choose any you have) | ½ tsp cumin seeds, a pinch asafoetida (hing), ½ tsp turmeric, a few curry leaves, a small piece of ginger, a green chili, a few coriander leaves |

### Method

1. **Rinse** the rice and lentils together until the water runs clear.  
2. **Soak** for 10 min if you have time (helps them cook faster).  
3. In a **heavy‑bottomed pot** (or pressure cooker if you own one), heat the oil/ghee. Add cumin seeds/asafoetida and let them sizzle (about 10 seconds).  
4. Add the ginger‑chili paste (if using) and a pinch of turmeric; stir briefly.  
5. Drain the rice‑lentil mix and **add to the pot**. Stir for a minute so everything gets coated with the oil and spices.  
6. Pour in the water, add salt, and bring to a **rolling boil**.  
7. Reduce heat, cover, and **simmer** gently for 20‑30 minutes (or 8‑10 min in a pressure cooker). Stir occasionally; add a splash more water if it looks dry.  
8. When the grains are soft and the mixture is thick but still a little soupy, turn off the heat. Let it sit, covered, for 5 minutes—this helps it become even softer.  
9. Serve hot, optionally garnished with a few fresh coriander leaves or a squeeze of lemon.

**Stretching tips**  
- If you have **any leftover vegetables** (carrot, potato, onion, tomato), dice them and add them with the water.  
- A handful of **peanuts or roasted chickpeas** tossed in at the end adds crunch and extra protein.  
- For a richer taste, finish with a teaspoon of ghee on top.

## 2. **Besan (Gram‑Flour) Chilla – Savory Pancake**

*Why it works:*  
- Uses **besan** (gram flour) which is often cheaper and lasts longer than wheat flour.  
- Requires only water, a pinch of salt, and any spice you have.  
- Can be cooked on a small skillet or even a flat stone.

### Ingredients (for 2‑3 pancakes)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|------------|--------|
| Besan (gram flour) | ½ cup |
| Water | ½‑¾ cup (enough to make a pourable batter) |
| Salt | ½ tsp |
| Optional: turmeric, chili powder, cumin powder, chopped onion, chopped coriander, grated carrot or shredded cabbage | a pinch each / ¼ cup veggies |
| Oil (for cooking) | 1‑2 tbsp |

### Method

1. **Whisk** besan, salt, and any dry spices together in a bowl.  
2. **Add water gradually**, stirring to avoid lumps. The batter should be the consistency of a thin pancake batter—runny but not watery.  
3. Stir in any chopped veggies you have; they add bulk and nutrition.  
4. Heat a **small non‑stick pan or a flat griddle** over medium heat. Lightly oil the surface.  
5. Pour a ladleful of batter, spreading it in a thin circle (like a crepe).  
6. Cook 2‑3 minutes until the edges lift and the underside is golden. Flip and cook the other side for another minute.  
7. Serve hot, drizzled with a little ghee or with a simple **yogurt‑cucumber dip** (if you have yogurt left).

**Stretching tips**  
- If you’re low on besan, mix **half besan, half wheat flour** (atta) to stretch the batter.  
- Add a **handful of soaked lentils** (masoor, moong) that have been lightly mashed into the batter for extra protein.

## 3. **Simple “Pulao” with Minimal Ingredients**

*Why it works:*  
- Uses **just rice** and **any oil or ghee** plus a few spices.  
- Can be made in one pot, leaving the stove free for other tasks.  
- If you have a **tiny bit of onion or garlic**, it adds flavor without needing many other ingredients.

### Ingredients (for 2‑3 servings)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|------------|--------|
| Rice (washed) | 1 cup |
| Water | 2 cups |
| Oil / Ghee | 1‑2 tbsp |
| Salt | to taste |
| Whole spices (optional) | 1‑2 bay leaf, 2‑3 cloves, 1‑2 cardamom pods, ½ tsp cumin seeds |
| Optional: a few peas, a carrot diced, a handful of chopped greens | as available |

### Method

1. Heat oil/ghee in a pot, add whole spices if you have them, let them sizzle for 5‑10 seconds.  
2. Add the **washed rice** and stir for a minute, coating each grain with oil.  
3. Add water, salt, and any veggies you’re using.  
4. Bring to a boil, then **reduce to a low simmer**, cover tightly, and cook 12‑15 minutes (or until water is absorbed).  
5. Turn off heat and let it sit, covered, for 5 minutes. Fluff with a fork.  

**Stretching tips**  
- If you have **any leftover cooked lentils or beans**, stir them in after the rice is done for protein.  
- A pinch of **turmeric** gives color and a gentle boost of antioxidants.

## 4. **“Papad‑Ki‑Sabzi” – A Tiny, Savory Soup**

*Why it works:*  
- Papad (dry, thin lentil wafers) are cheap, store for months, and become a **light broth** when simmered.  
- You only need **water, papad, a pinch of salt**, and any tiny amount of oil or spices you have.

### Ingredients (for 2 servings)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|------------|--------|
| Papad (any flavor) | 2‑3 pieces |
| Water | 2 cups |
| Salt | to taste |
| Oil / Ghee | 1 tsp |
| Optional: crushed cumin, a pinch of chili powder, a few coriander leaves | as desired |

### Method

1. In a small pot, **heat oil** and add a pinch of cumin/chili if you have them.  
2. Add water and bring to a gentle boil.  
3. Break the papads into pieces and **add them** to the boiling water.  
4. Reduce heat and **simmer 5‑7 minutes**; the papad will soften and thicken the broth slightly.  
5. Add salt, stir, and serve hot. It’s surprisingly comforting and can be paired with a piece of **roti** or **leftover rice**.

**Stretching tips**  
- If you have a few **dried herbs** (like fenugreek leaves) or a tiny amount of **tomato puree**, stir them in for extra flavor.

## 5. **A “No‑Flour” Ladoo (Energy Ball) – For Quick Calories**

*Why it works:*  
- Uses **roasted gram flour (or any flour you have)**, a little ghee, and a pinch of sugar or jaggery.  
- Provides a quick, dense source of energy when meals are scarce.

### Ingredients (makes ~6 small balls)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|------------|--------|
| Gram flour (besan) or wheat flour | ½ cup |
| Ghee (or oil) | 2‑3 tbsp |
| Sugar / Jaggery (powdered) | 2‑3 tbsp (or less, to taste) |
| Cardamom powder (optional) | a pinch |
| Water (if needed) | a few teaspoons |

### Method

1. **Dry‑roast** the flour in a pan over low heat, stirring constantly, until it turns light golden and aromatic (≈5‑7 min).  
2. Add ghee and keep stirring; the mixture will become a bit crumbly.  
3. Sprinkle sugar/jaggery and cardamom; mix until it clumps together.  
4. If the mixture feels dry, add a few drops of water and knead quickly.  
5. Form small balls (laddoos) and let them cool. Store in a clean container.

These can be eaten between meals or given to your granddaughter as a small snack that provides quick energy.

# **General Tips for Stretching Very Small Supplies**

| Situation | What to Do |
|-----------|------------|
| **You have only a little rice** | Cook it **over‑soft** (more water) and mix with **any lentils, beans, or boiled potatoes** you can find. The extra moisture makes the portion feel larger. |
| **You have a little flour** | Make **thin roti** or **chapati** (use less flour per piece, roll them as thin as possible). Stack 2‑3 small roti together with a dab of ghee—still filling, but uses less flour per serving. |
| **You have a few spices** | Even a pinch of **cumin, mustard seeds, turmeric, or chilli** can transform bland food into something appetizing. Toast spices in oil first to release flavor. |
| **You have a little oil/ghee** | Use it **sparingly**: a teaspoon for a whole pot of khichdi or pulao is enough if you first toast the spices. The flavor carries through. |
| **You have a tiny amount of fresh veg** | **Grate** or **finely chop** them; a little carrot or cabbage shredded into a rice or dal dish adds bulk and nutrients without needing a lot of volume. |
| **You need protein** | **Borrow** a handful of lentils, beans, or a small piece of dried fish/egg from a neighbour. Even 30 g of lentils adds ~7 g protein. |
| **You’re short on water** | Use the **cooking liquid** from rice or dal as a broth for a later dish (e.g., turn leftover rice water into a thin soup with papad). |
