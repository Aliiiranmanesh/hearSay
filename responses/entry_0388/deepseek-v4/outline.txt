When the ration hasn’t come and the shelves are nearly bare, a filling meal in a Rajasthan kitchen often leans on what might still be there — a little coarse grain flour, a spoon of besan, some buttermilk if the milk has soured, a single onion, a pinch of salt and chillies. These are meals that sit heavy in the stomach and warm in the chest, made from very little.

Here are a few that need only one or two main ingredients, and they’re deeply rooted in the region’s wisdom of making much from almost nothing.

---

**1. Bajra / Makki / Jowar Raab (savoury porridge)**  
This is perhaps the most direct answer — a hot, thick drink that is a meal in itself, made from just flour and water.  
*What you need:* 2–3 tablespoons of any coarse flour (bajra, makki, or jowar), water, salt, a tiny bit of ghee or oil if you have it. Optional: a pinch of turmeric or a crushed green chilli.  
*How to make it:* Dry-roast the flour in a heavy pan on low heat until it smells nutty. Slowly add about 2 cups of water, whisking so no lumps form. Add salt and chilli. Cook, stirring, until it thickens to a porridge-like consistency — about 10 minutes. If you have buttermilk (chhaas), you can whisk it in after cooking, or thin the raab with buttermilk instead of water for a tangy, more cooling version. A small spoon of ghee on top makes it deeply satisfying. It coats the stomach and keeps hunger away for hours.

**2. Besan Chilla (gram flour pancakes)**  
If there is a cup of besan (gram flour) in the house, you have a meal for two.  
*What you need:* 1 cup besan, water, salt, red chilli powder or a chopped green chilli, a pinch of ajwain (carom seeds) if available. A small onion or a handful of fresh green leaves like methi or spinach can be added but aren’t essential.  
*How to make it:* Mix besan with enough water to make a pourable batter (like dosa batter). Add salt, chilli, and any chopped onion or greens. Heat a tawa or flat pan, smear a few drops of oil. Pour a ladleful and spread thin. Cook until golden on both sides. Eat hot, maybe with a chutney made from just garlic, salt, and red chilli pounded together, or with a raw onion on the side. Two or three chillas are surprisingly filling.

**3. Dalia or any broken grain khichdi (if you have a handful of lentils or broken wheat)**  
Even a handful of moong dal or a mix of whatever lentils are left can become a soft, comforting khichdi without rice. If you have broken wheat (dalia) or even coarse maize grits, use that.  
*What you need:* 3-4 tablespoons of any lentil (yellow moong cooks fastest), or a mix of lentil and a little dalia / crushed bajra. Water, salt, turmeric, a spoon of ghee.  
*How to make it:* Wash the lentils and grain. In a pressure cooker or deep pot, heat a little ghee, add a pinch of cumin if you have it, then the lentils/grain, turmeric, and water (about 3 times the volume). Cook until completely soft and porridge-like. It should be slightly runny. Salt it well. This becomes a complete, gentle meal that’s easy on the stomach and very filling.

**4. Roti made from whatever flour is left, with a dry chutney or jaggery**  
If you have even half a kilo of bajra or jowar flour, you can make thick rotlas. No ghee needed on the roti itself — just dry-roast on the tawa and then maybe hold it over the flame to puff slightly.  
*What to eat it with:* A chutney pounded from a clove of garlic, a dried red chilli, and salt. Or a small piece of jaggery (gud) on the side. The combination of coarse grain and jaggery gives slow-release energy and a taste that feels like a full meal. If there’s a raw onion and a green chilli, even better. One or two bajra rotlas with this will keep you going.

**5. Kanji / Pej (thin gruel from leftover rice or millet)**  
If there is literally a handful of cooked rice from a previous meal, or a little cooked bajra, you can make a thin, soured gruel that’s a traditional farmer’s breakfast.  
*What you need:* Leftover cooked grain, water, salt, and a little buttermilk if available.  
*How to make it:* Mash the grain with water and leave it overnight in a warm place to ferment slightly (even a few hours helps). In the morning, add more water, salt, and drink it cold or lightly warmed. It’s hydrating, full of B vitamins, and surprisingly sustaining. If you don’t have time to ferment, simply mashing cooked grain into buttermilk with salt and a pinch of roasted cumin makes a quick, filling drink-meal called “chaach” or “mattha” with grain.

**6. A simple onion-tomato sabzi without much oil, mopped up with dry roti or eaten like a soup**  
If you have one onion, one tomato, and a green chilli, you can make a thin, tangy curry. Sauté the onion and chilli in a few drops of oil, add chopped tomato, salt, turmeric, and water. Let it boil until the flavours meld. Even without a roti, you can drink this like a thin soup, and it will warm and fill you.

**A few thoughts that might help beyond the recipe:**

- **Soak whatever grain or lentil you have.** Even a tablespoon of chana dal or whole bajra, soaked overnight and eaten with salt and a squeeze of lemon or raw onion, becomes a meal that swells in the stomach and releases energy slowly.
- **Use the buttermilk network.** In many Rajasthan villages, families who keep a cow or buffalo often share a glass of chhaas with neighbours. A glass of buttermilk with a pinch of salt and roasted cumin can stand in for a meal when there’s nothing else.
- **Ask the man at the counter softly if there’s any “broken” or sweepings grain.** Sometimes shops have grain that spilled or is rejected by the machine — it can be cleaned and used for raab or khichdi. He knows your face; he might find a way that the machine cannot block.
