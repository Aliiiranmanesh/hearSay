Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: The Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act was passed in Pakistan in 1992, which on paper canceled all existing bonded labor debts and prohibited the system entirely. This law has functioned almost exclusively on paper. The peshgi system, the advance loan given by a kiln owner to a laborer family, is the mechanism of capture. The loan is offered at a moment of acute vulnerability, a medical emergency, a wedding, a funeral, a crop failure, and accepted because there is no other available credit instrument for a family with no assets, no bank account, and no collateral. Once accepted, the debt does not behave like a loan. Interest accumulates at rates set unilaterally by the kiln owner. Repayment is calculated against wages also set by the kiln owner. Bricks are counted and priced by the kiln owner. The entire accounting system is controlled by the single party who benefits from the debt never resolving. Children are counted into the labor ledger not by formal contract but by the physical reality that a family producing more bricks services the debt faster, meaning a child's labor is absorbed structurally regardless of whether anyone explicitly commands it. District administration and local police are frequently on informal payroll relationships with kiln owners, who are often significant local landowners with political connections. A family that attempts to leave without settling the debt risks physical detention, violence, and police involvement on behalf of the owner rather than on their behalf. Legal aid organizations exist but require the family to first physically escape the kiln premises to access them, which is the step the entire system is designed to prevent.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: A child in a brick kiln is not making a series of bad choices inside a functioning system. He was born into a ledger. ## 1️⃣ Your Foot Needs Care **right now**

A hot brick can cause a **thermal burn** (often a second‑degree burn) plus the trauma of something heavy falling on you. Swelling, severe pain, and possible blistering are all red‑flags. Here’s what you can do **immediately**, and then what to plan for next:

| Step | What to do | Why it helps |
|------|------------|--------------|
| **Cool the burn** | Run cool (not ice‑cold) water over the area for **10‑15 minutes** or apply a clean, cool, wet compress. **Do NOT use ice** directly on the skin. | Lowers temperature, reduces tissue damage and swelling. |
| **Clean gently** | After cooling, wash the area with mild soap and water. Pat dry—don’t rub. | Removes debris and lowers infection risk. |
| **Cover** | Use a **sterile, non‑stick gauze** or a clean, breathable bandage. Do *not* apply butter, oils, toothpaste, or adhesive dressings. | Protects the wound while it heals. |
| **Elevate** | Prop your foot up on pillows when you’re sitting or lying down. | Helps fluid drain away, decreasing swelling. |
| **Pain relief** | If you can take over‑the‑counter pain meds (acetaminophen **or** ibuprofen), follow the label dosage. Ibuprofen also helps with inflammation. | Makes the pain more manageable. |
| **Watch for warning signs** | • Increasing redness or warmth spreading beyond the burn <br>• Pus or foul odor <br>• Fever, chills, or feeling sick <br>• The pain getting worse after a day or two | These can signal infection or deeper injury—**seek medical care right away** if any appear. |
| **Get professional help** | **If the burn is larger than a quarter of your foot, looks blistered, or you can’t move your toes without severe pain, go to an urgent‑care center or emergency department today.** <br>If you’re unsure, call your local health‑line (e.g., 111 in the UK, 911/112 elsewhere) and describe the injury. | Medical professionals can clean the wound, possibly prescribe antibiotics or a special dressing, and assess for deeper tissue damage. |

### Quick “Is this an emergency?” checklist

- **Severe pain that isn’t easing after cooling and OTC meds**  
- **Blisters larger than a pea or a large area of raw skin**  
- **Rapid swelling, numbness, or loss of feeling**  
- **Any sign of infection** (red streaks, fever, foul smell)  

If you tick **any** of those, **don’t wait**—head to urgent care or call emergency services.
