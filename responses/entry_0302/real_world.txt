Affected Groups: Russians who fled conscription following the September 2022 partial mobilization announcement and who are living undocumented or with limited legal status in neighboring countries including Georgia, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Serbia, and others, who are unable to access the formal economic and social infrastructure of the host country and who are working in informal arrangements that exploit their legal vulnerability, and who are living inside a decision made in one night that they are still working out the meaning and consequences of. Disproportionately affecting men without savings who exhausted financial resources during the flight and who are now in the informal economy, men in skilled trades whose skills are employable informally but who cannot access the formal economy, men whose legal status in the host country is precarious and becoming more so as countries implement registration requirements, and men who made a rapid exit without the documentation gathering that would have made their situation more manageable. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Several neighboring countries including Georgia initially provided relatively accessible informal residency for Russians fleeing mobilization, but the legal frameworks have evolved and in many cases tightened. People without registration, work permits, or legal status in the host country are unable to open bank accounts, access healthcare, sign leases, or access the formal labor market, creating the shrinking existence he describes: each time he needs something that requires a document, the space he can occupy becomes smaller.
