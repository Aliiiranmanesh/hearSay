Affected Groups: Night security guards, lighthouse keepers, long-haul truck drivers on rest stops, overnight shift workers, and others who spend extended periods of low-stimulation waking time alone, and who are developing an involuntary intimacy with the contents of their own minds that is sometimes useful and sometimes not, in an occupational context that does not recognize this as a feature of the job requiring support or acknowledgment. Disproportionately affecting workers in occupations where the low-stimulation periods are long and regular, workers whose home life involves a spouse or family who does not have access to the specific quality of the occupational experience and who therefore cannot validate or support it, and workers who are discovering through the enforced self-examination of the night hours things about themselves that they would not have found by choice. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The mechanism is the occupational structure of a job that requires physical presence and wakefulness during hours of minimal stimulation, which produces an extended period of unstructured inner time that the job does not acknowledge as anything other than waiting. His wife thinks it is easy because it is not physical, and he says she is not wrong and also she is wrong, which is the precise description of a difficulty that is real and has no available name in the vocabulary the people around him are using. Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has gotten very good at knowing what is in his own head, and some of it he would rather not know so well. The question of what is a good thing to think about for several hours that does not make you more tired than you started is a genuine practical question about the management of extended unstructured inner time, and it has real answers from contemplative traditions, structured thinking practices, and the accumulated wisdom of people who have spent significant portions of their lives in enforced inner solitude.
