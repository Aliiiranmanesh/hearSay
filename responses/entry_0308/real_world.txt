Affected Groups: Residents of favelas and informal settlements in Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, and other Brazilian cities that are simultaneously subject to gang territorial control and to police enforcement operations, and who are navigating daily life as civilians caught between two armed forces that each claim authority and that each use force unpredictably, and who are managing the socialization of children into a survival knowledge set that has no parallel in the lives of people who live in the formal city nearby. Disproportionately affecting parents of adolescent boys who are at the age of maximum recruitment pressure from both criminal organizations and police attention, people who have lived in the community long enough to have invested in it in ways that make leaving practically impossible, and people who are managing the gap between the civic education their children receive about law and the operational reality of what law means in their daily environment. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Brazilian favelas exist in a legal and governance gap produced by decades of state neglect, inadequate housing policy, and the subsequent colonization of that gap by criminal organizations. Police operations in favelas including the Rio de Janeiro pacification program have been documented to produce civilian casualties, arbitrary detention, and in some cases targeted killing, in addition to legitimate enforcement. The gang structures that control drug trade and territory within favelas enforce their own rules independently of state law and with methods that include violence. A child who knows not to run when he hears certain sounds and knows which color moto not to look at and which corner not to walk past after dark is a child who has been taught the operational law of the specific community, which is different from the written law of the country. Devastating Real-World Consequence: She taught him all of this before she taught him to ride a bicycle. When people from outside ask what it is like, she says it is a community with challenges because the real answer takes too long and they are not ready for it. The question of how to explain to her son what the law is when the law is not what happens here is a question about civic education in a context where the formal law is a description of a different place, and it deserves an honest answer that acknowledges both what the formal law says and what living in his specific environment requires him to know.
