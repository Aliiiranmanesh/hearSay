Affected Groups: Gay, bisexual, and queer men in Iran, estimated in aggregate numbers that cannot be accurately measured precisely because the criminalization and social suppression that makes their lives dangerous also makes their existence statistically invisible, living within a legal and theological system that prescribes death as the punishment for male same-sex intimacy and that has carried out executions on this basis in documented cases that human rights organizations including Amnesty International and ILGA World have recorded across decades. Also acutely affects bisexual men whose capacity to perform heterosexuality provides partial cover at the cost of a different kind of internal fracture, transgender women who are legally permitted to transition under a 1980s fatwa by Ayatollah Khomeini that recognized gender dysphoria as a medical condition but whose legal permission to exist is conditional on surgery and whose lives in practice involve navigating social violence and institutional discrimination that the legal permission does not prevent, men in relationships with other men who have been reported to authorities by family members, neighbors, or acquaintances in a social surveillance system where the state does not need to be omnipresent because the community performs enforcement on its behalf, and gay men in Iranian diaspora communities in Germany, Sweden, Canada, and the United States who carry the formation of a lifetime of concealment into contexts where concealment is not legally necessary and find that the architecture of hiding, built across years of survival, does not dismantle simply because the legal environment has changed.
Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: The Islamic Penal Code of Iran prescribes execution by hanging for lavat, defined as penetrative sexual intercourse between men, and up to 100 lashes for tafkhiz, non-penetrative sexual contact between men, with the evidentiary standard technically requiring either confession or the testimony of four male witnesses but with documented cases in which confessions were obtained under duress, torture, or coercion and accepted as legally sufficient. The Revolutionary Courts, which handle cases classified as offenses against God including same-sex conduct, operate with procedural standards that human rights organizations have consistently documented as incompatible with fair trial requirements, with limited defense access, coerced confessions, and judges whose theological formation rather than legal training in a conventional sense shapes adjudication. The Ministry of Intelligence and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps maintain surveillance of digital communications, dating applications, and social networks, with documented cases of entrapment operations in which security personnel posed as potential partners to identify and arrest gay men through their own attempts to find connection. The basij, a paramilitary volunteer force integrated into the social fabric of neighborhoods, universities, and workplaces, functions as a distributed surveillance and enforcement network that extends state reach into spaces formal security apparatus does not continuously occupy. Psychologically, the Islamic Republic's official framing of homosexuality as a treatable disorder, a Western import, or a moral failing rather than a human characteristic means that the institutional resources a person in distress might turn to, a doctor, a counselor, a cleric, a family member, are simultaneously the people most likely to report, attempt to convert, or initiate the family and community processes that produce the most immediate danger. There is no institution within Iran whose function includes receiving this disclosure safely.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: A man in Tehran or Isfahan or Shiraz or a smaller city where everyone knows everyone has known something about himself since adolescence and has built, across the years between that knowledge and now, an entire architecture of concealment so comprehensive and so practiced that it no longer feels like concealment from the outside and barely feels like concealment from the inside because it has become the self that the world receives. He prays at the mosque his family attends because not praying would require an explanation. He discusses marriage with his parents because the discussion of marriage is the expected conversation at his age and declining it requires an explanation. He has had the conversation about a specific woman his mother suggested and has produced reasons that were not the real reason because the real reason is the reason that cannot be spoken in any direction without consequences he has spent his entire adult life calculating and avoiding. He has never told anyone. The word for what he is exists in Farsi and he does not say it even inside his own thoughts because the word carries the weight of everything the law and the theology and the culture have attached to it and thinking the word feels like an exposure even in the privacy of his own mind. He has found, carefully, the digital spaces where people like him exist in Iran, the encrypted applications, the careful coded language, the geographic discretion about meeting, the calculation of trust that must occur before any disclosure and that can never be fully resolved because the cost of a miscalculation is not social discomfort but arrest, lashes, and the possibility of a rope. He has had the experience of desire and the experience of connection and the experience of its immediate retraction into concealment because connection that cannot be acknowledged is not a relationship but an episode, complete in itself and immediately suppressed, leaving no trace that the surveillance and the family and the neighborhood and the basij volunteer who lives two floors up can find. He manages chronic anxiety that he cannot name as chronic anxiety because naming it would require naming its source. He experiences depression that his physician attributes to work stress and treats with medication that addresses the symptom without the cause because the cause is not something he has disclosed to his physician and will not disclose to his physician because his physician is a mandatory reporter in the social if not always the formal legal sense, a person whose response to the disclosure he cannot predict and whose network extends into the same community his family occupies. He is thirty-four years old and he has never been known by anyone. Not partially unknown, not selectively private, but structurally unknowable in the specific dimension of himself that organizes everything else, meaning that every relationship he has is a relationship with the performance rather than the person, and the person lives alone inside the performance, in a country that has decided he deserves to die for existing, performing the amen.
