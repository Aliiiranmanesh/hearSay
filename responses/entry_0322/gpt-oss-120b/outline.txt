If you have to keep *why* you’re learning English under wraps, you can still make solid progress – you just need tools and habits that don’t raise eyebrows. ### 2️⃣ Build a “Silent Immersion” Routine  

| Time of Day | Activity | Why it stays low‑profile |

**Key tip:** Choose content that *doesn’t* feel like a language lesson. News, documentaries, cooking shows, or even the TV series the school shows (the “Arkadag” reference) are perfect because they blend learning with entertainment.

### 4️⃣ Practice Output Without a Partner  

1. **Talk to the Mirror** – Pretend you’re explaining the day’s news to an English‑speaking friend.

3. **Shadow‑Chatbots** – Apps like **Replika**, **ChatGPT**, or **Duolingo’s chatbot** let you type or speak without a human on the other side. Use a generic username so the purpose isn’t obvious.  
4. **Narrate Your Routine** – While washing dishes, say aloud: “I am washing the plates. The water is hot.” It feels silly, but it forces you to produce sentences in real time.  

All of these can be done alone, at home, and disappear when you turn off the device.

### 5️⃣ Keep a “Stealth” Vocabulary Bank  

- **Collect on the fly:** Whenever you hear an unfamiliar word (on TV, in a song, in a conversation), pause and write it down in your notebook or a digital note.  
- **Add context:** Write a short sentence using the word. This makes the word stick without needing a teacher.  
- **Review nightly:** Spend 2‑3 minutes scanning the list and saying each word aloud.  

Because the list lives only in your private notes, no one can infer why you’re cataloguing English words.

| **Speaking fluency** | Time how long it takes you to narrate a 2‑minute story without long pauses. |
| **Reading speed** | Use a short article, set a timer, count words per minute. |
