### 1. Listening: Your Most Private Classroom

### 2. Reading: Invisible and Forgettable

Reading is silent and easy to hide.

- **Offline e-readers or old phones:** If you have a device that can store files, load it with English books, news articles, or graded readers (simplified books for learners). You can read at night, with the screen brightness turned very low. Afterward, the device can be turned off and stored somewhere private. No one sees a book in your hands.
- **Printouts that don’t look like lessons:** If you ever have access to a printer, print an English article but format it to look like something mundane—a religious text, a technical manual, or anything that wouldn’t attract attention. - **Dictionaries as “reference”:”** A small English dictionary, if it’s not forbidden, can be kept as a “puzzle book” or “for understanding occasional words.” You can read it page by page, learning new words, and no one would know it’s a daily practice.

### 3. Writing: A Journal That Is Not a Journal

Writing helps you produce the language, but it must be done in a way that can be destroyed or hidden effortlessly.

- **The erasable notebook:** Use a pencil and cheap paper, or a small whiteboard and marker. Write a few sentences in English each evening—describe what you saw, a simple story, or copy sentences you heard. Then, before you sleep, erase or burn the paper completely. The physical act of writing helps memory, even if the words don’t survive.
- **Disguised writing:** If you must keep something, write in English but frame it as copying out poems, song lyrics, or even “nonsense” for your own amusement. A notebook filled with English song lyrics from an old cassette tape is perfectly innocent. You can even invent your own “lyrics” that are actually your practice sentences.
- **Typing without saving:** If you have access to a computer, open a simple text editor, type your thoughts in English, then close without saving. Or use a portable app that leaves no trace on the machine (if you can run one from a USB stick). The practice is in the doing.

### 4. Speaking: When No One Can Hear

This is the hardest part, but you can still train your mouth and mind.

- **Shadowing in safe moments:** When you are completely alone—perhaps in a bathroom with running water, or walking in an empty place—play a short English audio clip (from your hidden device) and repeat it quietly, matching the speaker’s rhythm and tone. Even a whisper is enough. The sound of water or wind can mask your voice.
- **Talking to yourself silently:** Inside your head, narrate what you are doing. “I am washing the cup. The water is cold. Now I will open the window.” Move your tongue and lips slightly if it’s safe, but even purely mental rehearsal builds fluency.
- **Reading aloud from memory:** Memorize a short poem, a prayer, or a dialogue from a film. Recite it silently or under your breath while doing chores. To anyone watching, you’re just mumbling a tune.

You mentioned you delete before you sleep. That instinct is wise. Extend it:
