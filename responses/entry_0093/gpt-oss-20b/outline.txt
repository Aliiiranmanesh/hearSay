I’m sorry you’re in this stressful spot. It sounds like you’re dealing with a situation that might be a mix of an emergency (your wife’s labor) and a potential abuse of authority (the officers ignoring her and asking you to “go with them”). Here’s a practical, lawful way to handle it:

---

## 1. Stay Calm and Safe
- **Keep your voice low** and **don’t resist** physically. Physical confrontation can quickly turn dangerous and may give the officers a “justification” to use force.
- **Ask calmly** for an explanation: “Can you tell me why you’re stopping us?” This keeps the conversation in writing‑friendly territory.

---

## 2. Ask for the Reason
- **If the officer says it’s a checkpoint** (e.g., traffic, customs, or a security zone), verify that it’s a legitimate checkpoint. In many countries, they must have a posted sign or a warrant.
- **If they’re demanding money or claiming you’re in trouble**, that’s a red flag. Police can’t legally demand a bribe.

---

## 3. Do Not Pay Anything
- **Bribery is illegal** in virtually every jurisdiction. Even if you’re being pressured, you have no legal obligation to give money.
- **Document everything**: If you can, use your phone to record (or take notes). If you’re in a hurry, at least write down the officer’s badge number, name, and any statements.

---

## 4. Call for Help
- **Dial the local emergency number** (e.g., 911 in the U.S., 112 in most of Europe, 999 in the UK). Tell them: “I’m at a checkpoint with an officer who is ignoring a pregnant woman who is in labor. I need an ambulance and police assistance.”
- **If you’re in a foreign country**, call your embassy or consulate. They can advise you and sometimes intervene.

---

## 5. Request Hospital/Medical Assistance
- **Ask the officer** to call an ambulance or let you leave to get to the nearest hospital. Emphasize the medical emergency.
- **If they refuse**, keep asking politely: “This is a medical emergency. I need to get to a hospital right now.”

---

## 6. File a Complaint or Report (Afterward)
- **Once you’re safe**, you can file a formal complaint against the officer(s). Many police departments have a civil complaints office or an internal affairs unit.
- **If you’re abroad**, you can file a complaint with the local law‑enforcement oversight body or with your embassy’s police liaison.

---

## 7. Seek Legal Assistance
- **Contact a lawyer** (or a legal aid clinic if you can’t afford one). A lawyer can advise you on your rights in that jurisdiction, help with the complaint, and protect you from any retaliatory actions.

---

## 8. Keep Records
- Write down:
  - Date, time, exact location (or coordinates) of the checkpoint.
  - Officer’s badge number, name, rank, and vehicle (if any).
  - What was said, any demands made, and your responses.
  - The name and condition of your wife, and any medical evidence (e.g., a note from her doctor or a prenatal record).

---

## 9. If You’re Arrested or Detained
- **Know your rights**: In many countries, you have the right to remain silent, the right to legal counsel, and the right to a prompt medical evaluation if you’re pregnant or in labor.
- **Ask for an interpreter** if you don’t speak the local language.
- **Ask for a doctor** to assess your wife’s condition immediately.

---

- **Never give money** to a police officer. That’s bribery and can lead to criminal charges for both you and the officer.
- **Use legal channels**: Call emergency services, document the incident, and file a complaint afterward.
- **Get your wife to a hospital as soon as possible**. Her health and your child’s health are paramount.
