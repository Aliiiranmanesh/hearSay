Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Egypt is a signatory to the 1951 Refugee Convention and UNHCR operates a refugee registration system in Egypt, issuing yellow cards to registered refugees that technically entitle holders to certain protections. A child's school enrollment requires a birth certificate in the Egyptian system, and a birth certificate from Khartoum is inaccessible by definition to someone who left Khartoum because it was not somewhere they could be. UNHCR's Community Based Protection program and NGO partners including Caritas, the Egyptian Foundation for Refugee Services, and others operate in Egypt specifically to help navigate these bureaucratic gaps, though coverage is uneven and waitlists are long. ## 3. Parallel move: Get a *school‑registration* document now

Even while you are waiting for the Sudanese birth certificate, you can **secure a provisional spot for your son**. Follow these steps:

1. **Gather the following “supporting” documents** (all should be originals or notarised copies):
   - Your **temporary residence paper** (Sixth of October office)  
   - Your **UNHCR card** or any refugee ID you have  
   - A **passport‑style photo** of your son (3 × 4 cm)  
   - A **letter from the Sudanese Embassy** (even if it only says “we are processing the request”)  
   - Any **medical record** that mentions the child’s name and date of birth (e.g., vaccination card, clinic receipt)  

2. **Visit the school’s administration office** and ask for the **“Form for Children without Birth Certificates”** (استمارة الأطفال دون شهادة ميلاد).  
   - In most governorates (Cairo, Giza, Al‑Mansoura, etc.) the form is standard and the principal can sign it.  
   - Fill it out, attach the documents above, and request a **“Certificate of No Objection”** (شهادة عدم ممانعة) from the school.  

3. **If the school says “no, you need the birth certificate”**, ask to speak to the **Vice‑Principal or the School’s Legal Advisor** and show them the UNHCR/NGO letters.  
   - Mention **Article 10 of the 2009 Education Law** that guarantees education for all children residing in Egypt.  

4. **If the school still refuses**, request a **written refusal** (خطاب رفض) and take it to the **Local Education Directorate (مكتب التربية)** in your district.  
   - Bring all the documents you already have. The directorate can issue a **“Special Enrollment Permit”** (تصريح تسجيل خاص) that forces the school to accept the child.

## 5. Practical tips for navigating the bureaucracy

| Tip | Why it helps |
