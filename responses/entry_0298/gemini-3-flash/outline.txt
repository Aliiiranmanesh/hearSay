If StARS is overwhelmed, RLAP (often associated with AMERA) provides specialized legal assistance for refugees in Egypt.
*   **Action:** Ask them specifically for help with **"Civil Documentation."** They can sometimes intervene when an Egyptian government office (like the school district) refuses to accept your UNHCR papers as proof of identity/age for your son.

### 3. The UNHCR "Protection" Desk (Not Registration)
The paper you have from the 6th of October is likely your registration certificate (the "Yellow Card" or an appointment slip). While the registration desk is for data entry, you need the **Protection Desk**.
*   **Action:** You need to report that you are facing a "Bar to Education." UNHCR has a partnership with **Save the Children** and **UNICEF** specifically to handle school enrollment issues for Sudanese refugees. 
*   **Ask for:** An "Attestation of Birth" or a document that verifies the child’s age and parentage based on your UNHCR file.

### 4. The Sudanese Embassy (With Caution)
Usually, the Embassy is where you would get a replacement birth certificate. However, many refugees are hesitant to visit their embassy, and the current situation in Khartoum makes communication with the central registry difficult.
*   **Note:** If you are registered as an asylum seeker, visiting your national embassy can sometimes complicate your refugee status. **Talk to a legal advisor at StARS first** before going to the embassy to ask for a replacement certificate.

Egypt has historically allowed Sudanese students access to public schools under the same conditions as Egyptians. However, because of the massive influx of people, some school directors are being more rigid about documentation than the law requires.
*   **The Strategy:** Do not go to the school alone. If you can get a letter of support from an NGO like **Save the Children** or **StARS**, the school is much more likely to accept your son without the Khartoum birth certificate.
