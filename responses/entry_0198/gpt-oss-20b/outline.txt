## 1. Understand the type of problem

* **Harassment / intimidation** – Someone is making you feel unsafe or uncomfortable (e.g., the man who lines up with you, his wife greeting you, etc.).
* **Discrimination / exclusion** – You’ve been moved to a different section or you’re being treated differently because of who you are.
* **Safety/health risk** – Something that could physically harm you or your children.

Knowing exactly what you’re dealing with helps you choose the right channel.

| What to note | Why it matters |
|--------------|----------------|

| Photos / videos (only if you can take them safely) | Visual proof can be powerful |

| Channel | How to use it | Anonymity | Notes |

**How to decide:**  
*If you think the person who caused the trouble is close to the camp management, start with an anonymous or third‑party line.

## 4. How to keep yourself safe while reporting

1. **Use a “safe” time and place** – e.g., after the distribution is over, when fewer people are around.  
2. **Bring a companion** – a child or a trusted neighbor can act as a witness and provide moral support.  
3. **Record the conversation** – If the camp allows it, record a brief voice note when you speak to a staff member.  
4. **Limit personal details** – Share only what’s necessary.  
5. **Keep a copy of your report** – In case you need to refer back to it.  
6. **Have a backup plan** – Know where you can go (e.g., a different distribution point or a shelter) if you feel threatened.

---

## 5. Sample “Safe‑Reporting” Script

> “Hello, my name is [you can use a pseudonym if you want]. I am a resident of [your camp section] and I have a concern that I would like to report confidentially. Last year, a similar situation happened and the person who reported it was moved to a different area. I am worried that if I report, I might face retaliation. I am looking for a way to let you know what happened so that it can be addressed without putting me at risk. Would it be possible to handle this in a confidential way?”

You can adapt this to the channel you choose. The key is to be clear, concise, and to emphasize the need for confidentiality.

## 6. If you think it’s a serious safety issue

- **Contact a protection hotline** (UNHCR, ICRC, or the local NGO that runs the camp).  
- **Seek medical attention** if you or your children have been physically harmed.  
- **Document injuries** with a photo or a doctor’s note.  
- **Ask for a “safety plan”** – many NGOs can help you create a plan (e.g., who to call, where to go, how to avoid the problem area).
