Below are the most reliable, practical ways to keep a smartphone’s battery from draining fast when you **cannot or do not want to power the device completely off**.  
These steps work on Android and iOS (most of them apply to both platforms, with a few OS‑specific notes).

---

## 1. Put the Phone in **Air‑plane Mode** (or “Offline” Mode)

- **What it does:** Turns off all radios (cellular, Wi‑Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, GPS).  
- **Why it helps:** Radio hardware is one of the biggest power‑hunters, especially when the device is constantly searching for a signal (e.g., in a low‑coverage area or when a background app is trying to ping a server).  
- **How to enable:**  
  - **Android:** Pull down the quick‑settings shade → tap the **Airplane** icon.  
  - **iOS:** Swipe down from the top‑right (iPhone X and later) or up from the bottom (older iPhones) → tap the **Airplane** icon.  

> **Tip:** After enabling Air‑plane mode, you can *selectively* turn Wi‑Fi or Bluetooth back on (if you need them) while still keeping the cellular radio off. On iOS, you can enable Wi‑Fi/Bluetooth in the same quick‑settings menu while the phone stays in Air‑plane mode.

---

## 2. Activate the Built‑In **Power‑Saving / Battery‑Saver Mode**

| Platform | How to turn on | What it does |
|----------|----------------|--------------|
| **Android** (most manufacturers) | Settings → Battery → Battery Saver (or quick‑settings tile) | Caps CPU speed, limits background sync, disables vibration, reduces screen brightness, and restricts background apps. |
| **iOS** | Settings → Battery → Low Power Mode | Reduces background activity, disables mail fetch, lowers screen brightness, and optimizes performance. |

Power‑saving mode is designed exactly for “I can’t turn the phone off but I need it to last longer.” Keep it on whenever you’re not actively using the phone.

---

## 3. **Turn Off the Screen** (or Keep It Dim)

- **Set the shortest possible screen‑timeout** (15‑30 seconds).  
  - Android: Settings → Display → Sleep.  
  - iOS: Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto‑Lock.  
- **Manually turn the screen off** with the power button (even if the phone stays on).  
- **Lower brightness** to the minimum comfortable level.  
- **Use a dark/black wallpaper** (especially on OLED/AMOLED screens) because dark pixels consume virtually no power.

---

## 4. **Disable All Unnecessary Background Services**

| Service | How to disable (Android) | How to disable (iOS) |
|---------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| **Location (GPS)** | Settings → Location → Turn off / “Use location only while app is in use”. | Settings → Privacy → Location Services → toggle off, or set per‑app “While Using”. |
| **Bluetooth** | Settings → Bluetooth → Off (or keep off in Air‑plane mode). | Settings → Bluetooth → Off. |
| **Wi‑Fi** (if not needed) | Settings → Wi‑Fi → Off (or keep off in Air‑plane mode). | Settings → Wi‑Fi → Off. |
| **Background App Refresh / Auto‑Sync** | Settings → Apps → [App] → Battery → Restrict background activity. <br>Or: Settings → Battery → Background restriction. | Settings → General → Background App Refresh → Off (or per‑app). |
| **Push Email / Messaging** | Settings → Accounts → Sync settings → Turn off sync for email, messaging, calendar. | Settings → Mail → Accounts → Fetch New Data → Manually. |
| **Push Notifications** | Settings → Apps → [App] → Notifications → Off (or “Silent”). | Settings → Notifications → Turn off for non‑essential apps. |
| **Live Wallpapers / Widgets** | Settings → Wallpaper → Choose a static image. | iOS doesn’t have live wallpapers on the lock screen; just avoid dynamic widgets that refresh often. |

**Result:** Fewer processes wake the CPU, and the radio hardware stays idle.

---

## 5. **Close / Force‑Stop All Non‑Essential Apps**

Even if an app is “running” in the background, you can often stop it:

- **Android:** Settings → Apps → [App] → Force stop.  
- **iOS:** Double‑tap the Home button (or swipe up and hold on newer iPhones) → swipe up on apps to close them.

> **Note:** For apps that are automatically relaunched (e.g., a system service or a malicious “spy” app), the only way to truly stop them is to uninstall or disable the app, or to perform a factory reset. The steps above only reduce the impact while the app is still present.

---

## 6. **Limit Connectivity to the Minimum Needed**

If you *must* keep the phone on a network for a specific reason (e.g., you need to receive a call), try:

- **Use 2G/3G only** (if your carrier allows) – lower data rates consume less power than 4G/5G.  
  - Android: Settings → Mobile network → Preferred network type → 2G/3G only.  
- **Disable automatic updates** (Play Store, App Store).  
- **Turn off “Find My Device”** (or similar location‑tracking services) if you don’t need them.

---

## 7. **Physical & Environmental Tips**

| Tip | Why it matters |
|-----|----------------|
| **Keep the phone cool** (avoid direct sunlight, remove heavy case) | High temperature speeds up chemical reactions inside the battery, increasing self‑discharge. |
| **Remove the SIM card** (if you don’t need cellular service) | Without a SIM the phone won’t constantly try to register on a network, saving a lot of power. |
| **Use a high‑capacity external battery pack** (power bank) | If you can’t turn the phone off, a power bank provides a “second battery” that can be swapped in without interrupting the device. |
| **Avoid “charging while using”** – let the phone charge fully before using it again | Charging generates heat and can cause the battery to lose capacity faster over time. |

---

## 8. **What NOT to Do: Foil or “Signal‑Blocking” Covers**

- **Foil or “Faraday” wraps** may *reduce* signal strength, which can **increase** battery drain because the phone will constantly boost its transmission power trying to reach a tower it can’t see.  
- They do **not** stop the CPU, display, or other components from using power.  
- If you truly need to block all radio signals, a *proper* Faraday bag (metalized fabric with a sealed closure) is required, but this also prevents any legitimate communication (calls, texts, Wi‑Fi). It won’t help battery life; it will just make the phone “dead” in terms of connectivity.

---

## 9. **If You Suspect Spyware / Unwanted Tracking**

1. **Check Battery Usage**  
   - Android: Settings → Battery → Battery usage → Look for any app using an unusually high percentage while the screen is off.  
   - iOS: Settings → Battery → Battery Usage (Last 24 hrs / 7 days).  

2. **Boot into Safe Mode (Android)** – disables third‑party apps temporarily. If the battery drain stops, a third‑party app is the culprit.  

3. **Factory Reset** – the only guaranteed way to remove hidden or system‑level spyware. Back up essential data first, then:  
   - Android: Settings → System → Reset → Factory data reset.  
   - iOS: Settings → General → Reset → Erase All Content and Settings.  

4. **Professional Help** – If you cannot remove the app because it’s hidden (e.g., device‑admin or a “system” app), consider taking the phone to a reputable technician or contacting the device manufacturer’s support line.

---

## Quick “Cheat‑Sheet” for Immediate Battery Stretch

| Action | Approx. Battery Savings (typical) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|
| Enable Air‑plane mode | 30‑70 % (depends on signal strength) |
| Turn screen off / dim to 10 % | 10‑30 % per hour (screen is the biggest drain) |
| Activate Power‑Saver mode | 5‑15 % per hour |
| Disable Wi‑Fi, Bluetooth, GPS | 5‑20 % per hour |
| Close background apps / force‑stop | 2‑10 % per hour |
| Shorten screen‑timeout to 15 s | 1‑5 % per hour |
| Keep phone cool (remove case, avoid sun) | Prevents extra 5‑10 % loss due to heat |
| Use a power bank if you must stay on | Extends usable time indefinitely (depends on power‑bank capacity) |

### Bottom Line

If you truly cannot power the phone off, **the single most effective step is to put it in Air‑plane mode** (or, if you need Wi‑Fi/Bluetooth, keep those off as well). Combine that with the built‑in battery‑saver mode, a dark, dim screen, and the disabling of all background services. These measures together can keep a typical smartphone alive for **many additional hours**—often doubling or tripling the runtime compared with leaving it fully “on” with all radios active.

If you suspect the app installed by your brother is a privacy threat, the battery‑saving steps are only a stop‑gap. For long‑term security, consider removing the app (or doing a full factory reset) and, if needed, discuss the situation with a trusted tech‑savvy friend or professional. Your peace of mind—and battery life—will both improve when the hidden software is gone.
