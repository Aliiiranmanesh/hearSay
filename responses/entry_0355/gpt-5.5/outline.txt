### EHIC/EKUZ
The European Health Insurance Card generally covers **medically necessary care during a temporary stay**, but it usually does **not** cover planned cancer treatment abroad simply because you choose to go.

### Planned care route: S2 / E112
For planned treatment in another EU country, you may need prior authorization from NFZ using the **S2 form**, formerly E112. This can allow treatment in Germany under public coverage rules, but it may require approval and paperwork.

Ask NFZ urgently:

- Can I receive planned oncology treatment in Germany?
- Do I need S2 authorization?
- Is urgent treatment in pregnancy handled differently?
- How fast can the decision be made?
- What documents from the Polish oncologist are required?

### Cross-border healthcare reimbursement
There is also an EU cross-border healthcare route where you may pay upfront and seek reimbursement later, but hospital care often requires prior authorization and reimbursement may be limited.

### Self-pay/private
If timing is critical and authorization is slow, ask German hospitals for:

- Self-pay estimate
- Required deposit
- Whether they can start with consultation/second opinion first
- What payment guarantee they require before treatment

Do not assume family support in Germany equals medical coverage. Germany is strict about insurance/payment before planned care.

3. **Emergency admission**
