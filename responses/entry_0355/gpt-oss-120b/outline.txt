## 5. Pregnancy‑Specific Considerations

1. **Trimester timing** – Most oncologists aim to start chemo **after 12 weeks** (first trimester) because organogenesis is complete. If you’re already past that, you may be eligible for standard regimens.  
2. **Delivery planning** – Discuss with both your obstetrician (in Poland) and the German oncologist whether a **planned early delivery** (e.g., at 34‑36 weeks) might be needed after a certain number of chemo cycles.  
3. **Medication safety** – Request a **list of drugs that are contraindicated** in pregnancy. The German team will usually have protocols for “pregnancy‑compatible” chemo.  
4. **Prenatal care continuity** – Ask if the German hospital can share prenatal ultrasound results with your Polish OB‑GYN, or arrange a **tele‑consultation** between the two doctors.  
5. **Emergency plan** – Keep a **copy of your pregnancy card** (including blood type, allergies, and last menstrual period) in your carry‑on, and a list of the nearest obstetric units in the city where you’ll be treated.
