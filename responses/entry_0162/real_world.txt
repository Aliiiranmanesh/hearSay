Affected Groups: Mothers, fathers, and other primary family members of people with severe substance use disorders, particularly opioid and stimulant addiction, who have been in sustained crisis relationship with their addicted family member for years or decades, who have developed sophisticated pattern recognition for the stages of the addiction cycle while retaining the emotional vulnerability that makes each stage as affecting as the first, and who live in a permanent state of contingent hope that is regularly disappointed in ways they correctly anticipate but cannot protect themselves from by anticipating. Disproportionately affecting mothers who have maintained primary contact with addicted children beyond the point where other family members have withdrawn, family members of people with co-occurring mental illness where the addiction and mental health conditions interact in ways that make standard addiction recovery approaches less predictable, family members in communities with limited access to family-oriented addiction support like Al-Anon or specialized family therapy, and family members whose own physical health has been affected by the sustained stress of years of crisis proximity. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism that addresses the position she is in. She can call emergency services if there is an acute overdose. She cannot compel treatment. She cannot prevent him from using. She cannot protect herself from the impact of his calls without cutting off the contact that is also the relationship. In some jurisdictions, involuntary commitment for addiction is available but the legal threshold is high, the process is invasive, and the outcomes are not reliably better than voluntary treatment. Family members in her position often become the primary crisis management system for their addicted family member by default, because they are present and responsive and the formal system is not, which means her availability and care become the infrastructure that enables continued survival without recovery. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She heard something in his voice at 3am that she has learned, across however many years this has been happening, to distinguish from the other versions of his voice, and it was the version that sounded like before, and she made up the couch because that voice has not been wrong before, and he did not come, and she waited until 6. She is asking whether to leave the door unlocked when she goes to work, which is a question about whether she is still the person who is responsible for keeping him alive on the days when he decides he wants to be kept alive, and whether she can afford to not be that person, and whether she can afford to continue being it.
