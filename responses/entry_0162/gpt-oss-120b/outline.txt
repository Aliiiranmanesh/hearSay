## 1. Immediate Safety: The Door

**Lock the door when you leave for work**  
- **Why:** An unlocked door can make it easier for anyone—especially someone who might be disoriented, intoxicated, or in crisis—to leave the house unsupervised, wander outside, or be exposed to other hazards (traffic, strangers, etc.).  
- **How to keep him safe while you’re gone:**  
  1. **Spare key in a lockbox** – Mount a small, combination‑locked box near the door and give your son the code. He can let himself in if he truly needs to come home, but the door stays locked for everyone else.  
  2. **Key under a magnet or in a discreet spot** – If a lockbox feels too formal, a hidden but known spot (e.g., under a magnet on the fridge) works as long as it’s not obvious to a stranger.  
  3. **Ask a trusted neighbor or friend** – If you have someone nearby you trust, you can give them a spare key and ask them to check in if they hear a disturbance.

If you feel that any of these options might enable him to leave the house unsupervised (e.g., he could go out and get into dangerous situations), you might consider **keeping the door locked and using a “call‑for‑help” system**—for example, a spare key that only you or a trusted adult have, and a plan for him to call you or a crisis line if he needs to get out.

## 3. Building a Safety Plan (Even While He’s Still at Home)

A safety plan is a short, written set of steps that both of you can refer to when feelings of crisis arise. It can be as simple as:

1. **Identify warning signs** – “I start thinking I can’t handle it” or “I feel very shaky.”  
2. **List coping actions** – “Take three deep breaths, call a friend, listen to a favorite song.”  
3. **Contact list** – 988 (U.S. Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), your therapist, a local crisis center, a trusted neighbor, your own emergency contact.  
4. **Environment safety** – Keep any pills, knives, or other means of self‑harm out of reach, or store them in a locked cabinet.  
5. **When to call emergency services** – “If I’m thinking about ending my life or if I’m unsafe to be alone.”

You can create this together (if he’s open) or on your own and place it somewhere visible (e.g., on the fridge). Having a written plan reduces the chaos of the moment.
