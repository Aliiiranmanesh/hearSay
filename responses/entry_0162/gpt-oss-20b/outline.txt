## 1. Create a *Safety Plan* for Your Son

A safety plan is a written list of things he can do when he feels a crisis coming on. It’s usually developed with a mental‑health professional, but you can start drafting one now and refine it later.

| What to Include | Why It Helps |
|-----------------|--------------|
| **Triggers** (e.g., feeling “lost,” “overwhelmed”) | He can spot early warning signs. |
| **Coping strategies** (deep breathing, grounding, music) | Gives him concrete tools that don’t require help. |
| **People to call** (therapist, psychiatrist, trusted friend, you) | He has a clear first step. |
| **Emergency contacts** (911, local crisis line, hospital) | Quick access if he’s in danger. |
| **Medication schedule** | Reminds him (and you) when doses are due. |
| **Where to go** (home, friend’s house, shelter) | Avoids staying in a place that feels unsafe. |
| **When to seek professional help** (e.g., thoughts of self‑harm) | Encourages timely intervention. |

If you’re not already working with a therapist or psychiatrist on the plan, ask for one. Even a brief review can give you a template to share with your son.

## 2. Decide on the Door‑Lock Policy

### Option A – *Locked, but Accessible*
- **Use a lock with a keypad or a key that you keep.**  
  *Pros*: He can still get in if he’s in a crisis but you maintain some control.  
  *Cons*: If he forgets the code or key, it could delay help.  
- **Place a spare key or code with a trusted neighbor or friend who can help in an emergency.**  
  *Pros*: You’re not the only gatekeeper.  
  *Cons*: Requires you to identify a reliable person and coordinate.

### Option B – *Unlocked, but with Boundaries*
- **Keep the door unlocked but put a “crisis phone” on the counter** (a phone with a dedicated contact list).  
  *Pros*: Immediate access for your son.  
  *Cons*: You can still set a time window (e.g., 7 am–9 pm) for “normal” calls and a separate number for 3 am “screams.”  
- **Use a “Do Not Disturb” system** (e.g., a label on the door that says “Emergency only”).  
  *Pros*: He knows when he can reach you.  
  *Cons*: Might feel too restrictive.

### What to Do in the Short Term
- **Keep the door unlocked until you have a plan** (if you’re worried about immediate safety).  
- **Tell your son you’ll have a lock after you both agree on a safety plan.**  
  This gives him concrete reassurance that you’re not abandoning him, while also moving toward a more secure arrangement.

## 3. Strengthen Your *Communication Window*

- **Set a “circuit” for calls**: e.g., “I’ll be available from 7 am–9 pm. If you’re calling after that, I’ll call my crisis line and then you can come to me.”  
- **Provide a “hotline” number** on your phone or in a visible place: 988 (U.S. Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or your local crisis line.  
- **Offer a “buddy” system**: If he’s on medication and feeling unstable, he could text a friend or neighbor first to confirm it’s safe to come home.
