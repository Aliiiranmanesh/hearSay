## 3️⃣ How to Keep All Learners Engaged  

| Strategy | Why it works for mixed ability | Quick implementation |
|----------|--------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Chunk the jargon** – Introduce a term, give a one‑sentence definition, then *immediately* use it in a concrete example. | Prevents the “lost in terminology” trap. | Example: “Militarism = a country’s obsession with building a big army. Germany built a huge navy to rival Britain.” |
| **Visual anchors** – Every slide has a picture, map, or diagram. | Visuals support English‑language learners and those who process info better graphically. | Use free images from Wikimedia (public domain) and label them clearly. |
| **“Think‑pair‑share” micro‑pause** – After slide 5 ask: *“Turn to your neighbour: Which cause do you think was most important and why?”* (30 s). | Gives lower‑level students time to formulate ideas, while higher‑level students can deepen analysis. | Keep it brief; you can collect a few quick responses. |
| **Use a story hook** – Begin with the headline “*Assassination in Sarajevo!*” and ask: *“What would you do if your country’s leader were killed?”* | Stories tap emotions and make abstract causes feel personal. | No extra prep needed; just a rhetorical question. |
| **Provide a handout** – One‑page “Four Cs + Spark” cheat sheet. | Allows students to follow along without writing everything down, and serves as a revision tool. | Print on cheap paper; hand out after the intro or at the start. |

| Tip | How to do it |
