### ⏱️ Minute-by-Minute Structure

**0:00–2:00 | Hook & Roadmap**

- **Give a 10-second roadmap:** `We’ll look at four long-term forces (Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism), then see how the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand ignited them.`

**2:00–10:00 | The Four Pillars (MAIN) – ~2 min each**
Use one slide per cause. Keep text minimal (3–4 bullet points max). Pair each with a simple visual.

| Cause | Core Idea | Example/Visual | Differentiation Tip |
|-------|-----------|----------------|---------------------|
| **Militarism** | Arms race & war planning | Graph of pre-war military spending or a map of conscription zones | Define "conscription" & "Schlieffen Plan" in plain terms. For advanced students: note how war plans reduced diplomatic flexibility. |
| **Alliances** | Defensive pacts became a domino system | Clear Venn or chain diagram (Triple Alliance vs. Triple Entente) | Use an analogy: `"Alliances were meant for safety, but they worked like group chats that auto-forwarded a crisis."` |

**12:00–14:00 | Synthesis & Takeaway**

- **1-sentence modern link:** `Understanding how interconnected systems amplify small crises remains relevant to diplomacy today.`
- End with a clear closing line, not a new idea.

**14:00–15:00 | Quick Check or Q&A**
- Instead of open Q&A (which can run long), ask one targeted question: `"Which of the four MAIN causes do you think was most responsible for turning a regional conflict into a continental war, and why?"`
- Take 1–2 brief responses, then close on time. Offer to chat after class or share a 1-page summary for anyone who wants to dig deeper.

### 🎯 Tips for a Mixed-Level Audience
- **Scaffold vocabulary:** Define 3–4 key terms upfront (mobilization, ultimatum, nationalism vs. imperialism). Put them on a slide that stays visible in the corner or on a handout.
- **Use consistent color-coding:** Assign each MAIN cause a color and use it across slides, diagrams, and your spoken transitions. This helps visual learners track the argument.
- **Pacing guardrails:** Practice with a timer. If you’re consistently over, trim examples, not concepts. Under 15 mins, you can add a 30-second historiography note (e.g., `"Historians still debate whether Germany bears primary responsibility or if it was a shared European failure."`) for advanced students.
- **Delivery:** Pause after each cause. Ask a quick comprehension check (`"Thumbs up if the alliance domino effect makes sense, thumbs sideways if you want me to clarify."`) This keeps lower-level students anchored without slowing down.
