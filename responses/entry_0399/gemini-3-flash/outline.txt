For a 15-minute presentation to a mixed-level class, the best approach is to use a **clear framework** that higher-level students can analyze deeply, while lower-level students can follow the basic "story."

The most effective structure for the causes of WWI is the **M.A.I.N.** acronym. Here is a suggested breakdown:

### Presentation Structure (15 Minutes)

#### 1. Introduction (2 Minutes)
*   **The Hook:** Start with a map of Europe in 1914. Mention that while the world seemed "peaceful" on the surface, it was actually a "powder keg" waiting for a spark.
*   **The Thesis:** Explain that the war wasn’t caused by just one event, but by a combination of four long-term tensions (M.A.I.N.) and one immediate trigger.

#### 2. The Long-Term Causes: M.A.I.N. (8 Minutes)
*   **Militarism (2 mins):** Explain the arms race. Countries were competing to have the biggest armies and navies (especially Britain and Germany). *Mixed-level tip: Use a picture of a "Dreadnought" battleship to show how expensive and scary this race was.*
*   **Alliances (2 mins):** Explain the "Friendship Chain." If one country got attacked, their friends had to join. This turned a small fight into a world war. *Visual: A simple diagram of the Triple Entente vs. the Triple Alliance.*
*   **Imperialism (2 mins):** European countries were fighting over colonies in Africa and Asia. This created jealousy and friction.
*   **Nationalism (2 mins):** This is the "desire to be your own country." Focus on the Balkans (the "Powder Keg of Europe"), where people wanted independence from the Austro-Hungarian Empire.

#### 3. The Spark: The Assassination (3 Minutes)
*   **The Event:** June 28, 1914. Archduke Franz Ferdinand (heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne) is killed in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip.
*   **The Domino Effect:** Explain how this one death triggered the **Alliances** mentioned earlier. Within weeks, all of Europe was at war.

#### 4. The Dutch Context (optional but good for your class) (1 Minute)
*   Since you are in a Dutch school, briefly mention that the **Netherlands remained neutral** during WWI. This adds a local connection that your teachers will appreciate.

#### 5. Conclusion and Q&A (1 Minute)
*   Summarize: WWI was the result of decades of tension that finally exploded.
*   Invite one or two questions.

### Tips for a Mixed-Level Class:
*   **Visuals over Text:** Use lots of maps and political cartoons. Lower-level students can understand a cartoon's meaning even if they struggle with complex historical terms.
*   **The "So What?" Factor:** For the higher-level students, ask a rhetorical question: *"Do you think the war would have happened anyway if the Archduke hadn't been killed?"* This encourages critical thinking.
*   **Vocabulary:** Provide a small "word bank" on your first slide (e.g., *Bondgenootschap* / Alliance, *Vloot* / Fleet) to help everyone follow along.
