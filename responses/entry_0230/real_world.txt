Affected Groups: People living under assumed identities in witness protection programs in the United States, United Kingdom, and similar programs globally, who have successfully integrated into new communities and navigated the practical requirements of a constructed identity, but who experience the structural loneliness of a life in which no one in their current environment knows who they actually are, and for whom the question of whether genuine intimacy is possible inside permanent concealment is not theoretical but the actual condition of their daily existence.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Witness protection programs require the protected person to sever contact with prior identity including family, friends, and any person who knows their original identity, as a security requirement. Disclosure of the program or original identity to anyone in the new community is a protocol violation that can result in removal from the program and loss of protection. The practical mechanism trapping them is the security logic of the program itself: the protection depends on the concealment, and the concealment precludes the kind of relational depth in which the actual self can be known.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: It was fine. It is always fine and it is never fine. The practiced answer to where are you from works every time and it is a perfect performance and it produces nothing he can build on. He would like to have one person who knows the actual answer to that question, which is the minimal formulation of the need for intimate recognition that the program's security logic makes structurally impossible.
