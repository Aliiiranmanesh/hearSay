I’m so sorry. What you’re describing sounds like a very particular kind of grief: not only the diagnosis, but the loneliness of carrying it in silence, and the shock of being treated differently even when no one is brave enough to say the thing out loud.

People survive this by finding *somewhere* the truth can exist safely.

Not necessarily everywhere. Not necessarily with family, or your employer, or even your closest friend yet. But somewhere.

A few things that may help:

- **Give the grief a name privately first.** You do not have to say it to anyone else before you can say: “I am grieving the life I thought I had,” or “I am grieving how people may see me,” or “I am grieving trust.” Writing it down can help when speaking feels impossible.
