## Step‑by‑Step Instructions  

1. **Prep the basics**  
   * Rinse the rice under cold water until the water runs clear – this removes excess starch and helps the rice stay separate.  
   * Peel and dice the carrot and potato into **tiny ½‑cm cubes**; the smaller they are, the quicker they soften and the smoother the final texture.  

2. **Sauté aromatics (2 min)**  
   * Heat the olive oil (or a small dab of ghee) in a medium saucepan over medium‑low heat.  
   * Add the chopped onion and a pinch of salt; stir until the onion becomes translucent (about 1 min).  
   * Toss in the minced garlic (if using) and stir for another 30 seconds—just enough to release aroma, not to brown.

3. **Build the soup base (5 min)**  
   * Add the diced carrot and potato to the pan; stir for 1 minute so they coat with the oil.  
   * Pour in the 2 cups of water or chicken broth. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to a simmer.  

4. **Add rice and spices (10 min)**  
   * Stir in the rinsed rice, the pinch of cumin, optional cardamom, and turmeric.  
   * Let the mixture simmer, partially covered, for **10‑12 minutes**, or until the rice is **very soft** and the vegetables are tender. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking.

5. **Integrate the chicken (2 min)**  
   * While the rice is cooking, **shred** the cooked chicken into tiny strips (the size of a pea).  
   * When the rice is almost done, add the shredded chicken and let it warm through (about 2 minutes).  

6. **Blend (optional but recommended for picky eaters)**  
   * For a smoother, “porridge‑like” consistency, use an immersion blender **just for a few seconds**—you want a creamy soup with tiny rice grains, not a full puree.  
   * If the child prefers a bit more texture, skip blending and simply mash a few spoonfuls of carrot‑potato with the back of a ladle.

7. **Final seasoning**  
   * Taste quickly; add **a pinch more salt** if needed, but keep it mild.  

8. **Serve with a swirl of yogurt**  
   * Ladle the hot soup into a child‑size bowl.  
   * Drop 1‑2 Tbsp of plain yogurt or labneh on top and swirl gently. The cool yogurt adds creaminess, a little tang, and extra protein without overwhelming the palate.

9. **Make it inviting**  
   * If you have a few fresh parsley leaves, place a tiny green leaf on top for a splash of colour (many kids are attracted to bright spots).  
   * Serve warm, not hot—test the temperature before giving it to the child.

## Quick Tips for a Fussy Eater  

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|------------|
| **Child refuses the soup because it looks “plain.”** | Sprinkle a few **tiny raisins** (soaked in warm water until soft) or **chopped dates** on top – sweet, familiar Saudi flavors that also add calories. |
| **Soup is too thick or too thin.** | If too thick, add a splash of warm water or extra broth. If too thin, let it simmer uncovered for a few minutes; the rice will absorb liquid and thicken. |
| **The child dislikes the smell of cumin.** | Omit cumin entirely; the soup will still be gentle and tasty thanks to the chicken and yogurt. |
| **You only have a little rice left.** | Substitute **crushed “maftoul” (small wheat pearls)** or **quick‑cooking couscous** – they cook in 5‑7 minutes and give a similar soft texture. |
| **No chicken on hand.** | Use **ground beef** (very finely crumbled) or **eggs** (soft‑boiled, chopped) for protein. The cooking method stays the same. |
