## 1. Immediate, Temporary Fixes (A Few Hours)

| What you can do | How it works | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Topical Brimonidine Gel (Mirvaso)** | FDA‑approved for facial redness; blocks the alpha‑adrenergic receptor that causes blood vessels to dilate. | Apply a thin layer to the affected skin 10–15 min before you leave.  It can keep redness down for ~8 h.  (Prescription required – ask your dermatologist or primary doctor.) |
| **Green‑Tinted Color Corrector / Concealer** | Green cancels out red (the opposite color on the spectrum). | Use a small brush or sponge to apply a thin layer of a green‑tinted concealer over the redness, then blot lightly with a clean pad.  Follow with your normal foundation. |
| **Cold Compress / Ice Pack** | Cold causes vasoconstriction, temporarily tightening blood vessels. | Wrap a few ice cubes in a clean cloth and press gently on the affected area for 1–2 min.  Repeat as needed before you head out. |
| **Hydrocortisone 1 % Cream** | Mild steroid that reduces inflammation quickly. | Apply a pea‑size amount once a day (usually in the evening).  Do *not* use it on the face more than 2–3 weeks total, as it can thin the skin. |

> **Tip:** Layering works well.  Start with the green corrector, then apply your foundation, finish with a translucent powder, and, if you have time, a light dusting of a green‑tinted setting powder (many makeup brands carry “color‑correcting” primers).

## 2. Daily Routine to Keep Redness Low (Longer‑Term)

| Habit | Why it helps | How to incorporate it |
|---|---|---|
| **Gentle Cleansing** | Harsh soaps can irritate and flare redness. | Use a fragrance‑free, non‑foaming cleanser twice daily. |
| **Moisturize with a Non‑Comedogenic Barrier** | A good barrier keeps skin hydrated and less reactive. | Apply a lightweight, oil‑free moisturizer (look for ceramides or niacinamide). |
| **Sun Protection** | UV light can worsen redness and trigger flare‑ups. | Use a broad‑spectrum SPF 30+ daily; consider a mineral sunscreen with zinc oxide/iron oxides. |
| **Trigger Avoidance** | Hot drinks, alcohol, spicy foods, extreme temperatures, and some skincare ingredients (exfoliants, alcohol) can cause flare‑ups. | Notice patterns; keep a simple log of foods and weather that precede flare‑ups. |
| **Topical Niacinamide (5–10 %)** | Improves barrier function & reduces redness over time. | Apply 2–3 × daily in the morning and/or night. |
| **Topical Azelaic Acid (15–20 %)** | Helps with redness, acne, and hyperpigmentation. | Use at night; start with 1–2 × weekly and build up as tolerated. |
| **Topical Ivermectin (1 %)** | FDA‑approved for rosacea; reduces inflammation and redness. | Apply nightly if prescribed. |

> **If you’re already on a prescription regimen** (e.g., topical ivermectin, metronidazole gel, doxycycline), keep it consistent.  The temporary fixes above are meant to “boost” the effect for the interview day.
