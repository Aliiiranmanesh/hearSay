### 🔹 Next 72 Hours: Stabilize & Document
1. **Talk to your landlord immediately.** Silence breeds eviction. Explain what happened, show any proof you have (photos, witness contacts, past rent records), and ask for a 1–2 week extension or a payment plan. Many landlords prefer communication over legal action.
2. **Get the confiscation in writing.** Ask the municipal office, police station, or market authority for an official receipt, case number, or seizure notice. If they refuse, write down everything you remember: date, time, officer names/badge numbers, vehicle plates, what was taken, and any witnesses. This is critical for any appeal or legal aid.
3. **Secure basics without draining cash.** Use community food banks, religious centers, or mutual aid networks. Preserve every possible dollar for rent and immediate next steps.

4. **Find out what permit they cited.** Visit the local municipal office, check their website, or ask a vendors’ association. Sometimes it’s a new bylaw, sometimes it’s selectively enforced, sometimes it doesn’t legally apply to your setup. Knowledge is leverage.

### 🔹 Rebuilding: From Survival to Stability
7. **Start micro, not massive.** Borrow or rent a small table, use a backpack, or partner with a shop that lets you display items for a cut. Begin with low-cost, fast-turnover goods you already know sell.
8. **Reconnect with other vendors.** They scattered out of fear, but they’re in the same boat. Form or join a vendors’ group. Shared information about permits, safe locations, and legal rights prevents future raids and builds collective bargaining power.
9. **Formalize gradually.** Once stable, apply for the required permit, explore licensed market stalls, or join a vendor cooperative. Paying for compliance is frustrating, but it’s often the difference between vulnerability and protection.
10. **Track everything.** Sales, expenses, customer preferences, enforcement encounters. This becomes your business record, your permit application evidence, and your roadmap forward.
