Affected Groups: Street vendors and informal market traders in Lagos, Nairobi, Accra, and other West and East African cities who are subjected to periodic enforcement raids by municipal police or task forces that confiscate their goods and equipment under permit requirements that are selectively enforced, non-transparent, and sometimes newly invented at the moment of enforcement, and who lose their entire inventory and working capital in a single encounter and must rebuild from zero. Disproportionately affecting vendors who have invested in equipment and stock over time and whose financial situation has no buffer below the level of the inventory itself, vendors in locations that have been previously tolerant and where the enforcement represents a change in policy or a new officer's interpretation rather than a consistently applied rule, and vendors for whom the goods taken represent months of accumulated savings and whose rent timeline has not moved. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Street vending regulation in Lagos is administered through the Lagos State Government and the Lagos State Market Development Board, with permit requirements that have been inconsistently defined, periodically revised, and selectively enforced. The permit requirement that the officer cited may or may not exist in the form described, and may or may not have been previously communicated to vendors in the area. The accountability mechanism for seized goods is in practice very limited, and the formal process for recovering seized goods or compensation requires navigating the same institutional relationships that produced the seizure. The officer's face is known and the relationship is real and none of it mattered, which is the description of informal enforcement that operates outside the relational norms that informal settings normally produce. Devastating Real-World Consequence: They took the cart, the generator, the phone cases, everything. Two months of savings to buy that stock. The rent is due in eight days. The question of where someone starts again from nothing when nothing is what they took is both a practical question about emergency financial resources including microfinance, market association emergency funds, and social network support, and a question about how to rebuild a livelihood when the capital base has been removed by the state.
