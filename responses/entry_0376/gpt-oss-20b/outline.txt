### 1.  Decide on a legal structure

| **Option** | **Pros** | **Cons** |
|------------|----------|----------|
| **Sole Proprietorship** | Simple to set up, no annual filing, profits taxed as personal income. | No liability protection. |
| **Private Limited Company (P.L.C.)** | Liability limited to share capital; easier to attract investment. | More paperwork, higher fees. |
| **Co‑operative** | Shared ownership; collective bargaining power. | Requires membership agreement, governance rules. |

*Tip:* For a one‑person operation, a **sole proprietorship** is usually the fastest route.

### 2.  Register with the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA)

1. **Obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)** – free and can be done at any GRA office.
2. **Register as a sole proprietor** – fill out the SA 1 form (or use the online portal).  
   *You’ll receive a Business Registration Number (BRN).*
3. **Apply for VAT** if your annual turnover exceeds the threshold (currently GHS 27,500).  
   *If you’re below the threshold, you can still register voluntarily for VAT to claim input tax.*

### 3.  Get an operating license

1. **Trade License** – apply at the Accra Metropolitan Assembly (AMA) or the relevant local authority.  
   *You’ll need a business name and a description of your activities.*
2. **Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) clearance** – especially if you handle hazardous waste.  

### 4.  Join a waste‑management association or cooperative

* **Benefits**: collective bargaining, shared equipment, joint marketing, access to training.  
* **Examples** in Accra: *Accra Waste Management Organization (AWMO)*, *Recycling Cooperatives of Ghana (RCG)*.

### 5.  Enroll in social protection schemes

| **Accident Insurance** | Private insurers offer commercial accident plans. | Compensation for work‑related injuries. |

### 6.  Keep proper records

## 4.  Quick “yes/no” answer to your daughter’s question

**“Do I have a boss?”**  
- **If you are registered as a sole proprietor or a co‑operative member,** you are your own boss.  
- **If you are still unregistered,** technically you have no boss – but you also have no employer‑employee relationship, which is why you can’t claim benefits or legal protections.

**“Who do I work for?”**  
- **If you’re registered,** you work *for yourself* (as a business owner).  
- **If you’re not registered,** you work *for the market* – the people who pay you for the service you provide. That’s why it’s called “informal” or “unregistered” – the state has no record of your employment.
