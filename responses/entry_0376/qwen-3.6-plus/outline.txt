### The Difference: Recognition vs. Erasure

**Being Self-Employed** means you are a recognized economic actor. Even if you have no employees, you are a business. In a functioning system, this means:
*   **Legal Identity:** You can register, open a business account, and access credit based on your cash flow.
*   **Rights:** You have the right to organize, to negotiate prices with buyers, and to operate without harassment.
*   **Protection:** If you are injured, there are mechanisms for insurance or compensation. If the market shifts, there are safety nets or retraining programs.
*   **Voice:** When the city makes a "recycling strategy," you are at the table. Your expertise is treated as data, not as a ghost.

**"Not Existing as a Worker"** means you are treated as a resource to be extracted, not a person to be partnered with. The city benefits from your labor, but the system is designed as if you aren't there. This means:
*   **Precarity:** You bear all the risk. If prices for cardboard drop, you starve. If you get sick, you lose income.
*   **Invisibility in Policy:** The city's strategy "works because of you" but "doesn't mention you." This is the definition of exploitation. They are using your free or underpaid labor to meet their recycling targets while avoiding the cost of integrating, protecting, or paying you fairly.
*   **Vulnerability:** Without recognition, you are subject to police harassment, eviction from sorting areas, and price manipulation by middlemen who know you have no legal recourse.
*   **Social Stigma:** Your work is essential, but because it is invisible to the state, it is often looked down upon by society.

### Does It Matter Practically?

**Yes. It matters for your survival, your health, and your daughter's future.**

1.  **Economic Security:** A recognized self-employed recycler can build a credit history. This means loans to buy a better cart, a scale, or a small storage space. It means the ability to negotiate directly with recycling plants rather than being forced to sell to predatory aggregators.
2.  **Health and Safety:** Waste picking is hazardous. If you exist as a worker, you have a claim to Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), vaccinations, and healthcare. If you don't exist, a cut from glass or a respiratory illness is a personal tragedy that can wipe out your family's savings.
3.  **The "Boss" Question:** Practically, when you have a boss (or a recognized contract), there is a limit to what can be demanded of you. When you are "self-employed" but invisible, the market is your boss, and it has no mercy. The city can change collection routes, ban informal sorting, or introduce trucks that undercut you, all without consultation.
4.  **Dignity and Legacy:** This is what your daughter is sensing. When a child asks, "Who do you work for?" they are asking, "Where do you fit in the world? Does the world value you?" If the answer is "I don't exist to them," it teaches a child that their parent's labor is worthless. If the answer is "I am a recycler, I provide a service the city relies on, and I am fighting to be recognized," it teaches pride and resilience.

You said the strategy works because of people like you but doesn't mention you. This is common in Accra and many cities in the Global South. It is a form of **greenwashing**. The city gets the environmental benefits of high recycling rates without the political or financial cost of formalizing the workforce.

A strategy that doesn't mention you is a strategy built on sand. It assumes you will always be there, cheap and compliant. But you know the neighborhood better than the trucks. If you and others like you stopped, the strategy would collapse in a week.

The difference between "self-employed" and "not existing" is the difference between **informality** and **invisibility**.
