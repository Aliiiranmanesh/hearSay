## 3. Simple “Stretch‑Your‑Flour” Recipes  

### A. **Super‑Thin Chapatis (One‑Pan Flatbreads)**
1. **Mix** 1 cup flour with ⅓ cup water, a pinch of salt, and 1 tsp oil.  
2. **Add** ¼ cup mashed beans or potatoes. Knead until smooth (you may need a little extra water).  
3. **Roll** the dough into very thin circles (≈ 10 cm diameter). The thinner the circle, the less flour per bite.  
4. **Cook** on a hot pan 30 sec each side; brush lightly with oil if you have it.  

*Result:* A larger number of flatbreads that are still filling because of the added bean/potato bulk.

---

### B. **Hearty Vegetable‑Flour Porridge**
1. **Boil** 2 cups water (or broth) with a handful of chopped carrots, cabbage, or pumpkin.  
2. **Stir in** ½ cup flour gradually, whisking to avoid lumps.  
3. **Add** ¼ cup powdered milk or a spoon‑ful of peanut butter for richness.  
4. **Simmer** 5‑7 min, stirring constantly, until thick.  

*Result:* A thick, warming porridge that serves 4‑5 people with only ½ cup flour.

---

### C. **Thick‑Soup Slurry with Cornmeal**
1. **Prepare** a base soup (vegetable broth, lentils, or any stew you already have).  
2. **Combine** 2 Tbsp flour with 4 Tbsp cold water (slurry).  
3. **Add** the slurry to the soup, stir, then **sprinkle in 2 Tbsp cornmeal** (or crushed rice).  
4. **Cook** another 5 min; the cornmeal will absorb liquid and thicken the soup further, letting you use less flour.  

*Result:* A fuller‑bodied soup that feeds more bowls.

## 4. Cooking‑Method Hacks to Make Flour Go Further  

| Hack | How to do it | What it saves |
|------|--------------|---------------|
| **“Water‑first” dough** | Start with a larger amount of water (or broth) and add flour gradually until you reach a workable consistency. | Less flour needed to achieve the right texture. |
| **“Layer‑and‑fold”** (for flatbreads) | Roll a thin sheet, brush with a little oil, fold, and roll again. This creates layers that feel more substantial. | Same amount of flour gives a “fluffier” bite. |
| **“Partial Fry”** | Cook the dough just enough to set it, then finish it in a covered pot with a splash of broth or water. The steam expands the bread. | Reduces the amount of oil needed and gives a larger final product. |
| **“Mix‑in‑bulk”** | Whenever you have a batch of flour, immediately mix in a bulk‑adding ingredient (mashed veg, beans, etc.) before cooking. | Prevents you from using a whole batch of plain flour for a small dish. |

## 5. Quick Checklist Before You Start

1. **Inventory:** Count how much flour you have and note any bulking ingredients (beans, potatoes, veggies, peanuts, cornmeal, etc.).  
2. **Portion Plan:** Decide how many people you need to feed and aim for a **target flour‑per‑person** (e.g., 30 g per adult for flatbread).  
3. **Add‑in Ratio:** For every cup of flour, plan to add **½‑1 cup** of a bulking ingredient.  
4. **Taste Test:** A small test batch helps you gauge texture and flavor before scaling up.  
5. **Cook in One Pot:** Whenever possible, use a single pot or pan to avoid extra water or oil loss.
