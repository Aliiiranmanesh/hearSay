### How to stretch a small amount of flour

When you have more mouths to feed than food, the goal is to increase the **volume** and the **satiety** (the feeling of being full) of the meal.

1.  **Make "Uji" (Thin Porridge):**
    This is the most effective way to stretch flour. Instead of making a solid bread or "ugali," whisk the flour into boiling water to make a thin porridge. The water adds volume to the stomach, and if you have even a tiny bit of sugar or salt, it makes it more palatable. It hydrates everyone while providing calories.

2.  **Make "Dampers" or Dumplings in Soup:**
    If you have any watery soup or even just boiling water with some salt/herbs, mix the flour with a tiny bit of water to make a thick, stiff dough. Drop small, marble-sized pieces into the boiling liquid. They will swell up as they cook. This makes the "bites" of food feel more substantial than just drinking the flour in water.

3.  **Very Thin Flatbreads (Chapati style):**
    If you have a small amount of oil, roll the dough as thin as paper. The psychological effect of eating a "whole" piece of bread, even if it is very thin, is often more satisfying than a small chunk of thick bread.

4.  **Use Fillers:**
    If you can find any edible greens (even wild ones that are safe to eat) or if your neighbor has any vegetable scraps, chop them very finely and knead them directly into the dough. This adds bulk and fiber to the flour.

### How to address the system error in Kakuma

The fact that you have your paper is your strongest tool. Do not let go of that original paper, but if you can, **take a clear photo of it with a phone** (yours or a trusted neighbor's) so you have a backup.

Here is what you should try next to get back on the food/cash list:

**1. Go to the "Protection Desk"**
Do not just go back to the standard food distribution line. Look for the **UNHCR Protection Desk** or the **Help Desk** specifically designated for "Registration Issues." The people at the food distribution desks are often just clerks who cannot change the database; you need a "Data Management" or "Protection" officer.

**2. Seek out the NRC (Norwegian Refugee Council)**
In Kakuma, the NRC often provides legal assistance and helps refugees resolve documentation and registration issues. They can act as an advocate for you when the system is failing.

**3. Speak to your Zonal Leader or Community Elder**
The "woman at the desk" may listen more closely if a community leader accompanies you. They often have direct lines of communication to the Refugee Affairs Secretariat (RAS) or UNHCR camp managers to report "ghost" cases where people have dropped off the system.

**4. Request a "Manual Verification"**
When you speak to an official, use these words: *"I am a registered refugee with valid documentation, but there is a synchronization error between my physical manifest and the digital database. I need a manual verification to update my biometric profile."*

**5. The WFP (World Food Programme) Help Desk**
Since the food and cash run through the system, the WFP usually has a specific desk during distribution days to handle "complaints and feedback." Look for the desk with the WFP logo and show them your paper.

It is exhausting to fight for what you are already owed, but please keep that paper safe—it is the bridge back into the system. In the meantime, the arrangement you have with your neighbor is a testament to the strength of the community in the camp. Keep supporting each other.
