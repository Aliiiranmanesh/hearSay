Affected Groups: Married women across Bangladesh — particularly in rural divisions including Rajshahi, Rangpur, Mymensingh, and Sylhet — for whom marital rape is not recognized as a legal category and therefore does not exist as a harm the state is equipped to acknowledge, investigate, or prosecute. Disproportionately affects women married young, often below the legal minimum age through the persistent operation of child marriage despite formal prohibition, women with no independent income or education who have no material basis for survival outside the marriage, women from deeply conservative communities where religious authority figures, family elders, and female community members collectively reinforce the understanding that a wife's body is a marital entitlement rather than a site of personal sovereignty, and women whose own mothers and grandmothers experienced the same thing and transmitted it forward not as trauma but as the basic terms of wifely existence — meaning the harm has no generational witness who can name it as harm because every available witness lived inside it too.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Section 375 of the Bangladesh Penal Code explicitly exempts marital rape from the legal definition of rape through a spousal exception clause inherited directly from British colonial law and never removed, meaning the act is not merely unprosecuted but legally classified as something other than the crime it would be if committed by any other person. A woman cannot file a police report for an act the law does not recognize as a crime. She cannot access a legal aid lawyer for a harm that has no legal existence. She cannot approach a court for a protection order against conduct that the same legal system has pre-emptively defined as her husband's right.
Shalish — the informal village arbitration system administered by male community elders — is the first and often only dispute resolution mechanism available in rural areas, and it operates from a framework that treats marital sexuality as a matter of family privacy, regularly instructs women to be more accommodating, and has no conceptual category for the complaint she is bringing because the complaint requires a premise — that she has sexual autonomy within marriage — that the system does not recognize as a valid premise. Religious authority structures including local imams reinforce the same framework through Quranic interpretations that emphasize wifely obedience and frame sexual availability as a religious obligation, giving the harm a sacred justification that operates above both law and community arbitration.
National and international NGOs operating domestic violence helplines and shelter networks in Bangladesh address physical violence and can sometimes intervene in cases of severe battery, but marital rape as a standalone harm falls outside their operational frameworks because it falls outside the legal framework their interventions are designed to work within — meaning even the organizations theoretically positioned to help her are institutionally unable to name what is happening to her as the specific thing it is.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: A twenty-four year old woman in rural Mymensingh has been married for six years and has three children and has never once in those six years understood what is happening to her at night as something with a name that belongs to the category of wrong. She was told before the marriage by her mother that this is what marriage is. She was told after the marriage by her mother-in-law that a good wife does not refuse. She was told by the imam at a community gathering that a wife's refusal is a sin. She has never encountered a single voice in her physical world that has offered her a different frame, and the absence of that frame is not experienced as oppression but as the basic texture of reality, because a harm that has never been named in any language available to you is not experienced as harm but as weather — as the neutral condition of the life you are living.
What has accumulated in her is not rage, because rage requires a target and a target requires a wrong and a wrong requires a framework for wrongness that she has not been given. What has accumulated is a formless and unaddressed exhaustion and a specific bodily knowledge that something is being taken from her on a recurring basis that she does not know how to refuse and has never been told she is allowed to refuse and would not know where to go with the refusal if she had it. She is twenty-four years old and she does not know that what is happening to her has a name in other languages. She does not know that in other places it is called a crime. She has never been given the single most basic instrument of resistance, which is the knowledge that there is something to resist, and the system surrounding her — legal, religious, familial, communal — has withheld that instrument with a thoroughness so complete that it does not feel like withholding. It feels like the truth.
