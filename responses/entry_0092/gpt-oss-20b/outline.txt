Affected Groups: Married women across Bangladesh — particularly in rural divisions including Rajshahi, Rangpur, Mymensingh, and Sylhet — for whom marital rape is not recognized as a legal category and therefore does not exist as a harm the state is equipped to acknowledge, investigate, or prosecute. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Section 375 of the Bangladesh Penal Code explicitly exempts marital rape from the legal definition of rape through a spousal exception clause inherited directly from British colonial law and never removed, meaning the act is not merely unprosecuted but legally classified as something other than the crime it would be if committed by any other person. A woman cannot file a police report for an act the law does not recognize as a crime. She cannot access a legal aid lawyer for a harm that has no legal existence. Shalish — the informal village arbitration system administered by male community elders — is the first and often only dispute resolution mechanism available in rural areas, and it operates from a framework that treats marital sexuality as a matter of family privacy, regularly instructs women to be more accommodating, and has no conceptual category for the complaint she is bringing because the complaint requires a premise — that she has sexual autonomy within marriage — that the system does not recognize as a valid premise. Religious authority structures including local imams reinforce the same framework through Quranic interpretations that emphasize wifely obedience and frame sexual availability as a religious obligation, giving the harm a sacred justification that operates above both law and community arbitration.
National and international NGOs operating domestic violence helplines and shelter networks in Bangladesh address physical violence and can sometimes intervene in cases of severe battery, but marital rape as a standalone harm falls outside their operational frameworks because it falls outside the legal framework their interventions are designed to work within — meaning even the organizations theoretically positioned to help her are institutionally unable to name what is happening to her as the specific thing it is.
