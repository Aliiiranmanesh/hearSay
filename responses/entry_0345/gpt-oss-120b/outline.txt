## 1. Pause and Ground Yourself

**Why:** The fact that you’ve been holding the swab for two weeks already shows you’re giving this decision the space it deserves. Before you act, give yourself a brief, intentional pause to check in with how you’re feeling right now.

**How:**
- **Body scan:** Close your eyes (if you can) and notice any tension—tight shoulders, clenched jaw, rapid breathing. Take a few slow, deep breaths, letting each exhale release a little of that tension.
- **Name the emotions:** Put words to what’s inside (“I feel scared, hopeful, angry, curious, exhausted”). Naming them reduces their power.
- **Mini‑journal:** Write a sentence or two about what’s most present for you in this moment. Sometimes seeing it on paper clarifies the intensity of each feeling.

## 3. Gather Information (Without Pressure)

**What you might want to know:**
- **What the DNA test will actually tell you.** Most agencies can confirm a match to a relative’s sample, but they may not reveal the full story (e.g., the circumstances of death, the identity of the perpetrator). Knowing the limits can reduce fear of the “unknown.”
- **How the results will be communicated.** Will you get a phone call, a letter, a meeting? Will you have an advocate present?
- **What support will be offered after you receive the results.** Some organizations partner with counseling services or legal aid; ask about those options now.

You can request this information in writing. Having a clear picture of the process can transform a vague, terrifying “what‑if” into a concrete series of steps you can prepare for.

## 5. Prepare for Both Possible Outcomes

### **If the DNA matches:**
- **Emotional impact:** You may feel relief, grief, anger, or a mixture of all three. It’s normal for the “joy of knowing” to sit alongside “pain of loss.”
- **Next steps:** The organization may ask you to participate in memorial activities, testify, or simply receive information about the burial site. Decide ahead of time what level of involvement feels right for you. You can say “yes” to some things and “no” to others.
- **Self‑care plan:** Identify calming activities that have helped you in the past (walks, music, art, meditation). Schedule them into the days following the notification.

### **If the DNA does **not** match:**
- **Emotional impact:** You might feel disappointment, confusion, or even a renewed sense of mystery. That can be painful, too.
- **Next steps:** You can ask the organization what other leads exist, or whether additional testing (e.g., broader family DNA databases) might be possible.
- **Self‑care plan:** Same as above—allow yourself to feel whatever comes up, and lean on your support network.

## 6. Give Yourself Permission to Change Your Mind

A decision you make today does not lock you into a permanent path. If you submit the swab and later feel you need a break before the results are processed, you can still:

- **Ask for a delay** in the analysis (some labs will hold the sample for a set period).
- **Request that the results be shared only with a designated person** (e.g., a therapist) first, so you’re not alone when you hear them.
- **Withdraw consent** if the organization’s policies allow it (most do, but it’s worth confirming).

Knowing you retain agency can lessen the fear that “once I send it, I’m stuck.”
