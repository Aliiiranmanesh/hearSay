There’s no “right” choice—only what aligns with your values, coping capacity, and what you believe will bring you the most peace in the long run.

## 6. If You Decide to Test

1. **Read the consent form carefully.** Highlight any points you’re unsure about.
2. **Ask questions**—to the organization, a doctor, or a counselor—about what the results will mean.
3. **Prepare emotionally:**
   - Take a few minutes of quiet breathing before you take the sample.
   - Write a short note to yourself: “I’m doing this because I want to know, not because I’m afraid.” This can help anchor your intention.
4. **Keep a “support plan” ready:**
   - List a trusted friend or family member you’ll call right after you receive the result.
   - If you’re likely to feel overwhelmed, set a time limit for how long you’ll sit with the news (e.g., 15–30 min, then step away).

---

## 7. After Receiving the Result

- **If it confirms what you hoped for**: Celebrate the closure, but also give yourself time to process the emotions that may arise.
- **If it turns out differently**: Remember that the fact is just data; the story you build around it is yours to shape. A counselor or support group can help you integrate the new information.
- **If the result is inconclusive**: Stay patient; sometimes it takes time for labs to get new samples, or for remains to be found.

Whatever you decide, practice a simple self‑care ritual after the decision or after receiving the result—a cup of tea, a short walk, or a favorite song. Small rituals can anchor you when the world feels uncertain.
