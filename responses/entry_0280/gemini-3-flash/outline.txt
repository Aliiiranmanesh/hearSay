I am so sorry you are going through this. Five days in a dark room is not just "being unwell"—it is a grueling physical and mental marathon. The isolation of an invisible illness is often as painful as the physical symptoms themselves. 

Because you have been in this state since Tuesday and your current toolkit isn't working, we need to address this in three stages: the immediate medical crisis, the workplace situation, and the long-term "functioning."

### 1. The Immediate Crisis (Medical)
If a migraine (which it sounds like you are describing) lasts more than 72 hours, it is medically classified as **status migrainosus**. 

*   **Seek Urgent Care/ER:** When you have "taken what you can take" and it hasn't broken, you likely need an IV "migraine cocktail" (usually a mix of hydration, anti-inflammatories, and anti-nausea meds) to reset your nervous system. 
*   **The "Rebound" Risk:** If you have been taking over-the-counter meds (like Excedrin or Ibuprofen) for five days straight, you may be trapped in a "medication overuse headache." The medicine itself is now keeping the pain cycle going. A doctor can help you break this cycle with a steroid taper or other prescription interventions.

### 2. The Workplace (The Report)
The guilt of the "invisible" illness is heavy, but you must protect your job with facts, not just apologies.

*   **Change the Language:** Stop saying "unwell." That sounds like a cold or a minor upset stomach. Use medical language: *"I am experiencing a severe neurological flare/migraine episode that has been unresponsive to treatment since Tuesday."*
*   **Medical Documentation:** Since this is the third time this quarter, you need a paper trail. If you go to Urgent Care today, get a doctor's note. 
*   **HR and FMLA (If in the US):** If you have been at your job for a year, look into FMLA (Family and Medical Leave Act) for "intermittent leave." ### 3. The "Invisible" Burden (The Sister vs. You)
You mentioned your sister’s surgery and the food people brought. There is a specific grief in having a chronic illness that doesn't come with a cast or a scar. People don't bring casseroles for migraines because they don't realize you are "suffering"—they think you are just "resting."

### 4. How to Function Well (Long-Term)
If this is happening three times a quarter, your current "reactive" plan (waiting in the dark) isn't enough to let you live your life. To function well, you likely need a shift in your medical strategy:

*   **See a Specialist:** If you aren't seeing a Neurologist or a Headache Specialist, make that your first goal when the pain breaks. 
*   **Preventatives vs. Abortives:** You are currently using "abortives" (trying to stop the pain once it starts). If you have frequent attacks, you need "preventatives" (daily medication or monthly injections like CGRP inhibitors) to stop the attacks from happening in the first place.
