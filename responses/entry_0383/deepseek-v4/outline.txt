Now, for your daughter’s shoes — a practical, loving act of care. Measuring a child’s foot without having her try on the shoes at the market stall is entirely possible with a little preparation at home. Here is a reliable way:

**1. Trace the foot on paper**
- Have your daughter stand on a piece of paper on a hard, flat floor. Weight should be evenly on both feet; standing gives a more accurate measurement than sitting.
- Use a pencil held straight up (not angled) and trace closely around her entire foot. Do this for both feet — most people have one foot slightly larger. You’ll use the larger measurement.

**2. Mark the length and width**
- On the tracing, mark the very back of the heel and the tip of the longest toe (this may not be the big toe; watch for a longer second toe).
- Draw a straight line between these two points and measure that line. This is the foot length.
- For width, mark the widest part of the foot (across the ball) and measure that distance.

**3. Add growing room**
- Children need space for their feet to slide forward when walking and for growth. Add **1 to 1.5 cm** (about a thumb’s width) to the measured foot length. For a market stall, where shoes may not come in half sizes, you can safely add up to 1.5 cm for closed shoes, or a little less for open sandals.
- The total (foot length + growing room) is the *insole length* you need. When you’re at the stall, you can check this by measuring the inside of the shoe.

**4. Make a portable “foot measure”**
Since you won’t have your daughter with you, create a tool to carry to the market:
- **String or ribbon:** Place a string along the line you drew for foot length plus the 1–1.5 cm extra. Cut the string to exactly that total length. At the stall, slide the string into the shoe and see if it lies flat from the back of the inside heel to the front without curling. If the string bends, the shoe is too short.
- **Paper cut-out:** Cut out the traced foot shape. Add a little extra length at the toes (the growing room) by taping a small strip of paper. Trim the sides to match the width. Take this “foot print” to the market and slip it inside any shoe you’re considering. It should fit without crumpling, and you should feel a small space beyond the toes.
- **Use a known object:** If you have no ruler, use a common item with a known length. For example, a Malawian 500 Kwacha note is roughly 12.5 cm long. You can measure the foot tracing against it to find the length in “notes” and then bring that knowledge with you.

**5. Check the stall’s shoes**
- Always measure the *inside* of the shoe, not the sole. Sellers often allow you to feel inside for seams and space.
- Press down on the toe area of the shoe after inserting your string or cut-out — there should be a finger’s width of room beyond the longest toe marking.
- Look at the width too: the tracing should not be squeezed at the sides.
