**A quick, reliable way to get a child’s shoe size when you can’t have her try shoes on**

> *This is a practical “market‑stall” method that works in any village or town, even when the stall owner has no measuring tape or shoe‑size chart.*

---

## 1. Gather the tools

| Tool | Why it helps |
|------|--------------|
| **Ruler or measuring tape (cm & inches)** | Gives an exact length. In most African markets a simple 30‑cm or 40‑cm ruler is enough. |
| **Piece of paper or thin cardboard** | Lets you trace the foot without moving the child. |
| **Pen or pencil** | To mark the outline. |
| **Stiff card or a small ruler to measure width** | Foot width matters for a comfortable fit. |
| **Optional: a small plastic cup or a shoebox** | To compare the measured length against a standard shoe size. |

If you’re in Blantyre, the local markets usually have a **"shoe size chart"** on a card or on the stall wall. These charts usually list **UK** or **US** sizes next to the foot length in centimeters.

---

## 2. The measuring routine

1. **Choose the right time**  
   *Feet swell a little by the end of the day. If you’re measuring in the morning, the child’s feet might be 5–10 mm shorter. If you’re late in the day, the measurement will be more realistic for a pair of shoes that will be worn. If you’re unsure, take the measurement twice and average the two.*

2. **Position the child**  
   *Have her stand upright on a flat surface (the floor or a wooden plank). Her weight should be balanced on both feet. The heel should be against a wall or a piece of cardboard.*

3. **Measure each foot**  
   *Even children have one foot slightly larger. Measure both and take the larger size.*

   - **Length**  
     1. Place the heel against the wall.  
     2. Using the ruler, measure from the heel to the tip of the longest toe.  
     3. Record the number in centimeters (cm) and inches (in) if you plan to convert.

   - **Width**  
     1. Identify the widest part of the foot (usually between the metatarsals).  
     2. Measure that span.

4. **Add the “growth margin”**  
   *Children grow fast—often 1–2 cm per year. Most shoe manufacturers recommend buying **½ to 1 size larger** than the last known size for a child. If you’re measuring for the first time and you’re not sure of her last size, you can add **1.0 cm** (≈0.4 in) to the measured length. This will give the toes room to move.*

5. **Cross‑check with a shoe size chart**  
   *Locate the foot‑length column on the stall’s chart. Find the nearest size that is at least as long as your measured length + growth margin. If the chart only lists UK or US sizes, use the conversion tables below.*

6. **Ask the vendor for confirmation**  
   *Show the vendor the measured length and the size you think matches. Many stall owners will have a quick “fit” check: put the child in a pair of that size and see if the toes have a small gap (about the width of a finger). If the child’s feet are still too tight or too loose, adjust accordingly.*

## 4. Things to watch for

| Issue | What to look for | How to fix |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| **The foot is too long** | Shoes feel loose, toes touch the front of the shoe. | Choose a smaller size or a narrower width. |
| **The foot is too short** | Shoes feel tight, especially around the toes or the ball of the foot. | Pick a larger size or a wider width. |
| **The foot is narrow** | The shoe’s arch and heel cups are too big. | Ask for “wide” shoes; if not available, choose a larger size to accommodate the width. |
| **The foot is wide** | The shoe feels cramped at the ball of the foot. | Ask for wide shoes or a larger size. |
| **The child has a foot problem (flat feet, bunions, etc.)** | If you notice any deformities, it’s worth getting a proper fit. | Seek a foot‑care professional or a larger retail store that offers fitting services. |

## 5. A quick “hand‑on” cheat sheet for the stall

1. **Measure length with ruler** → Record in cm.  
2. **Add 1 cm for growth** → New length to compare.  
3. **Match to chart** → Pick the smallest size that meets or exceeds the new length.  
4. **Confirm width** → Ask vendor if the shoe is “wide” or “regular.”  
5. **Try on** (if possible) → Check the toe space and heel fit.  
6. **Buy** → If everything looks good, grab the pair.

## 6. A word of caution

- **Shoe sizes in Zimbabwe are usually UK sizes.**  
- **If you’re buying in a foreign currency or a stall with no chart, bring a small ruler or measuring tape—most vendors will let you use it.**  
- **If the stall owner hesitates, show the child’s foot on a sheet of paper (you’ve traced it).** Many vendors will trust a visual measurement.

### Final tip

When you bring the shoes home, **let the child wear them for a short period (30–60 min)** before full use. If she complains of discomfort or the shoes feel tight, it’s better to return or exchange them quickly. This protects her growing feet and ensures she can walk and run in comfort—just what your daughter needs for school and play.
