## Why the dressing matters

1. **Keeps bacteria out** – A sterile, airtight dressing is the first line of defense against infection.  
2. **Maintains moisture** – A wound needs a moist, but not wet, environment to heal. Too much moisture (sweat, exudate) can dilute the dressing and create a breeding ground for bacteria.  
3. **Reduces heat build‑up** – In Dhaka’s hot climate, a dressing that lets a little air circulate prevents heat‐related irritation.

## What to do **once a day**

1. **Morning Routine**  
   - Gently wipe the dressing’s outer surface with a clean, damp cloth (no soap on the cloth).  
   - Re‑apply a fresh sterile gauze pad (re‑wash the wound if you have time).  
   - Re‑seal with a new adhesive bandage or plastic wrap.

2. **Evening/Before Bed**  
   - Repeat the same process.  
   - Keep the hand elevated while sleeping if possible.

3. **During School**  
   - If the daughter can, she can gently wipe the dressing with a clean, damp cloth and re‑apply a fresh gauze after school.  
   - Ensure the dressing is still sealed and dry.

## How to keep the wound **dry** in the heat

- **Use a breathable dressing**: Choose a semi‑elastic adhesive bandage or a thin plastic wrap that allows a bit of airflow.  
- **Keep the hand out of direct sun**: Place it on a table or use a small shade.  
- **Use a clean towel or cloth**: Keep a clean, dry cloth nearby to pat away sweat without touching the dressing.  
- **Elevate the hand**: Gravity helps reduce swelling and fluid build‑up.  
- **Avoid tight clothing**: Loose sleeves or a loose wristband let sweat evaporate.

---

## Signs you need to change the dressing more often

| Symptom | What to do |
|---------|------------|
| Redness spreading, heat, swelling, or pus at the wound site | Change dressing immediately; wash with saline. |
| Bad odor from the wound | Change dressing; consider seeing a clinician. |
| Fever or chills | Seek medical attention—possible infection. |
| The dressing becomes wet or starts to ooze | Replace with a fresh dressing right away. |

## Quick “budget” tips

- **Sterile gauze**: Small packs (5–10 cm) can be bought online or at a local pharmacy.  
- **Plastic wrap**: Use a small grocery bag or a piece of plastic from a packaging store.  
- **Antiseptic**: A small bottle of povidone‑iodine is inexpensive. If you can’t afford it, a mild soap wash is still helpful.  
- **Reusable cloths**: Wash them in hot water and let them dry in the sun—sunlight acts as a natural disinfectant.

## Bottom line

- Keep the wound **clean** and **moist** but **dry** overall.  
- Use a **single, sealed dressing** that can stay on for 24 h.  
- **Elevate** the hand and keep it out of the heat.  
- Clean the dressing once a day, and **change it immediately** if any sign of infection appears.  
- If you notice any red flags, don’t wait—seek professional help as soon as you can.
