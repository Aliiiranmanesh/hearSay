## 3. How to Verify “Medical‑Only” Practice Before You Walk In

1. **Ask a Screening Question**  
   - Call the clinic and say: “I have a specific medical issue (e.g., hypertension). I’d like a doctor who will focus only on the medical aspect and not ask about my personal life. Is that possible?”  
   - The response will usually tell you whether the practice is comfortable with that boundary.

2. **Check Their Confidentiality Statement**  
   - Private clinics often post a brief policy on their website or on a flyer in the waiting room. Look for wording like “All consultations are confidential; we only ask questions relevant to your health condition.”

3. **Observe the Intake Form**  
   - If you can get a copy (or see one in the lobby), note the fields. A “medical‑only” form will ask for: name, age, contact, medical history, current symptoms, medications. It will *not* ask about marital status, political affiliation, or religious practice unless directly relevant.

4. **Read Reviews from Locals**  
   - On Google Maps, Facebook, or the local forum “Tripoli Talk,” search the doctor’s name. Keywords like “respectful,” “confidential,” “focused on health” are good signs.

5. **Ask a Trusted Friend**  
   - If you have a neighbor or relative who’s used a doctor recently, ask them about the experience. Word‑of‑mouth is powerful in a post‑war setting where formal reviews may be sparse.
