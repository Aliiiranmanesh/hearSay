## Why it feels so strange

### 1. **Hormonal “roller‑coaster”**
After birth your body is going through a huge hormonal shift—estrogen and progesterone plummet, prolactin rises, and your brain’s reward centers are re‑tuned to focus on the baby’s needs. That can make it hard to feel the same mix of excitement, gratitude, and calm that you felt before the baby arrived.

### 2. **Sleep deprivation**
You mentioned you haven’t slept more than two hours in a while. Sleep loss alone can blunt emotions, increase irritability, and make it harder to feel joy or gratitude. It can also amplify negative thoughts (“I’m not doing enough”) that drown out the positive ones.

### 3. **The “baby blues” vs. postpartum depression**
- **Baby blues**: Up to two weeks after delivery, many people feel tearful, anxious, or emotionally irritable. It’s usually mild and resolves on its own.  
- **Postpartum depression (PPD)**: If these feelings last longer than two weeks, or if you feel hopeless, worthless, or have thoughts of harming yourself or the baby, it’s likely PPD and needs a professional evaluation.

### 4. **The “expectation” gap**
The world is full of “we’re so blessed” moments that can create a pressure to match that tone. When you’re tired and overwhelmed, it can feel like you’re falling short of that ideal. That’s a common source of guilt and self‑criticism.

---

## You’re not alone—this is common

- **Statistics**: About 10–20 % of new parents experience postpartum depression. And even those who don’t meet full criteria often feel a mix of joy, anxiety, and guilt.  
- **What others say**: Many moms say they “never felt this way” and that it was the “most difficult” part of motherhood. It’s a shared experience, not a sign of weakness.

## How to talk to your husband and mother‑in‑law

- **Use “I” language**: “I’m feeling overwhelmed and exhausted; I need help taking care of the baby so I can rest.”  
- **Ask for specific help**: “Could you feed the baby while I nap?” or “Can you take the baby to the park so I can get some fresh air?”  
- **Explain the emotional side**: “I’m not just tired. I’m feeling a mix of guilt and sadness, and it’s hard to feel the same joy the others do.”  
- **Set boundaries**: “I need a few moments alone after feeding so I can recharge. It doesn’t mean I don’t love you; it just helps me be a better mom.”  

If your mother‑in‑law’s watching feels like judgment, gently let her know how you’d prefer her support: “I appreciate you helping, but I’d feel better if you let me handle the baby for a bit, and then you could…”.

## When to seek urgent help

- You feel like you can’t keep yourself or your baby safe.  
- You have thoughts of harming yourself or the baby.  
- You’re feeling numb, detached, or in a “spirit‑less” state.  
- You’re unable to keep the baby’s basic needs met (feeding, diaper changes).  

If any of these apply, call 988 (U.S.) or your local emergency number right away. You deserve help.
