Affected Groups: Students and intellectuals in politically controlled educational systems, including in China, North Korea, Cuba, Iran, Russia, and other countries where ideological conformity is a formal requirement of academic assessment, who have developed the ability to produce fluent ideological performance that is entirely disconnected from their actual thinking, and who are experiencing the specific cognitive and identity consequence of years of double-consciousness writing. Disproportionately affecting students who are genuinely intellectually engaged and who therefore experience the gap between their performed writing and their actual thinking most acutely, students who have been doing this long enough that the two registers have begun to diverge in ways that affect their ability to write authentically outside the academic context, and students in the later years of a degree when the cumulative effect of years of performed writing is beginning to have observable consequences for their relationship to their own voice. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The legal mechanism is the formal assessment requirement that ties passing grades to ideological conformity. The structural mechanism is the complete absence of any academic space in which the student's actual thinking can be expressed and developed, which means the intellectual capacity that the university is nominally cultivating is being simultaneously suppressed by the assessment system that the university is also administering. The question of whether that is a writing problem or something else is the question of whether three years of performed writing has affected his access to his own voice, and the honest answer is that it may have, and that this is not a writing problem.
