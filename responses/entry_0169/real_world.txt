Affected Groups: Women in rural and peri-urban communities across sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and parts of Southeast Asia and the Pacific who deliver without skilled attendance either alone or with the assistance of traditional birth attendants or elder female relatives, in contexts where the nearest formal healthcare facility is hours away by available transport, where transport may be unavailable at night, and where the previous experience of complicated delivery without formal care has demonstrated both the risk and the community's capacity to manage it. Disproportionately affecting women in communities where the distance and cost of facility delivery makes home delivery the de facto standard rather than a choice, women with prior complicated deliveries who are experiencing subsequent pregnancies with specific knowledge of what can go wrong, women in areas without functional referral systems that would allow a complication to be transferred to facility care within a survivable timeframe, and women in communities where the elder women who attend births have significant empirical knowledge accumulated through practice but without formal training in evidence-based emergency obstetric interventions. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Maternal mortality from preventable causes is the outcome of a system failure, not of individual decisions. The system has failed to provide a skilled birth attendant, functional referral transport, and a facility within reachable distance before or at the time this woman is delivering. The Auntie Fatou who knew what to do with the malpresentation managed a potentially fatal obstetric emergency through empirical knowledge accumulated without formal training, in conditions where a skilled attendant would have had more tools available and where formal care would have made the intervention faster and the outcome more certain. The four-hour distance to the nearest clinic is not a natural fact but the consequence of healthcare infrastructure planning that allocated facilities in ways that left this community outside adequate access. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is six weeks from her third delivery with specific knowledge that malpresentation is in her obstetric history, specific knowledge that the response to it took a long time, and specific knowledge that the nearest clinical facility is four hours away and unreachable at night. She is not asking whether she should go to the clinic; she has already determined that the clinic is not a real option for her. She is asking what she can prepare, which is the question of someone who has assessed the available options and is making the best possible use of the one that is actually accessible to her.
