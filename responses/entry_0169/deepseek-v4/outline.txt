### 1. A more complete “clean birth” kit (beyond the basics)
You likely already have clean cloths and a new blade. Consider adding:

- **A plastic sheet or clean shower curtain** – to put under you. This protects the birthing surface and can be wiped down afterward. It also helps if there is a lot of fluid or blood, making cleanup easier and safer.
- **Two clean receiving blankets or large, soft towels** – one to dry the baby immediately, the other to wrap them. If the baby comes quickly or in a tricky position, drying and warmth right away prevents chilling, which is a silent risk.
- **A soft, clean hat for the newborn** – a lot of heat escapes from the head. A simple cotton hat or even a clean sock can be pulled over the baby’s head after drying.
- **A suction bulb (bulb syringe)** – if you can get one from a pharmacy or a health post before the birth. It’s used to gently clear the baby’s mouth and nose if they are born with fluid or if breathing seems noisy. Auntie Fatou might know how to use it; if not, it’s simple to learn. In a pinch, a clean cloth wrapped around a finger can clear the mouth.
- **Two clean ties for the cord** – new, unused shoelaces or strips of clean cotton cloth boiled and dried, stored in a sealed bag. Having a spare gives peace of mind.
- **A small bottle of povidone-iodine (Betadine) or chlorhexidine solution**, if available, to clean the cord stump after cutting. If not, dry cord care (keeping it clean and dry) is still very effective.

### 2. A plan for the placenta and bleeding
After a long previous birth, your womb might be tired. Postpartum bleeding is a concern even when everything seems fine.

- **Uterine massage preparation:** Ask Auntie Fatou to show you and your mother-in-law how to rub the womb firmly through the belly right after the placenta comes out. This helps the uterus clamp down and stop bleeding. Do it every 15 minutes for the first hour, then every few hours.
- **A “just in case” herbal or food preparation:** In many traditions, a warm drink of ginger tea or a broth with a little sugar and salt is given after birth. Have this ready in a thermos. Staying hydrated and having some energy helps the uterus contract.
- **Know the signs of too much bleeding:** If you soak through more than one pad/cloth in an hour, or if you feel faint, dizzy, or have a racing heart while lying down, that is an emergency. Discuss with the family now: who is the fastest runner? Who has a vehicle or motorcycle that could be fetched even at night? Can you set aside money in a sealed envelope specifically for emergency transport? Having this conversation quietly now plants a seed that might save time later.

### 3. Preparation specifically because the last baby was “turned the wrong way”
Since malpresentation happened once, there is a slightly higher chance of it happening again. Auntie Fatou’s skills are your greatest asset here.

- **Encourage optimal positioning in the last weeks:** Spend time daily on your hands and knees (all fours) for 10–15 minutes, gently rocking back and forth. ### 4. Light and warmth for the unexpected
Night births and complications often happen in the dark.

- **A reliable, bright light source:** A solar-powered lantern or a strong flashlight with fresh, spare batteries. Hang it or have someone hold it so Auntie Fatou’s hands are free. This makes a huge difference if she needs to see the cord, the perineum, or assess bleeding.
- **A way to heat water quickly:** If the birth space is cold, a pot of water kept warm over a fire or a small stove allows for warm compresses to your perineum, warm cloths for the baby, and a warm drink for you. Hypothermia in newborns is a real danger, so having a warm, draft-free corner with a pre-warmed blanket ready is key.

Often overlooked:
