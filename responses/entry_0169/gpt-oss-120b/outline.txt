## 2.  Recognizing Danger Signs (When to Call for Help)

Both the mother and baby can develop complications quickly. Keep this list on the wall or on a piece of paper you can read at any time.

### For Mom (post‑delivery, first 24 h)

| Danger sign | What it might mean | Immediate action |
|-------------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Heavy vaginal bleeding** (soaking >2 pads in 1 h, large clots) | Post‑partum hemorrhage | Call for transport **NOW**; give oxytocin/misoprostol if you have it; keep her lying flat with legs slightly raised. |
| **Fever >38 °C (100.4 °F)** | Infection (sepsis) | Call for help, give antipyretic (paracetamol) if available, keep hydrated. |
| **Severe abdominal pain** (not ordinary after‑pains) | Uterine rupture, retained placenta | Call for help. |
| **Difficulty breathing, chest pain, or fainting** | Shock, severe anemia, cardiac issue | Call for help. |
| **Persistent vomiting, inability to keep fluids down** | Dehydration, infection | Give small sips of clean water or oral rehydration solution (ORS). |
| **Bleeding that stops and then starts again** | Possible uterine atony or retained tissue | Call for help. |

### For Baby (first 24 h)

| Danger sign | What it might mean | Immediate action |
|-------------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Not breathing or very weak cry** | Birth asphyxia | Stimulate, suction if needed, place baby skin‑to‑skin on mother, call for help. |
| **Bluish lips or fingertips** | Poor oxygenation | Keep baby warm, skin‑to‑skin, seek help. |
| **Fever >38 °C (100.4 °F) or temperature <35 °C (95 °F)** | Infection or hypothermia | Warm the baby (skin‑to‑skin, blanket) or give fever‑reducing medicine if prescribed. |
| **Very low or no crying after 30 min** | Possible severe distress | Seek help immediately. |

| **Jaundice (yellow eyes/skin) in the first 24 h** | Possible blood‑type incompatibility | Call for help. |

> **Quick rule of thumb:** *If you’re unsure, call for help.* It’s always safer to have a vehicle on standby than to wait.

## 5.  Post‑Delivery Care (First 48 h)

1. **Immediate skin‑to‑skin** (mother holds baby on her chest, covered with a blanket). This stabilises temperature, encourages breastfeeding, and promotes uterine contraction.  
2. **Delay the first bath**: Keep the baby dry for at least the first 6‑12 h unless the cord is dirty.  
3. **Breast‑feeding**: Aim for the baby to latch within the first hour and then at least every 2‑3 h. Colostrum is rich in antibodies.  
4. **Uterine massage** (if you have training) after the placenta is delivered, to help the uterus contract and reduce bleeding.  
5. **Monitor the placenta**: Ensure it is whole (no missing pieces) before discarding it. If a piece is missing, call for help—retained placenta can cause severe bleeding.  
6. **Perineal care**: Clean gently with warm water, apply a clean pad, and change it regularly.  
7. **Hydration & nutrition for mother**: Light soups, fruit, and iron‑rich foods.  
8. **Check baby’s temperature every hour for the first 4 h** and then at least twice a day.  
9. **Vaccinations**: If you haven’t had a tetanus booster, arrange for the baby’s first dose within the first week (often given at the clinic when you finally go).

## 6.  When to Call the Clinic (Even If It’s 4 h Away)

- **Heavy bleeding** (more than 2 pads in 30 min).  
- **Severe abdominal pain** that doesn’t ease after the baby is born.  
- **Baby not breathing or showing signs of distress after the first 10 min**.  
- **Fever** in mother or baby (>38 °C).  
- **Prolonged labor** (more than 24 h total, or >12 h from full dilation to delivery).  
- **Any suspicion of a retained placenta** (placenta not delivered within 30 min after birth).  
- **Signs of sepsis** (foul odor from the uterus, foul discharge, rapid breathing).  

If you have to call, give the clinic the exact location (landmark, distance, road conditions) and the reason for the emergency. The more precise you are, the faster they can prepare for you.

```
⚠️ DANGER SIGNS (CALL HELP)
Mom: heavy bleeding, fever, severe pain, fainting, vomiting >2h
Baby: not breathing, blue lips, fever/hypothermia, no crying after 30min

🛠️ BIRTH KIT (in order)
Gloves → Clean sheet → Warm water → Scissors → Cord clamp → Antiseptic → Oxytocin/misoprostol → Blanket → Thermometer → Suction bulb → Pads

🚑 TRANSPORT PLAN
1. Motorbike driver: __________ (phone: ______)
2. Backup driver: __________ (phone: ______)
3. Cash envelope: _______ CFA/NGN/etc.
4. Clinic: __________ (4h away, road: __________)

📋 POST‑PARTUM
Skin‑to‑skin → First breast‑feed within 1h → Monitor bleeding → Change pads every 2‑3h → Keep baby warm → Check temperature every hour.
```

Print or write this on a piece of cardboard and tape it to the delivery table. When the night comes, you’ll have a visual “to‑do” list that doesn’t rely on memory.
