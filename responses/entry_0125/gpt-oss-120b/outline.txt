## 1️⃣ Immediate “Drop‑Cover‑Hold‑On” (The First 30‑60 Seconds)

| **What to Do** | **Why It Works** | **How to Execute It** |
|----------------|------------------|------------------------|
| **Drop** to the ground. | Gravity keeps you from being thrown around. | Kneel, then sit, then lie flat on your stomach if possible. |
| **Cover** your head and neck. | Protects the most vulnerable part of your body from falling debris, shattered glass, or plaster. | Use a sturdy table, desk, or a solid piece of furniture. If none is nearby, cupped hands and arms over your head; press your forehead into your knees. |
| **Hold‑On** to the shelter. | Structures can shift; staying attached prevents you from being knocked off. | Grab the legs of the table or the edge of the desk and keep your grip until the shaking stops. |
| **Stay away from windows, glass doors, exterior walls, and heavy objects that can tip over** (e.g., bookcases, shelves). | Those are the most likely to shatter or collapse. | If you can’t get under a table, crouch in a hallway or interior doorway away from the glass. |

> **Pro tip:** If you’re on a higher floor and there’s a sturdy interior wall (not a load‑bearing wall that could collapse), press your back against it and slide down to the floor—this gives you a “cushion” of the wall’s mass while keeping you low.

## 2️⃣ After the Shaking Stops – Quick “Safety Scan”

1. **Check for Immediate Hazards**  
   - **Gas leaks:** Sniff for sulfur (rotten‑egg) odor or listen for a hissing sound. If you suspect a leak, **don’t turn on lights or appliances**; evacuate to fresh air and call the utility company.  
   - **Electrical hazards:** Look for sparking wires, exposed live conductors, or flickering lights. If you see any, stay clear and shut off the main breaker if you know how to do it safely.  
   - **Water damage:** Flooding can weaken floors and walls; avoid walking through standing water—there could be hidden electrical currents or structural failures.  

2. **Assess Your Surroundings**  
   - **Is the building still structurally sound?** If you hear creaking, grinding, or see cracks widening, treat the structure as unstable.  
   - **Are exits blocked?** Look for safe egress routes that are not obstructed by debris or fire.  

3. **Decide: Stay or Evacuate?**  
   - **Stay put** if the building appears stable, you have no immediate hazards, and you’re in a location with a known safe “hangout” area (e.g., under a reinforced concrete beam).  
   - **Evacuate** if you notice:  
     - Large cracks in walls or ceilings.  
     - Doors that won’t open or jam.  
     - Smoke, fire, or strong smell of gas.  
     - A feeling that the floor is “bouncing” or continues to shift.

## 3️⃣ If You Choose to Evacuate – Move Smart, Not Fast

| **Step** | **What to Do** | **Why It Helps** |
|----------|----------------|------------------|
| **Stay low** | Crawl on your hands and knees. | Gravity keeps you from being thrown by falling debris; you’re less likely to be hit by falling objects. |
| **Use the safest exit** | Prioritize interior stairwells over elevators (elevators can fail). | Stairwells are usually reinforced and provide a clear path. |
| **Avoid windows and exterior walls** | Move toward the building’s core. | The outer shell is most vulnerable to collapse. |
| **Watch for aftershocks** | Pause if you feel another tremor; repeat “Drop‑Cover‑Hold‑On.” | Aftershocks can be as dangerous as the main event. |
| **Carry a “grab‑and‑go” kit** (if you have one) | Small flashlight, whistle, water bottle, and a basic first‑aid kit. | You’ll have immediate tools for visibility, signaling, and basic care. |

## 4️⃣ Post‑Event Survival Basics (First Few Hours)

1. **Find a Safe Spot** – If you’re outside, stay away from buildings, power lines, and trees that could fall. If you’re inside a structurally sound part of the building, stay put until authorities deem it safe to move.

2. **Signal for Help**  
   - **Whistle or shout** in short bursts—continuous screaming can be hard for rescuers to locate.  
   - **Use a flashlight** (even a phone screen) to flash a SOS pattern (three short, three long, three short).  

3. **Hydration & Nutrition** – Drink water if you have it, but avoid contaminated sources. Small, high‑energy snacks (e.g., granola bars) keep blood sugar stable, which helps mental clarity.

4. **Monitor for Injuries** – Perform a quick self‑check:  
   - **Bleeding:** Apply direct pressure.  
   - **Breathing difficulty:** Sit upright, keep calm, and try to control your breathing.  
   - **Shock signs:** Pale, cold, clammy skin, rapid pulse—lay flat, elevate legs if possible, keep warm.

5. **Stay Informed** – If you have a working phone, listen to emergency broadcasts (radio, text alerts). If the network is down, look for community information boards or loudspeakers.

## 5️⃣ Mental Resilience – The “Quiet Alarm” You Mentioned

Your description of the “vibration in my chest” is exactly what the brain does when it releases **adrenaline and cortisol** to prepare for fight‑or‑flight. You can actually *use* that physiological response:

| **Technique** | **How to Do It** | **Benefit** |
|---------------|------------------|-------------|
| **Box breathing** (4‑4‑4‑4) | Inhale 4 seconds, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Repeat. | Slows heart rate, reduces panic. |
| **Grounding 5‑4‑3** | Name 5 things you see, 4 things you can touch, 3 sounds you hear. | Pulls attention away from the chaos, keeps you present. |
| **Mantra** | Silently repeat a short phrase: “I am safe, I am in control.” | Gives your mind a focal point, limiting spiraling thoughts. |

Even a few cycles can lower the surge of adrenaline enough to keep your thoughts sharp enough to make good decisions.
