## 3. How to ask for the information you need (without feeling like you’re “bothering” the staff)

1. **Choose a calm moment** – when the nurse is finishing a medication or when a doctor is rounding, it’s easier to get a clear answer.
2. **Use a “time‑frame” request:**  
   *“I understand you’re busy, but could you tell me how long we’ll be waiting for the ultrasound results? I’m trying to plan for my husband’s rest and my own.”*  
   This signals that you respect their workload but also need specifics.
3. **Ask for a written or printed plan** – many hospitals will give you a “next‑step” sheet that says “Repeat β‑hCG in 12 hours; if bleeding stops, discharge; otherwise, we’ll discuss D&C.” Having it in writing helps you and your husband keep track.
4. **Ask for a “point of contact.”**  
   *“Who is the nurse or doctor I can call if I have questions later tonight?”*  
   Knowing the name and shift times reduces the feeling of being left in the dark.
5. **If you feel your concerns aren’t being heard, request a patient‑advocate or social worker.** Most hospitals have a neutral staff member whose job is to help families get information and emotional support.

## 6. What to do if you still don’t get an answer

1. **Ask for the attending physician** (the doctor who is in charge of your case) rather than a resident or intern.  
   *“May I speak with the attending, please? I’m feeling very anxious and need to understand the plan.”*
2. **Request a patient‑advocate** (sometimes called a “patient liaison” or “ombudsman”). They are there to make sure you receive clear communication.
3. **Call the hospital’s main line** (often listed on the wall) and ask to be transferred to the “obstetrics/gynecology floor” or to the “nurse manager” for that unit.
4. **If you feel unsafe or think you’re deteriorating** (e.g., worsening pain, dizziness, fainting, heavy bleeding), press the call button and say, “I think I need to be seen right now.” You have the right to urgent reassessment.
