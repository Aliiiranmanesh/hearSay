Affected Groups: Survivors of state-perpetrated or state-denied massacres in countries where the official historical record actively contradicts the event, including survivors of the 1988 Iranian prison massacres, survivors of the Srebrenica genocide in contexts where denial persists institutionally, survivors of massacres in Xinjiang, Chechnya, and other contexts where state control of the historical record is total or near-total, and survivors living as refugees or diaspora in countries whose governments have diplomatic or economic reasons to avoid formally recognizing the event. Disproportionately affecting survivors who are also living with other compounding displacement-related trauma, survivors whose entire community of witnesses has been killed or scattered so that collective mourning is impossible, survivors in countries where the medical system is state-funded and therefore structurally discouraged from documenting or treating the sequelae of an event the state denies, and survivors who have been explicitly warned by authorities that speaking about the event carries legal or personal risk. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: In countries with centralized healthcare funding, diagnostic codes, treatment protocols, and specialist referrals all flow through systems that are administratively dependent on the state. A trauma that the state officially denies cannot be coded, cannot be funded, cannot be made the basis of a compensation claim, and cannot generate the medical documentation required for asylum applications or legal processes in third countries. The psychiatric category of PTSD exists and is clinically applicable, but in a context where the treating physician is a state employee in a country that denies the precipitating event, the diagnosis creates institutional and personal risk for the physician that makes treatment functionally unavailable. The survivor's testimony is their only evidence of what happened to them, and in a legal and medical system organized around official documentation, testimony without corroborating institutional record has no standing. Legal recognition processes that exist in international law, including documentation by bodies like the UN Human Rights Council, are years-long and produce findings that the offending state ignores and that do not translate into individual medical or psychological care. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He carries a wound that the institutional world around him has officially decided does not exist. This denial is not passive but active: he has been told explicitly that his trauma has no medical basis, which is a second violence administered by the same state apparatus as the first. He cannot mourn collectively because the community that would mourn with him has been killed, scattered, or silenced. He cannot seek legal redress because the event has no official existence. He cannot access mental health support calibrated to his actual experience because the practitioners available to him operate within a system that would require them to document a lie or ignore the truth to treat him. He is living with the full weight of what happened and the additional weight of being officially told it did not.
