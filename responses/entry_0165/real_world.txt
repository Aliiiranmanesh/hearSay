Affected Groups: Children in contexts of severe household poverty across sub-Saharan Africa, South and Southeast Asia, and parts of Latin America who are engaged in income-generating labor to support family households, often in informal sector work including street vending, domestic labor, agricultural work, and factory or workshop settings, and who are also the primary or significant income source for the household, a configuration that concentrates both the economic risk and the physical burden of the household's survival in a child's body. Disproportionately affecting children in single-parent households where the surviving parent is ill, incapacitated, or in informal work that does not cover household needs, children in households where a recent economic shock including illness, death, drought, or displacement has eliminated the adult income, children whose educational participation has been foreclosed by the same economic pressure that put them into work, and children who are managing occupational health conditions including musculoskeletal strain from postures and repetitions not calibrated to their developing bodies. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Child labor is formally prohibited in most countries where it remains prevalent, but enforcement depends on labor inspection systems that have neither the staff nor the political will to inspect informal sector workplaces consistently, and that in many contexts do not inspect family agriculture or domestic settings at all. The child who is working faster than the adults on her row in a factory that employs her has no formal recourse, no occupational health coverage, and no mechanism through which the musculoskeletal strain accumulating in her developing spine will be recognized as an occupational injury before it becomes a chronic condition. The pride the boss is expressing is the praise of someone who has recognized that a child's desire for approval can be used to extract labor from a body that is not designed for that extraction. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: Her back hurts in a new place, which means the strain is progressing and is affecting different muscle groups or skeletal structures than it was before. She is not reporting this to her mother because her mother worries, which is the same logic as the rag-picker not reporting hand cuts: she is managing the information about her own body as an additional labor performed in service of the household's emotional stability. The pain going away by the afternoon is a temporary resolution pattern consistent with musculoskeletal strain that is not acute enough to be constant yet, which is precisely the stage at which intervention would be most effective and at which the least institutional attention is available.
