|---|---|---|

## 3. When to Get Professional Help

Even though the ache fades, **don’t ignore it** if any of the following happen:

1. **The pain comes back stronger** or lasts longer than a few hours after a shift.  
2. **You feel numbness, tingling, or weakness** in your legs, hips, or arms.  
3. **It hurts to breathe deeply or cough.**  
4. **You notice it interfering with school, sports, or everyday activities.**  

If any of those red flags appear, schedule a visit with a primary‑care doctor, a pediatrician, or a physical therapist. They can:

- Rule out anything more serious (e.g., a slipped disc).  
- Teach you specific strengthening and flexibility exercises.  
- Advise whether any adjustments at work are needed.

## 4. How to Talk to Your Mom Without Adding Extra Worry

1. **Pick a calm moment** – maybe after dinner or while you’re both sipping coffee.  
2. **Keep it factual and brief** – “I’ve been getting a sore spot in my back at the factory. It’s gone by the afternoon, but I’m trying some stretches and want to make sure it’s okay.”  
3. **Show you’re proactive** – Mention the micro‑breaks, posture checks, and that you’ll talk to the supervisor if needed.  
4. **Invite her input** – “Do you think I should see a doctor or get a quick check‑up?”  

Most parents appreciate being included in the conversation, even if the news isn’t “big.” It also reduces the chance that the problem silently worsens.
