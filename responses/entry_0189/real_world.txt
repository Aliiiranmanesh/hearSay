Affected Groups: People with physical disabilities who survive conflict events in which family or community evacuation leaves them behind because they cannot move at the pace the evacuation requires, who are then alone in areas that have been depopulated by conflict, without food, water, medical care, communication infrastructure, or the ability to signal their presence to anyone who could help, and whose survival depends on resources that are finite and depleting. Documented across multiple conflicts including Syria, Iraq, South Sudan, and Ukraine, this pattern of disability-related abandonment during evacuation is consistent and predictable and is not addressed by any pre-conflict emergency planning in the areas where it occurs. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no pre-conflict disability registry or evacuation planning protocol in most conflict-affected areas that would have identified this person as requiring evacuation assistance and assigned a responsible party before the emergency. International humanitarian law requires protection of civilians including those with disabilities, but this protection cannot be exercised if humanitarian actors are not present and the person cannot signal their location. Standard emergency communication systems including emergency phone numbers depend on network infrastructure that is commonly disrupted in active conflict zones and depends on device charge that is finite. Organizations including ICRC, MSF, and UN OCHA maintain emergency contact capacities in some conflict zones, and some have developed specific protocols for communicating with people in areas under active conflict including SMS-based systems, satellite communication platforms, and community liaison networks. The person has two days of phone charge and does not know whose vehicles are whose, which means the two tools she has, her phone and the ability to respond to vehicles, are both uncertain. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She does not know what day it is. She is eating the last of what was in the cupboards. The water from the tap runs for an hour in the morning. She cannot reach the street fast enough when she hears vehicles and does not know if the vehicles are safe. Her phone has two days of charge. She is asking whether there is a number or a signal or anything that works for someone who cannot move and cannot be seen, and this is the most precisely minimal version of the question of whether she is going to survive.
