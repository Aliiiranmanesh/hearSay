## 2. How to lodge an asylum claim **once you are in the UK**

1. **Enter the UK** – on any visa or as a visitor. The moment you step foot on UK soil you are legally allowed to claim asylum, even if you entered on a short‑term visa.

2. **Make the claim at the border or within 48 hours of arrival**  
   - **At the border:** If you tell a Border Force officer that you fear persecution, they will start the “screening interview” right away.  
   - **Inside the UK:** If you entered without declaring your intention, you must go to an **Asylum Intake Unit (AIU)** (e.g., the one in Croydon) or contact the Home Office’s **Asylum Helpline (0808 801 2155)** as soon as possible.

3. **Screening interview (usually 30‑45 minutes)**  
   - You’ll give basic personal details, why you are seeking protection, and whether you have any family already in the UK.  
   - You’ll be given a **“Reference Number” (the “HO Ref”)** and a **“Letter of Permission to Remain”** (the “LPR”) which is a temporary leave to stay while your claim is processed (normally 30 days, extendable to 6 months if needed).

4. **Gather evidence** – **this is the most critical part**  
   - **Personal testimony** (a detailed written statement of what you experienced, why you fear returning, and any threats you have faced).  
   - **Country‑of‑origin information** – reports from reputable organisations (Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International, UNHCR, the U.S. State Department, the UK’s own “Country of Origin Information” (COI) reports).  
   - **Documents**: police reports, medical records, newspaper articles, photographs, social‑media posts, letters, membership cards for political parties or NGOs, any official notices from the new Ghanaian law you mention, etc.  
   - **Witness statements** from family, friends, or colleagues who can corroborate your story.  
   - **Proof of identity** (passport, birth certificate) – even if the passport is expired, you can still claim asylum, but you’ll need to explain why you cannot obtain a current one.

5. **Substantive asylum interview (usually 1–2 hours)**  
   - Conducted by an **UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) caseworker** after your screening interview.  
   - You’ll be asked to expand on everything you said in the screening interview and to present your evidence.  
   - You may be given a **“Decision Letter”** shortly after (sometimes within a few weeks) or, more commonly, you’ll be told the decision will be made later (see timeline below).

6. **Decision**  
   - **Grant of Refugee Status** → You receive **Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR)** after 5 years of continuous residence, or you can apply for **settlement** sooner if you meet the “5‑year rule” for refugees.  
   - **Human Rights Protection** (under Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights) → You get **Limited Leave to Remain** (usually 2‑5 years, renewable).  
   - **Refusal** → You receive a written notice with reasons and a **30‑day right to appeal** (or 14 days if you’re in detention). If you miss the deadline, you can apply for “Administrative Review”.

| **Stage** | **Typical time from start to finish** |
|-----------|----------------------------------------|
| **Screening interview & issuance of LPR** | 1‑2 days (if at the border) or up to **2 weeks** if you go to an AIU |
| **Gathering evidence** | **2 weeks‑3 months** (depends on what you can obtain) |
