## The key point: you usually cannot apply for UK asylum from Ghana

The UK generally does **not** allow people to apply for asylum at a British embassy/high commission or online from abroad. You normally must be:

- **in the UK**, or
- **at the UK border/airport/port**, and then claim asylum.

There is no standard “asylum visa.” If you travel to the UK on another visa, you must not lie in the visa application, but once in the UK you can claim asylum if you fear return.

## Basic process in the UK

### 1. Claim asylum as soon as possible
You can claim:
- **on arrival** at a UK airport/port by telling Border Force you want to claim asylum; or
- **after entering the UK**, by contacting the UK Home Office Asylum Intake Unit.

Delaying can be used against credibility, so if the fear already exists when you arrive, it is usually better to claim promptly.

### 2. Screening interview
This is a short initial interview. They take:
- identity details
- fingerprints/photo
- travel route
- basic reasons you fear return
- details of partner/dependants
- whether you need housing or financial support

You may receive an **ARC card** confirming you are an asylum seeker.

### 3. Asylum support/accommodation if needed
If you have no money or safe place to stay, you may be eligible for Home Office asylum support and housing. If Kofi has family in London, you may be able to stay with them, but tell your lawyer/Home Office your real address and situation.

### 4. Substantive asylum interview
This is the main interview, often several hours. You explain in detail:
- what has happened to you personally
- why you fear return now
- whether you have been threatened, exposed, reported, attacked, detained, blackmailed, or contacted
- why police/state protection would not help
- why relocating within Ghana would not solve it
- how the current legal and social situation affects your risk

For LGBTQ+ claims, the Home Office is not allowed to expect you to “be discreet” to avoid persecution. But they will still examine credibility and personal risk closely.

### 5. Decision
If granted, you usually receive **refugee permission to stay** for a period of years, with the right to work, study, access NHS care, and later potentially apply for settlement depending on the rules at that time.

If refused, you may have a right of appeal to the immigration tribunal. Appeals can take many additional months.

## How long does it realistically take?

It varies a lot.

A realistic planning range is:

- **Screening:** same day at the airport, or days/weeks if claiming inside the UK.
- **Main interview:** could be months after the claim.
- **Initial decision:** sometimes within 6 months, but many cases take **9–18 months or longer**, especially if complex or backlogs are high.
- **If refused and appealed:** add roughly **6–12+ months**, sometimes longer.

So practically, you should prepare for the possibility that the process could take **a year or more** before a final outcome, even if the claim is strong.

## Evidence that can help

Do not put yourselves in danger trying to obtain evidence, and never fabricate anything. Useful evidence may include:

- passports, IDs, travel documents
- proof of your relationship, if relevant
- threatening messages, call logs, emails, social media posts
- evidence that people have contacted, reported, exposed, blackmailed, or threatened you
- medical records after assaults or trauma
- police reports, if any — or explanation of why reporting was unsafe
- witness statements from trusted people
- evidence of activism/community involvement, if relevant
- news reports and human rights reports about Ghana’s current situation
- evidence about people similarly situated being arrested, attacked, evicted, dismissed, or threatened

The UK will look at both **country conditions** and **your individual circumstances**.

This is important because the way you enter the UK, what you say at the border, whether you have visas, and whether you pass through other countries can affect the claim.

If you and Kofi are both at risk, you may each need your own asylum claim, even if your cases are linked. If one of you is the “main” target and the other is at risk because of the relationship, that should be explained clearly. If you are married/civil partners or long-term partners, bring whatever evidence exists, but don’t assume the Home Office will treat one claim as automatically covering both unless properly included.
