## 2. Set Clear Personal Boundaries  

1. **Speak Up (If You Feel Able)**  
   - You can say something like, “I’m not comfortable with you threatening me. I have the right to talk to neighbors or anyone else.”  
   - Keep the tone calm and firm; you don’t have to justify *why* you were talking—just that the threat is unacceptable.

2. **Use “I” Statements**  
   - “I feel scared when you say I will face consequences for speaking to someone.”  
   - This frames the conversation around your feelings rather than accusing him, which can sometimes keep the discussion from escalating.

3. **Repeat the Boundary**  
   - If he pushes back, repeat: “I’m asking you to stop threatening me. If this continues, I will have to get help.”

4. **Write It Down**  
   - A short note (text, email, or a handwritten note) can serve as a record: “I am asking you not to threaten me. I will seek help if it continues.”  

> *Why this helps:* Even if you can’t change his behavior right away, you create a documented trail that can be useful later if you need to involve authorities or a support organization.

## 4. Legal & Protective Options in Jordan  

1. **Criminal Protection** – Threatening violence is a crime under Jordanian law (Article 308 of the Penal Code covers threats of bodily harm). If the threat is serious, you can file a police report.  
2. **Restraining Order** – Jordan’s *Protection Order Law* (2009) allows victims of domestic violence or threats to request a protective order that legally bars the aggressor from approaching or contacting you.  
3. **Legal Aid** – The *Legal Aid Department* of the Ministry of Justice provides free legal counseling for women. You can also contact NGOs like *Legal Aid Society – Jordan* for help preparing a complaint.  

> **Tip:** When you go to the police or a women's center, bring the written note or any recordings of the threat. It strengthens your case.
