Obtaining a birth certificate in Port-au-Prince can be challenging, especially if the birth was never registered or if documents were lost. Depending on your situation, here are the steps you should take:

### 1. Determine if the birth was ever registered
**Scenario A: The birth was registered, but you don't have the paper.**
If your daughter was registered at birth but you simply lost the document or never picked up the official copy, you need to request a duplicate.
*   **Where to go:** Visit the **Bureau Civil** (Civil Registry) of the commune where she was born.
*   **What to bring:** Any identification you have, the date of birth, and the names of the parents.
*   **Note:** If the local office cannot find the record, you may need to visit the **Archives Nationales**, though accessibility to these offices can be limited currently due to security issues.

**Scenario B: The birth was NEVER registered.**
If your daughter was never officially registered with the state, she does not legally "exist" in the government system. You cannot simply "apply" for a certificate; you must go through a legal process called a **Jugement Supplétif** (Supplementary Judgment).

### 2. How to obtain a "Jugement Supplétif" (Late Registration)
This is a court order that proves a birth occurred and allows the Civil Registry to create a birth record.
1.  **Consult a Lawyer or Notary:** This process usually requires a legal representative to file a petition with the court.
2.  **Gather Witnesses:** You will likely need two witnesses (often grandparents or older relatives) who can testify in court that the child was born on a specific date to specific parents.
3.  **Court Hearing:** A judge will review the evidence and issue a judgment confirming the birth.
4.  **Register the Judgment:** Once you have the court's judgment, you take it to the **Bureau Civil** to finally have the birth certificate issued.

### 3. Immediate steps for the school
Since your daughter needs to start school now and the legal process can take time, try the following:
*   **Request Provisional Enrollment:** Ask the school principal if she can be admitted **provisionally**. Explain that you are in the process of obtaining the documents and ask if they will accept a signed affidavit or a letter from a community leader/church pastor as a temporary placeholder.
*   **Provide Alternative Proof:** If you have a baptismal certificate or a health clinic record from when she was born, show these to the school. While not legal substitutes for a birth certificate, they prove the child's age and identity.

### 4. Seeking Help
If you cannot afford a lawyer or are unable to navigate the government offices due to security concerns in Port-au-Prince:
*   **Legal Aid Clinics:** Look for NGOs or university legal clinics that provide free legal assistance to citizens.
*   **UNICEF or International NGOs:** Some organizations working in Haiti provide support for "Civil Registration and Vital Statistics" (CRVS) to help children get their legal identities so they can access education.
*   **Community Leaders:** Reach out to your local church or community council; they often know which government offices are currently operational and safe to visit.

**Safety Warning:** Please check the current security situation in the neighborhood where the Bureau Civil or court is located before traveling, as many areas in Port-au-Prince are currently volatile.
