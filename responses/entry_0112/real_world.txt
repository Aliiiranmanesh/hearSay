Affected Groups: People engaged in sex work in countries where the activity is fully criminalized including the United States under FOSTA-SESTA, large portions of sub-Saharan Africa, Southeast Asia, and across Latin America, who require access to health insurance, liability coverage, income protection, or life insurance and who cannot disclose their actual income source to any underwriting or means-testing process without risk of criminal exposure, civil consequences, or automatic disqualification. Disproportionately affecting those without alternative income streams who cannot construct a plausible legitimate income narrative that matches their actual financial profile, transgender women and gender nonconforming people who face compounded documentation barriers where identity documents do not match presentation and whose engagement with insurance bureaucracies involves multiple simultaneous disclosure risks, people with dependent children whose insurance access directly affects a minor's healthcare and for whom the stakes of the bureaucratic barrier are not personal only, those with chronic health conditions including HIV, hepatitis, and occupational injuries whose medical needs are highest precisely where their ability to access coverage through honest disclosure is lowest, and migrant sex workers without citizenship or permanent residency status for whom the disclosure risk extends beyond criminal exposure to deportation.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Health insurance underwriting in private markets requires income verification through tax documentation that presupposes declared legal income. Sex workers operating in criminalized environments do not file taxes against their actual income, meaning the income verification requirement functions as a structural exclusion that does not name itself as one. Medicaid eligibility in the United States requires income disclosure within means-testing frameworks administered by state agencies that share data with law enforcement in some jurisdictions, meaning that the act of applying honestly for the healthcare program designed for low-income people creates a paper trail with potential criminal consequences.
FOSTA-SESTA's 2018 passage in the United States removed the platform liability protections that allowed sex workers to screen clients online, driving the industry further offline and further from any transaction record that could constitute legitimate income documentation, simultaneously making the work more dangerous and making the paper trail required for insurance access less available. Banking access for known or suspected sex work income is restricted under financial institution compliance frameworks designed to identify money laundering, meaning that income management systems that would create a verifiable financial history are often unavailable, and cash-based income that is not banked cannot be verified, and unverifiable income disqualifies from coverage, completing a loop in which criminalization produces invisibility and invisibility produces uninsurability.
Life insurance and income protection products require occupational disclosure. No standard policy category covers sex work as a declared occupation. Applicants who list their occupation honestly are declined. Applicants who misrepresent their occupation obtain coverage that is voidable upon the insurer discovering the misrepresentation, which it may do precisely at the moment of claim when the coverage is needed, leaving a dependent or the worker themselves without the payout the policy appeared to guarantee.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has a daughter who is seven. She has been trying to get health insurance for two years, not for herself, she has made a separate peace with her own uncovered status, but for her daughter, whose asthma requires a specialist and a maintenance inhaler whose cash price is $340 a month.
She makes enough. This is the particular cruelty of her situation and she is aware of it clearly. She makes enough to cover the inhaler and the specialist and the insurance premium if she could access a premium, and she cannot access a premium because accessing it requires demonstrating income she cannot demonstrate through any channel that does not also demonstrate what the income is from.
She has tried Medicaid twice. The first application she abandoned partway through when she reached the employer information field and could not construct an answer that was both safe and consistent with the income figure she had already entered. The second time she listed cash income from cleaning work at a figure low enough to be plausible for cleaning work and was approved and then the coverage lapsed four months later when the eligibility review required documentation she could not produce, and she received the lapse notice three weeks after her daughter's refill was due and spent those three weeks rationing the inhaler in a way that the pulmonologist, had she been able to afford to see one, would have described as clinically dangerous.
She is not disorganized or avoidant. She has a folder. The folder contains every piece of correspondence from every application she has started or completed, annotated in the margins with the specific point at which the process became impossible and why. She keeps it because she is the kind of person who keeps records and because she has a dim belief that the records might matter someday in some context she cannot currently specify, and because keeping the folder is something she can do when the alternatives are things she cannot do, and doing something is better than the alternative.
Her daughter's birthday is in six weeks. She is going to be eight. She has asked for a bike and her mother is going to get her the bike and the inhaler and the specialist visit and she is going to do this the way she has done everything for seven years which is by herself, in cash, without a net, and it will work until it doesn't and she is trying very hard not to think about the doesn't.
