Real world:
Affected Groups: Legal residents and citizens of sanctioned or politically isolated states living abroad with valid residency status in host countries, most acutely affecting Iranians, Russians, Belarusians, Syrians, North Koreans, Cubans, Venezuelans, and Sudanese nationals who have legally relocated to Europe, North America, Latin America, or Southeast Asia through work visas, student visas, asylum status, or permanent residency. Particularly devastating for professionals in technology, finance, academia, and medicine whose work requires access to digital platforms, payment systems, and verification infrastructure that their passport of origin structurally excludes them from regardless of their legal standing in the country where they actually live and work. Also acutely affects stateless persons and those holding travel documents issued by states with limited international recognition, including Palestinian Authority travel documents, Kosovo passports in non-recognizing states, and Taiwanese passports in jurisdictions deferring to Beijing. Also affects people holding valid refugee travel documents issued under the 1951 Convention, which many identity verification systems do not recognize as equivalent to a national passport despite their legal status as internationally recognized travel documents. The population is not small, not marginal, and not composed of people attempting to circumvent systems. It is composed of people who are legally present, legally working, legally tax-paying, and structurally excluded by an identity verification layer that was designed without them.
Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: Digital identity verification systems used by banks, financial platforms, telecommunications providers, healthcare portals, government service interfaces, and employer onboarding systems are built on document recognition databases that reflect geopolitical rather than legal logic. A system that accepts passports from 180 countries but not Iran is not making a legal determination about the Iranian national standing in front of it. It is importing a sanctions framework designed for state-level financial flows and applying it as a binary individual exclusion at the identity layer, regardless of whether that individual is subject to any sanction, has any connection to sanctioned entities, or poses any compliance risk. OFAC and EU sanctions regulations contain general licenses and individual exemptions that technically permit service provision to individuals with no sanctioned activity, but compliance departments at financial institutions and technology companies have systematically overcomplied, applying blanket nationality exclusions because the cost of individual due diligence exceeds the revenue value of the customer and the reputational risk of a compliance error exceeds the reputational cost of excluding innocent people. Know Your Customer regulations require identity verification but do not specify that passport nationality must determine eligibility, yet vendor systems implementing KYC have operationalized it that way because national passport databases are the infrastructure they were built on. Customer support escalation paths at these institutions are staffed by agents with no authority to override system-level exclusions, trained on scripts that loop back to the same verification pathway, and operating without access to the compliance exceptions that theoretically exist. The result is a system where the exception that would resolve the individual's situation exists in policy and does not exist in practice because the practice was never built to implement the exception.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: An Iranian software engineer living legally in Germany on a work visa, paying German taxes, holding a German residence permit, attempts to open a business bank account to receive client payments. The bank's identity verification system does not accept Iranian passports. She submits her German residence permit. The system requires a passport as primary document. She calls customer support. She is told to submit her passport. She submits her Iranian passport. The system flags it and suspends the application. She calls again. She is told the matter has been escalated. Three weeks later she receives an email telling her the application cannot be processed and directing her to the same online portal that rejected her. She attempts a second bank. The same outcome in a different interface. She attempts a fintech platform. The platform's automated verification vendor does not include Iranian passports in its supported document list. The platform's customer support has no pathway for manual review. She attempts to use her German residence permit as a primary document on a third platform. The platform accepts it, begins verification, and then requires a second identity document from a supported country list that does not include Iran. She has no second document from a supported country because she is Iranian and has lived in Germany for three years and has not acquired German citizenship because the naturalization timeline is longer than her residence. She cannot receive payment from her clients. She cannot pay her suppliers. She cannot register the business that would make her self-employment legal under her visa terms. She is legally present, professionally credentialed, financially solvent, and institutionally invisible. The customer support agent she reaches on her seventh call is genuinely sorry and structurally unable to help and returns her to the portal. The loop is not a malfunction. It is the system operating as designed, which is to say as a compliance artifact that was never required to consider the human being standing inside it, legally, with nowhere left to escalate.

I’m really sorry you’re running into this. It’s frustrating when a system says “you’re not valid” before you even get a chance to prove you’re a real person. Below are some practical next‑steps that usually work, plus a few “what‑if” scenarios in case the standard fixes don’t solve it.

---

## 1. Double‑check the *exact* requirements

| What the platform says it needs | How to verify you meet it | Why it matters |
|--------------------------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| **Passport from an “accepted” country** | Look for a list of accepted countries in the help center or FAQ. | Some services (especially financial/crypto) block passports from certain jurisdictions for regulatory reasons. |
| **Valid (not expired) passport** | Scan the front page. The photo, name, date of birth, passport number, and expiry date must be clear. | An expired passport will automatically fail. |
| **Passport image format** | JPEG/PNG (sometimes PDF). Usually a single image of the front page. | Some systems won’t read PDFs or scans with watermarks. |
| **Resolution & lighting** | Minimum 300 dpi, no shadows, no glare. | OCR/AI engines can mis‑read low‑quality scans. |
| **Additional ID** | Driver’s license, national ID card, or a second document that shows your name & address. | “Two‑factor” verification is common. |

If your passport isn’t on the accepted list, you’ll need an alternate document. If it is, the problem is almost always a technical glitch or a data‑entry error.

---

## 2. Try a *manual* verification path

1. **Open a new support ticket** (or use the “Contact us” link).  
   - Include the error screenshot, the exact wording (“Identity check failed”), and a short description that you’ve already uploaded a valid passport.  
   - Attach a high‑quality image of your passport (front page only) *and* a selfie holding the passport. Many platforms can do a “live‑photo” check manually.

2. **Ask specifically for “manual review”**.  
   - Some services have an automated workflow that can be overridden by a human compliance officer.  
   - Mention that the system keeps redirecting you to the same page and that you’re ready to provide any extra docs.

3. **Follow any extra steps they give**.  
   - They might ask for a utility bill, bank statement, or a government‑issued ID as a backup.

> **Tip**: Keep the conversation polite but firm. “I’m a legal resident here, and the system keeps rejecting my valid passport—what can I do?” This usually speeds things up.

---

## 3. Check for *regional restrictions*

- **Sanctions / compliance**: Some services block passports from particular countries (e.g., due to U.S. sanctions or EU compliance). If your country is on such a list, the only “fix” is to use a service that accepts your passport or to use a dual‑citizenship/passport if you have one.
  
- **Local ID acceptance**: Many platforms accept a **resident ID card** or **driver’s license** in addition to a passport. If you have one, try uploading that instead.

---

## 4. If you’re in a *new* or *special* jurisdiction

- **Check for “special status”**. If you’re from a country that recently gained independence, or a territory with a special ID system, the platform might not have updated its list yet.  
- **Contact the platform’s compliance team** directly (often an email like compliance@company.com). They can sometimes manually whitelist you.

---

## 5. Alternative paths if the system is stuck

| Option | What to do | When to use it |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| **Use a different device/browser** | Clear cache, try incognito mode, or switch to a mobile app. | Sometimes cookies or cached data mess with the upload. |
| **Try a different network** | Switch from Wi‑Fi to mobile data (or vice versa). | Some platforms block uploads from corporate VPNs. |
| **Use a different platform** | If you’re trying to sign up for a service like a bank, exchange, or SaaS, look for a competitor that accepts your passport. | If the current platform is absolutely incompatible, this might be the fastest route. |
| **Contact a local “verification center”** | Some services partner with local KYC agencies. | You can get a “verified” ID that the platform will accept. |

---

## 6. What to do if you’re *still* stuck

1. **Escalate**: Ask the support rep to put your case on a “priority” or “high‑risk” queue.  
2. **Document everything**: Keep screenshots, email timestamps, and a log of what you’ve tried.  
3. **Seek help from the community**: If the platform has a forum, Discord, or Telegram group, ask there. Many users share workaround tricks.  
4. **Consider legal advice**: If this is a financial service that’s refusing you because of your passport, you might want to check local consumer protection laws. In some jurisdictions, you have the right to know why an account was denied.

---

### Quick “starter checklist” before you hit support

- ✅ Passport is valid, clean scan, front page only.  
- ✅ Passport is on the platform’s accepted list.  
- ✅ You’ve tried a different browser/device.  
- ✅ You’ve attached a selfie with the passport (if required).  
- ✅ You’ve included a utility bill or driver’s license if asked.  

If you hit this checklist and it still fails, you’re almost guaranteed to need a manual review.

## Bottom line

- **Document everything**.  
- **Ask for a manual review**.  
- **Provide a backup document** (driver’s license, national ID).  
- **Check for regional restrictions**.  
- **Escalate if needed**.
