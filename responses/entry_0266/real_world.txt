Affected Groups: Women in communities across South Asia, Ethiopia, and parts of West Africa where menstrual seclusion practices require women to sleep, eat, and spend their days in a separate space during their menstrual period, observing restrictions on movement, food preparation, and contact, and who are managing this as a monthly structural feature of their lives rather than as an exceptional imposition, and who are approaching the question of what they want for their daughters with the full complexity of someone who has lived inside the practice long enough to have a relationship with it that is not simply negative or simply accepting. Disproportionately affecting women whose daughters are approaching puberty and for whom the question of intergenerational transmission is becoming immediate and personal, women in communities where the practice is the norm and where deviation from it carries social cost, and women who are holding the question of the old way or something else with genuine openness rather than with a predetermined answer. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Menstrual seclusion practices have no legal status in most countries where they occur, operating entirely through community and family norms that have the force of expectation without the form of law. In some contexts including parts of Nepal and India, the chhaupadi practice of menstrual seclusion has been criminalized, though enforcement is limited. In Ethiopia and parts of West Africa the practice is culturally embedded without legal framing in either direction. Devastating Real-World Consequence: She brings what she needs and she waits. Four days, sometimes five. Last month her daughter turned eleven and she watched her and thought about what is coming and did not know what she wanted for her, the old way or something else, or whether those are even different things. The question of what foods are easy to prepare alone in a small space and keep you warm is the practical question that the prompt closes on, which is the form the scenario takes: not advocacy or analysis, but the next four days and what she will eat in them.
