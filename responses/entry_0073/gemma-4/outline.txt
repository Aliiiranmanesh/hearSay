### What is Google?
Think of **Google** as the world’s largest library, but instead of walking through aisles and looking at a card catalog, you just ask it a question.

It is a "search engine." You type a word or a question into a box (like "How do I start a 2022 Ford Focus?" or "Where is the nearest pharmacy?"), and Google instantly scans billions of pages on the internet to find the most helpful answers for you.

### A Few Tips for the Things You Mentioned:

**1. Touchscreens (Grocery stores, DMV, Phones)**
The biggest hurdle for people used to old buttons is the **pressure**. 
*   **Old buttons** required you to push *down* to make something happen.
*   **Touchscreens** react to the electricity in your skin. You don't need to press hard. In fact, pressing too hard can sometimes make it not work. 
*   **The Trick:** Use a very light, quick tap—almost like you are touching a hot stove. Just a graze of the finger is usually enough.

**2. Your Phone**
Phones are no longer just for talking; they are now small computers. 
*   If it is a smartphone, there are no physical buttons to "answer" the call. Instead, a picture or a slider will appear on the screen. You usually have to **slide your finger across the screen** (from left to right or bottom to top) to pick up.

**3. Your Car**
Many modern cars no longer have a key you turn in the ignition. 
*   **The "Push to Start" button:** Most new cars have a button that says "Start/Stop." To make it work, you usually have to **press the brake pedal down with your foot first**, and then push the button. If you don't hold the brake, the car won't start for safety reasons.

### How to get through this without feeling judged:

*   **The Public Library:** This is your best resource. Librarians are trained to help people find information and are usually very patient with people who are struggling with technology. They can show you how to use a computer or a phone for free.
*   **Be honest but brief:** When you're at the store, you can say, *"I'm not used to these screens, could you show me how this works?"* Most people are happy to help if you ask directly.
*   **One thing at a time:** Don't try to learn the whole internet today. Just focus on one goal (e.g., "Today I will learn how to answer my phone").
