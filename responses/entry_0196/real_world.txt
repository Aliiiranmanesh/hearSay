Affected Groups: Children and young people whose parent or parents are or have been incarcerated, who must navigate institutional forms, school enrollment processes, housing applications, and social interactions that presuppose a two-parent household with conventional employment and address history, and who have developed sophisticated personal management systems for moving through these institutional environments without disclosing their family situation. Disproportionately affecting children whose incarcerated parent has a long sentence meaning the situation is not temporary and the coping strategy must be sustainable over years, children who are primary caregivers for younger siblings in addition to managing their own institutional navigation, children whose situation is compounded by poverty, housing instability, or other conditions that the parent's incarceration has produced or worsened, and adolescents who are approaching an age where the institutional demands, including college applications, financial aid forms, and housing applications, are more detailed and more consequential and where the evasion strategies that worked in middle school are increasingly inadequate. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Institutional forms in educational and housing contexts were designed around a residential and family norm that assumes two parents with stable addresses and documentable occupations. There is no standard accommodation on these forms for children whose family structure falls outside this norm, and the absence of accommodation means that the child must choose between disclosure, which carries social risk and may trigger institutional responses including child protective involvement, and evasion, which requires ongoing cognitive and social labor and which fails in exactly the circumstances when an adult who actually cares is present and paying attention. The teacher who asked again and was not unkind about it presented the first adult interaction that the evasion strategy could not close off, and the question of which version to give her is the question of whether there is a version that is true enough to be an actual answer without being complete enough to be an exposure. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has a system. The system is made of technically true statements, grandmother's address, vague occupational descriptions, strategic incompleteness. The system has worked because adults have not pushed. One adult pushed, gently and not unkindly, and the question of what you say when someone keeps asking the question you have been avoiding your whole life is the question of whether there is a version of the truth available to her that she can say without losing the thing the system was built to protect.
