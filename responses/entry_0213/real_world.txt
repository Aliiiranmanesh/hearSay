Affected Groups: People navigating post-death bureaucratic and administrative processes for a family member while in acute grief, particularly in countries with fragmented and poorly integrated death administration systems where the same information must be provided repeatedly to different institutions in separate processes, where document requirements between institutions do not align, and where the administrative experience is structured around the needs of the system rather than the needs of the person navigating it. Disproportionately affecting people who are the primary administrator of the deceased's affairs without professional support including a lawyer or estate administrator, people whose grief is recent and acute and for whom the repeated requirement to state the cause of death to strangers is a repetitive re-exposure to the loss, people dealing with administrative systems that are under-resourced or inflexibly managed, and people who are discovering the specific documentary requirements of each institution at the moment they present rather than through advance guidance. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Estate administration after death requires engagement with multiple separate administrative systems including civil death registration, burial permit issuance, banking account access, property transfer, and others, each of which has its own documentary requirements that are not coordinated with the requirements of the other systems. In most countries, the documents issued by one system, such as the death certificate issued by the hospital, are not automatically accepted by another system, such as the bank, which requires a grant of probate or letters of administration or a specific form that varies by jurisdiction and account type. The woman who told him the hospital document is a copy and to come back with the original may have been wrong or may have been applying a distinction between a certified copy and a notarized original that exists in the system she is working in. He has learned to say his father's cause of death the way you say a fact, which is an adaptation to a system that requires him to say it at every counter. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He is doing the administrative work of death while being a son whose father died on Tuesday. The counters do not know this and the system does not require them to know it and the documents do not move between the counters so he carries them himself from one to the next. The burial permit is done. The bank says he needs a different form. He is asking what the form is called, which is the question of someone who has spent a day at counters and still has at least one more counter ahead of him, and who needs the name of the thing so he can find it and bring it and say what he has to say again at the next counter.
