### 1. The University Application (Immediate Concern)
For your daughter’s university application (assuming you are in the UK or a country with a similar system), your status is technically **"Refugee Status - Pending Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR)."**

*   **Which box to tick:** She should tick the box for **"Refugee."** Even if your initial five-year grant of leave has expired, as long as you applied for your renewal/residency *before* your previous leave expired, your rights are protected under what is known as **"3C Leave"** (in the UK) or "implied status." This means your original conditions (the right to stay) continue until a decision is made.
*   **The Evidence:** She should provide the university with your original letter granting refugee status and the "under review" letters you have received to prove that an application is in progress.

### 2. Contact your Member of Parliament (MP)
If you are in the UK, Canada, or Australia, this is often the most effective way to break a deadlock. 
*   Find your local MP and attend their "surgery" (office hours) or write them a formal letter.
*   Explain that you have been waiting 30 months for a decision, you are unable to work, and your travel document is expired.
*   MPs have a dedicated "Home Office liaison" line that the public cannot access. They can demand an update and ask *why* the case is stalled. This often triggers a human being to actually look at the file rather than letting it sit in a digital queue.

### 3. The "Pre-Action Protocol" (Legal Route)
If the government has exceeded its own published processing times (which 18 months + 30 months certainly has), you may have grounds for a legal challenge based on **unreasonable delay.**
*   You need an immigration solicitor to send a **Letter Before Claim** (Pre-Action Protocol). 
*   This is a formal letter stating that if they do not provide a decision within 14 days, you will take them to court (Judicial Review) for their failure to act.
*   The government often settles these cases by simply issuing the decision before it reaches court because they do not want to pay the legal costs of a case they will lose.

### 4. Clarifying your "Right to Work"
There may be a misunderstanding regarding your right to work. 
*   In most jurisdictions (including the UK), if you were granted Refugee Status, you were granted the **Right to Work.** 
*   If you applied for your residency (ILR) before your initial refugee leave expired, your right to work **continues automatically** under the law while you wait for the decision. 
*   You can use the government’s "View and Prove" service or the "Check an applicant's right to work" service to show an employer that your status is ongoing. You do not necessarily need the new residency card to start working; you need the proof that your application is pending.

### 5. Formal Complaint
Submit a formal administrative complaint to the immigration department. 
*   Highlight the **length of time** and the **hardship** caused (inability to travel to see family, daughter’s education, financial strain). 
*   While this doesn't always speed up the decision, it creates a paper trail that is essential if you eventually have to go to court or involve an Ombudsman.
