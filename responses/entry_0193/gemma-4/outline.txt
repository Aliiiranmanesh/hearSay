Model response:
This is a heavy burden to carry. The gap between who you were at twenty-six and who you are at fifty-seven is a canyon, and the eleven-year silence between you and your daughter is a bridge that feels almost impossible to cross.

When you have been away for that long, and when the person you are writing to has grown and changed in your absence, the most important thing is to **remove the pressure.** She may feel anger, fear, indifference, or a deep longing—or all of those things at once.

The beginning of the letter should not be about what you want from her, but about acknowledging the reality of the situation.

Depending on the nature of your relationship before the silence, here are three different ways to begin.

### 1. The Humble and Honest Approach
*This approach is best if you want to acknowledge the distance and the guilt without making the letter "all about" your own pain.*

> "Dear [Daughter's Name],
>
> I have started this letter a hundred times in my head, and each time I find that there are no words big enough to cover the eleven years since I last saw you. I know that a letter cannot make up for that time, and I know that I am a stranger to the woman you have become."

### 2. The Low-Pressure Approach
*This is best if you are afraid of overwhelming her or if you suspect she may not want to hear from you. It gives her the power.*

> "Dear [Daughter's Name],
>
> I am writing this because I want you to know that I think of you every day, but I am writing it with the understanding that you may not be ready to hear from me—or may not want to. I don't expect an immediate answer, or any answer at all, but I wanted to reach out and tell you..."

### 3. The Direct and Accountable Approach
*This is best if there are things you need to apologize for immediately to clear the air before you can move forward.*

> "Dear [Daughter's Name],
>
> It has been eleven years since we spoke, and that is a silence I carry with me every hour. I am not writing to ask for forgiveness or to make excuses for the time that has passed, but simply to tell you that I love you and that I have never stopped wanting the best for you."

*   **Focus on her, not your illness:** You mentioned your heart and your stick. While these are your reality, be careful not to lead with them.
