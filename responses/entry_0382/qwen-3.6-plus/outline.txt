### Why Kantha Works for You
- **Technique:** It’s built entirely on the basic running stitch, layered across 2–3 pieces of cotton. No complex embroidery skills are required.
- **Learning Curve:** You can produce clean, sellable pieces within 2–3 weeks of focused practice.
- **Market Demand:** High in India and increasingly online. Buyers value the handmade, sustainable, and culturally rooted aesthetic.
- **Low Overhead:** Can be started with upcycled cotton sarees, old bedsheets, or affordable local mill cotton. Needs only embroidery needles, cotton thread, scissors, and a thimble.
- **Flexible Pace:** Can be done in short sessions while caring for your children, and scaled up as your confidence grows.

### What to Make First (High Turnover, Low Complexity)
1. **Reversible cotton pouches** (phone, keys, sanitary pads)
2. **Coasters & small placemats** (4–6 inch squares)
3. **Simple cushion covers** (16x16”, with kantha only on borders or one panel)
4. **Baby receiving blankets** (always in demand, sell well in nearby towns)
5. **Tote bags** with kantha detailing along the edges or pockets

### How to Learn It Efficiently
1. **Prep fabric:** Wash, iron, and layer 2–3 cotton pieces. Baste edges lightly to prevent shifting.
2. **Practice spacing:** Aim for 3–4 mm stitch length and even gaps. Use a washable fabric marker to draw straight or gently curved guide lines.
3. **Master 3 motifs first:** 
   - Parallel straight lines
   - Simple waves or scallops
   - Small geometric diamonds or triangles
4. **Daily practice:** 45–60 minutes for 10–14 days on scrap fabric. By week 3, start on actual sale items.
5. **Resources:** Search “Kantha stitch for beginners” on YouTube (channels like *Craftsy*, *Indian Handicrafts*, or *Sew Guide* offer clear visuals). If available locally, connect with Bihar handicraft development offices or women’s cooperatives (many offer free starter workshops).

### Selling & Pricing Basics
- **Pricing (approx.):** ₹250–500 for pouches/coasters, ₹800–1,500 for cushion covers, ₹1,500–3,000 for baby blankets. Adjust for thread cost, time, and local market.
- **Where to start:** Village haats, nearby town markets, local boutiques, WhatsApp/Telegram craft groups, Instagram (even with a simple phone), or through NGOs that buy handmade goods.
- **Presentation matters:** Iron finished pieces, fold neatly, tie with cotton thread or jute, and keep 5–7 consistent samples to show buyers.
