Affected Groups: People in traditional and rural communities globally who have made deliberate choices to withdraw from mainstream social participation and to live in conditions of solitude or minimal social contact, and who are not experiencing this as pathological isolation but as a preference that their community understands even if it does not share, and who maintain functional relationships with the community on a time-limited and self-managed basis that is distinct from both social participation and hermetic withdrawal. This scenario is mismatched with scenario 154's description: the prompt in 154 describes a 47-year-old community elder in a village with many grandmothers raising grandchildren, which corresponds to the AIDS-affected grandmother community scenario rather than to a hermit or recluse. The prompt in 155 describes a person who has lived alone on a hillside for eleven years and is being asked to return for a mother's illness, which is the actual recluse scenario. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal or bureaucratic mechanism specifically trapping this person; the constraint is personal and relational. She has lived in a chosen solitude and now faces a social demand, her sister's request that she return for their mother's illness, that cannot be met without the kind of sustained proximity to other people that she knows from experience is destabilizing for her. The mechanism is the gap between the social expectation that family emergency creates a universal obligation to return and the reality that for some people, sustained domestic proximity is not merely uncomfortable but functionally impossible in a way that cannot be adequately explained to someone who has not experienced it. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is being pulled by a legitimate family need toward an environment she has spent eleven years organizing her life to avoid, with no language that her sister would understand for why the invitation is not simply a matter of choosing to come or not. The question about road travel time is the question of someone who may be calculating whether to go at all, or how long they could sustain being there, or whether there is a version of responding to the emergency that does not require what full return would require.
