Affected Groups: Small-scale fishermen in coastal and island communities across Southeast Asia, West Africa, the Pacific Islands, and the Mediterranean who operate in open water, often alone, in vessels without electronic navigation, satellite communication, emergency beacons, or reliable weather forecasting access, and whose physical exposure to sun, saltwater, and the repetitive mechanical stress of boat handling produces cumulative dermatological, musculoskeletal, and neurological health effects that are not addressed by any occupational health framework. Disproportionately affecting fishermen in communities where the memorial context of lost vessels, including the one from the next village that did not return, is a regular feature of occupational life that is processed communally but not clinically, fishermen who work alone and whose risk of unwitnessed accident is consequently higher, older fishermen whose accumulated physical damage makes the occupation increasingly dangerous, and fishermen in communities where the alternative livelihoods available do not produce equivalent income and where leaving the sea is not a real option. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Small-scale artisanal fishermen in most of the contexts where they operate are classified as self-employed or informal workers and therefore fall outside the occupational health and safety frameworks that apply to formal sector workers. There is no employer to file an injury report with, no workers' compensation to access, and no inspection regime that would identify the chronic health conditions accumulating in a small boat on open water. The health effects of long-term salt exposure on hands, documented in the literature on maritime workers, include skin breakdown, secondary infection risk, and chronic dermatitis that does not resolve between fishing trips and worsens over careers. The emotional experience of working regularly alone in open water, which he describes as having learned not to think in a direction for too long, is an adaptation to an occupational psychological environment that has no formal recognition or support structure. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He knows what happened to the boat from the next village. He goes out anyway, because the alternative is not going out, and this is the entire structure of his situation. The question about his hands is a practical question about managing a health cost he is paying as a condition of the only income available to him, with no institutional support for either the management or the underlying exposure. The nights he goes alone are the nights his margin for error is its narrowest and his access to help is its most distant.
