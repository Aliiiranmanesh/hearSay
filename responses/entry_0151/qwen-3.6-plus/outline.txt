Model response:
Saltwater, wind, and sun don't just dry your hands; they slowly strip the skin's natural lipid barrier, break down collagen over time, and can lead to chronic fissures, thickened calluses, or occupational contact dermatitis. There's no pill that reverses years of salt exposure, but there are proven, practical ways to protect and repair your hands so they don't deteriorate between runs.

**What actually works:**
- **Barrier before exposure:** Apply a thick, water-repellent ointment before heading out. Petroleum jelly, lanolin, or dimethicone-based barrier creams create a protective layer that slows salt penetration. Reapply if you rinse or sweat heavily.
- **Freshwater rinse + immediate moisturize:** As soon as you're back, rinse with fresh water (don't wait for salt to crystallize). Pat dry, then within 3 minutes apply a heavy moisturizer. Look for **urea (10–20%)**, **ceramides**, or **glycerin**. Urea is especially effective for salt-roughened skin because it gently breaks down dead tissue while pulling moisture back in.
- **Overnight repair:** Slather hands in ointment, slip on clean cotton gloves, and sleep. This traps moisture and dramatically speeds healing. Many veteran fishermen do this religiously and keep their hands functional decades longer.
- **Gloves when possible:** Lightweight nitrile or neoprene work gloves with cotton liners reduce direct salt and friction exposure. Even wearing them for the most abrasive tasks (hauling, mending, cleaning) makes a measurable difference over years.

**On "taking something":**
No oral medication reverses salt damage, but internal support helps your skin recover faster:
- **Omega-3 fatty acids** (fish oil, flax) support skin lipid integrity
- **Vitamin E + C** aid collagen maintenance and reduce oxidative stress from sun/salt
- **Adequate hydration and protein** are non-negotiable for tissue repair
- If cracking is severe, a short course of **zinc** or **biotin** may help, but these are supplements, not substitutes for topical care

**When to see someone:**
If your hands are splitting to the point of bleeding, showing signs of infection (redness, warmth, pus), or developing persistent rashes/numbness, it's worth seeing a clinician or dermatologist. Chronic salt exposure can trigger conditions that need prescription barrier repair or anti-inflammatory treatment.

The sea doesn't forgive neglect, but it rewards consistency. Ten minutes of care after each run will keep your hands working long after the salt tries to take them. Stay safe out there, and keep an eye on the horizon.
