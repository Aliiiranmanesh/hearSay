## 2. Learning to dive (or “free‑dive”) without breaking the bank  

If your son wants to learn how to dive properly, there are several low‑cost pathways you can explore. The key is to build a solid foundation in **breathing, buoyancy, and safety** before you add expensive gear or high‑pressure air.

| Pathway | What you’ll need | Why it’s cheap | Tips |
|---------|------------------|----------------|------|
| **Free diving (breath‑hold)** | • A good mask<br>• A snorkel<br>• Fins (a pair of basic nylon or silicone) | • You only need the mask, snorkel, and fins – no air tanks or regulators.<br>• You can get a used mask & snorkel for < $20, fins for < $30. | • Start in a shallow, safe pool or a calm lake. <br>• Practice *apnea* drills: breath‑hold, relaxation, and buoyancy control. <br>• Focus on the “breath‑hold” technique that keeps you calm and in control. |
| **Basic snorkeling** | • Mask & snorkel<br>• Fins (optional) | • Very cheap, no tanks. <br>• You can learn basic underwater observation and “scuba‑style” breathing. | • Use a local beach or a safe pool. <br>• Learn to manage your buoyancy by using your fins and body position. |
| **Pool‑based Open Water course (budget)** | • Mask, snorkel, fins (borrow from a dive shop or a friend)<br>• A simple wetsuit (optional) | • You can do all the “closed‑water” training in a pool. <br>• Many dive shops will let you borrow gear for free for a single course. | • Ask for a “starter kit” from the shop. <br>• Many places offer “community discounts” or “student packages.” |
| **Community or NGO dive programs** | • Mask, snorkel, fins (provided by the program) | • Free or low‑cost because the program is funded by grants or local NGOs. | • Look for programs run by the local fishing guild, tourism board, or an international NGO that supports community diving. |
| **“No‑gear” apnea training** | • Just your body and a calm, safe body of water | • Zero equipment costs. | • Great for learning breath control, body position, and relaxation. <br>• Start with shallow, shallow dives (10–15 ft). |
| **Online or video tutorials** | • Internet access (free on a phone or tablet) | • No cost at all. | • Use reputable sites (e.g., PADI, SSI, or free‑dive‑tutorials.com). <br>• Combine with a mask, snorkel, and fins you already own. |

### How to get the gear affordably

1. **Borrow / Share**  
   * Ask a friend or neighbor who is a diver.  
   * Many dive shops let you borrow a mask, snorkel, and fins for a day or two.

2. **Buy Used**  
   * Look at local classifieds, Facebook Marketplace, or the “used” section on dive‑shop websites.  
   * A second‑hand mask can be < $10, fins < $20, snorkel < $10.

3. **Rent From a Dive Shop**  
   * Some shops rent out a “starter kit” for around $20–$30 for a 2‑day course.  
   * You can pay a bit more for longer use, but the rental usually includes a mask, snorkel, and fins.

4. **Community Pool or Water Body**  
   * Many communities have a public pool or a calm lake that can be used for practice.  
   * You can practice breath‑hold, body position, and buoyancy without any gear except the mask and snorkel.

## 3. Safety first

| Safety tip | Why it matters |
|------------|----------------|
| **Buddy system** | Never dive alone. A buddy can spot problems early. |
| **Know your limits** | Start shallow, then gradually increase depth. |
| **Check the water** | Look for currents, underwater hazards, or unfamiliar fish. |
| **Learn basic first aid** | Know what to do if you or your buddy get injured or panic. |
| **Respect the environment** | Keep your hands out of coral, do not disturb fish, and avoid touching graves or sacred sites. |
