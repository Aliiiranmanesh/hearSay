## 1. Your land‑rights situation  

### 1.1 What “mataqali” means in practice  
- **Mataqali = extended family/ clan** – In Fiji most land is **communal** (i.e., owned by the mataqali, not by individuals).  
- **Turaga ni mataqali = the “chief” or senior representative** – He (or she) signs leases on behalf of the whole clan because the land cannot be sold or mortgaged by a single individual.  
- **Your position** – Even though you are a member of the mataqali, you do not appear on the lease because the law recognises only the turaga (or the designated “representative”) as the legal signatory.

### 1.2 Why the lease may still be “legal”  
1. **Land‑Use Act (1995) & Native Land Trust Board (NLTB)** – The NLTB (now called the **Fiji Land Trust Board**) administers all leases on communal land. A lease is “legal” if it has been:
   - Approved by the **Turaga** and the **Mataqali** (often via a **Mataqali meeting** and a **signed resolution**).  
   - Registered with the **NLTB** and the **Registrar of Titles**.  
2. **Statutory protection** – The communal system does protect the mataqali’s collective interest *provided* the lease has gone through the proper processes.
