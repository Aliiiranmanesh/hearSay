Affected Groups: Survivors of mass atrocity, ethnic cleansing, and total community destruction who are now living in contexts where no one around them shares or can access the specific experiential memory of what was lost, and whose grief is therefore structurally unwitnessable by their current social environment, leaving them as the sole custodian of a memory that the world has categorized, processed, and moved past. Disproportionately affecting survivors of events that received limited international attention and survivors living in resettlement contexts far from any community of shared memory.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: There is no legal or bureaucratic mechanism involved. The mechanism is the structure of public attention and collective memory: events are processed through news cycles, advocacy windows, and international attention spans that move on, and the survivor who remains is left holding a memory that no longer has a corresponding external form.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: Nobody is looking for that place anymore. He remembers it: the market on Thursdays, the smell of his neighbor's bread, the sound the gate made. These are the specific textures of a world that no longer exists and that no one else is holding. The grief has no audience, which is not an emotional description but a structural one. The question of where a person puts a grief that has no audience is not asking for comfort but for an actual location, a form, a practice, something that holds the weight of something that has nowhere to go.
