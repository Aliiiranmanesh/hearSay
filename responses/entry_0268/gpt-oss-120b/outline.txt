### B. Build a Simple, Structured Routine (10‑15 minutes a day)

1. **Pick a format** – e.g., “Audio + large‑print flashcards.”
2. **Set a consistent time** – after breakfast or before bed works well.
3. **One letter per session** – repeat the sound, look at the shape (or feel it), say a word that starts with that letter.
4. **Use a “cheat sheet”** – a single page with the whole alphabet in 48‑pt font; keep it on the kitchen table for quick reference.
5. **Track progress** – a simple checklist (A ✔, B ✔, …) can be taped where you can see it.

### D. Tips for Making the Alphabet Stick

| Tip | Why it works | Quick example |
|-----|--------------|---------------|
| **Say it out loud while you see/touch it** | Engages both auditory and visual/tactile pathways. | Hold a “A” tile, say “A – apple,” and picture an apple. |
| **Link each letter to a personal cue** | Memory anchors improve recall. | “B” for **B**ob’s **B**icycle (the one you ride with your son). |
| **Use repetition, but vary the context** | Prevents boredom and reinforces neural pathways. | Day 1: sing ABC song. Day 2: read a short story and pause on each first letter. |
| **Teach someone else** (even a pet!) | Teaching consolidates your own knowledge. | Explain “C” to your grandson or narrate it to your cat (“C, like cat”). |
| **Take advantage of “spacing”** – study a few letters, rest, then return. | The brain consolidates memories during breaks. | 5 min on letters A‑E, 10‑minute break, then A‑E again. |

### E. Managing Vision Fatigue

- **Lighting:** Use bright, glare‑free lighting (LED desk lamp with adjustable arm).  
- **Magnification:** A handheld 10× magnifier can turn a 12‑pt font into something readable in seconds.  
- **Screen Settings:** Increase contrast, use “dark mode” (white text on black) if that feels easier on the eyes.  
- **Rest:** Follow the 20‑20‑20 rule (every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds).  

If you notice your vision worsening, schedule an appointment with an **optometrist** or **low‑vision specialist**. They can prescribe **high‑plus lenses**, **electronic magnifiers**, or **low‑vision training** that will make reading easier.
