Affected Groups: Adult illiterates in countries with mandatory bureaucratic documentation requirements, where government correspondence, utility bills, legal notices, and medical communications arrive in written form and require written responses, and who have been dependent on children or other family members to manage this correspondence, a dependency that inverts the normal parent-child authority relationship and places adult responsibility on children at ages when that responsibility is developmentally inappropriate. Disproportionately affecting women in communities where girls' education was deprioritized in prior generations, people in rural areas where schooling was interrupted by agricultural demands, people who immigrated from communities with lower literacy rates into highly bureaucratic host societies, and older people whose literacy level was functional in simpler administrative environments that have since become more document-intensive.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Bureaucratic systems in most countries assume universal literacy and provide no accessible alternative to written communication as a standard feature of government service delivery. Audio or visual alternatives where they exist are supplementary rather than primary, and the person who cannot read is expected to find their own accommodation. The dependency on a fifteen-year-old who has been handling this since he was nine is not a family choice but the outcome of a system that has no accessible alternative for people it nominally serves. The son's face doing the thing it does when he doesn't want her to see what he is feeling is a description of a child managing adult information about adult problems while also managing the emotional labor of protecting his mother from what he knows.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He took the letter with him when he left. She does not know what it said. She is asking how to learn the alphabet when you are older and your eyes are not what they were, which is the most direct available response to the dependency she has correctly identified as a problem: she is trying to become capable of reading her own correspondence before the next letter arrives. The question about older eyes is a practical question about adult literacy learning with age-related visual impairment, and it has real answers involving large-print materials, magnification, and literacy programs designed for adult learners.
