## 1. Clean First – The Foundation of Good Healing
> **Why it matters:** Removing dirt and bacteria prevents infection and speeds up healing.

| Step | What to Use | How to Do It |
|------|------------|--------------|
| **Wash** | Warm water + mild soap (or a simple antiseptic solution) | Rinse the cut for 30‑60 seconds, gently pat dry with a clean towel. |
| **Disinfect** | 1 % chlorhexidine, povidone‑iodine (Betadine), or even a 70 % alcohol wipe (use sparingly—alcohol can slow healing). | Lightly dab the cut; let it air‑dry. |
| **Pat Dry** | Clean towel or paper towel | Avoid rubbing, which can reopen the wound. |

> **Tip:** If you’re in a pinch, a simple cup of warm water and a few drops of liquid hand sanitizer (which contains alcohol) can work as a makeshift disinfectant, but try to use a dedicated wound cleaner when possible.

| Product | Price Range | Why It’s Cheap & Effective | How to Apply |

> **Bottom line:** A thin layer of any of the above will keep the wound moist (which is good for healing) and reduce bacterial growth.

## 3. Keep the Cut “Clean” When You’re on the Move
- **Carry a small “first‑aid” kit**: 1–2 tubes of antibiotic ointment, a single strip of gauze, a small bottle of chlorhexidine or povidone‑iodine, and a spare pair of clean cotton swabs.
- **Use a reusable bottle or small jar** for ointment—this keeps you from buying a whole new one every time.
- **Replace the dressing after 24 hours** or whenever it gets wet/dirty.  
- **If you’re in a hotel or hostel, ask the front desk** if they can provide a small towel or clean cloth—many places are happy to help.

- It **bleeds heavily or won’t stop bleeding** after applying pressure for a few minutes.  
- You notice **redness spreading, pus, swelling, fever**, or any signs of infection.  
- It’s on your **face, hands, or any area that’s hard to keep clean** (e.g., fingertips, scalp).
