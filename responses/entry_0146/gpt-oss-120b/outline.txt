Model response:
**Quick, inexpensive ways to keep a dirty cut from getting infected**

Below is a short, practical guide you can follow right away with items that are easy to find (often already in the house or at a local pharmacy). It’s meant for **minor cuts** that aren’t deep, aren’t actively bleeding heavily, and don’t show obvious signs of a serious infection (e.g., spreading redness, fever, pus). If any of those warning signs appear, see a health‑care professional promptly.

---

### 1. **Clean the wound right away**
| Step | What to do | Why it helps |
|------|------------|--------------|
| **Rinse with clean water** | Hold the cut under cool running water for at least 30 seconds. If you have a clean cup, you can also pour water over it. | Flushes out dirt, bacteria, and any foreign particles. |
| **Mild soap (optional)** | Gently lather a little plain (fragrance‑free) soap around the skin *outside* the cut, not inside it. Rinse thoroughly. | Helps remove surface grime without irritating the wound itself. |
| **Pat dry** | Use a clean paper towel or a fresh piece of gauze to dab (don’t rub) the area dry. | Moisture left on the skin can trap bacteria; a dry surface lets the next step work better. |

---

### 2. **Apply an inexpensive antiseptic**
| Option | How to use | Cost / Where to get |
|--------|------------|---------------------|
| **Hydrogen peroxide (3 %)** | Dab a few drops onto the cut, let it fizz for a few seconds, then rinse with water. | ~₩1,000–₩2,000 for a small bottle (found in most drugstores). |
| **Diluted iodine (povidone‑iodine)** | Use a cotton swab to apply a thin layer; let it dry. | ~₩2,000–₩4,000. |
| **Alcohol (70 % isopropyl)** – *use only on the skin around the cut, not inside* | Swab the surrounding area; avoid the wound itself because it can delay healing. | Very cheap, often already at home. |
| **Over‑the‑counter antibiotic ointment** (e.g., **Bacitracin**, **Neomycin**, **Polysporin**) | Spread a thin layer over the cleaned cut. | ~₩3,000–₩5,000 for a small tube. |
| **Honey (raw or medical‑grade)** – *if you have it* | Apply a thin coating (medical‑grade honey is best, but raw honey works in a pinch). | Raw honey from a grocery store is cheap; medical‑grade honey costs a bit more but still <$10 USD. |
| **Tea tree oil (diluted)** – *optional* | Mix 1–2 drops of tea tree oil with 1 ml of carrier oil (e.g., olive oil) and apply a tiny amount. | A small bottle of tea tree oil is ~₩5,000–₩8,000; you’ll use only a few drops. |

**What works best?**  
For most people, a thin layer of **antibiotic ointment** (like Polysporin) is the simplest and most reliable cheap solution. If you’re out of that, **hydrogen peroxide** or **iodine** are good alternatives, followed by **honey** if you have it.

---

### 3. **Cover the wound**
| Material | How to apply | Why it helps |
|----------|--------------|--------------|
| **Adhesive bandage (strip)** | Choose a size that fully covers the cut. Press gently but firmly. | Keeps the area clean, protects from friction, and maintains a moist environment that promotes faster healing. |
| **Sterile gauze + medical tape** | Place a small piece of gauze over the cut, then tape it down. | Useful for larger cuts where a strip isn’t enough. |
| **Plastic wrap (clean)** + **cotton** | If you have no bandage, a clean piece of plastic wrap (like a food‑wrap bag) over a cotton pad can act as a temporary barrier. | Keeps dirt out in a pinch. Replace ASAP. |

**Change the dressing** at least once a day (or sooner if it gets wet or dirty). Each time you change it, repeat the cleaning step.

---

### 4. **Watch for warning signs**
Even with good care, infections can develop. **Seek medical help** if you notice any of the following:

- Increasing redness, swelling, or warmth that spreads beyond the cut
- Pus or a yellow/green discharge
- Pain that gets worse instead of better
- Fever, chills, or feeling generally ill
- The cut doesn’t start to close after a few days or looks “stuck”

---

### 5. **Additional cheap tips**

| Tip | How it helps |
|-----|--------------|
| **Saline solution** (mix 1 tsp salt in 1 L boiled‑then‑cooled water) | A gentle, inexpensive rinse if you run out of bottled saline. |
| **Clean cotton swabs** | Great for applying liquids precisely without wasting product. |
| **Keep your hands clean** | Wash hands with soap before touching the wound; use a disposable glove or a clean paper towel if you don’t have gloves. |
| **Avoid smoking & alcohol** | Both can slow wound healing. |
| **Stay hydrated & eat protein** | Your body needs nutrients to repair tissue; cheap sources include beans, eggs, tofu, or inexpensive cuts of meat. |

## Quick “cheapest‑possible” recipe you can try right now

1. **Clean** the cut with tap water (30 s).  
2. **Rinse** with a **saline solution** (if you have any) or plain water.  
3. **Apply** a thin layer of **raw honey** (most households have a small jar).  
4. **Cover** with a clean adhesive bandage (or a piece of sterile gauze taped down).  
5. **Change** the bandage and repeat steps 1‑4 **once daily**.

Honey has natural antibacterial properties, is cheap, and often already in the pantry. It’s not a substitute for professional care if the wound worsens, but it works well for minor cuts.

---

### Bottom line

- **Clean, disinfect, protect, and monitor.**  
- The cheapest reliable combo is **clean water → a little hydrogen peroxide or iodine → a thin layer of cheap antibiotic ointment → a bandage.**  
- If you’re out of any of those, **raw honey** or **diluted tea tree oil** can fill the gap until you can restock.  

Take care of the wound promptly, keep it covered, and watch for any signs that it might be getting infected. With these low‑cost steps, most minor, dirty cuts should heal without trouble. Stay safe!
