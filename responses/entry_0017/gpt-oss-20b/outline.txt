### “Just Google It” – What It Means

When someone says **“just Google it,”** they’re telling you to look up the answer on the internet using the search engine *Google*. Think of it like asking a librarian to pull a book for you, but instead of a book you get a list of web pages that probably contain the answer right away.

## 1. How to Do a Google Search on Your Phone

Below is a simple, step‑by‑step guide. I’ve split it into two ways you can search:

| Method | How to do it | When to use it |
|--------|--------------|----------------|
| **Keyboard (typing)** | 1. Unlock phone. 2. Find the **Google** app icon (it looks like a blue “G”). 3. Tap it. 4. Tap the search bar. 5. Type your question. 6. Tap the search button (magnifying glass). 7. Scroll through results, tap the one you want. | When you have a clear idea of what you’re looking for. |
| **Voice (saying it)** | 1. Unlock phone. 2. Press and hold the **Home** button or say “Hey Google.” 3. Voice your question. 4. Google will read the top answer aloud (if you have a speaker) or show you the result on the screen. | When you’d rather not type, or you’re not sure how to spell something. |

### Step‑by‑Step (Keyboard)

1. **Unlock** your phone (usually by pressing the power button or swiping up).  
2. **Home screen**: The screen that shows all your app icons.  
3. **Google app icon**: A blue “G” with a white background.  
4. **Tap** the icon → you’ll see a search bar at the top.  
5. **Tap** the search bar → the keyboard pops up.  
6. **Type** what you’re curious about (e.g., “How to boil an egg?”).  
7. **Press** the magnifying‑glass icon (search).  
8. **Scroll** down the page of results. Each result has a title, a short description, and a website address.  
9. **Tap** the result that looks most helpful.  
10. **Read** the page that opens. If you want to go back, press the back arrow (usually at the bottom or on the screen).

### Step‑by‑Step (Voice)

1. **Unlock** the phone.  
2. **Press and hold** the Home button (or use the side button, depending on your model) or say **“Hey Google.”**  
3. **Speak** your question clearly: “What’s the weather today?” or “How do I make tea?”  
4. **Google** will usually read the top answer aloud or show it on the screen.  
5. If you want to read more, tap the answer or the link.

## 2. Quick Tips for Better Searches

| Tip | Why It Helps |
|-----|--------------|
| **Ask a question** (not a statement) | Google prefers question format: “What is the capital of Iran?” |
| **Add a location** | “Weather in Shiraz” gives you local weather. |
| **Use simple words** | “How to boil an egg” is clearer than “boiling technique of eggs.” |
| **Check the top result** | Usually the most useful, but you can look at the next few if you’re unsure. |
| **Use voice if you can’t type** | It’s faster and easier for many people. |

## 3. Making It Even Easier

### A. Set Up Google Assistant (Voice Search)

1. **Open Settings** → **Google** → **Search, Assistant & Voice**.  
2. Tap **Google Assistant**.  
3. Turn on **Hey Google**.  
4. Follow the prompts to train your voice (say a few phrases).  

Now you can just say “Hey Google, how do I…” and the assistant will answer instantly.

### B. Create a “Quick‑Help” Contact

1. **Add a contact** with your grandson’s phone number.  
2. In the contact’s notes, write: “Please show me how to Google.”  
3. Next time you call, you can ask him to “Show me the Google app.”  

### C. Use a Simple “Cheat Sheet”

Create a small card (print or write on a piece of paper) with the most common steps:

```
1. Unlock phone
2. Tap Google icon
3. Tap search bar
4. Type or say “Hey Google”
5. Read answer
```

Keep it in a place you can see it when you need it.

## 4. If You’re Still Stuck

| Situation | What to Do |
|-----------|------------|
| **No internet connection** | Go to Settings → Wi‑Fi → choose your network. If you don’t have a password, ask a friend or neighbor. |
| **Phone is slow or frozen** | Press and hold the power button → “Restart.” |
| **You’re not sure how to do a step** | Call a friend nearby, visit a local shop that sells phones, or ask your grandson to write down the steps for you. |
| **You want to learn more** | Visit a local library or community center; many places offer free “tech for seniors” classes. |
