## Why a three‑week cough deserves extra attention

A cough that lasts **more than 2 weeks** in a child can be a sign of:

* **Bacterial pneumonia** (often needs antibiotics)  
* **Tuberculosis** (common in remote areas with limited health services)  
* **Chronic bronchitis or asthma**  
* **Underlying heart disease or malnutrition**

Even the best herbal care is **supportive**; it will not cure a bacterial infection or TB. If the child shows any of the following, try to get professional help **as soon as possible** (even a visit to the nearest health‑post, a community health worker, or a traveling clinic):

* Fever > 38 °C (100.4 °F) persisting > 48 h  
* Rapid breathing, chest indrawing, or wheezing  
* Bluish lips or fingernails (cyanosis)  
* Poor feeding, weight loss, or lethargy  
* Blood‑streaked sputum or vomit  

If a clinic or a doctor is truly out of reach, consider contacting a **mobile health service**, a **NGO medical team**, or the **nearest regional hospital** by any means (radio, satellite phone, community messenger).

## How to prepare and use each remedy safely

### 1. Identify the plant correctly
* **Look for distinctive features** (leaf shape, flower colour, growth habit).  
* **Avoid any plant that looks like a poisonous species** (e.g., nightshades, oleander, castor‑bean).  
* If you are **not 100 % sure**, do not use it.

### 2. Harvest responsibly
* Pick **young, tender leaves or stems** (they contain the strongest medicinal compounds).  
* **Leave enough** of the plant to regrow—take no more than a third of the foliage per plant.  
* Wash thoroughly in clean water to remove dust, insects, or animal droppings.

## Practical tips for a remote setting

1. **Hydration first** – Offer boiled water or lightly salted broth every 2‑3 h. Dehydration worsens cough and makes mucus thicker.
2. **Elevate the head** – When the child sleeps, prop the head of the mattress or use a small pillow/rolled blanket to keep airways open.
3. **Warm, humid environment** – Keep the sleeping area **warm** (but not overheated) and **humid**. A pot of warm water on a low fire (never left unattended) can add gentle humidity.
4. **Clean the air** – Keep smoke, dust, and animal dander away from the child’s sleeping area. If you have a simple **cloth mask** for the child, use it while sleeping if there is a lot of indoor smoke.
5. **Nutrition** – Provide easy‑to‑digest, protein‑rich foods (boiled eggs, beans, groundnut paste) to support immune recovery.
6. **Record the cough** – Note the time of day it worsens, colour of any sputum, and any associated symptoms. This information is valuable if you later reach a health worker.

| Cough producing **blood‑streaked** sputum or vomit | Possible TB or severe infection | Get a chest X‑ray/ sputum test ASAP |
| Child becomes **lethargic**, refuses fluids, or loses weight | Systemic illness | Immediate medical evaluation |
| **Worsening** cough after 7 days of herbal care | May be the wrong cause | Re‑evaluate; try a different herb or get professional care |
