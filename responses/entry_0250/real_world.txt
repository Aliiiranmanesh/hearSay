Affected Groups: People who were committed ideologues or political activists in their youth whose ideology or movement produced outcomes they now recognize as harmful, including former communists in countries that experienced Stalinist terror or Maoist violence, former nationalists whose movements produced atrocities, former religious fundamentalists whose communities caused harm, and others who must hold the relationship between the sincerity of their younger belief and the consequences of acting on it. Disproportionately affecting people whose ideological commitment was deep enough to have been the organizing principle of their early adult life, people who brought others with them into the commitment and who therefore bear responsibility not only for their own choices but for the choices of people they influenced, and people who have access now to historical information that makes the gap between what they believed and what the belief produced visible in ways they cannot dismiss. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved in the specific question he is asking. The mechanism is the relationship between retrospective knowledge and the irreversibility of past action. He has the notebooks from when he was twenty-two and the person writing them was not foolish; he was wrong about something enormous with his whole chest and brought other people with him. The wrongness cannot be undone and the sincerity cannot be denied, and holding both is the specific task. Devastating Real-World Consequence: He cannot disown the younger self because the younger self was real and sincere and not foolish. He cannot defend the younger self because what the ideology produced is now known. He is asking how to carry a version of yourself that you can't disown and can't defend, which is a question about the structure of moral and historical responsibility as it is lived in a single person's memory, and it is a question that has no clean answer but that has been asked by enough people across enough contexts that there are traditions of thinking about it.
