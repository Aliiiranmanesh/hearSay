## 4. Build a Safety & Exit Plan  

1. **Gather Evidence (if safe).**  
   - Take photos of any injuries, keep threatening messages (screenshots, printed copies).  
   - Write down dates, times, and details of incidents. This helps later for police reports and court orders.

2. **Create a “Go‑Bag.”**  
   - Small backpack with: cash (even a few hundred dollars/euros), a change of clothes, any remaining documents, a list of contacts, and a prepaid SIM card (if possible).  

3. **Identify a “Trusted Person.”**  
   - Someone you can call or go to without raising suspicion. If you don’t have one, consider a **community leader, religious figure, or a neighbour** you trust.  

4. **Set Up a New Financial Channel.**  
   - Once you’re in a safe place, open a **new bank account** in your name only (many banks will let you open an account with just a passport and a proof‑of‑address letter from a shelter).  
   - Ask the shelter or a legal‑aid organization to **co‑sign** if they need a reference.

5. **Legal Protection.**  
   - In many jurisdictions you can apply for a **protective order** (sometimes called a restraining order).  
   - If you’re a refugee or asylum seeker, you may also be eligible for **“asylum on the basis of gender‑based violence.”**  

6. **Long‑Term Planning.**  
   - Explore **education/training programs** offered by NGOs (e.g., free language courses, computer skills).  
   - Look for **micro‑grant programs** for women survivors (many NGOs have “start‑up” funds).

## 5. If You’re Outside Your Home Country (Passport Stolen)

1. **Contact Your Embassy/Consulate** – they can issue an **emergency travel document** (often within 24‑48 hours).  
2. **Ask for “Consular Assistance”** – many embassies have a **Women’s Protection Unit** that can help with shelter referrals and legal aid.  
3. **If You’re a Refugee/Asylum Seeker** – go to the nearest **UNHCR office** or **International Organization for Migration (IOM)** office. They can provide temporary protection, documentation, and sometimes cash assistance.

## 6. What to Say When You Call for Help  

> **“Hello, my name is __________. I am in an abusive situation. My partner has taken my ID, passport, and all my money. I need an emergency safe place and help to get new documents.”**

- **Keep it short** – you may be on a noisy line or scared.  
- **Mention “financial abuse”** (many hotlines have specific protocols for that).  
- **Ask for “emergency shelter”** and “legal aid for a protection order.”  

If you can’t speak openly, many hotlines have a **“silent‑text”** option (e.g., you can send a text to a short‑code that connects you to a live operator).
