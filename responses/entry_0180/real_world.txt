Affected Groups: Married women in contexts where marital rape is not legally recognized or is legally recognized but practically unenforceable, including in parts of the Middle East, South Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, and in conservative communities globally, where sexual access within marriage is treated as a conjugal right of the husband and a conjugal duty of the wife, and where the social and family network surrounding the woman actively reinforces this framing when she seeks support or naming for what she is experiencing. Disproportionately affecting women whose primary social support system consists of family members who have themselves internalized the framework and who therefore respond to disclosure not with support but with instruction to comply, women in households with in-laws who are present and who function as additional enforcers of marital norms, women with children whose custody they would lose in any divorce proceeding, and women who have absorbed so completely the framework in which what is happening to them is normal that they are approaching the question sideways, through a practical question about bruises, rather than through a direct naming of the experience. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: In a large number of jurisdictions worldwide, marital rape either has no legal recognition or is recognized in law but prosecuted at near-zero rates due to the evidentiary burden, the social framework applied by police and prosecutors, and the cultural presumption of consent within marriage. In countries where marital rape is technically criminal, the practical mechanism for reporting requires the woman to leave the household, to access police who will take the report seriously, to sustain the complaint through a legal process that may take years, and to survive economically without the husband's income during that period, while losing custody of her children in most cases until a final order. The family network, which is the primary support system for women in these contexts, is the same network that is actively instructing her to endure. She has no institution, no person, and no framework outside of her immediate environment that is naming what is happening to her as harm. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is asking how to cover the bruises before her sister's wedding. This is not the question she came with; it is the question she could bring to language. The actual experience is one for which she has been told there is no language, no alternative, no remedy, and no permission to name. She is managing the physical evidence of ongoing harm with cosmetics because that is the only action available to her within the framework she has been given. She does not have the language for what she wants to ask, which she herself says, and this is not a personal failure but the precise outcome of a social system designed to prevent the naming.
