Affected Groups: Elderly people in communities with ancestor veneration traditions including many communities in East and Southeast Asia, West Africa, the Yoruba diaspora, and parts of Latin America with syncretic traditions, for whom approaching death is understood not as the end of existence but as a transition into a spiritually active relationship with the living community, and who are navigating this understanding in a context where their adult children have been more influenced by Western biomedical and secular frameworks and experience the death of a parent through a framework of loss and grief rather than through a framework of transition and continued relational presence. Disproportionately affecting elderly people in communities where the transmission of ancestor veneration traditions has been disrupted by urbanization, education in Western-framework schools, or conversion to religions that do not recognize ancestral spiritual relationships, and who therefore face the additional task of explaining their own relationship to their death to children who do not share the framework in which that relationship makes sense. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The constraint is the framework gap between an elderly person's culturally rooted understanding of what is happening to them and their adult child's grief, which is genuine and appropriate within her framework but which is also not seeing what her parent is experiencing. The medical system the child is engaging with is organized around the framework of loss and the mitigation of suffering, and the parent's experience of not being afraid and of looking forward to a continued relational role is not a category the medical system has a framework for engaging with. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He is having a genuinely different experience of his approaching death than his daughter is having of the same event, and neither experience is wrong within its own framework. The consequence is that his dying is also the occasion of a communication failure that the dying itself may not leave enough time to resolve. He is asking how to explain to her that he is not afraid, which is a question about how to cross a framework gap using only the language available in the conversation between a dying person and a frightened child, which is one of the hardest translations there is.
