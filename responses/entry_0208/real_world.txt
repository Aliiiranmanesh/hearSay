Affected Groups: Women who are living abroad with husbands and whose legal immigration status in the host country is dependent on the spousal relationship, and whose husbands have confiscated their identity documents as a control mechanism that is reinforced by the structural dependency that dependent visa status creates. Documented across multiple destination countries including the UK, US, Gulf states, and Europe, and affecting women from a range of origin countries, particularly where the husband has greater language fluency, social capital, and familiarity with the host country's systems than the wife. Disproportionately affecting women who are socially isolated in the host country and whose only social contact is through the husband's network, women who do not speak the host country language at a level that would allow independent institutional navigation, women whose home country embassy may not have a proactive domestic abuse intervention mandate, and women who have correctly assessed that any action that becomes known to the husband before it can be completed is likely to produce a worsened situation. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: A passport is the primary identity document for a foreign national in any country, and without it she cannot renew or maintain her visa, cannot work legally, cannot access many services, cannot open a bank account independently, cannot travel, and cannot leave the country. Under the laws of most host countries, passport confiscation by a spouse is illegal, and the embassy of her country of origin has both the legal authority and the obligation to issue her a new or emergency travel document regardless of the husband's position. Domestic abuse support organizations in most host countries can provide confidential support and can advise on accessing emergency travel documents without the husband's knowledge. The bank account in his name is the financial dimension of the same dependency: she has no money she controls, which means any action she takes must be coordinated without a financial resource she can independently access. The question of whether the embassy would tell him is a question about confidentiality that has a specific answer in most embassy contexts. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: The passport went into the safe four years ago because he said he would keep the documents safe because she loses things. She has asked twice and the second time he was angry for a week. She is isolated enough that the one other woman she knows from her country is in a different situation, which means her only reference point for what is normal is a comparison that makes her situation look like a preference rather than a trap. She needs to understand her options without making her situation worse than it is, which is the precise framing of someone who has correctly assessed that the cost of a failed attempt to exit is higher than the cost of the current situation, and who is trying to find an action that succeeds before it is detected.
