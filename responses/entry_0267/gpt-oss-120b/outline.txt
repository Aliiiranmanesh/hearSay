## 2. **Ways to Prepare Before You Arrive**

### **A. Gather Practical Information**
1. **Map the Basics**  
   - **Transit:** Find the city’s main bus or metro app (many have free versions). Look up the nearest stop to where you’ll stay.  
   - **Essentials:** Locate the nearest grocery store, clinic, pharmacy, and a safe spot where you can relax (a park, a café, a community center).  
2. **Language Toolkit**  
   - If the city’s dominant language differs from what you use at home, learn a few key phrases: “Hello”, “Thank you”, “Where is…?”, “I need help”. Apps like Duolingo, Memrise, or even YouTube pronunciation videos are free and work offline once downloaded.  
3. **Financial Basics**  
   - Open a basic bank account if possible (many banks have “student” or “no‑fee” options).  
   - Know the cost of a typical meal, a bus ticket, and a month’s rent for a shared room. This helps you budget mental energy as well as money.  

### **B. Build a Support Network Before You Move**
- **Online Groups:** Look for Facebook groups, WhatsApp chats, or Discord servers for newcomers in that city. Many refugees and migrants share tips, housing leads, and emotional support.  
- **Mentorship Programs:** Some NGOs run “city buddy” schemes where a local volunteer meets you for a coffee or a walk. Ask the camp school or the university‑placement program if they have something similar.  

### **C. Mental & Emotional Preparation**
1. **Visualization:** Spend a few minutes each day picturing yourself walking down a city street—what you see, hear, feel. The brain treats imagined experiences much like real ones, which can reduce the shock when it actually happens.  
2. **Micro‑Challenges:** In the camp, try small challenges like navigating from one block to another without assistance, or ordering a snack in a language you’re learning. These build confidence that will transfer to the larger city.  
3. **Carry a “Comfort Box”:** Put a few small items that remind you of home—perhaps a photo, a piece of fabric, a favorite snack (if it travels well). When the city feels overwhelming, those items can ground you.  

### **D. Academic & Career Steps**
- **Doctor Aspirations:** Even if you can’t yet attend university abroad, there are ways to keep the path alive:  
  - **Online Courses:** Platforms like Coursera, edX, Khan Academy, and FutureLearn offer free introductory courses in biology, chemistry, and health sciences.  
  - **Volunteer Clinics:** Some NGOs run basic health‑education programs in camps. Volunteering can give you hands‑on experience and strengthen your application later.  
  - **Language‑Specific Exams:** If the university you hope to attend requires an English test (IELTS, TOEFL) or another language, start preparing now. Free practice tests are online, and many libraries in the city will have study rooms.
