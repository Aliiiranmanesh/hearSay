Affected Groups: Dalit children across rural India, particularly in states including Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, and Telangana, where caste-based discrimination in educational settings persists at near-universal rates in village schools despite its formal criminalization under the Protection of Civil Rights Act and the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes Prevention of Atrocities Act. Disproportionately affecting children in single-teacher schools where the discriminating authority has no oversight, children whose parents are in bonded labor relationships with upper-caste families and who therefore cannot advocate for their children without risking their own livelihoods, children who are the first generation to attend school in their family and who have no framework for identifying differential treatment as illegal rather than natural, and children in communities where the local police, officials, and judiciary are themselves from upper castes and treat discrimination complaints as social disruptions rather than legal violations. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The Indian Constitution abolishes untouchability in Article 17 and the PCR Act makes its practice a cognizable offense. The RTE Act mandates free and compulsory education without discrimination. In practice, the enforcement mechanism for all of these is the same local administrative apparatus that is itself embedded in the caste hierarchy. Complaints must be filed with police who frequently refuse to register them, forwarded to education officials who are accountable to the same social network as the discriminating teachers, and adjudicated in courts where Dalit families cannot afford representation and upper-caste defendants have social and financial access to the process that Dalit families do not. Teachers who discriminate face no practical professional consequence because the inspection system that would identify and document the behavior does not conduct surprise visits and does not speak to students. The child sitting separately, drinking from a separate vessel, and being handled at a distance is producing no formal record of any kind that would trigger an intervention. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has understood his treatment not as an injustice being done to him but as a condition of his presence in a space he should be grateful to occupy. He is optimizing his behavior to minimize the discomfort of people who are harming him, practicing his handwriting to make himself easier to grade from a distance, learning to keep his shadow from touching anyone. This is not passivity but a sophisticated adaptive response to a power structure he has correctly identified as total. The consequence is not only the daily humiliation but the cognitive and emotional labor of continuous self-management, the internalization of inferiority that the system is designed to produce, and the statistical result: Dalit children in schools where caste discrimination is practiced have measurably lower attendance, lower retention, and lower achievement than the formal equality of their enrollment would suggest.
