Affected Groups: Hajj pilgrims from very low-income countries including Mali, Niger, Bangladesh, Indonesia, and parts of sub-Saharan Africa, who have saved for years or decades to perform a religious obligation they have organized significant portions of their adult life around, and who are experiencing the arrival at Mecca as a sensory and emotional convergence of physical reality with a lifelong spiritual orientation that is overwhelming in ways they did not anticipate and cannot immediately describe. Disproportionately affecting pilgrims who have never traveled outside their home province, pilgrims for whom the journey is the first significant travel of their lives, people whose saving process was visible and shared with their family and community so that the arrival carries not only personal weight but the weight of collective witness, and people who are experiencing a kind of overwhelm that is not distress but intensity that requires a physical response.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The overwhelming experience he is describing is a well-documented phenomenon in pilgrimage contexts, including the experience of arrival at a place one has oriented toward for an entire life. The needing to sit down from something that is not physical is a description of an emotional and spiritual intensity producing a somatic response, which is a recognized and normal human experience in contexts of profound meaning.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has been saving since his youngest was born and she is now twenty-three. He has never owned a car. He has never traveled outside his province. He is standing in a place he has faced five times a day for his entire life and it is real and he is real and he does not know what to do with that much. He called his wife and she was crying before he said anything. The question of whether it is normal to feel like you need to sit down from something that is not physical is a question that deserves a direct yes.
