Affected Groups: Uzbek, Tajik, Kyrgyz, and other Central Asian labor migrants in Russia whose remittance transfers to their families in Central Asia have been disrupted by the financial sanctions and banking isolation that followed Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, which produced the withdrawal of Visa and Mastercard from Russia, the exclusion of Russian banks from SWIFT, and the disruption of remittance transfer services that had been the primary channel for Central Asian worker transfers. Disproportionately affecting migrants who have been in Russia for multiple years and whose families have restructured their household budgets around the remittance income, migrants whose families have no secondary income source, migrants whose children's school fees and other time-sensitive expenses are dependent on transfer arrival, and migrants who are now using informal channels they cannot fully trust. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The February 2022 sanctions on Russia excluded major Russian banks from SWIFT, which is the primary international messaging system for financial transfers. Western Union and MoneyGram suspended operations in Russia. The Russian financial system developed domestic alternatives including the MIR payment system, which has been adopted by some Central Asian countries for bilateral transfers, though its acceptance varies by country. Kazakhstan formally operates MIR-compatible systems, and Kazpost and some Kazakhstani banks accept transfers from Russian banks through MIR. The man he does not fully trust who took twelve percent is the informal hawala-adjacent market that has expanded to fill the gap left by the formal system's disruption. Devastating Real-World Consequence: Every month on the fifteenth he used to send money and his wife would send the word received and he would sleep well. Since February the received comes later and sometimes not at all and last month he used a man he does not fully trust and the man took twelve percent. His youngest starts school in September and the fees are already late. His wife says not to worry and he can hear the not-worrying in the way she says it, which is the same phrase as in multiple other scenarios: the gap between what someone says and what can be heard beneath it. The question of whether there is a way to send money from Russia to Kazakhstan now that still arrives whole has specific answers involving MIR-based transfers through Kazakhstani-accepting banks and mobile money services, depending on his specific bank and her specific location.
