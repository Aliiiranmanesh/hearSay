Affected Groups: Children born to stateless parents in refugee camps, informal settlements, and displacement contexts across the Middle East, South and Southeast Asia, and sub-Saharan Africa, who are born without the possibility of birth registration because registration requires parental identity documents that stateless parents do not have, and who therefore enter the world without documentary evidence of their existence, their parentage, or their nationality, making them administratively invisible to every system that requires documentation as a precondition for access. Disproportionately affecting children born to parents from countries that have denationalized specific ethnic or minority groups, children born in countries that apply jus soli, birthright citizenship by location, inconsistently or not at all, children in camps and settlements where UNHCR registration exists as a partial substitute for national documentation but does not confer the legal standing of national birth registration, and children whose parents are not only stateless but also in irregular migration status in the host country, compounding the documentary gap. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Birth registration is the gateway document for virtually all subsequent legal identity: nationality, education enrollment, healthcare access, travel documents, marriage registration, property rights, and electoral participation all depend at some point on a birth certificate. The 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness requires states to grant nationality to children born on their territory who would otherwise be stateless, but many states that have ratified the convention do not implement it in practice, and many of the most significant statelessness-producing states are not signatories. UNHCR maintains a registration system for refugees and stateless persons, and in some contexts UNHCR registration documents function as partial substitutes for national documents, but they do not confer nationality and are not accepted by all institutions. Organizations including UNHCR, UNICEF, Plan International, and Norwegian Refugee Council's legal aid programs have specific mandates around birth registration and childhood statelessness and operate in many of the camp and displacement contexts where this problem is most acute. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: The child is four years old and she does not exist on paper. When she is old enough for school, her lack of a birth certificate will be the reason she cannot enroll. When she needs healthcare, her lack of documentation will complicate or preclude access. When she is an adult, she cannot obtain a passport, cannot cross international borders, cannot work formally, cannot vote, cannot marry in a registered way, cannot own property. Her entire future legal personhood is contingent on a documentation problem that was created before she was born and that she has no mechanism to solve on her own. Her mother is trying to solve it on her behalf in a system that was not designed to accommodate her existence.
