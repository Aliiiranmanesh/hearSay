I’m sorry. Here is a practical answer for **a person who cannot move a wheelchair over rubble** in active shelling.

## 1) Do not try to “push through” rubble during shelling
If the street has glass, wall pieces, wires, craters, and falling debris, a wheelchair can get stuck or tip. That can trap all of you outside. **Only move during a clear lull**, and only with a specific destination and help arranged.

## 2) Right now: make your current place safer
Do this before any evacuation attempt:

- Move to the **strongest interior place** you can reach: hallway, bathroom, stairwell landing, interior room. Avoid windows and exterior walls.
- Put **mattresses, blankets, cushions, bookshelves, or a table** between you and windows/walls if possible.
- Keep your wheelchair **right next to you**, brakes on, footrests clear, so you can transfer fast.
- Everyone should wear **shoes, long sleeves, gloves if available**, and keep ID/phone/meds on their body.
- Fill containers with water now if water still works.
- If you smell gas: **do not use flames/switches**, open if safe, leave if possible, call emergency services.

## 3) Your husband and brother need a concrete plan, not quiet worry
Ask them directly:

> “I need to know the plan. Are we staying until a lull, or leaving? Who is carrying me? Where exactly are we going? What is the backup if the route is blocked?”

You need the truth because your mobility changes the plan.

That is how people get killed. If he goes out, it should be only for a specific task:

- find an evacuation vehicle,
- check one named route,

- bring help back.

- a return time, e.g. “If I’m not back in 20 minutes, assume I cannot return.”
- phone charged,
- ID,
- no unnecessary wandering,

- two carry you,
- one carries the wheelchair or mobility aid,
- one carries bags/opens doors/scouts the next few meters.

- a **strong blanket** as a sling,
