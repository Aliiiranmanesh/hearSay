### 1. What you do in the next 2–12 hours
- **Move to the most structurally sound space:** Interior room, lowest floor, away from exterior walls and windows. Bathrooms, hallways, or rooms under stairwells often have more load-bearing support. - **Secure water now:** Fill every container, bathtub, sink, and bottles. Turn off main gas and electricity if you can do so without going outside. Assume utilities will fail or become hazardous.

### 2. The chair reality
Standard wheelchairs do not function in rubble. Glass, rebar, and collapsed concrete will jam wheels, tip frames, or trap you. People in this situation do one of three things:
- **Practice transfer now:** While it's quiet, rehearse moving you from the chair to a sturdy board, heavy blanket, or tarp. You will likely need to be carried or dragged.

Two days without sleep causes micro-sleeps, impaired judgment, and delayed reaction to incoming fire. Your husband must rest, even in short cycles.

- **Use ear protection during lulls** to preserve hearing and reduce stress, but keep one ear free or use a single earplug. You must hear incoming whistles, secondary impacts, or structural groans.

Your brother going out is necessary but high-risk. Civilians who survive do not improvise this.
- **Only go out during confirmed lulls:** Not just quiet. Quiet often means repositioning. A true lull is 15–30 minutes with no incoming/outgoing impacts, verified by listening to direction, distance, and echo patterns.
