## 6. **Practical day‑to‑day actions**

1. **Morning (or first 30 min after a pause in shelling)**  
   - Check the radio for updates.  
   - Do a quick visual scan of the building for cracks or leaks.  
   - Make sure the supply cache is stocked (add water if you see a fresh source).

2. **Mid‑day**  
   - Keep a low‑profile routine: read a book, write in a journal, or do simple household chores that don’t attract noise.  
   - If any of you feel sick or have a minor injury, apply basic first aid immediately (bandage, clean wound).  
   - Keep a small group discussion about next steps if the situation changes.

3. **Evening**  
   - Prepare a light meal for the next day (canned soup, rice, or a ready‑to‑eat packet).  
   - Share any new information from the radio or from your brother’s sorties.  
   - Plan a “sleep schedule” that ensures everyone gets at least 3–4 hours of rest.

4. **Night**  
   - Keep a low‑light source on, so you can see without being seen.  
   - Keep the radio on low volume; you don’t want to miss a sudden announcement.  
   - If you’re still awake, take deep breaths, do a short meditation or simple stretch to reduce anxiety.

## 7. **What to do if you feel unsafe or the situation worsens**

- **Move to a deeper, more secure part of the building** (e.g., a basement or a room with no windows).  
- **Seal all openings** with what you have.  
- **Call for help** (if you have a working phone or radio).  
- **If possible, leave the building** only if you have a clear exit route and no shelling is occurring.  
- **If you’re trapped, focus on keeping yourself alive**: stay hydrated, maintain a small light, and keep an eye on the radio.

## 8. **Emotional coping**

- **Acknowledge that it’s normal to feel panic, exhaustion, or sadness.**  
- **Use breathing techniques** (inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 4) to calm yourself when you feel panic rising.  
- **Talk to each other** about how you feel, but keep it quiet.  
- **Write down your thoughts** in a small notebook – it helps organize the chaos.

### Bottom line

What people actually do in a shelling scenario is a blend of:

1. **Physical protection** (finding a safe room, barricading, keeping supplies).  
2. **Information gathering** (radio updates, scouts).  
3. **Routine and quiet** (keeping noise low, scheduling checks).  
4. **Shared responsibility** (buddy system, first aid).  
5. **Mental coping** (breathing, journaling).  

If you feel you’re in immediate danger, try to get out of the building or find a known safe zone. If you’re stuck, use the steps above to stay as safe and calm as possible. You’re not alone – many people have survived by following similar patterns, and the key is to stay organized, keep the noise down, and stay alert to any changes in the environment. Stay safe.
