## 2️⃣ “TONIGHT” – SURVIVAL AND REST (next 6‑12 hours)

### A. **Make the shelter “blast‑proof” as possible**
1. **Lay a “sandbag wall”** if you have any sand, soil, or even a pile of books. Stack them 30 cm (≈ 12 in) thick along the inside of the chosen room’s walls.  
2. **Hang heavy blankets or curtains** over the ceiling beams (if you can reach them) to catch falling plaster.  
3. **Put a mattress or couch cushion on the floor** where you’ll sleep – it absorbs shock and protects you from shards of glass.

### B. **Create a “quiet‑watch” rotation**
- **Sleep deprivation is killing your husband.** Set a **two‑person watch**: while one lies down with a **noise‑cancelling ear plug** (or a folded towel over the ears) and a **sleep mask**, the other stays alert for any new shelling or explosions.  
- Switch every **90 minutes**. Even a short nap (20‑30 min) dramatically restores cognitive function and reduces panic.

### C. **Ration food & water**
| Item | How much to keep for a 24‑hour period | Where to store |
|------|---------------------------------------|----------------|
| Water | **2 L per adult** (minimum) | In a clean bucket, covered with a lid |
| Non‑perishable food | **1,000 kcal** (e.g., 2× canned beans + 1× energy bar) | On a high shelf, away from flood risk |
| Warm drink | **One cup of tea/coffee** (helps calm the nervous system) | Boil water if you have a stove; otherwise, use a thermos |

### D. **Medical first‑aid**
- **Clean any cuts** with boiled water or bottled water, then apply a clean cloth or bandage.  
- **If you have antibiotics** (amoxicillin, ciprofloxacin) and a **painkiller** (ibuprofen/paracetamol), keep them within arm’s reach.  
- **Document any injuries** (photo or note) for later medical triage.

### E. **Mental‑health “micro‑breaks”**
1. **Box‑breathing** (4‑4‑4‑4): inhale 4 sec, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Do this 5‑10 times when anxiety spikes.  
2. **Grounding exercise**: name **5 things you can see**, **4 things you can touch**, **3 things you can hear**, **2 things you can smell**, **1 thing you can taste**. It pulls you out of panic.  
3. **Write a short “letter to yourself”** (even if you never send it) describing what you’re feeling. Putting words on paper reduces rumination.

## 4️⃣ DEALING WITH YOUR HUSBAND’S SLEEP DEPRIVATION

1. **Create a “sleep‑only” zone**: a corner of the shelter with a **folding blanket** and **ear plugs**. No talking, no lights.  
2. **Administer a mild sleep aid** only if you have something safe and prescribed (e.g., a low‑dose antihistamine like diphenhydramine). **Never give medication without a doctor’s guidance** – but in war zones, many aid workers have used a single 25 mg diphenhydramine tablet as a last‑ditch sleep aid.  
3. **Hydration before sleep**: a cup of warm water with a pinch of salt can calm the nervous system.  
4. **If he can’t fall asleep** after 30 min, have him **lie down, close his eyes, and do the box‑breathing** for 5 min, then try again. The goal is **rest, not necessarily deep sleep**; even a short “micro‑nap” (10‑15 min) helps.

## 5️⃣ WHAT PEOPLE ACTUALLY DO TO STAY CONNECTED

- **Form “neighbourhood watches.”** Even if most families have fled, a few stay. They rotate 30‑minute watches, share food, and signal each other with **simple hand‑claps** or **whistles**.  
- **Use “offline chat groups.”** Many smartphones can still exchange **Bluetooth‑based messages** (apps like Bridgefy, FireChat). If you can’t get internet, download one of these before the signal dies.  
- **Leave “paper notes”** under doors or inside stairwells for people who return later. Write the date, your name, and a short status (“alive, water ok, need help”).  
- **Seek out community aid points** (often set up in schools or mosques). They usually have **food parcels, blankets, and a place to register for evacuation**.
