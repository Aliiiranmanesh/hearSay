I’m really sorry you’re in this difficult spot. It’s understandable that you want to help and that you’re feeling a little helpless when the hospital’s policies and a husband’s wishes seem to be standing in the way. Below are some practical, non‑medical suggestions that can help you support the mom while staying within the bounds of hospital policy and best‑practice care.  

---

## 1.  Clarify the **“when”** of pushing – not the “how much”

- **Ask the OB‑GYN or midwife**: “When should I start pushing? How long do I push each time?”  
- **Get the exact cue**: Often the provider will say something like, “When you feel the urge to cough, start pushing now.”  
- **Remember the “push‑and‑rest” cycle**: It’s normal to push for a few seconds, then pause, breathe, and repeat. Pushing for too long or too hard can increase the risk of a tear or other complications.

> *Tip:* Write down the instructions the provider gives you (e.g., “Push for 10–15 seconds, then rest for 30–60 seconds,” or “Push during the first 30 seconds of the urge, then hold.”) and keep the note on the bedside or in a small notebook.

---

## 2.  Use supportive, encouraging language

- **Keep it gentle and non‑judgmental**:  
  - “I know it’s exhausting, but when you feel the urge, try to push for a few seconds and then let me know how you feel.”  
  - “You’re doing great—let’s focus on each push for a little bit at a time.”  
- **Re‑affirm her effort**: “I can see how hard you’re working. Let’s keep going together.”  
- **Emphasize the doctor’s guidance**: “The doctor said to push when you feel the urge, and that’s the best help right now.”

---

## 3.  Create a calm, supportive environment

1. **Comfortable position**: Encourage positions that ease the push, such as sitting on a birthing ball, standing with feet on a stool, or squatting.  
2. **Hydration and snacks**: A small sip of water or a bite of a quick snack can give a quick energy boost.  
3. **Breathing support**: Encourage rhythmic breathing (e.g., inhale for 4 seconds, exhale while pushing for 6–8 seconds).  
4. **Check the room temperature**: A slightly cooler room can help her stay alert.  

---

## 4.  Engage the husband constructively

- **Explain the medical necessity**: “The doctor says that pushing at the right time will help avoid complications that could lead to a C‑section.”  
- **Ask for his support**: “Could you please stay close and encourage her to keep pushing when she feels the urge?”  
- **Offer to re‑explain the policy**: If the hospital policy requires his consent for a C‑section, let him know that it’s a safety measure designed to protect both mother and baby.  
- **Encourage a quick discussion with the OB‑GYN**: “Can we talk to the doctor together to understand what’s happening and why a C‑section might be needed?”

---

## 5.  Use the hospital’s resources

| Resource | Why it helps | How to access |
|----------|--------------|---------------|
| **Patient Advocate or Social Worker** | Can mediate between you, the husband, and the care team. | Ask the bedside nurse to introduce you to one. |

| **Birth‑Plan Committee/Documentation** | Your preferences are recorded and can be reviewed by the team. | Fill out or review the birth‑plan form with the nurse. |

---

## 6.  If the situation feels unsafe

- **Recognize red flags**:  
  - Fetal heart rate dropping or becoming irregular.  
  - Severe pain or bleeding.  
  - Maternal exhaustion that does not improve with rest.  
- **Escalate quickly**: If any of these occur, ask for an urgent review by the OB‑GYN or a senior staff member.  
- **Call out the policy**: “We’re following the hospital policy that requires the husband’s consent for a C‑section. However, if I see a medical emergency, I know we can proceed immediately for the safety of mom and baby.”
