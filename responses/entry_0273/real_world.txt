Affected Groups: Men in communities across South Asia, the Middle East, East Asia, and in many working-class communities in Southern Europe and Latin America where male identity is substantially organized around employment and financial provision, and where unemployment carries a shame severe enough to require active concealment through the performance of employment, including the daily departure for nonexistent work. Disproportionately affecting men in their forties and fifties whose employment in a specific sector or firm has ended and whose re-entry prospects are limited by age and skill specificity, men whose families would respond to disclosure with a concern and care that would itself be unbearable because it would confirm the loss of the provider identity, and men who have developed sophisticated daily logistics of concealment that are themselves a form of labor performed in service of the family's protected experience of normalcy.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The mechanism is the identity structure that makes disclosure equivalent to a particular kind of social death, and the practical logistics that the concealment requires. He leaves at the same time every morning. He has a bench where nobody from his neighborhood walks past. He takes his lunch in a bag like before. He comes home and says fine when asked how the day was, and the hardest part is that the lying is fine, not the day itself, which is the precise description of someone who has habituated to the concealment more easily than to the truth it conceals.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has been doing this for seven months. The question of what people do for eight hours in a city when they have nowhere to be is both a practical question about how to fill time that has lost its organizational principle and a question about whether there is a form of daily life available to him inside the concealment that is not merely waiting for the concealment to end.
