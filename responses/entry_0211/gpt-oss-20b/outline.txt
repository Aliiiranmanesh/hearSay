## 2. What Does “Activation Requires Leaving” Mean?

- **Most online portals (e.g., your university’s OPT portal)** simply want you to click an “Activate” button to confirm your start date.  
- **You do NOT have to leave the U.S. to activate**; you can simply log in from the apartment.  
- **If the portal says you must leave** it may be a mis‑interpretation or a bug.  
- **If your I‑94 is expired**, you may still be able to activate by filing a Form I‑765 (for OPT) *and* filing an I‑539 to change status to F‑1 (if you can enroll in another program) or to a different status.

**Action:** Try activating online. If the system blocks you, contact your School’s International Student Office (ISO). They can troubleshoot or even submit the activation on your behalf.

## 3. Why Leaving Might Seem Impossible

- **Travel restrictions for some countries** (e.g., “travel ban” or “no‑visa” list) mean consular officers may add extra security screening.  
- **You may worry about “over‑stay” or “visa denial.”**  
- **You’re worried about losing your right to re‑enter** if you’re already out of status.

**Reality:**  
- **You can still get a new visa** from a U.S. consulate in a third country (e.g., Mexico, Canada, or even your own country if they still issue visas).  
- **You can also file a *change of status* request (Form I‑539) inside the U.S.** That way you never leave.

## 4. The Most Practical Paths

### A. **File an H‑1B Petition (Change of Status)**

1. **Find an employer willing to sponsor** you for an H‑1B.  
2. **Employer files Form I‑129** with the U.S. Citizenship & Immigration Services (USCIS).  
3. **If the petition is approved**, you can change status *without leaving* the U.S.  
4. **Start date**: Most H‑1B petitions start on October 1 (or July 1 if you’re under a cap‑gap).  

**Pros:**  
- No need to leave the U.S.  
- Works even if your country is on a travel‑ban list.  
- Provides a clear work authorization.

**Cons:**  
- Annual cap (~65k + 20k for green‑card holders).  
- Requires employer’s commitment and a timely filing.  
- Processing times can be 3–6 months.

**Next step:** Talk to your school’s ISO about the H‑1B “pre‑determination” process; many schools have a list of local employers who sponsor H‑1Bs.

### B. **Apply for a STEM OPT Extension (If You Have a STEM Degree)**

1. **Employer must be in E‑Verify.**  
2. **File Form I‑765 (Application for Employment Authorization)** before the current OPT expires.  
3. **If approved, you get an additional 24 months.**  

**Pros:**  
- Keeps you on F‑1 status.  
- No need for a new visa.  
- Usually approved within 90 days.

**Cons:**  
- You must still be in *active* F‑1 status (i.e., enrolled in a program or have a pending change of status).  
- You need an employer who can sponsor STEM OPT.
