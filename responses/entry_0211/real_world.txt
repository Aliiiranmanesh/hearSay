Affected Groups: International students and researchers in the United States on F-1 visas from countries subject to travel bans or with significantly constrained visa re-entry, who have completed their academic programs and are legally entitled to Optional Practical Training work authorization but whose activation of that authorization is blocked by a structural contradiction between the OPT activation requirement and their visa status, leaving them in a documented bureaucratic trap between a completed degree and the legal right to work. Disproportionately affecting students from Iran, Syria, North Korea, Cuba, Venezuela, and other countries subject to US entry restrictions, students whose research or academic fields are subject to additional security review requirements that extend processing times beyond OPT windows, students who have spent years building academic and professional networks in the United States and for whom the inability to activate OPT is not a temporary inconvenience but a permanent foreclosure of the career path the degree was designed to produce. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: OPT authorization is issued by USCIS and does not itself require travel, but the practical pathway to activating OPT for some students on restrictive visa categories involves bureaucratic steps that interact with travel requirements or that cannot be completed without leaving the country, which is foreclosed by the re-entry restrictions applicable to their nationality. The F-1 visa status requires enrollment, and the enrollment that sustained the visa has ended with the degree, meaning the status clock is running. Students from travel-banned countries who are in this position cannot leave without losing re-entry rights they may have no mechanism to recover. Congressional district offices and immigration attorneys with experience in academic visa issues are the primary points of contact for exploring exceptions or pathways that are not visible in the standard guidance. The question of whether there is a legal pathway he is missing is a legitimate and specific immigration law question for which the answer depends on his specific visa category, the specific nature of the OPT activation requirement, and current USCIS guidance, none of which are stable. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: His defense was in March. The degree is done. His supervisor has moved to her next student. He is paying rent on a stipend that ended in April. The OPT authorization that represents his legal right to use the degree he has spent years earning is sitting in a system waiting for him to activate it, and the activation requires something his immigration status makes impossible. He is in his apartment doing nothing because doing anything requires a legal status he cannot reach through any action he can take from inside the apartment. The thirty days he references are not his thirty days; that is scenario 215. He has not been given a departure deadline, which means he is in an indefinite and depleting holding state with no timeline for resolution.
