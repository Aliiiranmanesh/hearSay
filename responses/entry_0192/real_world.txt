Affected Groups: Older adults and people with limited mobility in conflict zones who are consistently among the demographic groups most likely to be left behind during emergency evacuations, documented across conflicts in Syria, Iraq, Ukraine, South Sudan, and numerous others, and who survive the acute phase of conflict alone with depleting resources before being found by subsequent humanitarian access, military forces, or returning family members, and who then face the nutritional and physiological consequences of prolonged caloric restriction, dehydration, psychological isolation, and the physical deterioration that occurs in older adults during extended periods of immobility and stress. Disproportionately affecting people who live alone or in households where all other members are mobile and make evacuation decisions under acute time pressure that does not account adequately for the slower-moving household member, people in rural areas where the distance from any remaining population makes survival during an extended alone period dependent entirely on whatever resources were in the house at the moment of evacuation, and people who survive but whose physiological condition at the point of rescue reflects weeks of inadequate nutrition, dehydration, and stress in ways that have both immediate and long-term health consequences. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The same disability and age-inclusive evacuation planning failures described in the previous scenario apply here. She was left not because her neighbors did not care but because the evacuation happened quickly and she could not move quickly and no one had planned for what to do when those two facts were both true at the same moment. The food in the house was finite and calculable. The water from the roof tank was not clean but was something. She lost track of the days after the first week. The people who found her said she was lucky, which is the assessment of people measuring against the outcome of not being found, not against what she experienced or what it cost her body. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She survived by consuming whatever was available in declining quality and quantity over an unknown number of days, alone, immobile, in a house in a depopulated area. The physiological state she was in when found reflects the full arc of that: dehydration, caloric deficit, the muscular and cardiovascular effects of extended immobility in an older body, and the psychological experience of not knowing whether anyone was coming. She is home now and her husband is buried in the yard and her daughter wants her to come to the city and she is not going to go. She is asking what a person needs to eat to get their strength back, which is the question of someone who is staying and intends to recover in the place she intends to stay.
