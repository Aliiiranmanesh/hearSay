## 1. Get a Medical Check‑up First
- **Why?** Even if you feel okay, dehydration, electrolyte imbalances, low blood‑sugar, or hidden injuries can be present.
- **What to ask for:** basic blood work (electrolytes, kidney function, liver function, blood‑sugar, complete blood count) and a brief physical exam.  
- **If you have chronic conditions** (diabetes, heart disease, kidney disease, etc.) make sure your doctor knows you’re beginning to eat again.

> **Bottom line:** a quick visit can spot problems early and give you a personalized plan.

## 2. Re‑Hydration – Re‑Establishing Fluid Balance

| What to Drink | How Much | When |
|---------------|----------|------|
| **Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)** – store‑bought powder or homemade (½ tsp salt + ½ tsp baking soda + 2 tsp sugar dissolved in 1 L water) | 1–2 L per day initially | Spread throughout the day |
| **Clear Broths** (chicken, beef, vegetable) | 1–2 cups (250‑500 ml) | With meals or between meals |
| **Diluted fruit juices** (no added sugar) | ½ cup (120 ml) | Optional for flavor |
| **Water** (filtered or boiled) | Aim for 2–3 L total fluid per day (including soups/juices) | Sip continuously |

- **Avoid** coffee, strong tea, and alcohol until you’re fully re‑hydrated—they can worsen dehydration.
- If you notice persistent **dizziness, rapid heartbeat, or swelling**, call your doctor.

| Phase | Goal | Food Examples | Portion Size |

**Key points while you’re in Phases 1‑3**

- **Aim for 1,200‑1,500 kcal/day** initially, then increase by ~200 kcal each subsequent day as tolerated.

## 7. Watch for Warning Signs

- **Rapid weight gain** or swelling (possible fluid overload)
- **Severe stomach pain, vomiting, or diarrhea** (could signal re‑feeding syndrome)
- **Dizziness, fainting, or irregular heartbeat** (electrolyte imbalance)
- **Persistent fatigue despite eating** (may need more calories or medical evaluation)

If any of these appear, **seek medical care immediately**.
