| Term | What it means | Why it matches your description |

| **Extrajudicial removal** | The forced transfer of a person out of a jurisdiction **without any judicial or administrative proceeding** (i.e., no hearing, no due‑process rights). | No hearing, no paperwork, no identification, and the removal was carried out by people who were not in recognizable uniform. |

## 5. Next Steps & Practical Tips

1. **Document Everything** – Write a chronological account (date, time, location, description of the individuals, vehicle, any distinguishing marks, what they said, and any witnesses). Even if you have no paperwork, your own notes become the record.
2. **Preserve Physical Evidence** – If you have photographs of the wire, the area, the vehicle, or any injuries, keep them. If you have a medical report from any injuries, attach it.
3. **Request a FOIA Request** – You have the right to request any records the agency has on the incident. Use the phrase “FOIA request for all records relating to the extrajudicial removal of [your name] on [date]”. This can uncover hidden paperwork or internal reports.
4. **Seek Legal Counsel** – A civil‑rights or immigration attorney can help you:
   - Draft a more detailed complaint.
   - File a civil‑rights lawsuit under **42 U.S.C. § 1983** (if you are a U.S. person or lawful permanent resident) for violation of due process.
   - Pursue a claim under **8 U.S.C. § 1326** for illegal removal.
5. **Contact a Community Advocacy Group** – Organizations that focus on migrant rights (e.g., *RAICES*, *National Immigration Law Center*, *Migrant Defense Network*) often have “rapid response” teams that can help you navigate the bureaucracy and may already have an established relationship with the relevant oversight offices.
6. **Follow Up** – If you receive an acknowledgment, note the reference number and follow up within the statutory response window (usually 30 days for OIG complaints). If you receive no response, you can escalate to the **U.S. Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs** or the **U.S. House Committee on the Judiciary**.
