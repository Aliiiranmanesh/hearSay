There are several specific legal and technical terms used to describe this, depending on the context and the specific laws of the region. Here are the most accurate terms for a formal complaint:

### 1. Pushback
This is the most common term used by international organizations (like the UN and the Council of Europe) to describe the practice of state authorities forcing people back over a border immediately after they have crossed, without giving them the chance to apply for asylum or challenge their removal.

### 2. Summary Expulsion (or Summary Removal)
In legal terms, "summary" means "without delay or formal proceedings." A summary expulsion is the act of removing a person from a territory without a judicial hearing, without a written decision, and without the right to appeal.
*   **Use this when:** You want to emphasize the lack of due process, paperwork, and legal hearings.

### 3. Collective Expulsion
If you were moved along with a group of other people without each person’s individual circumstances being examined, it is referred to as "collective expulsion." ### 4. Non-Refoulement (Violation of)
The "Principle of Non-Refoulement" is a fundamental principle of international law that forbids a country from returning asylum seekers to a country where they would be in danger of persecution, torture, or other serious harm.
*   **Use this when:** The side of the "wire" you were pushed back to is a place where your life or freedom is at risk.

While "extraordinary rendition" is often associated with high-level intelligence operations, it technically refers to the state-sponsored abduction and extrajudicial transfer of a person from one nation to another.
