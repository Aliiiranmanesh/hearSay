## Why that wording fits

| Term | Who it applies to | Typical legal context | What it implies |
|------|------------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| **Expulsion** | A citizen, resident, or person whose legal status is not that of a foreign national | Used in many civil‑law jurisdictions (e.g., the UK, France, Canada, many U.S. states) | The state removes you from its territory or a specific region. |
| **Deportation** | A foreign national (non‑citizen) | U.S. immigration law, EU directives, etc. | The state removes you from the country. |

| **Extrajudicial removal** | Any person | General legal term | The removal is carried out by force or administrative action **outside** the normal judicial or administrative process. |
| **Non‑judicial removal** | Any person | Common in immigration‑law parlance | Same as extrajudicial; the removal is not decided by a judge. |

### Why the “without a hearing” clause matters

- **Due process** (constitutional or statutory) requires that a person be given an opportunity to contest a removal.  
- In the U.S., *deportation* and *expulsion* are governed by the **Immigration and Nationality Act (INA)**. Section 212(a)(3) of the INA states that a removal proceeding must be administered by a **judicial officer** (an immigration judge).  
- In the European Union, the **Council Directive 2001/15/EC** on the **expulsion of non‑EU nationals** requires a **judicial decision** before expulsion.  
- Human‑rights treaties (ICCPR, Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, etc.) classify *arbitrary* or *extrajudicial* removals as violations of **Article 9** (right to liberty and security) and **Article 12** (right to leave any country).

Thus, describing the event as an **arbitrary expulsion** or **extrajudicial removal** signals that the person was taken across a border (or removed from a jurisdiction) **without** the procedural safeguards that are legally required.

## How to use the term in a formal complaint

> **“I was subject to an arbitrary expulsion from the state border without any hearing, documentation, or explanation of the legal basis for my removal.”**

or

> **“I was forcibly removed across the border by unidentified personnel without any judicial or administrative hearing – an extrajudicial expulsion in violation of due‑process rights.”**

If you’re filing a complaint:

1. **Identify the jurisdiction** (the country, state, or territory from which you were removed).  
2. **Name the authority** that should have overseen the removal (e.g., the Ministry of Interior, the border guard, the immigration department).  
3. **Reference the legal standard** (e.g., the relevant section of the immigration law, the national constitution, or the human‑rights treaty).  
4. **State that the removal was **arbitrary**/**extrajudicial** and therefore unlawful**.

## Quick checklist for your formal complaint

1. **Identify the event**: date, time, location, and the names (if known) or identifiers of the personnel involved.  
2. **Describe the removal**: state that you were moved from one side of a border to the other without any paperwork, hearing, or explanation.  
3. **Label it**: “arbitrary expulsion” or “extrajudicial removal.”  
4. **Cite the legal standard**: mention due‑process provisions (e.g., constitutional article, national law, or human‑rights treaty).  
5. **State the impact**: loss of liberty, violation of rights, etc.  
6. **Ask for remedy**: request that the agency investigate, hold the perpetrators accountable, and provide a formal explanation of the legal basis for the removal.
