### Basic Care Checklist
1. **Cage** – 1‑gal clear plastic or glass tank with a secure lid (hamsters are escape artists!). Include a solid‑base wheel (no wire spokes) to protect little feet.
2. **Bedding** – Paper‑based bedding or aspen shavings (avoid pine/cypress as they can irritate the respiratory system).
3. **Food** – Commercial hamster mix plus a few fresh veggies (carrot, broccoli) once a week. Avoid sugary treats.
4. **Water** – Small water bottle with a metal spout (pre‑filled and checked daily).
5. **Enrichment** – A tunnel system, chew blocks, and a small cardboard hideout. Rotate toys weekly to keep them stimulated.
6. **Handling** – Begin with short, gentle sessions (2‑3 min) after the hamster is settled in its new home; let the child watch first, then gradually involve them in feeding and cage cleaning.
7. **Health** – Look for signs of over‑grooming, hair loss, or lethargy. A quick vet visit once a year is enough for a hamster.

## Practical Tips for a Smooth Introduction

1. **Start Small** – Purchase a single hamster (or gerbil) rather than a litter. One animal is easier for a child to manage and reduces the risk of fighting.
2. **Safety First** – Supervise any interaction between the pet and children, especially the youngest. Hamsters can bite if startled, and they’re delicate.
3. **Allergy Check** – Spend a few minutes with a hamster at a local pet store or a friend’s home. If anyone sneezes or gets itchy eyes, consider a reptile or fish instead (they’re hypoallergenic).
4. **Pet‑Ready Space** – Choose a quiet corner away from direct sunlight, drafts, and heavy foot traffic. A stable surface (e.g., a nightstand) works well.
5. **Teach Responsibility** – Assign age‑appropriate chores: the older child can fill the water bottle and replace food; the younger can help spot‑clean the cage (with supervision) or simply observe.
6. **Plan for the End** – Even a short‑lived pet deserves a dignified farewell. Discuss the life cycle with your children early on to help them process loss.
