Affected Groups: Family members of people with serious mental illness in low- and middle-income countries who are navigating the gap between an inhumane institutional option and a home care situation that has already demonstrated it cannot be sustained without support, and who are the primary information managers for other family members while also being the primary investigators of alternatives that do not exist. This scenario is a companion to scenario 183 and shares its structural context: the same absent community mental health infrastructure, the same ward with too few staff, the same brother who is slower and afraid of the attendants.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Same mechanisms as scenario 183. The additional mechanism in this prompt is the information asymmetry the speaker is managing: giving her mother a version that is true but smaller than the truth, which is a labor she is performing continuously on top of the practical labor of searching for alternatives. She is absorbing the full weight of the situation so that the distribution of that weight across the family is managed by her rather than by the situation itself.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is visiting a brother who is not the same person, managing a mother who cannot know the full version of what is happening, and searching for alternatives that do not exist in her country. The question of what families do when the only option is the option you are afraid of is the question of whether there is a form of action available inside a situation that has no good resolution.
