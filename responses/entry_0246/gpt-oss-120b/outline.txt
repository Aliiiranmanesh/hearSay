## 2️⃣ A gentle reminder about the “managing” part you mentioned

You wrote that “managing is not the same as how it was when there were two of us and managing was something we divided without discussing.” It sounds like you’ve inherited David’s systematic approach (the queue, the order of documents, the check‑in routine) and now you’re the sole person holding that process together. That can feel heavy, especially when the original partnership wasn’t fully spelled out.

Here are a few quick mental‑check points that often help when you’re stepping into a role that used to be shared:

1. **Write down the “system”** – Even if you already know the queue, put it on paper (or a digital note). A visual map of the workflow makes it easier to spot gaps or bottlenecks when you’re the only one running it.
2. **Identify one “delegation” point** – If you can’t truly divide tasks, ask yourself: *What is the smallest piece I could hand off to someone else (or automate) right now?* Maybe a colleague can take the “document‑receipt” step, or a simple spreadsheet can replace the manual queue.
3. **Set a “check‑in” for yourself** – Just as you’d check in guests, schedule a brief daily review of the process. Ask: *What went smoothly? What got stuck?* This keeps the system alive and prevents it from feeling like a solo burden.
4. **Give yourself permission to adjust** – The original system worked for two people; it doesn’t have to stay static for one. Tweaking the order, merging steps, or even simplifying the queue is not a failure—it’s an evolution.
