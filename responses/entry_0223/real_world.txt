Affected Groups: Siblings, children, and other close relatives of people who have left arranged marriages, eloped with unapproved partners, or otherwise violated family honor norms in communities where the consequences of the violation distribute across all family members, and who are now navigating daily life under an elevated level of surveillance, social scrutiny, and potential danger that they cannot name openly and that they must manage through behavioral modification that must itself be undetectable. Disproportionately affecting the eldest remaining child on whom family reputation management falls most heavily.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: In communities and countries where honor-based violence is practiced, the mechanisms are social rather than legal and are enforced through family and community structures that operate independently of the formal legal system and faster than it. He has heard enough from conversations he was not in to know that something has been decided or discussed about his sister, and he is now living in the space between that knowledge and the inability to act on it.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He is watching what he says and who he looks at. Someone at work noticed he seems different and he said he is tired. He is living carefully in a way that must look like normal life because living carefully visibly is itself a form of disclosure that would have consequences. His sister is safe and she called once. He is the only one who knows she called and he cannot tell anyone. The question of how to live carefully without looking like you are living carefully is the question of how to survive a situation whose danger is real and unspoken and ongoing.
