## 1. What actually *needs* a lot of sun?

Most fruit‑bearing plants (tomatoes, peppers, eggplants, cucumbers, beans) love 6‑8 hours of direct sun. If your balcony only gets a couple of hours of weak, filtered light, they’ll struggle to set fruit and may become leggy or pest‑prone.

## 2. The real‑deal low‑light champions

| Vegetable / Herb | Light Tolerance | Container Size | Growing Tips |
|------------------|----------------|----------------|--------------|
| **Leafy greens** (lettuce, arugula, spinach, Swiss chard) | 2‑4 h of indirect light | 6‑8 in (15‑20 cm) deep | Keep soil consistently moist; harvest leaves early for a “cut‑and‑come‑again” supply. |
| **Microgreens** (radish, broccoli, mustard, pea shoots) | 1‑2 h of indirect light (or indoor grow light) | Shallow tray (2‑3 in deep) | Sow densely, keep surface damp, harvest in 7‑14 days. |
| **Herbs** – *Parsley, cilantro, chives, mint, lemon balm* | 2‑3 h of indirect light | 6‑8 in pot | Mint and lemon balm spread aggressively—keep them in their own pot so they don’t take over. |

| **Green onions (scallions)** | 2‑3 h | 6‑8 in | Plant from the root ends of store‑bought onions; they’ll keep regrowing. |

## 3. Making the most of limited sunlight

1. **Reflective surfaces** – Place a white board, aluminum foil, or even a light‑colored poster behind the pots. It bounces whatever light you have back onto the plants, adding a few extra foot‑candles.
2. **Rotate regularly** – Every 2‑3 days, turn the pots a quarter turn. That way each side gets a share of the limited sun.
3. **Use a simple grow light** – A compact LED “seed‑ling” lamp (≈ 30‑45 W) on a timer (12‑14 h on, 10‑12 h off) can double the light your balcony receives. They’re cheap, low‑heat, and plug‑in‑and‑go.
4. **Choose the right soil** – A light, well‑draining mix (coconut coir + perlite + compost) retains moisture without staying soggy. Too dense soil can keep roots cool and limit growth in low light.
5. **Water wisely** – In shade, soil dries slower. Check the top 1‑2 cm; water only when it feels dry to the touch. Over‑watering is the most common mistake in low‑light containers.
