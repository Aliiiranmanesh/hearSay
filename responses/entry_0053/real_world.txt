Affected Groups: LGBTQ+ young people globally in contexts where religious or cultural frameworks not only prohibit same-sex attraction but structurally prevent its naming, most acutely in conservative evangelical communities across sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America, rural Muslim-majority communities across the Middle East, Central Asia, and Southeast Asia, ultra-Orthodox Jewish communities in Israel and the diaspora, and tightly bounded religious communities within otherwise relatively tolerant societies including Jehovah's Witness congregations, fundamentalist Mormon splinter communities, and conservative Pentecostal networks in the American South and Midwest. Particularly devastating for those whose family and community constitute their entire social world with no outside reference point, those in communities where the concept of same-sex attraction exists only as sin or pathology with no neutral vocabulary available, those whose religious framework is not experienced as external imposition but as genuinely held personal faith creating an internal conflict that is not simply resolved by leaving, those in countries where homosexuality is criminalized meaning the absence of language is also the absence of legal safety, and adolescents at the developmental stage where identity formation is occurring without access to any mirror that reflects what they are experiencing as a livable human condition rather than a condemnable aberration.
Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: The mechanisms here are not primarily legislative but architectural, built into the structure of daily life in ways that are more total than law because they require no enforcement. The community is the mechanism. Religious education systems that teach same-sex attraction as intrinsically disordered or demonically influenced do not merely prohibit the identity but provide the cognitive framework through which the young person understands their own interior experience, meaning the prohibition is installed before the thing being prohibited has been named. Conversion therapy, still legal in the majority of countries and actively practiced in religious community contexts globally, operates not only as a formal intervention but as an ambient social pressure where the possibility of change is presented as both spiritually necessary and personally achievable, locating the failure of change in the individual's insufficient faith or effort. In countries where homosexuality is criminalized, Uganda, Nigeria, Iran, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, and others, the legal system does not need to actively prosecute to function as a mechanism of control; the existence of the law shapes behavior, suppresses disclosure, and removes any institutional actor including healthcare providers, teachers, and counselors from the category of safe disclosure recipients because mandatory reporting or social reporting norms make disclosure to a professional a potential legal event. Family systems in tight religious communities function as total institutions in the sociological sense, controlling housing, economic survival, social belonging, and spiritual standing simultaneously, meaning the cost of disclosure is not one thing but everything at once.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: A seventeen year old in a Pentecostal household in rural Nigeria or a conservative Muslim family in rural Indonesia or an ultra-Orthodox community in Brooklyn knows something about themselves that has no name in the language their community gave them. They have encountered the feeling, recognized it as the thing the sermons are about, and concluded privately that they are the thing being condemned without ever having heard the thing described as something a person could be and survive. They do not have language for what they are experiencing not because the words do not exist in the world but because the words do not exist in their world, and the world they have is the only world they have access to. They perform. The performance is not occasional but total, covering every interaction, every expressed preference, every response to the question of who they find attractive, every moment in a gender-segregated space, every prayer meeting where they bow their head and ask to be changed and feel the asking fail and interpret the failure as a further confirmation of their fundamental wrongness. They become expert at a self that is not theirs. The expertise is exhausting in a way that has no name either, because naming the exhaustion would require naming the thing causing it. They do not seek help because the help available in their community is the thing harming them, and the help outside their community requires leaving, and leaving requires resources, language, and an imaginable destination that their formation has not given them. The research on what this costs is measurable in rates of depression, self-harm, and suicidal ideation that are multiple times higher in this population than in the general adolescent population, but the research exists outside the community and the community has reasons to dispute it. The young person does not have access to the research. They have access to the sermon. They sit in the pew on Sunday next to the parent who would be destroyed by knowing, inside a faith that is genuinely theirs and genuinely harming them simultaneously, performing the amen.
