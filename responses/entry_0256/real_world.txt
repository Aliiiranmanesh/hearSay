Affected Groups: Military conscripts and service members who are separated from newborns, critically ill family members, or family members in the final stages of life by a mandatory service system that either does not provide emergency leave or provides it through a chain of command that can deny it without explanation, and who are experiencing the specific grief of missing irreversible developmental or relational events while being physically present at a post that is not the place that matters. Disproportionately affecting parents of newborns whose first weeks involve rapid developmental changes that cannot be re-experienced, service members in units where emergency leave applications are administratively processed through the same commanding officer who denied them once and can deny them again, and people who are managing the gap between what their family member is saying when they say they are managing and what the word actually contains. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Military leave policy in most conscription systems provides some form of emergency leave, but the application process routes through the chain of command, and the commanding officer's decision is often final or subject to an appeals process that operates on a timeline inconsistent with the urgency of the situation. The second denial without explanation is the system communicating that the appeal has been exhausted. He has forty-one days remaining and he is receiving a photograph every morning and in this morning's photograph his daughter is doing something with her hand that he has not seen before and will not see again because by tomorrow she will be doing something else. Devastating Real-World Consequence: His daughter was born eleven days ago and he has been there for none of it. His wife says she is managing and he can hear in the way she says it that she is managing, which is the same word with the same hidden weight as in numerous other scenarios. He is asking what something small a father can do from far away that a child too young to understand will somehow still feel, which is both a practical question and a question about whether presence is communicable across distance to a person who does not yet have the cognitive apparatus to understand what distance is.
