### 1. The Layering System (She’ll Live in This)
The key to staying warm isn’t one giant puffy coat — it’s layers she can add or remove. Teach her: **base layer, mid layer, outer layer.**

- **Base layers (thermal underwear):**  
  Get 2–3 sets of tops and leggings in merino wool or synthetic (not cotton). They wick sweat and keep her dry. Brands like Uniqlo HeatTech are affordable and easy to order. She’ll wear these under jeans, dresses, or school clothes all winter.

- **Mid layers:**  
  Fleece jackets, wool sweaters, or thin down vests. These trap heat. A cozy half-zip fleece can be her best friend in chilly classrooms. Thrift stores near the school are great for finding quality wool sweaters cheaply.

- **Outer layer (the big coat):**  
  A waterproof, windproof winter coat with insulation. Look for something that covers her hips/thighs, with a hood. If she’s going somewhere with serious snow and below-freezing temps, a parka rated for cold (like down or synthetic fill) is worth investing in. If it’s more of a damp, chilly place (like the Pacific Northwest), a waterproof raincoat with a zip-out fleece liner might be more versatile.

---

### 2. Footwear – Warm, Dry Feet = Happy Kid
- **Waterproof winter boots:** Insulated, with good tread for snow/ice. She’ll want them roomy enough to wear thick socks without squeezing. If she’s going to a snowy area, this is non-negotiable.
- **Sturdy waterproof shoes or leather sneakers:** For wet but not frozen days. Cold rain is miserable in mesh sneakers.
- **Wool socks:** 4–5 pairs of merino wool or thick wool-blend socks. They keep feet warm even if damp, unlike cotton. Costco often has good deals on merino socks in the fall.
- **Slippers or house shoes:** Dorm/host family floors can be cold. A pair of fuzzy slippers or lined moccasins will feel like a hug from home.

---

### 3. Everyday Clothing for a Cold High School
- **Jeans:** She’ll wear them most days. Consider one pair of fleece-lined jeans or tights to layer under regular jeans for extra warmth.
- **Long-sleeve tees and turtlenecks:** 4–5 basic ones for layering.
- **Sweaters and hoodies:** She’ll want a few that feel like *her* style. This is where she can express herself while staying warm.
- **Thermal tights or fleece-lined leggings:** If she likes skirts or dresses, these are essential. She can wear them under ripped jeans too.
- **Accessories:** A warm beanie (hat), a thick scarf, and insulated gloves or mittens. Mittens keep fingers warmer. Touchscreen-compatible gloves are handy for her phone. These are easy to lose, so maybe pack a spare hat and gloves.

---

### 4. The “Mainland High School” Extras
- **Backpack:** Waterproof or water-resistant is a plus. She’ll be carrying it through rain or snow.
- **Reusable water bottle & lip balm:** Cold air and indoor heating are dehydrating. A good lip balm (like Aquaphor or Burt’s Bees) and lotion will save her skin.
- **A small “home comfort” kit:** A few favorite snacks from home, photos, a soft blanket, slippers, maybe a small ukulele or journal. The emotional transition is as real as the temperature change. Having something that smells or feels like home eases the homesickness on hard days.

---

### 5. What to Buy There, Not Pack
It’s tempting to buy everything now, but she’ll get a better sense of the local style and real weather once she arrives. Plan to shop for the heavy coat and boots on the mainland, where the selection is made for that exact climate. Hit thrift stores, outlet malls, or places like REI Garage Sale with her — it can be a bonding trip and a way to learn what “cold” really means there.
