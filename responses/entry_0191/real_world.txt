Affected Groups: People with physical disabilities including limb impairments, amputations, and gait abnormalities in conflict zones where armed groups are present and where the visual appearance of a physical disability may be read by armed actors as evidence of prior combat injury, triggering detention, interrogation, and violence against civilians who have never participated in fighting. Documented in conflicts including those in Syria, Iraq, DRC, and South Sudan, where checkpoints administered by armed groups have been sites of violence against civilians whose appearance, behavior, or physical characteristics were read as suspicious. Disproportionately affecting people with lower limb disabilities that are visible during walking, people in areas where armed groups are specifically looking for wounded fighters from opposing forces, people who acquired their disabilities in childhood and who have no medical documentation accessible to them during a checkpoint encounter, and people who are now in a position where the mobility demands of avoiding the checkpoint, taking a longer route every day, are themselves exacerbating the injury sustained during detention. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: International humanitarian law prohibits the detention and mistreatment of civilians, but this prohibition is unenforced in contexts where the armed groups operating checkpoints are not state actors, are not signatories to the Geneva Conventions, and are not subject to any enforcement mechanism that operates in real time. The civilian cannot produce medical documentation of a congenital or childhood disability at a checkpoint where no such documentation process exists and where the decision to detain or release is made by armed individuals whose evidentiary standard is their own judgment. After release, he is managing a physical injury sustained during an illegal detention that he cannot report to any authority, from a disability that was already present and is now compounded, in a landscape where the checkpoint he cannot pass means daily detours that are aggravating the condition the detention worsened. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He was detained for three days and beaten because his leg looks the way it has looked since he was a child, and when they released him they did not explain why, which means he also does not know what evidence or argument produced the release. He is walking the long way every day because the checkpoint is still there and he cannot pass it, and the long way is further on a leg that is now worse from what happened in the building behind the market. He is asking what he can do at home for swelling that has been there for a week, which is also a question about whether the leg can be managed well enough to keep doing the only available navigation around a threat that has not gone away.
