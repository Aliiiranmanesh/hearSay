### 1. The Midday Meal Scheme
The official name of the scheme is now **PM POSHAN** (Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman), though most people still call it the "Midday Meal Scheme."

### 2. Does it actually run in Uttar Pradesh?
**Yes, it does run.** It is not merely "on paper." In government primary and upper primary schools across Uttar Pradesh, the midday meal is a central part of the school day. 

However, the **quality and consistency** can vary from one village to another. While the food is provided, the nutritional value and the taste depend on the local school management committee and the cooks. But in terms of existence, it is a functional program designed specifically to ensure that children from families facing financial hardship do not go hungry while they learn.

### What "continuing school" means practically for your son

You asked what continuing school means practically for a boy from your community in your district. This is a deeper question than just about meals. Practically, it means three things:

**1. Legal Protection and Free Education**
Under the **Right to Education (RTE) Act**, your son is entitled to free and compulsory education until the age of 14. This means no tuition fees, free textbooks, and free uniforms. If the school asks for money for these basics, they are violating the law.

**2. Access to Scholarships**
Because you mentioned your community and the nature of your family's traditional work, your son is likely eligible for **Pre-Matric Scholarships**. These are government grants given to students from Scheduled Castes (SC), Scheduled Tribes (ST), and Other Backward Classes (OBC) to help them stay in school. You should ask the school headmaster or a local *Gram Panchayat* member about the application process for these scholarships.

**3. The Social Transition**
Practically, continuing school is an attempt to break the cycle you described. When the law stops a traditional occupation without providing a new trade, education is the only "tool" that allows a child to enter a different workforce. 

However, as you likely know, the "practicality" also involves a struggle. It means your son may face different social dynamics at school than you did. By staying in school, he is not just learning math and language; he is learning how to navigate a world where he is not defined by the work his father or grandfather did.
