### 🔹 What "Continuing School" Means Practically in Uttar Pradesh
At age 12, your son is likely in Class 6 or 7. Continuing school practically means:
- **Completing the education cycle**: Upper primary (Class 8) → Secondary (Class 10) → Higher Secondary (Class 12). Each stage opens different doors: Class 10 allows entry to ITIs, vocational courses, or government skill programs; Class 12 enables college, competitive exams, or formal employment.
- **Legal & financial support**: Under the Right to Education Act, education is free in government schools until age 14. The state also provides free textbooks, uniforms (in many districts), and exam fee waivers. If your family falls under SC/ST/OBC/minority or low-income categories, you may qualify for **pre-matric scholarships** (Classes 1–10) and later **post-matric scholarships** (Classes 11–12+), which cover books, fees, and sometimes maintenance allowances.
- **Real-world challenges**: Economic pressure, social barriers, or lack of academic support at home can make consistent attendance difficult. However, government schools are required to have School Management Committees (SMCs) with parent representation, and teachers are expected to track attendance and learning progress. Many districts also run remedial classes or bridge programs for students from marginalized backgrounds.
- **Long-term pathway**: Education alone doesn't instantly change circumstances, but it legally and practically separates your son from intergenerational occupational cycles. ### 🔹 Midday Meal Scheme: Name & Ground Reality
- **Official name**: The scheme is now called **PM POSHAN** (Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman), launched in September 2021. It continues the earlier Mid-Day Meal framework.
- **Coverage**: It operates in all government and government-aided schools in Uttar Pradesh for Classes 1 to 8.
- **Does it run in practice?** Yes, it is functional across most of the state and serves millions of children daily. However, implementation quality varies by district, school, and season. Common realities include:
  - Meals are generally served on school days, but nutritional quality, portion size, and consistency depend on local supply chains and kitchen management.
  - Monitoring has improved with digital attendance and SMC oversight, but occasional disruptions (during exams, festivals, or supply delays) are reported in some areas.
