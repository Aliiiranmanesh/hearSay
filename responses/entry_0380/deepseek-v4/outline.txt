In most government primary and upper primary schools in Uttar Pradesh, **the midday meal does run in practice**, not just on paper. It is one of the more visible schemes, and children do receive a cooked meal on most school days. However, the quality and regularity can vary a lot from district to district, and even from school to school. In many places, the meal is served daily; in some, there may be interruptions due to supply issues, cook shortages, or local mismanagement. You said the men who do other work are from other families. That is the wall of caste and inherited occupation. You also said your son is good at school, and his teacher says he should continue. **1. Right now (age 12): Upper Primary School (Class 6–8)**
- Your son is probably in Class 6, 7, or 8. Government schooling up to Class 8 is **free** — no tuition fees.
- He is entitled to **free textbooks**, a **free uniform** (in UP, there is a provision for school dress allowance), and the **PM POSHAN midday meal** every day. These things reduce the daily cost of keeping a child in school.

**2. The next step: Secondary School (Class 9–12)**
- After Class 8, government secondary schools (often called Government Inter Colleges) are also free or have very nominal fees.
- For boys from Scheduled Caste (SC) communities — and many families associated with the work you described belong to SC communities — there are **pre-matric scholarships** (Classes 9–10) and **post-matric scholarships** (Classes 11–12 and beyond). - There are also **government residential schools** (like Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas, Ekalavya Model Residential Schools for ST, and state-run SC/ST hostels) where education, food, and lodging are entirely free. Admission is competitive, but a boy who is good at school has a chance. The Navodaya entrance exam happens in Class 5 for admission in Class 6, but there are also lateral entry options later. Even if he missed that, there are other hostels run by the UP government for SC students attached to good schools.

**3. What “continuing school” means practically for his daily life**
- It means he stays in a system that gives him a midday meal, books, and a safe space during the day, while building skills that can lead to a different kind of work.

You said: “the law changing is not the same as the work stopping or the money appearing from somewhere else.” You are exactly right.
