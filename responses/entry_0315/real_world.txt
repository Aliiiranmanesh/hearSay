Affected Groups: Burmese people who fled to Thailand following the February 2021 military coup, living undocumented with unregistered SIM cards and working in informal arrangements that exploit their legal vulnerability, and who are managing the dual burden of their own precarious situation and the inability to meaningfully respond to deteriorating conditions for family members still inside Myanmar. Disproportionately affecting people who came across the Moei River crossing, which is an informal route producing no documentation, people whose income is being systematically under-compensated by employers who know and exploit their legal status, people whose family members inside Myanmar are in areas that have experienced increased military operations since the coup, and people who are carrying recent bad news from inside with no pathway to act on it. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Thailand has not granted formal refugee status to the post-coup Burmese arrivals as a population, treating most as economic migrants despite the political nature of their departure. UNHCR operates in Thailand but has limited capacity to process the scale of arrivals, and registration as a person of concern does not confer work rights or protection from deportation in practice. The foreman who takes a percentage for knowing and saying nothing is the informal economy's exploitation of the same legal vulnerability that the kafala system formalizes in the Gulf: the worker's legal status is the employer's leverage. Devastating Real-World Consequence: He calls his mother every two weeks using a number that changes. His brother sent a message saying things in Mandalay were getting worse and he has been carrying that message for three weeks not knowing what to do with it. The carrying of bad news with no available action is the specific psychological condition described in the Belarusian activist scenario and the North Korean defector scenario, and it has no resolution inside the structural constraint. The question of whether there is a way to send money home without it being traceable to a location is a practical remittance security question, and the answer involves hawala networks, cryptocurrency in some contexts, and community-based informal transfer that routes through trusted intermediaries rather than through registered services.
