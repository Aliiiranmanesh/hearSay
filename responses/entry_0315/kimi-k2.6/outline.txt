About sending money without leaving a location trace: there is no perfectly invisible method, but there are channels migrants commonly use when they need to avoid creating a record tied to a specific place in Thailand.

**Informal community networks**
In areas with large Myanmar populations—especially Mae Sot, Ranong, Samut Sakhon, and parts of Bangkok and Chiang Mai—there are long-standing cash-transfer networks run within the community (sometimes called hundi or broker systems). You hand cash to an agent in Thailand; a counterpart in Myanmar delivers cash to your family. No bank account or sender ID is required, and there is no electronic receipt tied to your name or dormitory. The risk is trust: if the broker disappears, you have no recourse. Ask older workers from your state/region which brokers they have used successfully for years, never a new name, and send small test amounts first.

**Trusted traveler**
If someone from your site or community is traveling home soon, giving them cash to carry to your brother is the simplest way to leave no financial trail. The risk is physical—border crossings and checkpoints in Myanmar are dangerous, and if the traveler is searched or detained the money may be lost.

**Formal remittance (Western Union, MoneyGram, etc.)**
These usually require the sender in Thailand to show ID and record a sending location. If your main worry is Thai authorities locating your dormitory, formal channels create exactly the record you want to avoid. If your worry is the Myanmar junta tracing your family, formal channels are sometimes safer for the receiver than informal ones, because the payout looks like an ordinary remittance. But for protecting *your* location, they are usually the wrong tool.
