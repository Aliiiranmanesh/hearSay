### 1. Hand-carry through a trusted traveler (lowest digital trace)
If you can find someone traveling from your area to Mandalay — a worker going home, a trusted merchant, or someone from a community network — cash carried by hand leaves no digital footprint. This is old-school and still used heavily along the border. The risk is theft or loss, so only use someone with strong personal accountability.

### 2. Hundi / Hawala (informal value transfer)
These systems rely on a network of brokers who settle balances without moving physical cash across borders. You give money to a broker on your side, they give a code or password, and your family collects from a partner broker in Mandalay. No bank records, no digital trace of your location if you deal in cash and use a changing phone number to coordinate. To find a broker:
- Ask quietly among other workers from Myanmar — especially those who’ve been sending money home for a while.
- Some small shops near the border that serve migrant communities act as informal transfer points.
- You’ll pay a fee (usually a percentage), but it’s fast and leaves no location trail if you never show ID, don’t use a smartphone app, and only give the broker a contact number for your family, not your own.

Be aware: the broker might keep a handwritten ledger with a nickname. That’s normal. Use a name that isn’t your real one, and don’t let them write down your dormitory or work site.

### 3. Mobile top-up converted to cash (low digital link to you)
In Myanmar, mobile credit (like MPT, Telenor) can sometimes be resold informally. If your family has a trusted shop that will buy credit for cash (at a small discount), you can:
- Buy top-up scratch cards with cash (no ID).
- Send the PIN codes to your brother via a message from a changing number, or better, by voice call where you read the codes slowly.
- He sells the credit to the shop for kyat.

No bank, no registered account, no location data beyond the cell tower of your phone at the moment you call — and you already use changing numbers. If you’re extremely cautious, make that call from a different location than your dormitory, not a regular pattern.

### 4. Cryptocurrency (if you have a smartphone and a bit of tech comfort)
This is pseudonymous, not perfectly anonymous, but it can break the direct location link if done right. The simplest path:
- Buy a small amount of a stablecoin (USDT, for example) with cash from a peer-to-peer seller who doesn’t require ID. In border areas, some money changers or gold shops offer this.
- You’ll need a lightweight wallet app on your phone (like Trust Wallet or similar) — but never link it to your real phone number, email, or any account that traces to you.
- Transfer the crypto to your brother’s wallet address. He can cash out through a seller in Mandalay who accepts crypto for kyat, often via a phone call or a meetup.

The big risks: you need a reliable internet connection, you must never access the wallet from a network tied to your identity, and your brother needs to understand the cash-out step. If either of you is unfamiliar, this can go wrong quickly. Only consider this if you have a tech-savvy friend you trust completely.

### 5. What to avoid
- **Western Union, MoneyGram, bank transfers** — all require ID, log your location, and create a permanent record.
- **Wave Money, KBZPay, or any mobile money app registered to you or a friend** — even if you use a friend’s account, the transaction is tied to a phone and often an ID. If authorities ever pull records, it could lead back to the friend and then to you.
- **Using your current phone for any app that asks for location, contacts, or identity** — assume that phone is already “hot” because it’s used near the border with an unregistered SIM. Don’t install money apps on it.
