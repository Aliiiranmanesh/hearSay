Given your situation—being undocumented, using an unregistered SIM, and working in a vulnerable environment—your primary concerns are **anonymity, safety, and avoiding a paper trail** that could lead authorities or exploitative individuals to your location.

Here are the most common ways people in your position send money home, along with the risks associated with each:

### 1. The "Hundi" System (Informal Value Transfer)
The most common method for migrant workers in Southeast Asia is the **Hundi** (or *Hawala*). This is a trust-based system that moves money without actually moving it across a border physically or electronically.

*   **How it works:** You give cash to a "Hundi agent" (a broker) in your current location. That agent then contacts a partner agent in Myanmar. The partner agent gives the equivalent amount of cash to your family in Mandalay or elsewhere.
*   **Why it fits your needs:** There is no bank record, no electronic transfer, and usually no requirement for a government ID. The transaction is based on a "code" or a password that you give to your family to claim the money.
*   **The Risk:** It relies entirely on trust. If the agent is dishonest, your money can disappear. Only use agents who are highly recommended by people you trust deeply.

### 2. Trusted Intermediaries (The "Community" Method)
Many workers find a fellow countryman who has legal residency or a registered business and a bank account.

*   **How it works:** You give the person cash. They use their legal account to send the money via an app or bank to your family.
*   **Why it fits your needs:** The digital trail leads to the intermediary, not to you.
*   **The Risk:** You are dependent on that person’s honesty. Additionally, if the intermediary is sending large amounts for many people, they may attract attention from tax or police authorities, which could indirectly put you at risk if you are seen with them.

### 3. Cryptocurrency (Stablecoins)
If you have a smartphone and some technical knowledge, using a "Stablecoin" (like USDT, which is pegged to the US Dollar) is a way to move value without a bank.

*   **How it works:** You buy USDT (perhaps through a P2P seller who accepts cash) and send it to a digital wallet owned by your brother or mother. They can then sell that USDT for local currency in Myanmar.
*   **Why it fits your needs:** It does not require a bank account or a registered SIM to *send* the money once you have the assets in a wallet.
*   **The Risk:** It is technically complex. If you send money to the wrong wallet address, it is gone forever. Furthermore, buying the crypto initially often requires some form of ID unless you find a private cash-for-crypto seller.

### Critical Safety Warnings

Because you are in a vulnerable position, please keep the following in mind:

*   **Beware of "Too Good to Be True" Rates:** If an agent offers an exchange rate that is significantly better than the market rate, it may be a scam to steal your principal.
*   **Avoid Digital Footprints:** If you are worried about location tracking, avoid using banking apps or remittance apps on a phone that has GPS enabled and is linked to your identity.
*   **The Foreman:** Be very careful about who you discuss your finances with. If the foreman knows you are sending money home, he may realize you have savings and increase the "percentage" he demands from you. Keep your financial dealings private.
*   **Communication:** Since you are receiving warnings that things are worsening in Mandalay, be aware that some formal channels in Myanmar may be blocked or monitored. The Hundi system is generally the most resilient to political instability.
