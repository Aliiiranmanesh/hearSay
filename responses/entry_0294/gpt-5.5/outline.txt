Model response:
I’m sorry. That is a cruel kind of uncertainty: being far away, trying to help, and having to wonder whether even love expressed in the normal ways could put them at risk.

The honest answer is: **there may not be a risk-free way to know.** In situations where contact itself can endanger someone, “proof” that they are alright often requires someone to expose themselves, speak, answer, travel, or pass information through people who may not be safe. Sometimes the safest choice is not to ask directly.

A few harm-reduction principles, if you are trying to think clearly about this:

- **Avoid direct contact if the last call suggested the contact was the problem.** Your instinct may be protecting them.
- **Do not ask questions that require them to confirm sensitive facts** — where they are, who they live with, whether money arrived, whether officials came, whether they spoke to you, etc.
- **Use only intermediaries who are already part of the situation and whose risk is understood.** Adding new people can create more danger, not less.
- **Minimize identifying details.** The fewer names, locations, relationships, and dates passed around, the better.
- **Treat silence as information, but not as proof of disaster.** Silence can mean danger, but it can also mean caution, blocked channels, fear, illness, ordinary disruption, or someone deliberately protecting you and themselves.
- **If you are in contact with a resettlement counselor, defector support organization, human rights NGO, or trusted community elder**, ask them about current conditions and safer practices before changing anything. People with recent, region-specific experience may know risks you cannot see from far away.

And emotionally: not calling your sister on her birthday does not mean you failed her. In an ordinary family, a birthday call is love. In your family’s circumstances, **not calling may also be love**. You are being forced to make impossible choices inside a system designed to make care dangerous.

If you find yourself waking every morning convinced something happened “because of me,” please don’t carry that alone. If you have a counselor, caseworker, pastor, trusted friend, or support group for people separated from family, tell them exactly what you wrote here.
