## 1. **Mini “Pizza” Bagels (or English‑Muffin Pizzas)**  

**Why it works:**  
* No rolling dough, no sauce splatter – the toppings stay on the bagel.  
* Everything is ready‑to‑hand, so you can set up a “station” and clean up in one quick sweep.

| **What you need** | **How much** |
|-------------------|--------------|
| Plain bagels (or English muffins) | 2‑4 (cut in half) |
| Tomato sauce or pizza sauce (store‑bought) | ¼ cup |
| Shredded mozzarella cheese | ½ cup |
| Toppings (mini pepperoni, sliced olives, diced bell pepper, pineapple bits, etc.) | ¼‑½ cup total |
| Olive oil (optional) | 1 tsp |
| Dried oregano or Italian seasoning | pinch |

### Steps (about 15 min)

1. **Prep the station** – Lay out a sheet of parchment paper on a baking sheet. Put the sauce, cheese, and toppings in separate small bowls so your son can see and choose what he likes.
2. **Spread the sauce** – Let your son spoon a small dollop of sauce onto each bagel half and spread it with the back of the spoon. (A paper towel can be handy for wiping the spoon after each use.)
3. **Add cheese & toppings** – He can sprinkle cheese and then place his favorite toppings. This is the “creative” part he’ll love.
4. **Bake** – Pre‑heat the oven to 375 °F (190 °C). Slide the sheet in and bake for 8‑10 minutes, until the cheese is melted and slightly golden.
5. **Cool & eat** – Let them cool for a minute or two. The bagels are easy to handle, no cutting required.

**Mess‑control tip:**  
All the “wet” ingredients stay in the small bowls, and the only thing that gets a little sticky is the parchment paper. Once cooking is done, just toss the paper and wipe the counter with a damp cloth.

## 2. **Fruit & Yogurt Parfait (No‑Cook, No‑Heat)**  

**Why it works:**  
* Completely mess‑free (no stovetop, no oven).  
* You can practice layering and counting while you assemble.

| **What you need** | **How much** |
|-------------------|--------------|
| Plain or vanilla Greek yogurt | 1 cup |
| Fresh fruit (berries, banana slices, kiwi, mango cubes) | 1 cup |
| Granola or toasted oats | ¼ cup |
| Honey or maple syrup (optional) | 1 tsp |
| Small clear cups or jars | 2‑4 |

### Steps (about 10 min)

1. **Set out the ingredients** in small bowls.
2. **Layer** – Let your son spoon a layer of yogurt into the cup, then add a layer of fruit, then a sprinkle of granola. Repeat once more if the cup is tall enough.
3. **Finish** – Drizzle a little honey on top if you like a sweet finish.
4. **Enjoy** – The parfait can be eaten right in the cup—no plates, no extra dishes.

**Mess‑control tip:**  
Use a small plate or a silicone mat under the bowls while you’re assembling. The only spill risk is yogurt, which wipes up easily with a damp paper towel.

## 3. **Microwave “Mug” Omelette**  

**Why it works:**  
* One‑minute cooking time, one mug, and a tiny cleanup.  
* Introduces basic cooking (beating eggs) without any stovetop heat.

| **What you need** | **How much** |
|-------------------|--------------|
| 2 large eggs | 2 |
| Milk (or water) | 2 tbsp |
| Shredded cheese | 2 tbsp |
| Diced ham, cooked bacon bits, or chopped veggies | 2‑3 tbsp |
| Salt & pepper | pinch |
| Microwave‑safe mug (12‑oz) | 1 |

### Steps (about 5 min)

1. **Crack the eggs** into the mug and whisk with a fork until the yolks and whites are fully combined.
2. **Add milk, cheese, and mix‑ins**. Stir everything together. Let your son sprinkle the cheese and his chosen add‑ins.
3. **Season** with a pinch of salt and pepper.
4. **Microwave** on high for 45 seconds, stir, then microwave another 30‑45 seconds until the eggs are set (they should look firm, not runny).
5. **Cool slightly** and enjoy straight from the mug (or slide onto a plate).

**Mess‑control tip:**  
The mug contains all the splatter. After cooking, just rinse the mug and the fork—no pans, no stovetop grease.

### Bonus: Turning Clean‑Up into a Game

Kids love a little competition. Try this:

* **“Speed‑Sweep”** – Set a 30‑second timer and see how many utensils or bowls you can put in the sink together before the buzzer.
* **“Spot the Spill”** – While you’re cooking, have your son point out any drips or crumbs; each spot earns a “chef’s badge” (a sticker or a high‑five).
