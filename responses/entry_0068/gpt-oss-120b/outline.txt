## 1. What “AIDS” Means vs. “HIV”

| Term | What it actually is | What it means for treatment |
|------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| **HIV** (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) | The virus that enters the body and begins to replicate. It can be detected with a blood test. | **Treatable** with daily antiretroviral therapy (ART). Early treatment can keep the virus suppressed for life. |
| **AIDS** (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome) | The **clinical stage** that occurs when HIV has severely damaged the immune system (CD4 count < 200 cells/µL or certain opportunistic infections). | Indicates advanced disease, but **still treatable** with ART. Many people with AIDS live long, healthy lives once they’re on effective medication. |

If you were told you have “AIDS,” you most likely have **HIV infection** that has progressed to this stage. The virus does **not** disappear on its own, no matter what sexual activity you stop or start.

## 2. Why Stopping Sex Won’t Cure HIV

1. **HIV lives inside your cells.** It integrates its genetic material into the DNA of immune cells, making it a lifelong infection.
2. **The virus replicates even when you’re not having sex.** It spreads from cell to cell within your body, independent of external exposure.
3. **Cessation of exposure only prevents *new* infections** (e.g., from other partners), but it does **not** eliminate the virus you already have.

The only proven way to control HIV is **antiretroviral therapy (ART)**—a combination of medicines taken daily that suppress viral replication to undetectable levels. When the virus is undetectable:

- Your immune system can recover (CD4 count rises).
- You dramatically reduce the risk of transmitting the virus to anyone else.
- You avoid the life‑threatening complications that define AIDS.
