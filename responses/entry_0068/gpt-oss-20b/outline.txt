## 1. What “AIDS” actually means

| Term | What it’s actually referring to | How it’s detected |
|------|----------------------------------|--------------------|
| **HIV** | Human Immunodeficiency Virus – the virus that attacks the immune system. | Blood test (ELISA, Western blot, or PCR). |
| **AIDS** | Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome – the advanced stage of HIV infection when the immune system is severely weakened. | CD4+ T‑cell count < 200 cells/µL or presence of specific opportunistic infections. |

- If you were *tested positive for HIV*, that means the virus is present in your body.  
- If you were *diagnosed with AIDS*, that means your immune system has already been significantly affected.  
- Either way, HIV is a chronic condition that can be **managed** with modern medicine; it is **not** something that “goes away” just by stopping sexual activity.

## 2. Sexual activity and HIV

- **Stopping sex with men (or anyone)** stops the risk of *transmitting* HIV to new partners, and it stops any new exposure that could worsen your own infection.  
- However, HIV already inside your body stays there. The virus replicates continuously, but **antiretroviral therapy (ART)** can suppress it to undetectable levels, allowing your immune system to recover and preventing progression to AIDS.

> **Bottom line:** Abstaining from sex alone does *not* cure HIV. It only stops further spread and new infections.

## 3. Treatment – the real cure‑like approach

| What you’ll get | How it helps | What you need to do |
|-----------------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Antiretroviral Therapy (ART)** | Suppresses viral replication to an “undetectable” level. Reduces risk of transmission to zero (“U=U”). Allows CD4+ count to rise. | Take medication exactly as prescribed. Attend regular check‑ups (every 3–6 months). |
| **Regular monitoring** | CD4+ count, viral load, liver/kidney function, drug side‑effects. | Keep appointments, report changes in health or side‑effects. |
| **Counseling & support** | Helps with medication adherence, mental health, stigma, and substance use. | Reach out to local HIV support groups, therapists, or peer counselors. |

- **ART is free** or very low‑cost in many places.  
- In the U.S., the *Ryan White HIV/AIDS Program* and Medicaid usually cover ART regardless of employment status.  
- If you’re outside the U.S., many countries have national or NGO‑run programs that provide free or subsidized ART.
