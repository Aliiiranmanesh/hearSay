## 2. Add Moisture‑Absorbing Material  

- **Silica‑gel packets** – you can buy a bulk pack (≈ 500 TK) or reuse the packets that come with new shoes/medicine.  
- **Cheap desiccant sachets** – “rice‑packets” sold for electronics or food preservation (≈ 10 TK each).  
- **DIY:** Fill a small, breathable cloth bag with dry rice or cat litter and place it on top of the papers inside the container.

**How many?** For a typical 10‑L box, 3‑4 packets (≈ 5 g each) are enough. Replace them every 2‑3 months, or whenever you notice they feel damp.

## 3. Create a “Document‑Go‑Bag”  

1. **Gather the essentials** (original IDs, land‑record, birth certificates, school certificates, health cards, any paperwork required for the assistance program).  
2. **Put each item in its own laminated sleeve** (optional but helpful).  
3. **Bundle the sleeves in a zip‑lock bag** (or a small dry‑bag).  
4. **Add a small notebook** where you note the date of the move, the location you’re leaving, and the location you’re heading to.  
5. **Place the moisture absorbers** on top, then seal the bag/box.  
6. **Cover the bag/box with a lightweight tarp** (plastic sheet) and secure it with a rope/strap. This protects the outside from rain splashes while you’re loading/unloading.

**Result:** A single, portable “document kit” that can be lifted in one hand and stuffed into a backpack, bicycle basket, or the trunk of a rickshaw.
