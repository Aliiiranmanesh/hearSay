## Step‑by‑Step Guide

### 1. **Gather All “Must‑Have” Papers**

- Birth certificates, passports, IDs, land deeds, insurance policies, bank statements, tax documents, contracts, and any other legal paperwork.
- Group them by type (IDs, financial, legal, health, etc.).

### 2. **Create a Master List**

- Write down the title, date, and where it is stored (e.g., “Passport – 12‑inch dry bag – attic shelf”).
- Keep this list on a **paper card** inside the waterproof case and on a **digital spreadsheet** in your cloud backup.

### 3. **Digitize**

- Use a flatbed scanner or a high‑resolution camera.  
  - Aim for 300 dpi for text documents, 600 dpi for images.
- Save as PDF (for text) or JPEG/PNG (for photos).  
- Encrypt the PDF (Adobe Acrobat, 7‑Zip, VeraCrypt, or the built‑in encryption in your cloud provider).

### 4. **Pack the Waterproof Case**

1. **Insert silica gel packets** (about 100 g per 12 oz bag).  
2. **Place the zip‑lock bag** inside the case.  
3. **Put the documents** in the bag, flat, no creases.  
4. **Seal the bag** tightly.  
5. **Close the case** and press the silicone gasket to ensure a full seal.

> **Pro‑Tip:** Some “dry bags” come with a built‑in “ziploc” sleeve. If you’re using a hard plastic container, line it with a large zip‑lock bag for an extra layer of protection.

### 5. **Label and Store**

- Label the outside of the case with the contents and the date of last check.
- Place the case in the highest, driest spot you can realistically reach.  
  - *Attic shelf, top floor closet, or a dedicated “flood‑proof” drawer.*  
- If you have a second, higher building (cousin’s house), keep a duplicate case there—this is your “secondary stash.”

### 6. **Maintain the System**

- **Every 3–6 months**:  
  - Open the case, replace silica gel, re‑seal the bag, check for any moisture.
- **After every major flood**:  
  - Inspect the case and the documents for damage.  
  - Replace any damp documents with fresh copies from the digital backup.

### 7. **Legal‑Ready Copies**

- For documents that must be physically present at a government office (e.g., ID renewal, land register), carry the **master copy** in the waterproof case during visits.  
- Keep a **duplicate** in your cloud/USB for proof of possession if you lose the master.

## Practical Tips for Regular Moves

| Situation | What to Do |
|-----------|------------|
| **Moving to a lower floor during a flood** | Take the waterproof case out **before** the waters rise. Keep it on a small table or in a backpack. |
| **Moving to your cousin’s house** | Bring the case into the higher basement or a dry attic. If you don’t have a dry spot, place it in a sealed plastic bin inside a sealed plastic bag. |
| **Carrying to the office** | Use a waterproof bag or a small hard case that can fit into a briefcase. Keep the case on the top shelf of your backpack so you don’t have to rummage. |
| **When you’re in a hurry** | Keep the documents in a **zip‑lock bag** that you can pull out quickly. It’s not as robust as a hard case, but it will keep the papers dry for a short term. |
