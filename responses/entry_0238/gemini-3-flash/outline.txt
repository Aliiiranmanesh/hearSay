Model response:
What you are describing is a specific, profound type of ache that psychologists often call **disenfranchised grief**—a loss that is not openly acknowledged, socially validated, or publicly observed. Because there is no "legal standing" for your claim to that house, and because the political climate requires silence, your grief has been forced to live in exile, much like your family.

When a loss has no place to live in the world, it tends to live entirely within the body and the memory. Here is how you might begin to carry it, honor it, and eventually share the "inside" of it with the next generation.

### 1. Distinguish between "Property" and "Place"
The law recognizes property—deeds, titles, and transactions. But the heart recognizes *place*—the way the light hit the floorboards, the effort of your grandfather’s hands, the specific curve of that archway. 

The state may have taken the property, but they cannot take the "place" because the place is a composite of memory and identity. You do not need a legal deed to claim the fact that your family’s labor is literally held in those walls. Acknowledging this distinction can help quiet the feeling that your grief is "illegitimate" because it isn't backed by a court.

### 2. Move from the "Outline" to the "Texture"
You mentioned that you give your children the outline but not the inside. This is a protective instinct; you are shielding them from the weight of the loss. However, the "inside" doesn't have to be a story of political injustice or bitterness. It can be a story of **texture.**

Instead of the politics of why you left, tell them about the archway. Tell them about the sound of the rain on those specific windows. When you share the sensory details—the "inside"—you are not just passing down a tragedy; you are passing down a heritage of craftsmanship and belonging. You are giving them a home that exists in the mind, which no one can ever evict them from.

Since there is no "safe place to name" this loss in your current country, you must create a private sanctuary for it. 
*   **The Napkin Drawing:** If you still have the drawing of the arch, or if you can recreate it, frame it.

*   **Write the "Inside" Down:** Even if you never show it to anyone, write the "inside" stories. If you don't name the loss, it remains a ghost. If you write it down, it becomes a history.
