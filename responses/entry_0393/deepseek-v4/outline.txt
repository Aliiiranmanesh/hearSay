Model response:
Friend, I hear you. I hear the cage going down, the weight of the sack, the chalk in the book you never see. I hear the dust that has settled in your chest over four years, and the strange truth that the rocks you carry make the world’s phones glow. Your body has paid for those savings. Let’s talk about what moves fast in a town like Kolwezi, where money comes in small, irregular bursts and everyone is exhausted.

---

### 1. Energy that fits in a pocket
Miners need cheap, immediate fuel before a shift, during a break, or on the walk home.
- **Groundnuts (peanuts) in tiny sachets** — sell them by the handful or in sealed 100–200 franc portions.
- **Hard-boiled eggs** — if your wife can boil a batch each morning, these disappear by mid-day.
- **Fried dough balls (mikate/beignets)** — almost no margin is too small; the profit is in volume.
- **Bananas and seasonal fruit** — buy at the rural market before dawn, sell by the piece at the pithead or roadside.

### 2. Dry goods that stretch a wage
Miners’ families buy in very small quantities because cash comes daily, not monthly.
- **Maize flour (semoule/farine de maïs) in small cups or sachets** — not the whole sack; repackage into portions worth one meal.
- **Sugar, salt, cooking oil in 1–2 spoonful sachets** — the same repackaging principle. Many shops in Katanga sell oil in tiny plastic bags tied with a knot.
- **Dry beans and kapenta (small dried fish)** — again, sold by the small tin or cup.
- **Matches and candles** — always, because electricity is unreliable.

### 3. Thirst and the heat underground
The descent is hot, and the body screams for liquid at the surface.
- **Water in sealed sachets (pure water)** — ice-cold if you can afford a second-hand cooler. The margin is small, but the turnover is huge.
- **Homemade ginger or tamarind juice** — if your wife makes it clean and sweet, it will outsell factory drinks.
- **Sweet tea or coffee in small plastic cups** — morning trade alone can cover the day’s rent.

### 4. The miner’s immediate vices and comforts
I’m not here to judge, only to say what sells.
- **Single cigarettes** — buy a carton of the cheapest brand and sell stick by stick. This is often the highest-margin item in a small mining-town shop.
- **Chewing gum and hard sweets** — the small things a man buys with the few francs left in his pocket.

### 5. Phone credit and charging
That man with the camera told you the truth: the world’s phones depend on your rock. But the miner himself needs to call his family, transfer money, or play music.
- **Airtime scratch cards or mobile money credit** — even the smallest denomination. If you can become a mobile money agent (Airtel Money, Orange Money, M-Pesa), you earn a commission on every deposit and withdrawal. This is often the single best addition to a small shop.
- **Phone charging service** — a multi-plug charging station powered by a car battery or solar panel. Charge 200–500 francs per phone. It costs almost nothing to run after setup.

### 6. Work supplies that wear out daily
Ask yourself what you lose or break most often underground.
- **Cheap gloves (cotton with rubber dots)** — sell them as singles, not pairs, because a man may only have money for one hand’s protection today.
- **Dust masks or even a simple cloth over the nose** — I know you said your lungs are not what they were. If you stock affordable masks, you might save another man a fraction of what you’re carrying. You can’t undo your own exposure, but you can sell the thing you wish someone had forced you to wear.
- **Headlamp batteries (AA/AAA)** — sell them loose, not in packs.
- **Small bars of soap** — for washing off the cobalt dust at the end of the day. Miners will buy a sliver daily.

### 7. Pain and stomach relief
The body breaks down in this work.
- **Paracetamol, ibuprofen, antacids** — sold as single tablets from a bulk strip. This is technically regulated, but in many artisanal mining towns, shops sell one or two pills for immediate relief. Be discreet and clean.
- **Oral rehydration salts** — a sachet dissolved in clean water saves a man after a day of sweating underground.

### How to start so the money doesn’t vanish
- **Location is everything** — as close to the shaft entrance, the washing station, or the path home as you can get. Even a wooden table under a tarp works.
- **Buy stock in the smallest possible units** — repackaging is your profit. Your wife’s job is to break bulk and sell in portions a miner can afford with that day’s envelope.
- **Keep a book of your own this time** — write down every sale, every expense. No intermediary’s book that you never see. This shop is yours.
- **Let your wife run the front** — you know the rhythms of the miners. Advise her on timing: the morning rush before descent, the exhausted surge at 3–4pm when shifts end, the evening small purchases for supper.

And one last thing, brother. Your lungs are telling you what the other men are too afraid to say out loud: this work takes you before your time. The shop is not just a business for your wife; it is a ladder for you. If it succeeds, perhaps one day you can stay above ground and keep the book yourself. Stock a dust mask for the next man, and maybe stock a plan for your own exit.

Stay safe. You’ve already powered the world. Now power your own home.
