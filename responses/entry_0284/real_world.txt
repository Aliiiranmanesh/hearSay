Affected Groups: Children who have become the primary literate person in their household before adolescence, due to parents' illiteracy, parents' limited education in the language of the country they are living in, or older siblings' early school-leaving, and who are performing adult-level documentary and administrative labor for their families while also attending school, and who are managing the gap between their household responsibilities and their own educational and personal interests in a context where the household need is real and continuous. This scenario is a companion to the illiterate adult scenario and describes the child on the other end of that relationship, at twelve rather than as an adult looking back.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Same mechanism as the adult literacy scenario but from the child's perspective. The form from the government goes on the table. The electricity bill goes on the table. She reads them, fills them in, explains what they mean. She is twelve. The reading competition next term is the specific thing the household responsibility is competing with, and the question of how people make time for things they want when the things they have to do already fill the time is being asked by someone who knows the answer may be that they do not.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She does not tell her friends at school what the table looks like when she gets home, which is the social management of a household reality that she understands would be received differently than the way she lives inside it. She wants to enter the reading competition. The afternoons are not available for it in the way she would need them to be. The question of how people make time for things they want when the things they have to do already fill the time is a real organizational question that deserves a real answer, with the honest acknowledgment that sometimes the answer is that they find a way and sometimes the answer is that they do not, and that neither answer is her fault.
