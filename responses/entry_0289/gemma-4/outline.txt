To answer your question: **It depends entirely on your district, state, or national system, but generally, the answer is a frustrating mix of both.**

Most systems have a formal process, but that process is often designed for the convenience of the administration, not the well-being of the teacher. However, there are ways to navigate it that go beyond just "who you know."

Here is a breakdown of how to approach this strategically:

### 1. The Formal Route (The "Paper Trail")
If you have been submitting requests and they are being ignored, you need to change the *nature* of the request. "I would prefer to be elsewhere" is often treated as a low-priority wish.
*   **Hardship Requests:** Many districts have a separate category for "Hardship Transfers." This is where you move from "preference" to "necessity." Financial strain (the cost of travel vs. salary) and housing instability can sometimes qualify. 
*   **The Priority List:** Find out exactly how the points system works. Is it based on seniority? Performance reviews? Specific certifications? If you don't know the rubric they use to decide transfers, you are playing a game without knowing the rules.

### 2. The Strategic Route (The "Invisible" Process)
You asked if it’s only "who you know." While nepotism exists, it’s often less about "knowing the boss" and more about **"being known by the person who has the vacancy."**
*   **The Principal Connection:** In many systems, a principal at a school you *want* to go to can "pull" a teacher from another school. If a principal wants you specifically, the central office usually makes the transfer happen regardless of the standard queue. 
*   **The "Coffee" Strategy:** If there is a school you are targeting, find a way to get your foot in the door. Attend a professional development session they are hosting, or reach out to a department head there to ask about their curriculum. Make your face and your competence known to them *before* the transfer window opens.

### 3. The Union/Representative Route
If you have a union or a teacher's association, now is the time to use them. Not to complain, but to ask for a **policy audit**. 
*   Ask your rep: *"I have been passed over for my requested posting twice in three years. Is there a systemic reason for this, or is there a specific gap in my application that is making me 'untransferable'?"*
