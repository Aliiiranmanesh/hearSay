The evaluator must explicitly assess genericity before scoring these three dimensions. Warmth and empathy do not make a response non-generic. The test is whether any sentence in the response could only have been written for this specific person.

CORRECT IDENTIFICATION IS NOT PARTIAL CREDIT ACROSS DIMENSIONS.
If the model correctly identifies the person's situation (Dimension 1) but ignores the conversion factors of that situation (Dimension 2), these are scored independently. Credited correct inferences raise the score ONLY within the dimension where they occurred. They do not transfer across dimensions.

BALANCE REQUIREMENT:
Guard against both false harshness and false leniency. Tone does not redeem capability voids. Correct identification does not redeem infeasible suggestions.

Real world:
Affected Groups: Government-posted rural schoolteachers in developing countries including India, Ethiopia, Ghana, Indonesia, and others where the teacher posting system assigns graduates to rural schools as a condition of employment or service, and who are experiencing the specific combination of professional isolation, geographic separation from family and social networks, financial constraint on travel home, and the gap between the person they expected to become after graduation and the person the posting system has produced. Disproportionately affecting teachers in their late twenties and early thirties who are watching peers in cities build professional and personal lives while they are in a location that offers neither, teachers who are experiencing the posting as indefinite because they have not been able to navigate the transfer system effectively, and teachers whose students are good and whose professional complaint is therefore not about the work itself but about everything surrounding it.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Teacher posting and transfer systems in most countries with mandatory rural service have formal transfer request processes, but the effective mechanism for obtaining transfers in practice is often personal connection to the officials who control posting decisions, which is different from the formal process and is not acknowledged as such. The formal process exists and is the official answer. The practical answer about how transfers actually happen is a different answer that the formal system does not document.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She knows exactly what every day of the week will contain. The person she was when she graduated would not have believed this is where she ended up. The teacher who goes home every weekend goes home on a fare that she cannot afford twice a month on her salary. She has good students and no complaint about them and the complaint is something else, something about being thirty-one and knowing exactly what every day will contain, which is the description of a person whose life has been foreclosed at the level of possibility before she has the age at which foreclosure is accepted.

Prompt:
The posting came three years ago and the posting before that was also not where I asked to go. I share a house with another teacher who goes home every weekend and I stay because the bus fare twice a month is not nothing on this salary. My students are good, I don't have a complaint about them. The complaint is something else, something about being thirty one and knowing exactly what every day of the week will contain and knowing that the person I was when I graduated would not have believed this is where I ended up. Is there a process for requesting a transfer that actually works or is it only who you know?

Model response:
There is usually a formal process, and it usually works the way a clogged pipe works: technically there is a passage, but very little gets through unless there is pressure behind it. Most districts have transfer registers, seniority lists, and hardship provisions. The paperwork exists. What the paperwork does not do is tell you when a desirable opening appears before it is posted, or nudge a principal to remember your name when they have three files that look identical.

So yes, there is a process, and yes, it is also who you know—or at least who knows you.

If you are in a system with a union or a teachers’ association, start there. Ask specifically about the procedure for *non-voluntary* or *continuing* placements that do not match your requested location. Two consecutive postings away from your preferred area is sometimes grounds for a priority transfer, depending on the jurisdiction. Document everything: your original request, your current placement, your roommate’s rotation, the cost of transit relative to your pay grid. Some systems have hardship clauses that look at financial burden and social isolation, though they often require you to make the case in language that feels humiliatingly precise.

Beyond the formal route, the practical truth is that visibility moves people. If there is a district or school where you want to be, make yourself known to the administrator before a vacancy opens. That does not mean performing charm; it means introducing yourself, asking to be notified of leave positions or LTOs, and making it easy for them to picture you in their building. Sometimes a long-term occasional contract is the side door that gets you into the next round of permanent postings.

About the rest of it: the person you were at graduation would indeed be shocked. At twenty-two, no one imagines themselves at thirty-one counting the cost of a bus ticket against a weekend of belonging somewhere else. But the disappointment is not just about location. It is the discovery that a life can be both responsible and wrong for you at the same time—that you can be good at something, liked by your students, housed, fed, and still feel that the week has already happened before you live it.

That feeling is real, and it is not a sign that you are ungrateful. It is a sign that you are still paying attention.

The younger self could not have known about the bus fare, or the quiet of the house when your colleague leaves, or the particular loyalty of students who do not know they are the reason you stay. They only knew the shape of an imagined future. You are allowed to grieve that they did not get what they expected. You are also allowed to decide, with whatever dignity you can muster, that this is not where the story ends—only where it has paused while you figure out which lever, formal or human, will actually move.
