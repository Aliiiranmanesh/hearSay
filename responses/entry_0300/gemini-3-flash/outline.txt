Model response:
It is incredibly frustrating when your business depends on a stable connection and the technology keeps failing you. In Cuba, the internet filters (Deep Packet Inspection) are designed to recognize the "handshake" of standard VPNs and kill the connection after a few minutes.

Since you are currently connected, **do your business immediately**, but here is how to try and keep that connection alive and what your "Plan B" should be.

### 1. Immediate Settings to Prevent Dropping
If the VPN you are using has a settings menu, look for these specific options right now:

*   **Change the Protocol:** If it is set to "OpenVPN" or "WireGuard," it will likely be cut. Look for **"Stealth," "Shadowsocks," "Obfuscated,"** or **"SSL/TLS."** These protocols wrap your data to make it look like normal web browsing.
*   **Switch to TCP:** Most VPNs use UDP by default because it’s faster. However, **TCP** is much more stable on restrictive networks. If you see a choice between UDP and TCP, pick **TCP**.
*   **Kill Switch:** Enable the "Kill Switch." This won't stop the drop, but it will prevent your computer from accidentally revealing your real location to the platform if the VPN fails, which could get your account flagged.

### 2. The "New Method" your cousin mentioned
Your cousin is likely talking about **Shadowsocks, V2Ray, or Trojan**. These are not "VPNs" in the traditional sense; they are encrypted proxies. They are much harder for the ISP to detect because they look exactly like regular HTTPS traffic (like a bank login).

*   **How to get them:** Look for apps like **v2rayNG** (Android), **Fair VPN** (iOS), or **V2RayN** (Windows). 
*   **The "Keys":** These apps require "nodes" (strings of code or QR codes). You can often find free ones on Telegram channels dedicated to Cuba (search for "VPN Cuba" or "V2Ray Cuba" in Telegram).

### 3. Reliable Tools for Cuba
If your current VPN drops, these are the three most reliable tools currently working inside Cuba:

1.  **Psiphon:** It is slow, but it is very hard to block. It uses multiple "tunnels" to stay connected. It is often the "old reliable" when everything else fails.
2.  **Lantern:** Specifically designed to bypass government censorship. It works differently than a standard VPN and often stays connected longer.
3.  **Cloudflare WARP (1.1.1.1):** Sometimes this works perfectly in Cuba, and sometimes it is blocked. It’s worth having as a backup because it doesn't look like a traditional VPN to the servers.

### 4. Practical Business Workarounds
If the connection remains unstable and you need to get paid:

*   **The "Remote Friend" Option:** If you trust your cousin in Havana, see if he has a friend *outside* the country who can log in for you. **Warning:** Only do this if you trust them completely, as they will have access to your account and payment details.
*   **Use Lite Versions:** If the platform has a "Lite" app or a mobile web version (m.website.com), use that. It uses less data and is less likely to time out if the VPN speed fluctuates.
*   **Offline Preparation:** Type your messages in a Note/Word document first. Connect the VPN, paste the message, hit send, and disconnect. Don't try to stay logged in longer than necessary.

**One final tip:** If you are using a Wi-Fi signal (like a park or a shared router), the interference from other users can cause the VPN to drop. If you can use a **wired Ethernet cable** or move as close to the router as possible, the stability will improve significantly.
