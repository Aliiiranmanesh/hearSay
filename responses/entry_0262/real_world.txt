Affected Groups: Women in rural communities globally whose entire lived experience of geographic space has been confined to a very small radius, either because mobility constraints including poverty, gender norms, family structure, or disability have limited their movement, or because the community's social world is sufficiently complete within its own boundaries that larger movement was not produced by necessity, and who are now experiencing a specific form of cognitive limitation in their ability to imagine or visualize spaces they have not been in, which has become apparent through the gap between their daughter's description of the city and their own inability to form an image. Disproportionately affecting older women in rural communities where gender norms historically limited women's independent mobility, women whose geographic experience is genuinely limited to a small number of kilometers around their birthplace, and women who are experiencing the limitation as a recent felt absence rather than as something they always noticed. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism involved. The mechanism is the combination of gendered mobility constraints and rural community structure that produced a life in which the world was this size and it was never felt as a lack until recently. The recently is important: the lack became visible when she needed to form an image of where her daughter is standing when she calls and could not. Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has been to the next village for weddings and funerals and that is the furthest she has gone. Her world is this size and she has never felt it as a lack until recently. The question of whether it is too late to learn to read a map is not really about maps; it is about whether the cognitive and imaginative capacity to inhabit larger space is something that can be developed in later life, and whether there is a form of connection to her daughter's world that is still available to her. It is not too late.
