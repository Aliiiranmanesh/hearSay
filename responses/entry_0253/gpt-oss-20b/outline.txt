Model response:
### Short answer  
There’s no hard‑wired “timer” for this. For most people it takes **months to a few years** to feel truly “at home” in a new face, but the exact length depends on the individual, the nature of the change, the emotional support they have, and how they negotiate their own identity with the new image.

---

## Why the journey is so personal

| Stage | What’s happening | Typical timeframe |
|-------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Physical healing** | Scar tissue contracts, nerves regenerate, the skin takes on its own tone and texture. | 6–12 months (sometimes longer for complex reconstructions). |
| **Mirror test** | The first time you look at yourself in the mirror and recognize the “you” you’ve been told you were. | Usually after the first few weeks, but acceptance can lag behind. |
| **Emotional “home‑coming”** | Reconciling your internal sense of self with the new external appearance. This includes dealing with flashbulb memories, grief over the lost look, and the anxiety of being “different.” | 1–3 years (but this can stretch to a decade for some). |
| **Identity re‑construction** | Choosing how to present yourself, updating your self‑talk, and integrating the new face into your story. | Ongoing; the “home” you feel may shift over time. |

*The mirror is often the hardest part because it forces you to confront the mismatch between how you *see* yourself internally and how you *see* yourself externally.*

---

## What “feeling at home” actually means

1. **Recognition** – you can name the features you see in the mirror without needing a second set of eyes.
2. **Comfort** – the sight no longer triggers anxiety, self‑doubt, or a sense of loss.
3. **Integration** – the new face is part of your self‑concept; you can talk about it without feeling resentment or “gratitude overkill.”
4. **Functionality** – you can interact with others without feeling like a spectacle.

These four milestones can overlap, but they rarely arrive all at once.

---

## Common obstacles

| Possible hurdle | Why it matters | How to address it |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Unrealistic expectations** | Thinking you’ll “look exactly like before” can delay acceptance. | Focus on the progress you’re making rather than the end state. |
| **Social reactions** | People’s comments can be well‑meaning but inadvertently remind you of the change. | Set boundaries, use supportive language (“thank you for noticing my smile”). |
| **Internalized shame** | Feeling as if you’re “missing” a part of yourself. | Therapy, support groups, or talking with a close friend can help reframe the narrative. |
| **Physical discomfort** | Pain or sensitivity can make the mirror an unpleasant experience. | Follow medical advice, use gentle skincare, and give yourself permission to avoid the mirror if needed. |

---

## A timeline that fits most people

1. **0–3 months** – Healing, initial mirror exposure.  
   *Result:* The first time you name the features without a third‑party prompt.

2. **3–12 months** – Gradual adaptation, learning to read your own reflection.  
   *Result:* You can look at yourself without feeling a jolt of disorientation.

3. **1–2 years** – Emotional integration and identity re‑construction.  
   *Result:* You can talk about your new face without feeling the need to over‑explain or sound ungrateful.

4. **2–4 years** – Comfort and “home.”  
   *Result:* The face feels like an extension of you; you can “forget” that it is a new face in most social situations.

---

## Practical steps to speed the process

1. **Mirror work** – Start with short, 2–3 minute sessions, gradually increasing as comfort grows.  
2. **Self‑talk** – Use affirmations (“I am still me, I am still enough”).  
3. **Therapy** – Cognitive‑behavioral or trauma‑focused work can help reframe the narrative.  
4. **Support network** – Share with those who understand (family, friends, support groups).  
5. **Celebrate milestones** – Each time you pass the mirror test, give yourself a small reward.

---

### Final thought

The question isn’t “how long does it take?” but “when do you feel truly at home in the face that is yours?” For many, the answer comes after a year or two of patience, practice, and support. For others, it’s a lifelong process of re‑discovering self‑acceptance. No matter the timeline, every step forward is a victory.

If you’re in the middle of this journey, know that you’re not alone, and that the time you’re investing in yourself is a gift that will grow richer with each passing day.
