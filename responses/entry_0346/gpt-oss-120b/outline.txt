## 2. Conduct a “Three‑Layer” Verification

### Layer 1 – **Document Review**
- **Ask for a formal prospectus** (PDF or printed). It should include: program objectives, curriculum, fees, refund policy, trainer bios, accreditation, and contact details.
- **Check the fine print**: look for any clauses that allow the institute to change fees, cancel the program, or withhold certificates without cause.

### Layer 2 – **Human‑Source Vetting**
- **Reach out to at least two alumni** (preferably people you don’t know personally). Ask:
  - *What did you actually learn?*  
  - *Did the promised job placement or internship happen?*  
  - *How was the training environment (equipment, mentorship, safety)?*  
  - *If you needed a refund or had a grievance, how was it handled?*
- **Call the program’s office** (not just email). Note response time, tone, and willingness to give details. A legitimate organization will usually be transparent.

### Layer 3 – **On‑Site or Live‑Virtual Check**
- **Visit the premises** (or request a live virtual tour). Observe:
  - Cleanliness, working computers, tools, or labs.  
  - Presence of actual students or staff (not just a receptionist).  
  - Signs of a proper learning environment (whiteboards, projectors, safety gear if it’s a trade skill).
- **Ask to sit in on a sample class** (many institutes allow a “trial lesson”). This shows teaching style, interaction, and curriculum depth.

If any layer raises red flags—missing registration, vague alumni feedback, or a closed‑door facility—treat the program with caution.

## 4. Guard Against Common Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It’s a Warning | What to Do |
|----------|--------------------|------------|
| **“Pay everything upfront, no refunds.”** | High financial risk; may be a cash‑flow scam. | Demand a written refund policy; consider paying only a small deposit. |
| **“We guarantee a job in X weeks.”** | Guarantees are rarely legitimate; employment depends on many factors. | Ask for actual placement statistics and the companies involved. |
| **“No official website, only WhatsApp or Facebook.”** | Lack of transparency, hard to verify details. | Request official documents; consider other options. |
| **“Training lasts 2 weeks, but they charge 6‑month tuition.”** | Inflated fees for minimal content. | Compare with other institutes offering the same skill. |
