Here are practical ways to vet a program and find out if it is a genuine opportunity or a performative gesture before you risk your time and safety by showing up.

### 1. Seek "Alumni" Evidence (The most reliable way)
Programs that are "real" leave a trail of successful graduates. Do not look at the program's own brochures; look for the people who finished the course.
*   **The Search:** Try to find people from your community who have completed the program. Ask them: *"Did you get a job after this?"* and *"How were you treated by the instructors when the doors were closed to the public?"*
*   **The "Ghost" Check:** If the program claims to have trained dozens of transgender people, but you cannot find a single person currently employed because of that training, the program is likely performative.

### 2. The "Probe" Communication
Before going in person, engage with them via email or phone. The way they answer your questions will tell you a lot about their culture.
*   **Ask Specifics:** Instead of asking "Are you inclusive?" (to which they will always say yes), ask: *"What is your policy for handling harassment from other students?"* or *"Do you provide job placement assistance specifically for transgender candidates?"*
*   **Observe the Tone:** Are they professional and detailed, or are they overly sentimental and vague? A program that focuses on "helping the marginalized" (charity mindset) is often more discriminatory than a program that focuses on "professional skill acquisition" (empowerment mindset).

### 3. Cross-Reference with Trusted NGOs
There are human rights organizations and legal aid groups in Lahore and across Pakistan that specifically track the treatment of the Khwaja Sira community.
*   Reach out to a trusted lawyer or an activist who works with the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act. Ask them if they have heard of this specific program.
*   Often, NGOs know which "government-backed" programs are actually safe and which ones are just trying to meet a quota for a photo opportunity.

### 4. The "Safe Visit" Strategy
If you decide to investigate in person, do not go alone and do not go as a "student" first.
*   **The Reconnaissance:** Visit the area first. Observe the atmosphere. 
*   **The Wingman:** Go with a trusted friend or a mentor who is not part of the program. Having a witness changes the power dynamic; people are less likely to be openly hostile when there is a witness.
*   **The Short Visit:** Do not commit to a full day. Go for a 15-minute inquiry. Observe the faces of the staff. Do they look at you with curiosity, pity, or professional respect?
