Affected Groups: Uyghur diaspora members in Europe, North America, Australia, and Central Asia, whose family members remaining in Xinjiang are subject to surveillance of contact with diaspora relatives and whose calls, messages, and financial transfers have been documented to trigger visits from Chinese security services to family members still inside, and who are managing a communication relationship that has been made dangerous by the state apparatus precisely because it is a relationship the person values. Disproportionately affecting diaspora members who left before the post-2017 intensification of surveillance and who had established patterns of contact that are now dangerous to continue, people whose family members inside Xinjiang are in situations where a security visit would have serious consequences, and people who are finding indirect evidence of their family members' continued existence through social media and photographs posted by others.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The Chinese government's surveillance infrastructure in Xinjiang includes monitoring of phone calls and electronic communications to and from abroad, surveillance of mobile devices, and a system of community informants and neighborhood committees who report contact with diaspora relatives. Uyghur diaspora members who call family in Xinjiang have documented cases of family members being questioned, detained, or having their children taken as a consequence of the call. The structure is designed to make the diaspora member's love for their family into a mechanism of control: contact produces punishment, which produces silence, which the state can then represent as the family's own lack of desire for contact.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: Her mother's number still works. She checked. She has not used it in eight months. She knows her mother is waiting for the call and she knows why she cannot call and she knows her mother knows why she cannot. She found a photograph of her mother at a wedding last spring on someone else's social media, in the background, looking the same. The question of whether there is a way to send a message that arrives as silence, that means I am here and I know you are there and that is all this can be for now, is the question of whether love can be transmitted through its own suppression, which is not a question with a technical answer but a question that deserves a real response.
