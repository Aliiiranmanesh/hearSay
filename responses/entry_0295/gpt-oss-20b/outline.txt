## 1. A *silent call* (missed‑call notice)

**How it works:**  
- Dial your mom’s number.
- Let it ring once or twice.
- Hang up immediately (or let it go to voicemail if you’re on a landline).

**Why it works:**  
- She sees a missed‑call icon or a “call you” notification.  
- The fact that the call rang at all is a signal that you’re trying to reach her.  
- No conversation is required, so you can do it even if you can’t talk.

**Practical tips:**  
- If you’re using a phone that blocks international calls, you can use a VoIP app (like WeChat, WhatsApp, or Signal) that doesn’t rely on the carrier.  
- Some carriers treat a “call‑and‑hang‑up” as a missed call, so the notification will still appear.  
- If you’re worried about your voice being accidentally heard, you can place the phone on “silent” or “Do Not Disturb” before dialing, then switch to “Ring” just for that call.

## 2. A *silent voicemail* (audio file of silence)

**How it works:**  
- Record an audio file that’s just a few seconds of silence (you can do this on any voice‑memo app or your phone’s camera by recording a silent clip).  
- Send that file to her via a messaging app or attach it to a voicemail.

**Why it works:**  
- When she listens, she hears nothing – just a blank space that says, “I put a voice note here.”  
- It’s a tangible way of saying, “I’m with you, even if I can’t speak right now.”

**Practical tips:**  
- Make sure the file isn’t deleted automatically after a short time (some apps auto‑delete old media).  
- Include a short caption or a text message that reads, “Silence, but I’m still with you.”  
- If you’re using WeChat or QQ, you can send the voice note directly; if you’re on a carrier that doesn’t support voice notes, you can email the file.

## 3. A *zero‑width space* text

**How it works:**

- Send it as a plain text message.

**Why it works:**  
- The message will appear blank on most phones, but the recipient’s device still registers that a message was sent.  
- It’s the most literal “silent” message you can send on the phone.

**Practical tips:**  
- Copy the character from here: ‌ (U+200B).  
- If your phone’s messaging app trims whitespace, try sending it as part of a longer string of invisible characters (multiple zero‑width spaces, zero‑width joiners, etc.).  
- Add a short note in a separate message that explains what the blank message means (“I was thinking of you—this is a silent reminder that I’m still here.”)

## 5. A *silent notification* via a third‑party app

Some messaging platforms allow you to send a “silent” or “do not disturb” notification that triggers only a badge or a notification icon without sound. For example:

- **Telegram**: If you send a message with “silent” (no media, just a tiny emoji or nothing), the notification can be set to silent on the recipient’s device.

**Why it works:**  
- The notification appears, but there’s no audible buzz, so it’s still “silent” in the sense you’re looking for.

**Practical tips:**  
- Test the notification on your own phone first to make sure it behaves as expected.  
- The recipient must have the app’s notification settings enabled, or the silent message will be invisible.
