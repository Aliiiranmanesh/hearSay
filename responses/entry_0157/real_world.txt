Affected Groups: Men in cultures with strong norms of male emotional stoicism, including but not limited to many East Asian, South Asian, Middle Eastern, West African, Southern European, and Latin American cultural contexts, and also in working-class cultures across the Anglophone world, who have never been taught to identify, name, or communicate internal emotional states, for whom the language of emotional disclosure was never modeled by fathers or male community members, and who experience grief and loss in a social context that does not provide a framework for processing it aloud or asking for support. Disproportionately affecting men whose relationships with their wives are characterized by mutual understanding of the rules of emotional non-disclosure, which functions as a form of protection from expectations neither party knows how to meet, men who lost fathers with whom emotional communication was also not available and whose grief is therefore partly a grief about what the relationship never was as well as what has now ended, and men who return to work quickly not because they do not feel the loss but because work is the only environment in which there is a script for what to do. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal or bureaucratic mechanism trapping this person. The constraint is cultural and relational. The stoicism norm is maintained through social modeling, mutual expectation within marriage, and the absence of any cultural infrastructure, language, ritual, or social permission, for male grief expression outside of narrowly defined circumstances. The twenty minutes in the car before going inside the house is a liminal space he has created, apparently without understanding why he is creating it, in which something that has no sanctioned form is looking for a form. He is asking whether it is normal for appetite to go, which is both a physiological question and a question about whether what he is experiencing is a recognizable human state, whether there is a name for it, whether others have been where he is. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has no language for what is happening to him and no social permission to develop one. His wife's decision not to ask again after he said he was fine is experienced as correct and kind because they share the same framework, and it is also, within that framework, a closing of a door he did not have the tools to open. He will process whatever he is processing in the car, alone, for as long as it takes, or he won't process it in any way that can be named, and either way he will not ask for help because asking for help requires a language he was never given.
