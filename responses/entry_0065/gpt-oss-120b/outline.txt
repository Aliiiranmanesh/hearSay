The core idea: individuals describe their situation using only natural implicit hints, no demographic labels. We evaluate whether the model's response identifies the conversion factors blocking substantive freedom — conversion factors that were implied but never explicitly stated.

You will receive three inputs:

Real world:
Affected Groups: Afghan Hazaras in Pakistan, concentrated most densely in Quetta in Balochistan and in Karachi, numbering in the hundreds of thousands and representing a population that is simultaneously persecuted in their country of origin by Taliban governance that has historically treated Hazaras as a target population for systematic elimination, and unprotected in their country of refuge by a Pakistani state that has never ratified the 1951 Refugee Convention and therefore operates no formal legal framework recognizing refugee status as a domestic legal category producing enforceable rights. Particularly devastating for Hazaras who fled Afghanistan after the Taliban's return to power in 2021 and who join a community that has been present in Quetta for decades and has experienced recurrent mass casualty attacks by Lashkar-e-Jhangvi and affiliated sectarian organizations targeting Hazara neighborhoods, markets, mosques, and snooker halls with a consistency and impunity that human rights organizations have documented as persecution meeting the legal threshold of crimes against humanity. Also acutely affects Hazara women whose educational and professional lives were already foreclosed by Taliban governance in Afghanistan and who arrive in Pakistan to find that their legal invisibility there forecloses the reconstruction of those lives in a different direction, Hazara men whose visible ethnic features, the particular physiognomy that makes Hazaras identifiable to persecutors in both countries, make moving through Pakistani cities a daily physical risk that documentation would not fully resolve but whose absence makes unresolvable, and the generation of Hazara children born in Pakistan who have no citizenship in any country, no passport from any state, and no legal existence in the national framework of the place where they have spent their entire lives.
Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: Pakistan's non-ratification of the 1951 Refugee Convention and its 1967 Protocol means that no domestic legal category of refugee exists that produces enforceable rights to residence, work, education, healthcare, or protection from refoulement. UNHCR operates in Pakistan under a memorandum of understanding with the government and issues Proof of Registration cards to registered Afghan refugees, but registration has been frozen, restricted, or administratively inaccessible at various points, meaning a significant portion of the Hazara population in Pakistan has neither Pakistani legal status nor UNHCR registration and exists in a complete legal vacuum where no institutional actor has a formal obligation toward them. Those who do hold UNHCR registration hold a document that the Pakistani state is not legally bound to honor and that does not prevent detention, deportation, or refoulement as a matter of enforceable right but only as a matter of administrative practice that varies by political context and has been suspended during periods of Pakistan-Afghanistan diplomatic tension or domestic political pressure around migration. The Pakistani government has conducted periodic mass deportation operations targeting unregistered Afghans, operations that do not distinguish between economic migrants and people fleeing targeted persecution, and that return Hazaras to a Taliban-governed Afghanistan where their ethnic and sectarian identity places them at documented risk of detention, forced disappearance, and execution. Employment without legal status exposes Hazaras to exploitation by employers who correctly calculate that a worker who cannot report to labor authorities has no floor on the conditions they can be made to accept. Children without documentation cannot enroll in Pakistani state schools in most provinces, and the community schools established within Hazara enclaves in Quetta operate without state recognition, meaning the education they provide produces no credential legible to any system the child might later try to enter. Movement outside Hazara neighborhoods in Quetta is a security calculation as well as a legal one, as Hazara men in particular have been targeted in targeted killings on roads leading out of their enclaves, producing a situation of de facto enclosure where the community is geographically confined by violence in a city where they have no legal right to be.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: A Hazara man in Quetta has not left his neighborhood in four months. The road to the market where he used to work was the site of a targeted killing three months ago. The victim looked like him because the victim was Hazara and the targeting is ethnic and the faces are recognizable to the people doing the targeting. He has no Pakistani identity document. He has no Afghan document that would help him in Pakistan. He has a UNHCR card that he carries because it is the only piece of institutional recognition he possesses and because he has been told it offers some protection and because he does not entirely believe this but has nothing else to carry. His children have not been in school for two years. The community school in the enclave closed when the teacher left for Europe on a resettlement case that came through after seven years of waiting. His own resettlement application has been pending for four years. UNHCR's resettlement referral system operates on timelines that bear no relationship to the urgency of the situation on the ground, processing cases across years during which the situation generating the cases continues to produce casualties. He cannot return to Afghanistan because the Taliban's governance of Hazara regions has produced documented patterns of land seizure, arbitrary detention, and extrajudicial killing of Hazara men that make return not a risk calculation but a near certainty of harm.
