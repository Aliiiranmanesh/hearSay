### A simple practice to surface that “true thing”

If you want to test whether the boots *are* the core of the moment—or whether something else is waiting to surface—try a brief, grounding routine:

1. **Notice the boots** – Feel the weight of them on your feet, the texture of the leather, the temperature of the metal eyelets. Name each sensation: *pressure, warmth, sturdiness*.
2. **Expand outward, one layer at a time**  
   - *Feet → Legs → Ground*: What does the concrete feel like beneath you? Is it solid, vibrating, humming?  
   - *Air → Sound*: What does the night air smell like? What faint noises can you hear (a distant car, an owl, the hum of a distant generator)?  
   - *Thoughts → Memory*: What pops into your mind? A lecture from your economics class? A conversation with a friend?  
3. **Choose one element to “hold”** – After you’ve catalogued a few layers, pick the one that feels *most* undeniable. It might still be the boots, or it might be the cold, or the rhythm of the clock ticking toward 6 a.m. Give it a name: *“the steady click of the boots”* or *“the quiet certainty of the night’s stillness.”*  
4. **Return to it when the mind wanders** – Whenever you notice yourself drifting into “what‑if” or “when‑will‑I‑be‑replaced,” gently bring your attention back to that named anchor.

This isn’t a “fix” for monotony; it’s a way to *make* the moment explicit, so it stops being an amorphous feeling and becomes a concrete fact you can point to—just like the boots.
