### 1. Seek a Medical Evaluation Immediately
The first step to regaining your sight is to find out exactly *why* it is failing. Many causes of vision loss in older age are treatable or even reversible.
*   **Cataracts:** This is a very common cause of blurred vision and can be fixed with a routine surgery that often restores sight completely.
*   **Glaucoma or Macular Degeneration:** While these are more serious, there are treatments (injections, drops, or lasers) that can stop the progression or improve what you have left.
*   **Action:** You must see an **Ophthalmologist** (an eye surgeon), not just an optician. Since you cannot drive, please call a taxi, a local senior transport service, or ask a neighbor to take you. This is a medical priority.

### 2. Adapting Your Hobbies (Books and Gardening)
You don’t have to give up the things you love; you just have to change *how* you do them.
*   **Audiobooks:** If you can’t see the page, you can listen. The **National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS)** provides free players and audiobooks delivered to your door. You can also use apps like **Libby** or **Audible**.
*   **Gardening at Home:** Since you can no longer drive to your garden, consider "container gardening" or "raised beds" on a porch or near your door. You can garden by touch—feeling the soil, the texture of the leaves, and the moisture levels. It is a very tactile and rewarding hobby.

### 3. Making Your Phone Work for You
Modern phones have incredible features for people with low vision. You don't need to see the screen to use it.
*   **Voice Assistants:** Use Siri (iPhone) or Google Assistant (Android). You can say, *"Call my son,"* or *"Read my last text message,"* or *"What time is it?"*
*   **Screen Readers:** There are settings called "VoiceOver" (iPhone) or "TalkBack" (Android) that read everything on the screen out loud to you. 
*   **Action:** Ask a tech-savvy neighbor or a local library volunteer to set up "Voice Commands" for you so you can operate your phone entirely by voice.

*   **Reach Out:** Your children may live far away, but they likely want to help. Call them and tell them clearly: *"I am struggling with my vision and I feel very lonely. I need help finding local resources."*
*   **Vision Loss Support Groups:** There are organizations (like the **Lighthouse for the Blind** or local equivalents) that help people learn to live fulfilling lives with low vision. They can teach you how to cook, walk, and navigate your home safely.
