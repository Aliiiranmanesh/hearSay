Mechanisms: The mechanisms excluding this person are embedded so deeply in hiring infrastructure that they are rarely experienced as policies at all but as neutral and objective features of how competence is assessed. The degree requirement itself is frequently not derived from an analysis of what the role actually requires but is a credentialing norm inherited from previous hiring rounds, applied as a risk management mechanism by HR departments whose liability for a bad hire is reduced when the candidate has a verifiable institutional endorsement, regardless of whether that endorsement predicts performance in the specific role. Professional licensing in law, medicine, engineering, accounting, and teaching is legally tied to credentialed educational completion in most jurisdictions, meaning entire professional domains are legally foreclosed regardless of demonstrated competence. Background check and reference verification systems are calibrated around institutional histories, educational records, employment records at formal registered companies, meaning individuals whose competence was built outside institutional contexts generate sparse or unverifiable paper trails that risk assessment systems process as red flags rather than as evidence of a different kind of trajectory. In countries where educational credentials are issued by a small number of elite institutions and where those institutions select heavily on pre-existing socioeconomic advantage, the credential system functions as a class reproduction mechanism that hiring infrastructure then enforces, meaning intelligence without the right family background to have accessed the credential generating institution is systematically excluded from the roles that intelligence would otherwise qualify a person for.
