Affected Groups: Women in legal systems where gender inequality is codified in family law, including in countries where testimony is formally weighted by gender, divorce requires male consent or a judicial process that takes years, custody defaults to the father after a certain age or upon the father's request, and where a woman's ability to leave a marriage is therefore structurally constrained not by force or fear but by the legal architecture itself. Including countries with personal status laws derived from conservative religious interpretations applied in family courts in the Middle East and North Africa, and also in countries where formally neutral laws produce gender-unequal outcomes in practice. Disproportionately affecting women whose husbands are aware of the legal framework and are using it as a strategic tool, women with children for whom the custody default is an effective trap, women who have professional lives that allow them to maintain the appearance of a functioning marriage during the multi-year legal process, and women who are not experiencing physical violence and whose situation therefore does not fit the framework of crisis intervention but is nonetheless a form of structural entrapment with no short-term exit. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The legal mechanisms are the family law itself: a divorce process that takes between three and seven years, custody rules that make taking the children without a custody order actionable against the mother, and a system in which the husband who knows the law and is patient can simply wait while she lives inside the process. He is not violent. He is simply patient. The house they share is the geography of the legal trap: she cannot leave with the children, and leaving without the children is not leaving. She goes to work and comes home and they eat dinner and anyone watching would see a family, which is the description of a situation whose harm is structural rather than visible and which therefore triggers no intervention. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is being required to survive years inside a marriage she has decided to leave while the legal system processes her decision in its own time. The years are real. She is living them. The husband who is patient is not doing anything illegal; he is using the legal system's timeline as a tool, which the legal system permits. Anyone watching would see a family, which is the precise description of the invisibility of structural entrapment: it produces no evidence, triggers no intervention, and requires her to perform normalcy for the duration of the process. The question of what people do with years that have to be survived rather than lived is not a rhetorical question; it is the operational problem of how to live inside time that is being consumed by someone else's patience.
