### 3. The long-term legal struggle: turning the white card into recognition

You said “none of us exist on paper in the way that allows you to do things.” That means you likely need a pathway to citizenship or permanent legal residency. This is the hardest part, but people do fight and win.

- **Birth registration and late registration:** Even if you were all born in the country, were your births ever formally registered with a civil registry? - **Citizenship by birth (jus soli) claims:** In some countries, being born there *should* automatically grant citizenship, but bureaucratic hurdles or discrimination prevent it. If the law says you’re a citizen but the state denies it, you can take the matter to court. - **Statelessness determination procedure:** Some countries have a formal process to recognize someone as stateless and grant them a legal status that eventually leads to citizenship. - **Naturalization on the basis of long residence:** Even without birthright citizenship, many countries have provisions for people who have lived there for decades. You may need to prove continuous residence, which the white card ironically does. Again, legal assistance is crucial.
