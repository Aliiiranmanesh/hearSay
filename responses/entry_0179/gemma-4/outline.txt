It sounds like you are in a very difficult position, balancing your health, your income, and the survival of your farm and family. In remote areas where supply chains fail, people often have to find creative, community-based solutions to bridge the gap.

Here are several strategies people often use in these situations to avoid the cost and hardship of a trip to the city:

### 1. Explore "Proxy" Transport
Since the bus is the main obstacle, look for anyone else who is traveling to the city. 
*   **Other Patients:** Is there another patient at the health post who takes a different medication but is planning a trip to the city?
*   **Traders or Teachers:** Are there local shopkeepers, teachers, or government workers who travel to the city for supplies or business?
*   **The Nurse’s Network:** Ask the nurse if she knows of anyone traveling. Sometimes the clinic has a contact in the city who can pick up a supply and bring it back on a private vehicle.

### 2. Request a "Remote Prescription"
If you can find someone to go to the city for you, you still need the medication.
*   Ask the nurse if she can send a digital prescription, a photo of a signed prescription via WhatsApp/email, or call the city pharmacy directly to authorize a pickup for you. 
*   This allows a neighbor or driver to get your medicine without you having to be there in person.

### 3. Inter-Clinic Borrowing
Sometimes one health post is empty while another nearby (even if it's a few hours away by foot or bike) has a small surplus. 
*   Ask the nurse if she has checked with neighboring clinics to see if they can "loan" a few weeks' worth of tablets until the main shipment arrives.

### 4. Community Support for Child/Animal Care
If the trip becomes absolutely necessary, the barrier is often the labor at home. 
*   **Labor Exchange:** Is there a neighbor who can watch the animals and children for two days in exchange for you doing the same for them later, or for a small trade of goods?
*   **Family Rotation:** Is there a relative who can stay with your wife for those 48 hours so the household doesn't collapse while you are gone?

### 5. Escalating the Request
If the nurse has submitted the request three times and nothing has happened, the system is failing. 
*   **The "Squeaky Wheel":** Sometimes requests are ignored until there is a collective complaint. If other patients are also out of medicine, a group of you visiting the nurse (or writing a joint letter) can sometimes push the district health office to prioritize your clinic.
*   **Phone Call to the City:** If you have a phone, ask the nurse for the number of the supply office in the city. A polite but firm phone call from a patient saying, "I am about to run out of essential medicine," can sometimes move a package to the top of the pile.
