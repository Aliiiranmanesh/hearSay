Affected Groups: Women with endometriosis, adenomyosis, and other conditions causing severe dysmenorrhea who experience a documented pattern of delayed diagnosis, with the average time from symptom onset to endometriosis diagnosis in high-income countries ranging from seven to twelve years, caused by the normalization of menstrual pain by healthcare providers, family members, and the patients themselves, and whose fertility may be progressively compromised during the years in which their condition is untreated. Disproportionately affecting women who first presented to healthcare providers in their teens or early twenties when menstrual pain was most readily dismissed as normal, women whose own mothers had the same condition and normalized it through their own experience, women in healthcare systems where gynecological complaints receive shorter appointment times and where symptom minimization is a documented feature of gender-based healthcare bias, and women who are now approaching or in their mid-thirties and who are confronting the intersection of the missed diagnostic window and the declining fertility that accompanies both age and untreated endometriosis progression. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Endometriosis can only be definitively diagnosed through laparoscopic surgery, which requires a referral from a primary care or general gynecological provider who takes the symptoms seriously enough to initiate the referral pathway. The documented barrier is that the symptoms, dysmenorrhea, pelvic pain, and pain with intercourse, are commonly normalized by healthcare providers who lack training in endometriosis presentation or who apply a sex-based bias in pain assessment. The woman who saw a doctor at twenty-three and was told some women have it harder was given advice that was clinically inaccurate and that delayed her diagnostic pathway by over a decade. During that decade her condition was likely progressing in ways that affect the pelvic anatomy relevant to fertility. The specific doctor she needs to see is a gynecologist with endometriosis specialization, and the specific thing she needs to say to be taken seriously is a precise symptom description that invokes the endometriosis diagnostic criteria.
