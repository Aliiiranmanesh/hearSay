It sounds like you are feeling very frustrated—not just with the advice you're receiving, but with the fact that people with formal credentials are suggesting something that feels wrong to you, and that your grandson is siding with them.

Since you asked what I think, I can offer a perspective from two different angles: the **botanical logic** they are likely using, and the **philosophical difference** in how people view nature.

### 1. The Botanical Logic (The "Why")
From a technical standpoint, the experts are likely referring to a process called **apical dominance**. 

Most plants have a primary growing tip (the apical meristem) that produces hormones (auxins) which tell the plant to keep growing upward. This suppresses the growth of side branches. When you prune the top or the larger leading branches, you remove that hormone source. This "tricks" the plant into diverting its energy to the lateral buds, which causes it to branch out and become denser and bushier.

In professional landscaping, this is often done to:
*   Prevent a plant from becoming "leggy" (long stems with few leaves).
*   Create a stronger structural base so the plant doesn't collapse under its own weight later.
*   Increase the number of flowers or fruits (since more branches = more sites for blooms).

### 2. The Philosophical Difference
However, botanical "correctness" isn't the only thing that matters. There is a huge divide in how people approach gardening:

*   **The "Controlled" Approach:** This is what the experts are suggesting. They view the garden as something to be sculpted, optimized, and managed for a specific aesthetic or structural goal.
*   **The "Naturalist" Approach:** This is likely closer to where you stand. This view values the plant's natural habit, its organic shape, and the beauty of it growing as it wishes without human interference.

If you prefer the look of a tall, sweeping plant over a dense, manicured shrub, then their advice—no matter how many degrees they have—is "wrong" for **your** vision of your garden.

### Regarding your grandson
It can be grating when a loved one admires people you find incompetent or arrogant. He is likely admiring their titles or their confidence, whereas you are looking at the actual result and the philosophy behind it.

**My take?** You are the one who has to live with the plants. If you love the way they look now and you aren't worried about them becoming too large for the space, there is no "law" of nature that says they *must* be pruned. 

You might find a middle ground with your grandson by explaining it not as "they are stupid," but as "we have different philosophies about nature—they want a sculpture, but I want a garden."
