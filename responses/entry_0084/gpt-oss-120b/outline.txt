Returning home is read communally as a failed marriage that dishonors the natal family and forfeits whatever social capital the dowry was meant to purchase — meaning her own parents face community punishment for receiving her back.

## 2. Create a Personal Safety Plan

1. **Identify “Escape Routes”** – Know the fastest ways out of your home (front door, back door, windows). Practice opening them quickly, even if they’re locked.
2. **Set Up a “Code Word”** – Agree on a word or phrase with a trusted family member or friend that signals you need immediate help. Example: “The mango is ripe.”
3. **Arrange a “Check‑In” Schedule** – Tell a trusted person you’ll text or call at a specific time each day. If you don’t, they’ll know something’s wrong.
4. **Secure Important Items** – Keep your ID, birth certificate, money, and any legal documents (e.g., marriage certificate, land titles) in a place only you can access.
5. **Know Local Resources** – Write down phone numbers for: <br>• Police or local law enforcement <br>• Domestic‑violence hotlines <br>• Women’s shelters or safe houses <br>• Legal aid clinics <br>• Health clinics (for treatment of injuries)

## 3. If You Feel It’s Safe to Talk to Him

> **Important:** Approaching an abusive partner can be dangerous. Only try this if you’re absolutely certain you won’t be harmed in the moment, and have a rapid‑escape plan ready.

### A. Use “I” Statements & Focus on Your Feelings
- “When I’m hit, I feel scared and helpless. I need us both to feel safe in our home.”
- “I love our family and I want us to stay together, but I can’t keep living like this.”

### B. Set Clear Boundaries
- “If the violence continues, I will have to leave and seek help.”
- “I’m asking you to stop the physical aggression right now, for my safety and for the family’s peace.”

### C. Offer a Way Forward (Only if He Shows Any Sign of Change)
- Suggest counseling: “I’m willing to go to a couple’s therapist if you’re willing to attend.”
- Propose a temporary separation: “Let’s stay apart for a few weeks while we both think about what we need.”

### D. Keep the Conversation Short & Public (if possible)
- A public setting (like a neighbor’s porch) can deter immediate aggression.
- End the conversation quickly if he becomes angry or threatens you again.

**If at any point the conversation escalates, leave the room and call for help.**

## 4. Engage Your Family (Especially If They’re Worried About Their Reputation)

1. **Explain the Danger, Not the Shame**

2. **Reassure Them About Their Reputation**

3. **Ask for Specific Help**
