## 1. Understand what “grieving correctly” actually means

| Traditional view | Modern psychological view |
|-------------------|----------------------------|
| Grief is a *process* that must follow prescribed stages (denial → anger → bargaining → depression → acceptance). | Grief is *individual*.  It can appear in any order, any intensity, and any form. |
| “Correct” grief = *doing the right rituals*, *suppressing tears*, *moving on quickly*. | “Correct” grief = *allowing yourself to feel* what you need to feel, *accepting help*, *not rushing* the healing. |
| The goal is to *return to normalcy* as soon as possible. | The goal is *meaning‑making*: finding a new sense of self and purpose after loss. |

**Bottom line:** The “right” way to grieve is the one that lets you acknowledge the loss, process your emotions, and eventually find a new equilibrium—without forcing yourself into a mold that doesn’t feel authentic.

## 4. Redefining “don’t cry”

**Why the village says “don’t cry”**  
- To avoid overt displays of grief that could destabilize communal order.  
- To encourage stoicism and resilience.

**Why crying can be healthy**  
- Crying releases oxytocin and cortisol, helping to regulate emotions.
- It signals to others that you need support, which can foster communal bonds.

**How to re‑frame the rule**  
- Replace “don’t cry” with “express your grief in a way that feels safe for you.”  
- Create a private space (a corner in the communal hall or a quiet room) where tears are allowed.  
- Offer a communal “grief line” (a box or a digital forum) where people can anonymously post their feelings.

## 5. The science of “different” grief

- **Neuroplasticity**: Our brains adapt to new patterns.  Introducing new rituals can help rewire the brain’s response to loss, potentially reducing complicated grief.
- **Social Support Theory**: The more varied the support (emotional, instrumental, informational), the higher the chances of adaptive coping.
- **Narrative Therapy**: Re‑writing the story of the loss—turning it into a narrative that includes agency, meaning, and hope—helps integrate the experience.
