Affected Groups: Intersex infants and children across Iran whose conditions are identified at birth or in early childhood, encompassing the estimated 1.7 percent of the global population born with sex characteristics that do not fit typical binary definitions of male or female, including conditions such as congenital adrenal hyperplasia, androgen insensitivity syndrome, hypospadias, and gonadal dysgenesis, within a medical and legal system that processes intersex variation exclusively as a pathology requiring urgent surgical correction rather than as a natural human variation requiring careful long-term support. Most acutely felt by infants whose ambiguous genitalia are identified immediately at birth and whose parents are placed under immediate institutional pressure to authorize irreversible surgical intervention before the child has any capacity to participate in or later remember the decision, and by children identified later whose developing sense of self and body is disrupted by medical procedures whose justification was never explained to them in terms they could engage with. Particularly devastating for children whose assigned sex through surgery does not align with their later gender identity, who must then navigate the consequences of an irreversible physical alteration made without their consent in a country where gender nonconformity is legally and socially catastrophic, and for families in rural and lower income contexts where the authority differential between the medical institution and the family is most pronounced and where the capacity to seek second opinions, access alternative information, or resist institutional pressure is most limited.
Legal & Bureaucratic 
Mechanisms: The legal and medical frameworks governing intersex intervention in Iran operate through a specific convergence of Islamic jurisprudence, state medical authority, and the absolute binary of Iranian civil registration that collectively eliminate every possible space in which a non-surgical outcome could be considered. Iranian civil law requires every citizen to be registered as male or female with no alternative category, meaning an intersex child who is not surgically assigned generates a registration crisis that the administrative system has no mechanism for resolving except through medical intervention that produces a registerable outcome. This is not incidental. The civil registration requirement creates a legal deadline for surgical decision that transforms what should be a careful long-term medical and ethical deliberation into an administrative emergency experienced by parents in the immediate postpartum period when they are least equipped to resist institutional authority. Islamic jurisprudence as interpreted in Iranian medical ethics has historically framed intersex conditions as errors of creation requiring correction, producing a religious legitimation of surgical intervention that forecloses ethical objection on the grounds most available to religious Iranian families, meaning the institution that might otherwise serve as a source of resistance to medical authority instead reinforces it. Iranian medical training in obstetrics and pediatric urology follows protocols that categorize intersex variation as a disorder of sex development requiring early surgical normalization, a framework that was dominant in global medical practice through much of the twentieth century and that has been significantly challenged and partially revised in international medical ethics but that has not been systematically updated in Iranian medical education or hospital protocol. No independent patient advocacy infrastructure exists within the Iranian healthcare system for intersex individuals or their families. No legal guardian separate from parents is appointed to represent the child's interests. No requirement exists for families to be informed of non-surgical alternatives, long-term psychological outcomes research, or the existence of intersex adult communities whose testimony about early surgical intervention is overwhelmingly negative. Informed consent in this context is a signature on a form presented by an authority figure in an institution the family has no framework for challenging.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: An intersex child in Iran has no advocate in any room where decisions about their body are being made. Their parents are sitting across from a physician in a white coat in a hospital where they have already been told that their child has a condition requiring correction, that delay is medically inadvisable, that the surgery is routine, and that the outcome will be a normal child, a word that in this context means a child whose body conforms to the binary that the legal, religious, and social systems surrounding them require. The parents are not being given a choice in any meaningful sense. They are being given a signature line at the end of a process that was structured to produce consent regardless of what they actually understood or wanted. They are postpartum, frightened, confronting a situation for which nothing in their life prepared them, in an institution whose authority they have been socialized to defer to, within a religious framework that has been invoked to legitimate the surgery, and without access to any counter-narrative, any intersex adult testimony, any international medical ethics literature, any advocacy organization, or any second opinion that would reach a different conclusion because the entire medical system they have access to has reached the same one. The child for whom all of this is being decided is present in the room as the object of the decision and absent from it as a subject with interests that the decision is supposed to serve. Years later that child will be an adult who was given a body they did not choose by people who were themselves not fully choosing, inside a system that was designed to ensure the surgery happened before anyone in the room had the information, the time, or the institutional support to consider whether it should. They will live in that body in a country where the gender it was assigned is legally and socially and religiously enforced, where the medical records of what was done to them are inaccessible or were never fully explained to them, where no support community for intersex adults exists openly, and where the questions their body raises about the binary their society insists upon cannot be asked out loud without consequences that the society has also made certain are severe.
