In recent years, New Zealand law has begun to recognize that nature can have its own legal rights, independent of human ownership.

### 2. Cultural Impact Assessments (CIA)
While the lawyers ask for deeds, the Resource Management Act (RMA) and its successors require developers to consider "Cultural Impact."
*   **Don't just speak; document:** A formal Cultural Impact Assessment is a document that translates your spiritual connection into a format the court is forced to read.

### 3. The Waitangi Tribunal
If the current laws are failing to protect your ancestral land, it may be a breach of *Te Tiriti o Waitangi* (The Treaty of Waitangi).
*   **The Principle of Partnership:** The Crown has a duty to protect Māori interests and *Tino Rangatiratanga* (sovereignty/self-determination) over their lands.
*   **The Strategy:** A claim can be lodged with the Waitangi Tribunal. While the Tribunal’s recommendations are not always binding, they carry immense moral and political weight and can halt development by bringing the Crown’s failures into the international spotlight.

*   **Ihumātao:** This is a prime example of where the law said "this is private land," but the people said "this is sacred." Through peaceful occupation and massive public pressure, the government was forced to intervene.
