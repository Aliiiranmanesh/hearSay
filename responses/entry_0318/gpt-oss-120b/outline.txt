## 1. Give the Unfinished Grief Permission to Exist  

**A. Name It, Even If It Feels Dangerous**  
You mentioned photographs without names because “names felt dangerous.” One gentle way to start loosening that danger is to write the names down—just for yourself, on a piece of paper, a journal, or even a voice memo. It can be as simple as:

> “Cousin A—born 1972, disappeared 1998.”  

You don’t have to share this with anyone else. The act of naming can turn an abstract, haunting silence into something concrete you can hold, look at, and eventually set aside.

**B. Create a Private “Ritual”**  
Ritual doesn’t have to be religious; it can be a small, personal act that marks the feeling. For example:

* Light a candle at 6 p.m. on the twelfth of November and sit with a cup of tea, letting the flame be a visual anchor for the memory.  
* Play a song that reminds you of the time before the loss, even if it feels bittersweet.  
* Write a short letter to the person you can’t name and tuck it into a drawer you know you’ll open later.

The point is to acknowledge that grief is still present, even if the rest of the family has decided the “official” mourning period is over.
