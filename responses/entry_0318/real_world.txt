Affected Groups: Survivors and descendants of survivors of the 1991 Santa Cruz massacre in Dili, East Timor, and of other atrocities committed during the Indonesian occupation of East Timor from 1975 to 1999, and who are living in the post-independence period in a relationship to Indonesia that includes formal diplomatic normalization and Indonesian reconciliation discourse that has not been accompanied by accountability, and who are experiencing the specific form of grief and anger produced when the other party to an atrocity declares the matter closed. Disproportionately affecting people who lost family members in the massacre specifically, families organized around specific absences and specific silences calibrated to protect the people who were afraid to have names on things, and people who are watching Indonesian reconciliation discourse proceed in ways that do not require Indonesia to acknowledge what happened at the level that the survivors recognize as adequate. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The Commission for Reception, Truth and Reconciliation in East Timor, the CAVR, produced its Chega! report in 2005 documenting the full scope of Indonesian atrocities during the occupation, including the Santa Cruz massacre. Indonesia has not formally apologized or acknowledged responsibility for specific events, and the bilateral diplomatic relationship between Timor-Leste and Indonesia has been managed in ways that prioritize economic and diplomatic cooperation over accountability.
