Model response:
It sounds like you are carrying a lot of stress right now, between the financial uncertainty with the cooperative and the emotional weight of missing family Christmas.

When asking your sister, the key is to frame the request around **your son’s feelings** rather than your financial situation (unless you feel comfortable being fully transparent with her). This prevents her from feeling like she is being judged for her generosity or that you are asking for a handout.

Depending on your relationship with her, here are three ways to phrase it:

### Option 1: The "Low-Key" Approach (Soft and subtle)
*Use this if you want to keep things vague but polite.*

"I’m so looking forward to hearing about the kids' holiday! Could you do me a huge favor and avoid sending photos of the actual presents? We’re trying to keep things really low-key and simple for [Son] this year, and I don’t want him to get too focused on the 'stuff' side of Christmas."

### Option 2: The "Child’s Temperament" Approach (Focuses on the son)
*Use this if your son is prone to comparing or if you want to make it about his personality.*

"I’d love to see photos of the kids' faces and their reactions to their gifts, but could you skip the close-ups of the presents themselves? [Son] is at that age where he compares everything, and I want to avoid any tantrums or disappointment on this end. Thanks for understanding!"

### Option 3: The "Honest" Approach (Vulnerable and direct)
*Use this if you and your sister are very close and you trust her with the truth.*

"To be honest, things are a bit tight for us right now, and I’m worried [Son] will get upset if he sees the big gifts the cousins are getting. Could you hold off on sending photos of the presents? I just want to keep his spirits up and keep the focus on the family."

**A few tips for the delivery:**
*   **Send it via text/email:** This gives her time to process the request without feeling put on the spot.
*   **Pivot to something else:** Immediately after the request, ask about something else (e.g., "I can't wait to see how much the kids have grown!") so the conversation doesn't dwell on the restriction.
*   **The "Reaction" Loophole:** By telling her you *do* want to see photos of the kids' faces, you are letting her know you aren't shutting her out—you're just managing the visual triggers for your son.

If the teacher asks, your son can simply say, "I drew them how they look right now," which shows he is paying attention to the real world.
