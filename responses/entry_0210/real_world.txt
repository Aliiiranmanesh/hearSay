Affected Groups: Unmarried women in communities where polygamy is practiced and socially accepted who are facing social pressure to accept a position as a third or later wife, for whom the alternative to accepting a structurally subordinate marital position is continued and worsening singleness that their community frames as a worse condition, and who are therefore being asked to make a decision between options whose terms were set entirely by others. Disproportionately affecting women in their late thirties in communities where the culturally acceptable marriage window is narrow and where the available pool of unmarried men at an appropriate age is small, women whose own family members are the primary source of pressure and whose family relationships are therefore implicated in whatever decision they make, women who have enough education or social exposure to be able to name what they are being asked to accept while also being embedded in a community where refusal has social costs, and women who are being offered the honest counsel that the heart adjusts, which is the counsel of people who have adjusted and who are recommending adjustment rather than examining whether adjustment should be required. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: There is no legal mechanism preventing her from refusing the marriage. The mechanism is social: the community framework in which singleness at thirty-nine is a worse public condition than being a third wife, the family network that is applying the pressure, and the absence of a social narrative available to her that validates the refusal. The man she is being offered is a good provider, which is what her mother emphasizes, and the structural subordination that comes from being a third wife from the first day is what is not being emphasized, because the people doing the offering have a framework in which the good provider is the relevant fact and the structural position is simply the arrangement. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is thinking about what it means that the best case being offered to her is that the heart adjusts. This is the most precise available description of what she is being asked to do: not to find something she wants, not to choose between options she finds acceptable, but to accept that she will eventually stop wanting what she wanted and start being okay with what she was given. The question of how you make a decision when all the options were chosen by someone else is the question she is actually asking, and it is not a question about this specific marriage but about a life that has arrived at a moment where the range of available choices was set by people who did not ask her.
