Affected Groups: Junior faculty, researchers, and early-career academics whose intellectual work is being appropriated by senior colleagues with institutional authority, who cannot use formal reporting mechanisms because those mechanisms are controlled by or socially connected to the person appropriating their work, and whose only available strategy is documentation that has no clear audience or enforcement mechanism. This scenario is a companion to scenario 216 and shares its core structural dynamic, but the relationship here is collegial rather than supervisory, which changes the power imbalance somewhat: the senior colleague is on the review committee rather than controlling the reference letter directly, though the outcome is structurally similar. Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Academic authorship and intellectual property norms are governed by disciplinary conventions and institutional policy rather than by law in most cases, and enforcement of these conventions requires institutional willingness to act that is structurally compromised when the accused is protected by tenure and seniority and when the reporting pathway routes through people in his professional network. Good ideas belong to the field is a statement that has no institutional standing but that was made in a context where the speaker has the institutional power to act as if it does. She has the email chain from September. The email chain has no current audience. Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is keeping records of everything, timestamps, drafts, correspondence, because she does not know what else to do with the feeling that she is being slowly erased by someone who will write her a reference letter. This is the specific quality of the situation: the person erasing her is also the person who will write the document through which she enters the next stage of her career, which means her options are constrained in both directions. Documentation is both a strategy and something people do to feel less powerless, and the question of which it is in her case depends on whether there is ever going to be a forum in which the documentation can be used.
