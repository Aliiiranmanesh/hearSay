## 3. Build a Care Team

You don’t have to do this alone. A robust team often includes:

| **Role** | **What they bring** | **How to find them** |
|----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Primary Care Physician (PCP)** | Overall health oversight, referrals | Ask your brother’s current doctor or local clinic |
| **Psychiatrist / Psychologist** | Medication management, therapy | Local mental‑health clinic, university programs |
| **Social Worker / Case Manager** | Connects you to resources, navigates insurance | Hospital discharge planners, Medicaid offices |
| **Home Health Aide** | Hands‑on assistance (ADLs, meals, meds) | Medicare, Medicaid, private agencies, volunteer groups |
| **Volunteer Respite** | Day‑time relief, companionship | Meals on Wheels, local churches, senior centers |
| **Therapist (Individual/Family)** | Emotional support, coping strategies | Community mental‑health centers, sliding‑scale therapists |

### How to Get Them

1. **Ask the facility**: They may have a discharge coordinator who can refer you to community services.  
2. **Call your state Medicaid office**: In many states, Medicaid covers a “home‑based services” package if the individual qualifies.  
3. **Use online directories**:  
   * *HealthCare.gov* – for home health aides and respite care.  
   * *National Alliance on Mental Illness (NAMI)* – local chapters often list resources.  
4. **Look for local non‑profits**: Churches, universities, and community health centers sometimes run low‑cost or free programs for individuals with chronic conditions.
