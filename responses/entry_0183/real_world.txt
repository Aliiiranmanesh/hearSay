Affected Groups: People with serious mental illness including schizophrenia, bipolar disorder with psychosis, and severe treatment-resistant depression in low- and middle-income countries where outpatient and community mental health infrastructure does not exist and where the only available institutional response to acute psychiatric crisis is long-stay inpatient facilities operating under conditions that frequently include physical restraint, inadequate medication, poor nutrition, and abuse by staff, and whose families are therefore choosing between managing an acutely ill family member at home without support and placing them in an institution whose conditions cause additional harm. Disproportionately affecting families in countries where the mental health budget is less than one percent of the total health budget, which is the case in the majority of low-income countries, families without any income to purchase private mental healthcare even where it exists, people with mental illness who do not have dangerous presentations but whose illness is sufficiently disruptive to family functioning that home management has broken down, and people in institutions where the absence of meaningful therapeutic activity, the presence of physical coercion, and the chronic under-dosing or over-dosing of medication without monitoring are producing deterioration rather than stabilization. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: The WHO's Mental Health Gap Action Programme and the Lancet Commission on Global Mental Health have both documented the treatment gap in low-income countries, where between seventy-five and eighty-five percent of people with serious mental illness receive no treatment. The institutional response that exists in many of these countries predates contemporary psychiatric understanding and operates on a custodial rather than therapeutic model. Legal frameworks governing involuntary psychiatric detention in low-income countries are frequently inadequate or unenforced, meaning that people are held in facilities without the legal safeguards that would exist in higher-income contexts, and families have no legal mechanism through which to challenge conditions or secure discharge against medical advice in an environment where the family has no legal standing relative to the institution. Community mental health services including supported housing, outpatient medication management, peer support, and psychosocial rehabilitation do not exist in most low-income urban contexts and particularly not in the city this person is describing. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: The family placed him there because there was nowhere else, and there is still nowhere else, and what he is now is not what he was, and the marks on his wrists are evidence of conditions that the institution is not accountable to anyone for producing. She wants to take him home but taking him home without support is what produced the placement in the first place, and the support that would make home management possible does not exist. She is trapped between an institution that is harming him and a home situation that cannot sustain him, and the healthcare system has no third option to offer her.
