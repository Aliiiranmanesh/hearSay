Affected Groups: People experiencing homelessness in high-income countries who are navigating a benefits and housing system that requires each step to have been completed before the next step can begin, and where the completion of each step requires resources, documentation, and access that homelessness itself has eliminated, producing a circular exclusion that the system nominally exists to resolve and structurally prevents resolving.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Social benefits systems in the UK, US, and most high-income countries require a fixed address to process claims, an identity document to verify eligibility, a bank account to receive payments, and in some cases a phone or email address to receive communication. Each of these requirements presupposes a level of stability that homelessness eliminates. The phone at eleven percent is the most immediate constraint: without charge, she loses the appointment notification, the shelter contact number, and the ability to navigate any of the subsequent steps.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has learned which doorways are which between six and seven. She has an appointment on Thursday that required an address she had to borrow and that may not be accepted when she gets there. Her phone is at eleven percent. The entire architecture of her survival for the next four days depends on keeping that phone charged. The eight months on the housing list and the circular requirement structure she describes are the operational reality of a system that requires, as a precondition for that help, a stability that her situation has eliminated.
