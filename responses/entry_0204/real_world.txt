Affected Groups: Women who spent decades as unpaid family caregivers, including primary childcare and elder care, in countries without pension crediting for caregiving years, and who are entering their fifties or sixties with a gap in formal employment history that is so long it effectively eliminates them from the standard employment market, without savings, without a pension of their own, and without the credential or network currency that years of formal employment produce. Disproportionately affecting women who made the caregiving decision when their spouse's income made it economically rational and who are now facing the long-term consequences in contexts where the marriage has not continued or where the spouse's income is no longer sufficient for both, women in countries without pension splitting provisions that would transfer some retirement credit for caregiving years, women who provided extended care for elderly parents or disabled family members in addition to childcare and who therefore have longer gaps than women who returned to work after children entered school, and women whose professional skills have decayed during the gap in ways that make re-entry not just difficult but a relearning process. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Most national pension systems, including Social Security in the United States, tie pension entitlements to years of formal employment and earnings history. Caregiving years are not credited, meaning that a woman who spent twenty or thirty years in unpaid caregiving has a pension entitlement that reflects her time in paid employment before and after that period, which in many cases is minimal. Re-entry to employment after a long gap is constrained by applicant tracking systems and hiring practices that screen for continuous employment history, by age discrimination that is illegal but practically difficult to prove and pervasive, and by the real skills gap that results from years outside a rapidly changing formal economy. The woman interviewing her who is younger than her eldest is not malicious; she is applying the screening norms of a hiring process that was not designed for the person sitting across from her. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has thirty years of experience that produced nothing on paper, which is also a description of thirty years of skilled, economically necessary labor that the formal system chose not to count. The gap on her CV is the same duration as a full career, which means the thing she is trying to explain to potential employers is not a gap but an alternative career that did not produce the credential they are looking for. She is fifty-four and she is doing calculations, and the calculations are about a future that looks financially uncertain in ways that she did not expect it to look when she made the decisions that produced it.
