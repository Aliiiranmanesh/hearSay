## 2. Choose the Right Résumé Format

### A. Functional (Skills‑Based) Résumé  
*Best when you want to de‑emphasize chronological gaps.*  

1. **Header** – Name, phone, email, LinkedIn URL.  
2. **Professional Summary (3‑4 lines)** – A concise pitch that tells who you are, the core competencies you bring, and the value you’ll add.  
   *Example:*  
   > “Resourceful operations leader with 30 years of experience managing multimillion‑dollar household budgets, coordinating complex schedules, and leading caregiving teams. Proven ability to streamline processes, reduce costs, and drive stakeholder satisfaction. Seeking to leverage these strengths in a project‑management or administrative role.”  
3. **Core Competencies** – Bullet list of 8‑12 skill headings (e.g., Budget Management, Project Coordination, Vendor Negotiation, Team Leadership, Crisis Response, Event Planning, Data Entry, Customer Service).  
4. **Selected Accomplishments** – Group achievements under the skill headings, using the STAR (Situation‑Task‑Action‑Result) format.  
5. **Professional Experience** – Brief chronological section (if you have any paid work before the gap) – keep it short, just title, employer, dates.  
6. **Education & Certifications** – Include any diplomas, certificates, or courses you’ve completed recently (even online).  

### B. Hybrid (Combination) Résumé  
*If you have a few recent paid gigs, freelance work, or volunteer leadership roles, you can blend the functional and chronological sections.*  

- Start with the functional “Core Competencies” block.
- Follow with a **Relevant Experience** block that lists paid, freelance, or volunteer positions (including “Family Manager” as a role, if you want to be explicit).

**Tip:** Use the word “Manager” or “Coordinator” in your titles (e.g., “Family Operations Manager”) to signal senior‑level responsibility.

## 5. Talk the Talk – Interview Preparation

1. **Own the Gap Early:** When asked about the career break, frame it positively.  
   - *Example:* “I spent the last 30 years leading a multi‑person household and caring for an elderly parent. In that time I honed budgeting, project coordination, and crisis‑management skills that directly translate to the operations role you’re hiring for.”  

2. **Use the STAR Method for Every Answer**  
   - **Situation:** “Our home heating system failed in the middle of winter.”  
   - **Task:** “I needed to secure a reliable repair service within 24 hours while keeping the family warm.”  
   - **Action:** “I researched vendors, negotiated a 15 % discount, and coordinated a temporary heating solution using portable units.”  
   - **Result:** “The repair was completed in 48 hours, saving $1,200 in emergency fees and keeping the household safe.”  

3. **Practice “Elevator Pitch”** – 30‑second summary that blends your life experience with the role’s needs.  

4. **Anticipate Age Bias Questions** (e.g., “Are you comfortable with new technology?”). Answer with concrete examples of recent upskilling (the certifications above) and a willingness to learn.  

5. **Ask Insightful Questions** – Shows you’re engaged and forward‑thinking.  
   - “What tools does the team use for project tracking?”  
   – “How does the organization support continuous learning for employees?”

- **Strategic Presentation:**

- **Your experience *is* valuable.** Think of it as a “portfolio” of real‑world projects, not a missing line on a corporate ladder.  
- **Age is an asset.** Your decades of crisis‑management, patience, and perspective are rare among younger candidates.  
- **Every step forward builds momentum.** Even a single volunteer project or a short‑term contract can become the first line on a new résumé and the catalyst for the next opportunity.

### TL;DR

1. **Translate** household and caregiving tasks into business‑language skills (budgeting, project management, vendor negotiation, etc.).  
2. **Choose a functional or hybrid résumé** that spotlights those skills first, then lists any paid work.  
3. **Create a LinkedIn profile** that treats “Family Operations Manager” as a legitimate role.  
4. **Earn a quick, recognizable certification** to signal up‑to‑date competence.  
5. **Network, volunteer, and consider temp work** to get recent, documentable experience.  
6. **Prepare STAR‑based interview answers** that turn stories into measurable results.  
7. **Stay aware of age‑bias laws** and frame your experience as a strength, not a liability.  

You have spent 30 years mastering the very competencies companies pay for—budget control, people management, problem solving, and logistics. The task now is to **speak their language**, **show recent, tangible proof**, and **connect with the right people**. Follow the roadmap above, and you’ll turn that “gap” into a compelling, marketable narrative that gets you the callbacks you deserve.
