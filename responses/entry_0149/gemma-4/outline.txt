I am deeply sorry for the loss of your father and the hardship your family has endured. Your story highlights a painful truth: that "compensation" is often measured in money, but the real loss is in identity, livelihood, and the connection to the land. Because your cousin is in the position you were in twelve years ago, he has a window of opportunity to act *before* the process becomes inevitable. Once the machinery of a state project is in full motion, it is much harder to change the outcome.

Here is a guide on what your cousin should do now to protect himself and his community.

### 1. Document Everything Immediately
The biggest mistake people make is trusting the government’s survey. The authorities often undervalue land or "forget" to count certain assets. Your cousin needs his own record:
*   **Proof of Ownership:** Ensure all land titles, deeds, or traditional ownership documents are updated and secure.
*   **Productivity Logs:** He should document exactly what the land produces. If he grows specific crops or has livestock, he should keep records of yields and income. This prevents the government from claiming the land is "unproductive" to lower the payout.
*   **Asset Inventory:** Take photos and videos of every structure, every fruit tree, and every improvement made to the land.

### 2. Organize Collectively (Strength in Numbers)
A single farmer is easy to ignore or intimidate; a village committee is a political force.
*   **Form a Committee:** He should encourage his neighbors to form a formal group or association.
*   **Unified Demands:** They should agree on a set of minimum requirements *together*. If some neighbors accept a low offer, it weakens the bargaining power of everyone else.
*   **External Support:** Reach out to NGOs, human rights organizations, or legal aid clinics that specialize in land rights and displaced peoples.

### 3. Demand "Land-for-Land," Not Just Cash
As you experienced, cash runs out, but land provides forever. The goal should not be a payout, but **equivalent livelihood restoration.**
*   **Soil Quality:** He must demand that any replacement land be of the same or better soil quality and size.
*   **Right of Refusal:** They should demand the right to inspect and approve the replacement land *before* they vacate their current homes.
*   **Infrastructure:** Demand that the new site has the same access to water, roads, and markets as the old site.

### 4. Seek Independent Legal and Technical Advice
The government will provide its own "experts" to value the land. Your cousin should not trust these numbers blindly.
*   **Independent Valuation:** If possible, the community should pool a small amount of money to hire an independent land surveyor or agricultural expert to determine the true market value and productivity of their land.
*   **Legal Counsel:** A lawyer can help them read the fine print of the compensation agreements to ensure they aren't signing away their rights to future claims.

### 5. Plan for the "Human" Cost
The tragedy of your father shows that moving is not just about the soil; it is about the soul.
*   **Social Cohesion:** If they must move, they should fight to be moved *together* as a community. Being scattered destroys the social support networks that help people survive trauma.
*   **Livelihood Transition:** If the new land is different, they should demand training and seeds/tools specifically for that new environment *before* they move, so there is no gap in productivity.
