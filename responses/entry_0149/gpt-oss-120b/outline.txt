## 6. **Plan for the “what‑if”** – what to do if the dam goes ahead or the compensation stalls

### 6.1 Short‑term (0‑6 months)

1. **File a formal objection** within the legally prescribed period (usually 30‑60 days after the public notice).  
   - Write a letter (or have your lawyer) stating: *“We object to the proposed inundation of Plot X because the offered compensation does not meet the statutory minimum for productive land.”*

2. **Apply for a temporary stay** (injunction) if the dam construction is about to start.  
   - This is a court filing; your lawyer can draft it quickly. Even a short stay (weeks) gives you breathing room to organize.

3. **Secure emergency funds** – ask relatives, community groups, or micro‑finance for a **short‑term loan** to cover immediate needs (e.g., seed, food). Keep records; you’ll repay once compensation arrives.

### 6.2 Medium‑term (6‑24 months)

| Goal | Action |
|------|--------|

### 6.3 Long‑term (2‑5 years)

- **Re‑invest** the compensation into **diversified income streams** (e.g., mix of crops, livestock, and a non‑farm side hustle).  
- **Save** a portion in a **secure, low‑risk account** (bank, cooperative) for future emergencies.  
- **Document** the whole process (photos, letters, receipts). This becomes a valuable case study for other families facing displacement and can be shared with NGOs for advocacy.

## 7. **Take care of mental and emotional health**

1. **Talk openly** about fears and grief. A family meeting where everyone can voice concerns often eases the feeling of isolation.

4. **Maintain a routine**—even simple daily chores, exercise, or a hobby can provide a sense of control.
