### What your cousin should do now

1. **Find out who is behind the dam**
   - Which government agency?
   - Which company or contractor?
   - Who is financing it—government, development bank, private investors?
   - Is there an Environmental and Social Impact Assessment being prepared?

   He should make copies and photos of:
   - Land titles, customary land records, tax receipts, lease papers
   - House, farm buildings, wells, trees, fences, irrigation, grazing areas
   - Crops, orchards, livestock, fishing areas, forest products, small businesses
   - Graves, sacred places, community sites
   - Income from land, animals, markets, seasonal work
   - Photos and videos with dates if possible

3. **Do not sign anything alone or quickly**
   He should not sign compensation forms, “consent” papers, attendance sheets that may later be used as agreement, or relocation documents without:

   - Getting a copy
   - Having someone trusted explain them
   - Speaking with other affected families
   - Getting legal or advocacy advice if possible

   One family alone is easy to pressure. A community can demand:

   - Proper consultation before decisions are made
   - Independent valuation of land and assets

   - Livelihood restoration, not just cash

   - Transitional food/income support until the new land produces
   - Compensation for lost harvests, trees, and the years needed for orchards to mature

   - Farms

   - Grazing routes
   - Water sources
   - Forest areas
   - Fishing sites
   - Burial grounds and sacred places
   - Seasonal uses of land

   - Grievance mechanism
   - Timeline
