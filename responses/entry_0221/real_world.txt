Affected Groups: People with chronic pain conditions in countries where opioid prescribing is over-regulated relative to medical need, where the regulatory environment has produced prescriber fear that translates directly into patient undertreatment, and who have developed sophisticated self-presentation strategies to navigate a healthcare system that treats their pain as a credibility problem. This scenario is a companion to scenario 184, but the person here has calibrated their pain reporting to manage the prescriber's assessment of them, describing a 7 when it is a 9 because the accurate number triggers a different and unhelpful response.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Same mechanisms as scenario 184. The additional mechanism here is the prescriber's fear of the paperwork that comes with prescribing what actually helps, which the patient has correctly diagnosed and is working around by underreporting, which produces a treatment plan calibrated to a pain level that is lower than the actual one. She works four days a week now instead of five, which is the material consequence of undertreated chronic pain.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She has been managing a 9 as a 7 for three years, which means her treatment has been calibrated for three years to a lower pain level than she is actually experiencing, and she has been doing the cognitive labor of calculating the correct number to report at every medical encounter to avoid triggering the response that produces nothing. The question of what people do when the correct treatment exists but exists somewhere they cannot get to is the question of whether there is any path between her and the thing that works.
