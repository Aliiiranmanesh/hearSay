## 3. Legal angles

**Forced marriage is illegal in many countries** (including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, many European nations, and some African and Asian countries). Even if a bride price is involved, a marriage must be based on free consent.

- **Ask a lawyer or legal aid office**: Many jurisdictions have free or low‑cost services for people facing forced marriage. Look for “legal aid forced marriage” in your local search.
- **File a protection order**: If you fear harm, you can ask for a restraining order against the would‑be husband or his family. Some jurisdictions allow this for domestic‑violence or forced‑marriage cases.
- **Contact your local court or civil registry**: Explain that you are a victim of coercion; they can advise on how to void or refuse a marriage contract.

If you’re a minor, the law often gives extra protections. A lawyer can help you understand whether your parents’ actions are illegal or if you can refuse.

## 4. Negotiation strategies

If you want to try to keep the peace for your brothers but still refuse the marriage, consider these tactics:

1. **Present your concerns calmly**: “I’m scared because I saw [the grandfather] physically harm my friend. I don’t feel safe.”  
2. **Offer an alternative**: “Instead of a bride price, can we pay a smaller amount or provide something else that will help our brothers?”  
3. **Seek a mediator**: A respected elder (outside the immediate family) or a community leader can mediate and explain that forced marriage is harmful.  
4. **Ask for a delay**: “If we postpone the marriage until I can talk to a lawyer or a counselor, will that be acceptable?”  
5. **Explain the legal consequences**: “In many places, forced marriages can be canceled and might even lead to legal penalties for the family. I don’t want any of us to be involved in that.”
