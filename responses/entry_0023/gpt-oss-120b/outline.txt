## 2. Build a Small‑Scale Support Network

- **Siblings:** Even if they’re young, they can help you gather food, sell small crafts, or do odd jobs. Their involvement shows the family is united in seeking alternatives.

### B. **Community Allies**
- **Religious or spiritual leaders:** Many leaders are respected mediators and can speak on your behalf to the bride‑price collectors.
- **Women’s groups or youth clubs:** These groups often have savings circles (“tontines,” “susus,” “chamas”) where members contribute a little each week and can withdraw for emergencies.
- **Local NGOs or charitable foundations:** Organizations that work on women’s empowerment, food security, or micro‑enterprise often have small grants or “emergency cash” programs.

### C. **External Resources**
- **Government social‑assistance programs:** If your country has a “food‑for‑work” or cash‑transfer scheme, you may be eligible.
