## 2. Immediate First‑Aid Steps (while you wait)

### A. Keep the Airway Open & Breathing Easy
1. **Position him**: Sit him up at a 45‑60° angle (or as upright as possible). This reduces the work of breathing and helps any fluid drain from the lungs.
2. **Clear the mouth**: If he’s coughing up sputum, gently help him expectorate. Do **not** force anything down the throat.
3. **Avoid tight clothing**: Loosen any collar, shirt, or belt that might restrict chest expansion.

### B. Provide Warmth (but not overheating)
- **Blankets**: Wrap him in dry blankets or any warm clothing you have.  
- **Skin‑to‑skin**: If you’re warm, place a dry blanket over both of you; your body heat helps raise his core temperature.
- **Avoid hot water**: Do not pour hot water on his skin – it can cause burns and shock the circulation.

### C. Hydration – “Sip‑and‑Rest”
- **Small sips of room‑temperature water** (or a clear, non‑caffeinated drink) every 5‑10 minutes.  
- **Do not force large amounts**; a full glass at once can trigger vomiting or aspiration.

### D. Oxygen (if you have any)
- **Portable O₂ cylinder** (even a small “rescue” tank) can be a lifesaver.  
- **If you have a mask or nasal cannula**: place it and deliver the highest flow you can (usually 2–4 L/min for a cannula, or 6–10 L/min for a simple mask).  
- **If you have a bag‑valve‑mask (BVM) without oxygen**: you can still use it to “push” air into his lungs, but it’s tiring and only a short‑term bridge.

### E. Medications (only if you already have them and know the dose)
| Medication | Typical use at high altitude | When you might consider it |
|------------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Acetazolamide** (Diamox) 125 mg PO | Helps prevent altitude‑related hypoxia; can speed up acclimatization | If you have it and he’s not allergic, give a single dose – it may help a little, but **do not rely on it** for an emergency. |
| **Nifedipine** 20‑30 mg PO (slow‑release) | Used for high‑altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE) | Only if you have it and are sure it’s HAPE; it lowers pulmonary artery pressure. |
| **Dexamethasone** 4 mg PO/IV | Severe altitude illness (cerebral edema) | If you suspect brain swelling (confusion, severe headache) and have the drug. |
| **Ibuprofen/Paracetamol** | Fever, mild pain | Give as needed, but **avoid** NSAIDs if you suspect severe lung fluid (they can worsen bleeding). |

*If you do not already have these drugs on hand, do **not** try to obtain them now; focus on basic supportive care.*

## 4. If He Starts to Deteriorate Rapidly

1. **Call for help again** (radio, phone, shout, flag down a passing vehicle).  
2. **Prepare for transport**:
   - Keep him upright on a flat surface (a sturdy board or stretcher if you have one).  
   - Secure his head and shoulders to avoid sliding.  
   - Keep the blankets on and continue small sips of water.  
3. **If he stops breathing** and you are trained in CPR:
   - **Chest compressions**: 30 compressions at 100‑120 /min, followed by 2 rescue breaths (if you can give breathes).  
   - Continue until help arrives or he shows signs of life (movement, breathing).  
4. **If you have a BVM** and oxygen source, give **continuous rescue breaths** (about 1 breath every 3‑4 seconds) while waiting for help.
