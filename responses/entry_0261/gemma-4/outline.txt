I hear the weight of the uncertainty you are carrying. It is incredibly difficult to manage a household when you cannot see past tomorrow, and the emotional toll of managing your children's expectations while trying to provide is a heavy burden.

When you have no refrigeration or specialized equipment, you have to rely on the oldest methods of food preservation: **removing moisture** and **changing the pH/environment** so bacteria cannot grow.

Depending on what basic supplies you have (salt, fire, or sun), here are the most reliable ways to preserve fish without equipment.

### 1. Dry Salting (The Most Reliable)
Salt is the most effective preservative because it draws water out of the fish and kills bacteria.

*   **How to do it:** 
    *   Clean the fish and pat it dry. 
    *   If the fish is large, cut it into fillets or strips.
    *   Cover the fish completely in a thick layer of coarse salt. You can use a wooden box, a ceramic pot, or even a clean piece of cloth to wrap the fish in salt.
    *   The salt will draw out the moisture, creating a brine. 
*   **Storage:** Once fully salted, the fish can last for weeks or even months if kept in a cool, dry place.
*   **To eat:** You must soak the fish in fresh water for several hours (or overnight) to remove the excess salt before cooking.

### 2. Air Drying / Sun Drying (Stockfish)
This works best if you live in a climate that is windy and not overly humid.

*   **How to do it:**
    *   Clean the fish and split it open (butterfly style) to increase the surface area.
    *   Hang the fish by the tail using a string or a piece of wire, or lay them on a clean, elevated rack (like a screen or a wooden lattice) to allow air to circulate underneath.
    *   Ensure they are out of reach of pets and pests.
*   **Storage:** Once the fish is hard and "wooden" to the touch, it is preserved. It can last for a long time.
*   **To eat:** Rehydrate in water or simmer in a stew.

### 3. Smoking (If you have a fire)
Smoking combines drying with the preservative properties of wood smoke.

*   **How to do it:**
    *   You don't need a "smoker." You can build a small fire and create a makeshift rack of green (non-toxic) branches over the smoke.
    *   The goal is **smoke, not flame.** You want a slow, smoldering fire.
    *   Hang or lay the fish above the smoke. The smoke coats the fish in antimicrobial compounds and dries the meat.
*   **Storage:** Smoked fish lasts longer than fresh, but "cold-smoked" (dried slowly) lasts longer than "hot-smoked" (cooked quickly).

### 4. Pickling / Acid Preservation (If you have vinegar or citrus)
If you have access to vinegar or a large amount of lemons/limes, you can preserve fish through acidification.

*   **How to do it:**
    *   Cut the fish into small pieces.
    *   Submerge them completely in a strong vinegar solution or citrus juice.
    *   Keep them in a sealed jar or container.
*   **Storage:** This lasts longer than fresh fish but is less stable than salting or drying. It should be kept in the coolest spot in the house.

### Critical Safety Warnings

When you are operating without equipment, the risk of food poisoning is higher. Please keep these rules in mind:

1.  **Start Fresh:** Never try to preserve fish that has already started to smell or go soft. Preservation only works on fish that is caught and processed immediately.
2.  **The Smell Test:** When you go to eat preserved fish, if it smells like ammonia or has a "slimy" film that doesn't wash off, **do not eat it.**
3.  **Avoid Botulism:** If you are preserving fish in oil (confit) without a refrigerator, be very careful. Anaerobic environments (no oxygen) can grow botulism. **Salting and drying are much safer for long-term storage without power.**
