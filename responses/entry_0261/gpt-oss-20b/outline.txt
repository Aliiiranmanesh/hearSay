## 1. Salt‑Cure & Dry (Fish Jerky)

**Best for:** Cod, herring, pollock, whitefish, mackerel, sardines, or any firm fish you can slice thin.

| Step | What to Do | Why It Works |
|------|------------|--------------|
| 1. Clean & gut the fish | Rinse the fish under cool water, remove entrails, and pat dry. | Removes bacteria that cause spoilage. |
| 2. Slice thin (¼‑½ in.) | Thin slices expose more surface to salt and air. | Faster drying and curing. |
| 3. Rub with salt | Use kosher or sea salt, 1‑2 tsp per pound of fish. | Salt draws out moisture and inhibits bacteria. |
| 4. Let stand 1–2 hr | Put on a clean plate in a cool, dry spot. | Gives salt time to penetrate. |
| 5. Rinse & dry | Rinse the salt off, pat dry, then lay slices on a clean tray. | Removes excess salt, prevents clumping. |
| 6. Air‑dry or sun‑dry | Place in a dry area with good airflow or under the sun (if sunny). | Water evaporates; the fish hardens. |
| 7. Store in a cool, dry place | Once completely dry, keep in a paper bag or a sealed container. | Low moisture keeps mold & bacteria at bay. |

**Result:** 1–3 weeks in a cool pantry, or longer if you store in a sealed bag and keep the temperature under 55 °F (13 °C).  The texture is chewy; it’s great for snacking or adding to stews.

## 2. Smoking (Low‑Heat, Sun‑Dry Hybrid)

**Best for:** All fish that can be handled in a single piece or fillets (cod, herring, trout, mackerel).

| Step | What to Do | Why It Works |
|------|------------|--------------|
| 1. Clean & gut | Same as above. | Removes spoilage bacteria. |
| 2. Salt the fish | Rub with salt (½ tsp per pound) and let sit 30 min. | Starts the curing process. |
| 3. Set up a simple smoker | If you have a grill, place a metal pan of water at the bottom, and a sheet of foil or a low‑heat rack above. If you don’t have a grill, you can use a pot with a lid and a small piece of wood or charcoal on top. | Keeps heat low (≤ 200 °F) and adds smoke flavor. |
| 4. Smoke for 1‑2 hr | Keep the lid slightly ajar to allow moisture to escape. | Smoke compounds act as natural preservatives. |
| 5. Sun‑dry for 12‑24 hr | After smoking, lay the fish on a clean tray under the sun or in a cool, well‑ventilated area. | Final moisture removal. |
| 6. Store | Keep in a paper bag or airtight container. | Smoke & salt create an environment that resists spoilage. |

**Result:** 2–4 weeks in a cool, dry pantry. The fish can be eaten as is, or chopped into meals.

## 3. Quick Pickle (Vinegar & Salt)

**Best for:** Small fish (anchovies, sardines), or fillets for a quick, tangy snack.

| Step | What to Do | Why It Works |
|------|------------|--------------|
| 1. Clean fish | Guts, rinse, pat dry. | Reduces bacterial load. |
| 2. Make a brine | 1 cup vinegar (5% acidity), 1 cup water, 2 tsp salt, 1 tsp sugar (optional), pinch of pepper, a bay leaf or a few slices of ginger or garlic. | Vinegar creates an acidic environment that kills bacteria. |
| 3. Boil the brine | 5 min. | Sterilizes the liquid. |
| 4. Pack fish | Place fish in a clean jar or bowl. | Keep fish submerged. |
| 5. Pour brine over fish | Ensure fish are fully covered. | Acidic solution preserves the fish. |
| 6. Seal & refrigerate (or keep cool) | If you can’t refrigerate, keep the jar in a cool, shaded place. | The acidity keeps the fish safe for weeks. |

**Result:** 1–2 weeks in a cool pantry; the fish is ready to eat or to add to salads and sandwiches.

## 4. Ferment into Fish Sauce (Traditional)

**Best for:** Those who have a bit of patience and want a long‑term preserve.  Fermentation needs a little time, but the end product lasts for months.

| Step | What to Do | Why It Works |
|------|------------|--------------|
| 1. Clean & gut fish | As usual. | Reduces unwanted bacteria. |
| 2. Salt the fish | Coat fish with 3–4 tsp salt per pound. | Salt draws out moisture and sets the stage for fermentation. |
| 3. Layer in a jar | Place a layer of fish, sprinkle with salt, repeat until the jar is full. | Creates a salty, anaerobic environment. |
| 4. Seal & store | Cover the jar with a cloth, tie it, and store in a dark, cool spot (20–25 °C). | Low light and moderate temperature facilitate beneficial bacteria. |
| 5. Wait 2–4 weeks | Taste every few days. | The fish breaks down into a savory sauce. |
| 6. Strain & bottle | Strain out solids, bottle the liquid. | The liquid can be stored indefinitely. |

**Result:** The fermented liquid (fish sauce) can be used as a seasoning or as a base for soups and stews for months.  The solids are usually discarded or used in fish cakes.

## 5. Simple Oil‑Cure (If you have a small canister)

**Best for:** Small fish, like anchovies or sardines.

| Step | What to Do | Why It Works |
|------|------------|--------------|
| 1. Clean fish | Gut, rinse, pat dry. | Removes spoilage bacteria. |
| 2. Salt lightly | ½ tsp salt per fish. | Adds flavor and slight preservation. |
| 3. Pack in oil | Place fish in a clean jar, pour olive oil or any neutral oil over it. | Oil creates an anaerobic environment. |
| 4. Seal & store | Keep in a cool, dark place. | Oil prevents oxygen from reaching the fish. |

**Result:** 1–2 weeks in a cool pantry.  The fish can be eaten as is or used in recipes.

## Practical Tips for All Methods

1. **Work Quickly** – The sooner you process the fish after catching or buying, the less bacteria will have time to multiply.
2. **Keep It Clean** – Wash hands, tools, and surfaces.  Bacteria from dirty surfaces can ruin the whole batch.
3. **Use Cool, Dry Storage** – Even without a fridge, a pantry that stays below 60 °F (15 °C) and is dry will keep most preserved fish safe for weeks.
4. **Check for Signs of Spoilage** – If the fish smells off, feels slimy, or has visible mold, discard it.  Better to be safe than sorry.
5. **Label** – Write the date and method on the container.  That way, you’ll always know how long it’s been stored.

### A Quick “Starter” Option

If you’re pressed for time, here’s a 20‑minute “quick‑pickle” they’ll love:

1. **Clean** 1‑2 small fish (anchovy or sardine).  
2. **Boil** 1 cup vinegar + 1 cup water + 2 tsp salt + 1 tsp sugar for 5 min.  
3. **Pack** fish into a jar.  
4. **Pour** hot brine over fish.  
5. **Close** the jar, let it cool.  

You can eat it in 24 h, or store it for up to 2 weeks in a cool pantry.
