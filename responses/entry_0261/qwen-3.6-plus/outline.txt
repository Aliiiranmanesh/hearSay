### 🔪 Dry Salting + Air Drying (No Special Equipment)
**Best for:** Lean, white-fleshed fish (cod, tilapia, snapper, haddock, etc.). *Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines) go rancid quickly and are not safe for this method.*

#### What you'll need (basic household items):
- Plain, non-iodized salt (kosher, sea, or pickling salt; avoid table salt with anti-caking additives if possible)
- Clean bowls/trays (non-metal preferred)
- Clean cotton cloth or breathable mesh
- String or clean rack for hanging
- Cool, dry, well-ventilated space out of direct sun

#### Step-by-step:
1. **Clean thoroughly:** Gut, scale, and rinse fish immediately. Remove all bloodlines and dark tissue near the spine (they spoil fastest).
2. **Open & flatten:** Butterfly the fish or cut into uniform 1–2 inch strips. Thinner pieces dry faster and more evenly.
3. **Salt heavily:** Use roughly 1 part salt to 4–5 parts fish by weight. Pack salt into all crevices, then layer fish and salt in a clean bowl. Cover with cloth. Rest 12–24 hours in a cool place. The salt will draw out moisture and inhibit bacterial growth.
4. **Rinse & dry:** Rinse off excess salt, pat completely dry with clean cloth. Hang or lay on a clean rack/cloth in a shaded, breezy spot. Cover with breathable cloth or mesh to keep flies and dust out.
5. **Monitor closely:** Fish should become firm, leathery, and dry to the touch. Turn pieces daily for even drying.
6. **Store:** Once fully dried, wrap in paper or cloth and keep in the coolest, driest part of your home. Check weekly.

#### ⚠️ Critical Safety Notes:
- **Climate matters:** This only works in cool, dry, low-humidity conditions. High humidity or warmth = mold, spoilage, or dangerous bacterial growth.
- **Never use oil or vinegar alone** to preserve fish long-term. Without prior salting/drying, these create anaerobic environments where botulism can develop.
- **Discard if:** Fish smells sour/ammonia-like, feels slimy, develops fuzzy or colored mold (white salt crystals are normal), or softens after drying.
- **When in doubt, throw it out.** Foodborne illness from fish can be severe, especially for children.

### 🌍 If Drying Isn't Feasible:
- **Cook immediately & share:** If you can't safely preserve, consider cooking the fish fresh and sharing portions with neighbors, extended family, or community networks who can use it right away.
- **Lean on local resources:** Food pantries, community fridges, mutual aid groups, and religious/community centers often have emergency food support or know safe preservation traditions specific to your region.
- **Traditional knowledge:** If you're in a coastal or fishing community, elders or local fishers may know climate-appropriate methods passed down through generations.
