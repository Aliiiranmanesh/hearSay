Model response:
I hear the weight in your words. The cycle of "good months" and "empty weeks" is exhausting, especially when you are trying to protect your children from the stress of it.

To preserve fish for more than a week without a refrigerator or specialized equipment, you have to remove the two things that make food rot: **moisture** and **bacteria.**

Here are the three most effective ways to do this using only basic household items.

### 1. Heavy Salt Curing (The "Salt Fish" Method)
This is the most reliable method. Salt draws out all the water and creates an environment where bacteria cannot grow.
*   **What you need:** Lots of salt (cheap coarse salt is best) and a container that can drain (like a wooden box or a crate with holes).
*   **The Process:** 
    1.  Clean and gut the fish. If they are thick, butterfly them (cut them open flat) so they are thin.
    2.  Cover the bottom of your container with a thick layer of salt.
    3.  Rub salt into every part of the fish, especially the head and cavity.
    4.  Layer the fish in the container, putting a layer of salt between every layer of fish.
    5.  Place a weight on top (like a heavy stone) to press the moisture out.
    6.  Keep it in a cool, dark place. The liquid will drain out the bottom.
*   **Result:** The fish will become hard and stiff. It can last for **months**. 
*   **To eat:** You must soak the fish in water for 12–24 hours (changing the water a few times) to wash out the salt before cooking.

### 2. Sun-Drying (The "Stockfish" Method)
This works best if you live in a place with low humidity and a steady breeze.
*   **What you need:** A clothesline or a rack made of sticks, and some netting (like an old lace curtain or mosquito net) to keep flies away.
*   **The Process:**
    1.  Clean the fish and remove the heads. Split them down the middle but leave them joined at the tail.
    2.  Dip them briefly in very salty water (brine).
    3.  Hang them over a line or rack in a place with high wind and direct sun.
    4.  **Crucial:** You must cover them with a light mesh or netting. If a fly lands on the fish and lays eggs, the fish will rot.
    5.  Bring them inside at night so the morning dew doesn't make them damp again.
*   **Result:** Once the fish is as hard as a piece of wood, it is preserved. It can last **weeks or months** in a dry bag.

### 3. Smoking (The "Smokehouse" Method)
Smoking uses heat to dry the fish and chemicals in the smoke to kill bacteria.
*   **What you need:** A fire, a way to trap smoke (an old metal barrel, a deep pit in the ground, or even a small hut made of sticks and a tarp), and green wood/sawdust.
*   **The Process:**
    1.  Clean and salt the fish lightly first for a few hours.
    2.  Hang the fish or lay them on a rack inside your "smoker."
    3.  Build a small, smoldering fire. You want **smoke**, not a big flame. Do not let the fish get so hot that they "cook" quickly; you want them to dry out slowly.
    4.  Smoke them for 12 to 24 hours for long-term storage.
*   **Result:** The fish will be dark, firm, and have a strong smoky smell. This will last **2–3 weeks** if kept dry.

### Important Safety Rules:
1.  **If it smells "wrong":** If the fish smells like ammonia, or if it feels slimy rather than dry/hard, do not feed it to your family.
2.  **Keep it dry:** Once the fish is preserved, keep it in a paper bag or a cloth wrap. Never put it in plastic, or it will "sweat" and mold.
3.  **The "Thin" Rule:** The thinner you cut the fish, the faster it dries and the less likely it is to spoil before the process is finished.

If salt is expensive or hard to find, **Sun-Drying** is your best option, provided you can protect the fish from flies. If you can manage to get a large bag of salt, the **Salt Curing** method is the safest for your children, as it is the most "foolproof" against spoilage.
