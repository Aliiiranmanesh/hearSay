## 1. Start with a clear symptom “snapshot”

Write down a short, organized list. Think of it as a quick note you can copy‑paste into a text or email. Use the **S.O.A.P.** framework (Subjective‑Objective‑Assessment‑Plan) – it’s a format that most clinicians love and helps you stay focused.

| Section | What to note |
|---------|--------------|
| **Subjective** (what you feel) | “I’ve had a dull ache on the left side of my body for 8 months.” |
| **Objective** (what you notice) |  • Location (e.g., left chest, left abdomen, left thigh) <br>• Type of pain (sharp, dull, burning, pressure) <br>• Intensity (0‑10 scale, with 10 being worst) <br>• Duration (constant, intermittent, wakes me up) <br>• Any triggers (movement, eating, rest) <br>• Any relieving factors |
| **Assessment** (your own thoughts) | “I think it might be a muscle strain / maybe something else.” |
| **Plan** (what you want to do next) | “Would a phone call to my primary care doctor help? Or should I go to urgent care?” |

> **Pro tip:** If you’re unsure how “intense” the pain is, think of a familiar reference: “It feels like the pain I get when I fall off a bike and scrape my knee.” That gives clinicians a relatable baseline.

## 6. Managing the anxiety loop

You mentioned that you’ve been avoiding looking up the symptoms. That’s a common coping strategy when we’re scared of what we might find. A few quick exercises can help break that cycle:

1. **Set a timer**: Give yourself 10–15 minutes to read about the symptom, then close the tab.  
   *During this time, write down exactly what you found and any questions that arise.*

2. **Ask a trusted friend or family member**: Share your notes and ask if they see anything concerning.  
   *Sometimes, a fresh pair of eyes can filter out the worst‑case scenarios.*

3. **Schedule a “check‑in” appointment**: Even a 15‑minute virtual call can give you peace of mind and a plan.  
   *You can frame it as a “symptom review” rather than a full diagnosis.*

4. **Use breathing or grounding techniques** while you’re researching or waiting for a call.  
   *Practice 4‑7‑8 breathing: inhale 4 seconds, hold 7 seconds, exhale 8 seconds.*
