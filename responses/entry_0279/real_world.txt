Affected Groups: Deaf students in universities and higher education institutions in countries without developed sign language infrastructure or without consistent implementation of disability accommodation mandates, and who are experiencing the gap between the educational achievement that got them into university and the inaccessibility of the educational environment they have arrived in, and who are considering abandoning a goal they worked hard to reach because the institution was not designed for them. Disproportionately affecting deaf students from rural areas where sign language education was unavailable and who may be using a sign system with limited recognition at the university, students in countries where disability accommodation in higher education is legally required but practically unimplemented, students whose informal accommodation through a kind roommate is reaching the limits of what informal accommodation can sustain, and students who are asking a practical question about writing speed as a proxy for the larger question of whether they can stay.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Most high-income and many middle-income countries have disability discrimination legislation that requires universities to provide reasonable accommodation for deaf students, including sign language interpretation and captioning services. In practice, the availability of qualified interpreters, the cost of captioning services, and the institutional will to implement accommodation vary significantly, and students in countries with less developed disability rights infrastructure have less access to formal accommodation pathways. A professor speaking for two hours facing the board with no interpreter and no captioning is a violation of accommodation standards where those standards exist and an ordinary classroom in a context where they do not.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He worked hard to get here. He understood nothing from the two-hour lecture. His roommate is kind and is getting tired of writing everything down and he can see when she is tired even though she does not say it. He is asking how to write faster, which is asking whether the informal accommodation can be made to work well enough that he does not have to leave, because leaving is the alternative and he does not want to leave. The writing speed question deserves a real answer, and so does the larger question of what accommodations he can formally request.
