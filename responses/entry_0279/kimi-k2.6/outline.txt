### Immediate steps to take
**1. Contact your Disability/Accessibility Services office immediately.**
If you are not already registered, do so now. If you are registered but the accommodations are not working, escalate. Specifically request:
*   **ASL interpreters** (or the sign language you use) for every class.
*   **CART** (Communication Access Realtime Translation) — a professional captioner who types what is being said in real time.
*   **A professional notetaker** or access to lecture notes. Your school is required to provide this; it should never be your roommate’s job.
*   **An accommodation letter** sent to all professors stating they must face the class when speaking and not lecture with their back to you.

**2. Document everything.**
Write down the date your professor lectured facing the board for two hours. Write down the mocking behavior. Emails create a paper trail. If the university does not respond, this documentation is evidence that they are denying you access.

**3. Report the mocking as harassment.**
Being mocked for using sign language is harassment and discrimination. Report it to student conduct, the equity/Title IX office, or the dean of students. You have a right to be safe.

**4. Relieve your roommate.**
It sounds like she cares about you, but she is burning out because she is being used as a free accommodation. Thank her for her kindness, but transition to professional supports (interpreters, CART, notetakers) so your relationship can go back to being a friendship, not a service.

### About your question: “How can I write faster?”
I want to gently reframe this. If the system worked correctly, you would not be in a position where your only access depends on how fast you or your roommate can write.

*   **For talking with hearing people:** Use a text-to-speech app on a phone or tablet (like Google’s Live Transcribe, Ava, or Microsoft Translator’s conversation mode). You can type or sign into the device; it speaks or shows text. This is much faster than handwriting back and forth.
*   **For note-taking:** If you must take your own notes, a laptop or tablet with shorthand/text-expansion (like AutoHotkey or built-in keyboard shortcuts) is faster than handwriting. But again, **this should be temporary.** You are entitled to a notetaker so you can focus on learning through an interpreter or captions.
