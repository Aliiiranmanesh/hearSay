**Guidelines for use**

1. **Read the label** – Most OTC cough meds are “non‑drowsy,” but a small percentage contain codeine or diphenhydramine, which can make you groggy. Stick to the products listed above.
2. **Start low** – If you’re new to a medication, take the lowest effective dose for the first day or two to see how you react.
3. **Watch for side effects** – DXM can sometimes cause dizziness or a mild sense of “buzz.” Guaifenesin can cause a mild stomach upset.
4. **Avoid alcohol** – Even non‑drowsy cough meds can interact poorly with alcohol and increase the risk of CNS depression.
5. **Check drug interactions** – If you’re on prescription meds (e.g., antidepressants, blood pressure meds, or painkillers), ask a pharmacist or doctor before adding a cough syrup.

## Why a cough‑suppressant alone isn’t enough

- **Underlying exposure**: The dust you’re breathing in at the smelter is a known irritant/toxin that can cause chronic inflammation and fibrosis. A cough medicine will mask the cough but won’t stop the dust from damaging your lungs.
- **Early detection is key**: If your cough is due to a developing occupational lung disease, early treatment (e.g., corticosteroids, pulmonary rehab, or a change in work duties) can slow progression.
- **Workplace safety**: Even if you’re on the “pay‑check” side, you might still be eligible for workers’ compensation or occupational health benefits. These can cover medical monitoring and potentially help with school fees—check with your HR or local labor board.

## Practical next steps

1. **Schedule a medical check‑up** – Ask for a spirometry test and a chest X‑ray (or low‑dose CT if indicated). Bring your work history so the doctor can assess exposure.
2. **Talk to your employer** – Request a workplace medical review or occupational health assessment. Some companies offer “health‑check” programs for high‑risk workers.

6. **Use the cough medicine only as needed** – If you’re working a shift and the cough is intermittent, a single dose of a non‑drowsy suppressant can help you finish the day. If you’re at home and the cough persists, see a doctor.
