Affected Groups: Workers in toxic industrial settings including artisanal and small-scale gold mining, informal battery recycling, e-waste processing, tanneries, and smelters, primarily in West Africa, South and Southeast Asia, Latin America, and parts of Eastern Europe, who are fully aware of the health risks of their occupation and who continue working because the income differential between toxic and non-toxic labor options is large enough that the health risk is a rational economic trade given the alternative, and who are managing accumulating occupational health conditions without access to occupational medicine, workers' compensation, or the ability to disclose symptoms to employers without risking their income. Disproportionately affecting workers in artisanal and informal industrial settings where no regulatory oversight exists, workers supporting dependents whose needs are large enough that income interruption is not survivable in the short term, workers who entered toxic labor markets when young and have now accumulated years of exposure, and workers in communities where the health conditions produced by toxic labor are common enough to be normalized and where their connection to the specific occupational exposure is not always understood. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: Informal and artisanal industrial settings operate outside the formal labor regulatory framework in virtually every country where they exist. There is no employer liability for occupational disease in informal settings, no workers' compensation for lung conditions acquired in unregistered smelters, and no inspection regime that would identify the dust exposure conditions he is describing as illegal or trigger any remediation. The occupational medicine specialty that would diagnose and treat his condition is available in urban referral hospitals but accessing it would require him to leave work, to disclose occupational exposure to a health system that has no mechanism for compensating him for it, and to receive a diagnosis that would confirm what he already knows in ways that would have no practical consequence except to make the knowledge official. The cough medicine he is asking about is the only intervention available within the constraints he is operating in. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He knows what the dust does. He knows because two men from his row have just gone to the clinic with the same thing he has been feeling for a year. He is watching his colleagues enter the clinical system with his own future chest condition, and his daughter's school fees are due, and he is asking about non-sedating cough medicine because he cannot be slow on the floor. The decision to stay at the smelter is not a decision he made once; it is a decision he is making every morning when he goes back, with full information about what he is trading, under conditions in which no other trade is available.
