### Short‑answer  
**Go to a local “walk‑in” urgent‑care or a community health clinic that accepts cash.**  
Most of these places will let you get an X‑ray for a single fee (≈ $100–$200) without asking for insurance or a job contract. If you can’t afford that, look for a free or sliding‑scale clinic in your area.  

Below are the steps, costs, and practical tips to keep you as inexpensive as possible.

---

## 1. Quick on‑the‑spot assessment

| Symptom | What to check | What it means |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| **Visible deformity** (wrist looks twisted or out‑of‑place) | Look for any bump or angulation | High chance of fracture |
| **Swelling** | Feel for a firm or hard swelling | Could be a sprain or fracture |
| **Pain on pressure** | Press at the back of the wrist, along the forearm, and at the knuckles | Pain in a specific spot is a red‑flag |
| **Limited range of motion** | Try to bend, extend, and rotate the wrist | Loss of motion suggests injury |
| **Numbness or tingling** | Feel for pins‑and‑needles sensation | Possible nerve involvement |

**If any of the above are present, stop using the wrist (no lifting, no twisting) and keep it immobilized with a splint or a simple bandage.**  
If the injury feels mild (just pain, no deformity, no swelling), you can still get an X‑ray to be absolutely sure.

## 2. The cheapest imaging options

| Option | Typical cost | How to get it | What to bring |
|--------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Walk‑in urgent‑care** (e.g., *Crisis Care*, *Urgent Care Center*, *Walk‑in Clinic*) | $100–$200 (cash only) | Call ahead, walk in, pay at the front desk | Photo ID; if you have a health card or a copy of your pay stubs, bring it, but most places will only ask for ID and cash |
| **Community health clinic / Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC)** | Sliding scale (0–$50, depends on income) | Find one via **Health Resources & Services Administration** (HRSA) website | Photo ID, proof of income (pay stub, bank statement) – some may ask but it’s typically minimal |
| **Retail pharmacy X‑ray** (e.g., *CVS*, *Walgreens*, *Walmart* + *Kaiser*) | $80–$150 (cash only) | Call the pharmacy, ask for the “X‑ray service” | Photo ID; some pharmacies only need ID and cash |
| **Diagnostic lab (e.g., *CureMD*, *Radiology 24/7*)** | $120–$180 (cash only) | Call the lab, request a wrist X‑ray | Photo ID |
| **Telemedicine + local lab** (e.g., *Teladoc*, *Doctor on Demand*) | $100–$200 (cash or credit) | Video consult → doctor orders X‑ray at local lab | Photo ID, phone, a way to pay |

**Why this works**  
- **No insurance required**: Each of these places can bill you directly.  
- **No contract needed**: They don’t care if you’re employed or not.  
- **Fast turnaround**: You often get results the same day.

## 3. How to avoid paperwork

1. **Call ahead**: Ask the front desk if they accept cash and whether you need to provide any documents.  
2. **Bring the minimal ID**: A driver’s license or passport is usually enough.  
3. **Explain your situation**: “I’m injured, I need an X‑ray, I don’t have insurance.” Many places will still accept cash.  
4. **If the clinic asks for income proof**: Bring a recent pay stub or a bank statement. It’s only to verify your sliding‑scale eligibility.

## 4. Free or low‑cost community options

| Resource | How to find | What to expect |
|----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Local health department clinics** | Google “free health clinic near me” or check your city’s health department website | Often free or $10–$20; they may offer X‑rays if available |
| **University medical schools** | Search for “student clinic” | Free or low cost; students under supervision |
| **Non‑profit organizations** (e.g., *The Salvation Army*, *YMCA*, *St. Jude Medical Center*) | Call or visit their website | Some run health outreach clinics |

These options are usually **cash‑only** and don’t ask for insurance.

## 5. What to do if you can’t pay even the lowest fee

1. **Ask for a payment plan**: Many urgent‑care centers will allow you to pay over a few months.  
2. **Look for a “free walk‑in” clinic**: Some clinics run on a donation basis.  
3. **Contact a local charity**: Organizations like *Doctors Without Borders* or *HealthCare Without Borders* sometimes help with diagnostics.  
4. **Check if your employer has a workers’ compensation or short‑term disability program**: Even if there’s no formal contract, you may still be eligible for a claim.

| Sign | How to test | Decision |
