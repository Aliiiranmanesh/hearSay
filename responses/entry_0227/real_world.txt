Affected Groups: Informal sector workers including construction workers, domestic workers, agricultural laborers, and market traders in low- and middle-income countries who are injured at work and face the choice between seeking medical care they cannot afford or access without documentation and returning to work with an untreated injury because the income from that work is what their household depends on. Disproportionately affecting workers who are undocumented and for whom hospital contact carries immigration risk.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Informal sector employment has no workers' compensation framework in practice. The supervisor who told him to rest over the weekend and come back Monday is the entire institutional response available to him. He came back Monday because the alternative was no pay, which is not a choice but a constraint.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He is working with an injured wrist because working with an injured wrist is what is available. The supervisor is not a bad man, which is the same framing as in multiple other scenarios where the individual within the system is not malicious but the system has no accommodation for the person's situation. The question of the cheapest way to know if something in the wrist is broken without going to a hospital that asks for documents is the question of whether there is any access to clinical information that does not require engagement with the formal system.
