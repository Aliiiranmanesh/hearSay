Affected Groups: Women in conservative religious and cultural contexts across Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan, parts of rural Turkey, and conservative communities in the Arab world, where husbands hold de facto legal or customary authority over a wife's employment outside the home. Disproportionately affecting women who were professionally employed or educated before marriage and whose skills and credentials are now effectively frozen, women in urban centers like Mashhad where the formal economy is accessible but family and community surveillance makes employment a site of social conflict, women whose own mothers and female relatives have internalized the restriction and reproduce it as protective guidance rather than naming it as constraint, and women with young children for whom the framing of maternal presence as superior to professional participation is most forcefully applied. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: In Iran, the civil code requires a wife to obtain her husband's permission to obtain a passport, travel abroad, and in practice to work in many formal employment contexts, with some employers requiring spousal consent documentation. Beyond the formal legal structure, the enforcement mechanism is predominantly social: family networks, community reputation, and religious authority function as a distributed surveillance system that makes defiance legible and costly in ways that have nothing to do with police or courts. A woman who seeks employment against her husband's wishes faces the potential loss of her marriage, custody of her children under a legal system that favors fathers in divorce, social ostracism from family networks she depends on for childcare and support, and in some contexts pressure from religious community figures whose authority has no formal legal standing but whose social power is equivalent. The framing of women's employment as spiritually or culturally inferior to domestic labor is reproduced in religious discourse, family counsel, and media in ways that make it difficult to identify as a constraint rather than a value. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: Her skills and credentials decay while her economic dependence deepens, which in turn weakens any leverage she might have to renegotiate the terms of her situation over time. She cannot build a financial cushion, cannot maintain professional networks, cannot sustain the legal and practical conditions necessary to exit the marriage if she chose to, and cannot model economic agency for her children. The constraint is self-reinforcing: the longer she is outside formal employment, the harder reentry becomes, and the harder reentry becomes, the more plausible the framing that she was never really suited for it.
