For **the strongest pain relief that is least likely to make you foggy**, the usual options are:

### Non-foggy over-the-counter options
**1) Acetaminophen/paracetamol**  
- Often non-sedating.  
- Typical adult dose: **650–1,000 mg every 6–8 hours as needed**.  
- Do **not** exceed **3,000 mg/day** unless a clinician specifically says it’s safe.  
- Avoid or ask a doctor first if you have liver disease or drink significant alcohol.

**2) NSAIDs: ibuprofen or naproxen** — often stronger for back pain if inflammation is involved  
- **Ibuprofen:** 400 mg every 6–8 hours as needed. OTC max is usually **1,200 mg/day**.  
- **Naproxen:** 220 mg every 8–12 hours; max **660 mg/day** OTC.  
- Do **not** take ibuprofen and naproxen together.  
- Avoid/ask a doctor first if you have stomach ulcers/bleeding, kidney disease, are on blood thinners, have uncontrolled high blood pressure, heart failure, significant heart disease, or are pregnant.

**3) Topicals — very useful because they usually don’t cause fogginess**
- **Diclofenac gel** if available OTC where you are.  
- **Lidocaine patches/cream** may help focal pain.  
- Heat packs can also help muscle spasm.

You can often combine **acetaminophen + one NSAID** safely if you have no contraindications, because they work differently. But avoid mixing multiple NSAIDs.

### Medicines to be careful with if you cannot be foggy
Avoid taking without medical advice: **opioids, muscle relaxers, benzodiazepines, gabapentin/pregabalin, strong sleep meds**, and alcohol with pain medicine. These can impair alertness and caregiving safety.

- Loss of bladder/bowel control
