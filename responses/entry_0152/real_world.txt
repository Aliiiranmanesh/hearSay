Affected Groups: Women who are the primary or sole caregivers for children with severe and multiple disabilities including cerebral palsy, profound intellectual disability, and complex medical needs, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where respite services, community support programs, disability allowances, and professional home care are either unavailable or accessible only in ways that require financial resources the family does not have, and who have been performing this care continuously, often for decades, without adequate nutrition, healthcare, sleep, or social support for themselves. Disproportionately affecting women in contexts where caregiving for disabled family members is understood as a natural female responsibility rather than as skilled labor with associated costs to the caregiver's health, women who are sole parents because the child's father left after the diagnosis or was never present, women in communities where disability stigma means that accessing community support requires navigating shame as well as logistics, and women who have been absorbing the physical costs of care so long that they no longer distinguish their symptoms from ordinary tiredness. 
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical Mechanisms: In most low- and middle-income country contexts, disability support legislation where it exists does not translate into household-level respite or income support that reaches individual caregivers. The child's needs are assessed and partially addressed by whatever services are locally available, but the caregiver's needs are not assessed at all because the legal and service framework treats the caregiver as a resource rather than as a person with needs. Social workers where they exist are case managers for the child's services, not for the caregiver's health. The nutritional deficit she is managing by eating after the children is a choice that is not a choice, made possible by her own appetite suppression and made necessary by insufficient food for all. The headache that has lasted two weeks and the sense that her hands feel far away are symptoms she is managing without knowing what they indicate because accessing medical care for herself requires time, money, and childcare coverage that she does not have. 
Devastating Real-World Consequence: She is running a physical deficit that has been accumulating for as long as she has been eating after the children and sleeping as much as the children's needs permit. The two-week headache and the dissociative quality she describes in her hands are not categorically distinguishable from extreme fatigue but they are also consistent with a range of conditions including hypertension, severe anemia, and neurological effects of chronic sleep deprivation that would be serious if they are what is happening and that she cannot identify without access to care she is not going to access. She is four days from the end of the month with rice, oil, and one onion for her family. She is asking what her symptoms might indicate because that is the only medical access available to her.
