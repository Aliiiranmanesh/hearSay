Affected Groups: Young people transitioning from gender-segregated schooling systems, common in parts of the Middle East, South Asia, and some religious school systems globally, into mixed-gender university or professional environments, who face a social and interactional transition that is as significant as any they have experienced but that has no formal acknowledgment, no preparation curriculum, and no institutional support structure, because the system that educated them treated segregation as natural and therefore produced no framework for navigating its end.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: Gender-segregated schooling systems were designed without any transition planning for the moment when graduates enter mixed-gender environments, because the systems' ideological framework treats segregation as permanent or treats the transition as something that should not be necessary. The university does not acknowledge that students from segregated systems are making a significant transition. There is no orientation module for this.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He graduated first in his class and he does not know where to look or how to speak in a lecture hall where everyone sits together. This is not a failure of intelligence or character; it is the precise outcome of an educational system that produced academic excellence and social inexperience in mixed-gender contexts simultaneously, and that provided no bridge between the two. The question of whether it gets easier on its own or whether there is something he should be doing is the question of whether accumulated exposure is sufficient or whether there is a learnable skill he is missing access to.
