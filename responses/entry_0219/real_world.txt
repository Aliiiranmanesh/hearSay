Affected Groups: People who have undergone significant intellectual or philosophical change, particularly religious deconversion, in family and community contexts where the original belief system is central to family identity, social belonging, and the relationship between the person and everyone they love, and who have correctly assessed that disclosure of the change would cause more harm than permanent concealment, and who are therefore living an ongoing performance of a belief they do not hold. Disproportionately affecting people in religious communities where apostasy carries formal religious consequences, people from families where the parent's emotional relationship to the faith is so total that the person has correctly calculated that disclosure would be experienced as a greater loss than death, and people who are in the middle years of a life-long concealment that has no endpoint and no relief valve.
Legal, Bureaucratic, or Physical 
Mechanisms: In countries where apostasy is criminalized including in parts of the Muslim world, disclosure is a legal risk as well as a social one. In countries where it is not criminalized, the enforcement mechanism is entirely social and familial and is no less total for that. The Friday prayer his father leads and his brothers follow is a weekly ritual that requires his full physical and verbal participation in something he has not meant since he was nineteen, and there is no accommodation available, no absence that would not itself be an admission.
Devastating Real-World Consequence: He has read everything he was not supposed to read and arrived somewhere his family would not recognize, and he is alone in that place with no one who knows he is there. He is performing the identity that was given to him so completely that the performance is indistinguishable from the person his family knows, which means the person his family knows does not exist, and he does. The question of whether it is possible to live an entire life as a translation of yourself and not lose the original is the question of whether the self that exists inside the performance survives the performance.
